\section{Growing-depth collision geometry}

Let \(\PP_Q\) be the primes in \((Q,2Q)\), put \(h=4LQ\), and assume
\(L<Q/4\).  Every \(q\in\PP_Q\) is a unit modulo \(h\), and every active
multiplier has magnitude below \(2L<q\).  Thus each row is internally
injective.

\begin{lemma}[One-wrap normal form]
If distinct rows \(q_1,q_2\in\PP_Q\) share a residue, then their multipliers
have opposite signs and, for positive \(a,b<2L\),
\[
 a q_2+b q_1=4LQ. \tag{1}
\]
Moreover \((a,b)=1\), at most two rows occupy one residue, and every channel
has exactly two residues related by global sign.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
A shared residue gives
\[
 m_1q_2-m_2q_1\equiv0\pmod h. \tag{2}
\]
For equal signs, the magnitude in (2) is below \(h\).  Equality to zero would
force an active prime to divide a nonzero multiplier shorter than that prime.
Hence the signs are opposite.  The resulting positive sum is below \(2h\),
so its only possible positive multiple of \(h\) is \(h\), proving (1).

Primitivity gives \((a,h)=(b,h)=1\).  A common divisor of \(a,b\) would divide
the right side of (1), and is therefore one.  If three rows occupied a
residue, two multipliers would have the same sign, contradicting the first
part.  Finally, changing both multiplier signs gives a second residue.  The
two cannot coincide because that would make \(h\) divide a nonzero integer
of magnitude below \(4L\).  No third residue is possible by the same
classification.
\end{proof}

This lemma upgrades the first \(3\)--\(7\) collision into a complete
growing-depth channel compiler.  It does not assert that any particular
channel contains prime solutions.
