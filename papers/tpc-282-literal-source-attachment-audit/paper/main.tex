\begin{abstract}
The typed $L^2$ interface in the preceding stage leaves a source-identification
question: does the actual comparison-weight readout see the projected output
of the literal prime-shell operator?  This note answers the question on the
registered finite schedule without replacing the source by a model functional.
For each of six growing-cutoff scales and both recorded kernel exponents, we
recompute the literal V59 operator, the three-contrast block projection, the
source profile, and the projected output.  Outward interval arithmetic certifies
that the actual scalar attachment $C=\langle w_\perp,S\rangle$ is separated
from zero on all twelve rows: eleven are negative and one is positive.  The
smallest normalized attachment coefficient $C^2/(\|w_\perp\|^2\|S\|^2)$ is
only about $3.36\times 10^{-5}$.  Thus the finite source lock is real but weak;
it supplies neither a uniform asymptotic nondegeneracy theorem nor fixed-power
credit.  An independent exact replay and hostile mutation audit are included.
\end{abstract}

\section{Question and scope}

The four-packet analysis records a source vector $\beta$, a literal finite
operator $A$, and a block projection $P_3$.  The output used by the signed
reassembly is
\[
  S=(I-P_3)A\beta.
\]
The arithmetic readout is not an arbitrary dual vector.  It is the actual
comparison-weight vector $w$ projected onto the same transverse space:
\[
  w_\perp=(I-P_3)w,\qquad C=\langle w_\perp,S\rangle.
\]
The purpose of this paper is to audit this scalar on the frozen finite source
chain.  It is not to infer a power law from a finite table.  The distinction is
important because the typed Gate-B interface of TPC-281 requires an additional
source-identification or nondegeneracy statement; the present computation
tests the literal object that such a statement would have to control.  The
packet and typed-interface inputs are the registered artifacts of
TPC-275 and TPC-281~\cite{tpc275,tpc281}.

The physical matrix has entries, for distinct indices $u,t$,
\begin{equation}
 A(u,t)=\sum_{q\in\mathcal Q}qK_H(u-t)
  {\bf 1}_{q\nmid u}{\bf 1}_{q\nmid t}
  \left({\bf 1}_{u\equiv t\pmod q}-\frac{1}{q-1}\right),
 \label{eq:operator}
\end{equation}
with $K_H(h)=H^{2s}(H^2+h^2)^{-s}$.  The six triples $(X,H,Q)$ are
\[
 (64,15,4),(96,20,5),(128,24,5),(192,32,6),
 (256,38,6),(384,50,7),
\]
and the comparison cutoff is the registered growing cutoff from the upstream
source certificate.  The source interval is the outward interval for the
literal prime-power weight minus the finite comparison profile.

\section{Projection formula and attachment coefficient}

Let the index interval be divided into four consecutive blocks of size $b$.
The three contrast vectors are
\[
 a_1=(1,1,-1,-1),\quad a_2=(1,-1,0,0),\quad
 a_3=(0,0,1,-1),
\]
with denominators $4b,2b,2b$, respectively.  For a block-summed vector $z$,
the projection energy is the sum of squared contrast sums divided by these
denominators.  Orthogonality therefore gives the following exact identity.

\begin{proposition}[source-lock identity]
For the literal output $g=A\beta$ and source weight $w$,
\[
 C=\langle w,g\rangle-\langle P_3w,P_3g\rangle
   =\langle (I-P_3)w,(I-P_3)g\rangle.
\]
If $W=\|w_\perp\|^2>0$ and $Y=\|S\|^2>0$, then the normalized attachment
coefficient is
\[
 \rho^2=\frac{C^2}{WY},\qquad 0\leq\rho^2\leq 1.
 \label{eq:rho}
\]
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
The three declared contrasts are mutually orthogonal after block expansion.
Writing the orthogonal projection as the sum of the three rank-one pieces
gives the first equality.  The second follows from $S=(I-P_3)g$ and
$I-P_3$ being self-adjoint and idempotent.  The final inequality is
Cauchy--Schwarz.
\end{proof}

The certificate evaluates $C$ and $W$ with outward intervals.  The matrix,
$\beta$, and $S$ are rational, so $Y$ is exact rational arithmetic.  A row is
called \emph{sign-separated} when the interval for $C$ lies strictly on one
side of zero.  This is a finite, reproducible predicate rather than an
asymptotic assertion.

\section{Finite source-lock result}

\begin{theorem}[registered literal source attachment]
On the twelve registered rows $(X,H,Q,s)$, every interval for $C$ is
sign-separated, every $W$ and $Y$ is positive, and every interval for $\rho^2$
lies strictly inside $(0,1)$.  The sign census is eleven negative rows and one
positive row.  The smallest lower endpoint of $\rho^2$ occurs at
$(X,H,Q,s)=(256,38,6,2)$ and is
\[
 \frac{167799905983}{5000000000000000}
 \approx 3.357\times10^{-5}.
\]
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
The executable certificate uses the exact operator in \eqref{eq:operator},
the locked source intervals, and the three projection contrasts.  Its
independent replay reconstructs the same quantities without importing the
producer.  The twelve resulting intervals are listed in Table~\ref{tab:rows};
their endpoints separate zero and the lower endpoint of each $\rho^2$ is
positive.  Comparing the twelve rational lower endpoints gives the stated
minimum.  The source code also binds each row to the exact signed output energy
in the TPC-275 certificate.
\end{proof}

\section{What the result does and does not buy}

The finite result resolves one ambiguity left by an abstract equal-norm
functional: the actual source readout is genuinely present on each tested row.
It does not resolve the uniform question.  In particular, the minimum of
$\rho^2$ is roughly four orders of magnitude below one, and the sign is not
constant.  No inequality of the form
\[
 |C_X|\geq cX^{-\theta}
 \quad\text{or}\quad \rho_X^2\geq cX^{-\kappa}
 \label{eq:open}
\]
is proved here.  Such an inequality would have to be uniform on the same
growing source, shell, height, and projection schedule.

The distinction also matters for TPC-281.  A typed operator estimate
$\|A_X\|_{2\to2}\leq KX^{-\sigma}$ controls an output from above, while
the scalar $C$ identifies one particular source direction.  The present
certificate supplies finite evidence for that identification, but neither the
operator estimate nor a power-saving lower bound for the attachment.  Therefore
the arithmetic $L^2$ gate, fixed-power credit, and full Gate B remain open.

\section{Reproducibility and claim firewall}

The producer hash-locks the TPC-275 producer and canonical result, as well as
the TPC-268 source engine that defines the comparison intervals.  It reruns all
twelve rows and writes canonical sorted-key JSON.  The independent checker
reconstructs the matrix output, block projection, scalar, source norm, exact
output norm, and cosine interval directly.  A hostile checker mutates an
endpoint, sign, cosine interval, provenance hash, budget flag, and row census;
all six mutations are rejected.  Normal and optimized Python executions are
required to have empty standard error and byte-identical output.

These checks establish a finite numerical certificate.  They do not turn a
finite source scan into a theorem uniform in $X$, and they do not claim a
twin-prime conclusion.

\section{Conclusion}

The literal source attachment is now source-locked on the complete registered
TPC-275 schedule: it is nonzero on twelve rows, with eleven negative and one
positive signs.  The strongest obstruction is quantitative rather than binary:
the weakest normalized attachment is small.  The next natural problem is to
compute the exact perturbation radius at which this attachment can vanish and
to test whether the observed weak margin is stable under nearby source choices.
