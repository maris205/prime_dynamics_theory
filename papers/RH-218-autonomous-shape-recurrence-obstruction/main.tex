\title{An Autonomous Recurrence Obstruction for Finite Shape Orbits\\
\large Holdout Failure Versus Exact Polynomial Interpolation}
\author{Liang Wang}\date{July 2026}
\begin{document}\maketitle
\begin{abstract}
The physical quartet shape has one finite monotone coordinate and an exact
anisotropic coefficient geometry.  It is tempting to infer a
scale-independent recurrence.  We test that inference on the equal-log-step
dyadic subsequence
$\sigma=0.04,0.02,0.01,0.005,0.0025,0.00125$.

A two-dimensional affine map fitted separately to the first three
transitions interpolates its training data to $10^{-16}$, yet its maximum
error on the final two transitions is $0.0426$ on the left and $0.0506$ on
the right.  A pooled two-channel affine map has training error $0.00205$ and
holdout error $0.04685$.  Among scalar axial recurrences, a power-gap
transform is best on both channels, but its holdout RMS error remains about
$0.0159$.

We then prove an exact non-identifiability theorem: every finite
two-dimensional orbit whose current axial coordinates are distinct is an
orbit of an autonomous polynomial map.  Lagrange interpolation constructs
$F(u,\eta)=(P(u),Q(u))$ with $F(x_k)=x_{k+1}$.  The physical six-state orbit
therefore admits a degree-four exact map, with residual below $2\times10^{-15}$,
regardless of whether an underlying semigroup exists.

The combined verdict is negative and structural.  The tested low-complexity
autonomous maps do not extrapolate reliably, while unrestricted finite
recurrence existence has no identifying power.  No scale semigroup or
continuous generator is obtained.
\end{abstract}

\section{The stronger claim behind a shape clock}

RH-214 establishes a finite monotone axial ordering, and RH-217 shows that
the coefficient map increasingly suppresses transverse motion
\citep{WangRH214,WangRH217}.  Neither statement implies that equal increments
of log scale are generated by one map.  That stronger hypothesis would be
\begin{equation}\label{eq:autonomous}
 x_{n+1}=F(x_n),qquad x_n=(u_n,\eta_n),
\end{equation}
where $F$ is independent of $n$, channel, and scale.

An autonomous map becomes useful for Gate A only if it has more than
interpolation value.  It should extrapolate, remain stable as data are added,
respect both channels, and ideally embed into a semigroup $F_t$ with
$F_{t+s}=F_t\circ F_s$.

\section{Two-dimensional affine audit}

Let
\begin{equation}\label{eq:affinemap}
 F(x)=Ax+b,qquad A\in\mathbb R^{2\times2},\quad b\in\mathbb R^2.
\end{equation}
There are six scalar parameters.  Three two-dimensional transitions provide
exactly six scalar equations in generic position, so channelwise training
interpolation should not be mistaken for evidence.

On the left, the fitted matrix and offset are approximately
\begin{equation}
 A_L=\begin{pmatrix}0.8505&-0.0746\\0.0731&-0.1027\end{pmatrix},
 \qquad b_L=\binom{0.1885}{-0.1297}.
\end{equation}
Its training residual is $7.9\times10^{-17}$, while its two holdout state
errors have maximum $0.04256$.  On the right, the maximum is $0.05058$.

A pooled fit uses six training transitions and one common map:
\begin{equation}
 A_{LR}=\begin{pmatrix}0.8618&-0.0793\\0.0773&-0.1044\end{pmatrix},
 \qquad b_{LR}=\binom{0.1843}{-0.1324}.
\end{equation}
Its maximum training error is $0.00205$, but its maximum holdout error is
$0.04685$.

\begin{proposition}[Finite affine failure]\label{prop:affinefail}
The tested equal-step affine maps do not provide stable out-of-sample
transport of the physical shape orbit: every channelwise or pooled holdout
maximum exceeds $0.04$.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
This is the frozen least-squares audit above.  The proposition concerns only
the declared affine class and split.
\end{proof}

\section{Why exact finite recurrence is non-identifying}

The opposite danger is to enlarge the model class until interpolation is
guaranteed.

\begin{theorem}[Finite-orbit interpolation]\label{thm:interpolation}
Let
\begin{equation}
 x_k=(u_k,\eta_k)\in\mathbb R^2,qquad k=0,\ldots,n,
\end{equation}
and suppose $u_0,\ldots,u_{n-1}$ are distinct.  Then there exists an
autonomous polynomial map $F:\mathbb R^2\to\mathbb R^2$ of the form
\begin{equation}
 F(u,\eta)=(P(u),Q(u))
\end{equation}
with degrees at most $n-1$ such that
\begin{equation}
 F(x_k)=x_{k+1},\qquad k=0,\ldots,n-1.
\end{equation}
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Let $L_k(u)$ be the Lagrange basis polynomials for the distinct nodes
$u_0,\ldots,u_{n-1}$.  Define
\begin{equation}
 P(u)=\sum_{k=0}^{n-1}u_{k+1}L_k(u),
 \qquad
 Q(u)=\sum_{k=0}^{n-1}\eta_{k+1}L_k(u).
\end{equation}
Then $P(u_k)=u_{k+1}$ and $Q(u_k)=\eta_{k+1}$.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}\label{cor:noidentify}
The mere existence of an autonomous map fitting a finite monotone shape
orbit provides no evidence for a physical semigroup.
\end{corollary}

For each six-state physical channel, Theorem~\ref{thm:interpolation} gives a
degree-four map fitting all five transitions.  The numerical construction
has maximum residual below $2\times10^{-15}$.

\section{What would identify a semigroup}

A meaningful positive result would require constraints not supplied by
finite interpolation, for example:
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=2em]
\item a map fixed before additional scales are computed;
\item a uniform one-step error tending to zero;
\item consistency across both channels and alternative discretizations;
\item a composition estimate comparing one double step with two single
steps;
\item compactness or regularity sufficient to extract a limiting map;
\item for a continuous generator, embeddability into $F_t$.
\end{enumerate}

The current data satisfy none of these at theorem level.  Exact polynomial
interpolation actually underscores why such additional structure is needed.

\section{Route decision and claim boundary}

Further high-degree recurrence fitting to the same fixed quartet is
deprioritized.  The next question is structural: even if one perfectly
controlled this four-root orbit, could it generate the growing zero count
required of an infinite determinant?  RH-219 answers that question.

This paper identifies no scale semigroup, continuous generator, Fredholm
operator, or Gate-A closure.  Gates B--E and all zeta/RH statements remain
untouched.
