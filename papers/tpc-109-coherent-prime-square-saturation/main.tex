\title{\bfseries Coherent Prime-Square Blocks on Literal Residual Fibers:\\
Exact Gram Geometry, Sharp Saturation, and a Route-Switch Certificate}
\author{Liang Wang}
\date{July 2026}

\begin{document}

\begin{abstract}
In the literal fixed-shift residual carrier, the terminal-prime
coordinate is injective before it is forgotten, while the physical
output coherently adds all terminal primes in one residual fiber.
We isolate one actual fiber \(y=(\gamma,s,j)\), write its inherited
amplitudes as \(b_{y,p}\), and study the prime square
\[
  \cG_y=\left|\sum_{p\in\cP_y}b_{y,p}\right|^2
\]
against the resolved diagonal
\(\cD_y=\sum_p|b_{y,p}|^2\).
The exact Gram operator is the rank-one all-ones matrix.  For fixed
moduli \(a_{y,p}=|b_{y,p}|\), the complete free-phase range is
\[
 \left[(2a_{y,\max}-R_y)_+^2,\ R_y^2\right],
 \qquad R_y=\sum_pa_{y,p}.
\]
Consequently the usual Bessel bound
\(\cG_y\le|\cP_y|\cD_y\) is sharp, and no coefficient-blind
improvement follows from terminal-prime injectivity, support size,
occupancy, or the diagonal energy.  Equal moduli already admit both
perfect saturation and maximal alignment on the same resolved
support.

We give an exact coefficient-specific route certificate in terms of
the physical alignment ratio, and a dominant-atom lower certificate.
Applied to the literal TPC residual map, these are \(\Lone\)
identities and stop/go tests.  They do not estimate the actual
growing coefficients and therefore provide no \(\Ltwo\) fixed-shift
arithmetic saving, parity breakthrough, prime-pair asymptotic, or
twin-prime conclusion.
\end{abstract}

\medskip
\noindent\textbf{Keywords:} coherent prime square; residual fiber;
rank-one Gram matrix; phase saturation; fixed shift; parity barrier.

\section{Question and claim boundary}

TPC-MVP1 lists a coherent prime-square test as an alternative attack
on the generic signed gate \citep{WangMVP1}.  The phrase
``prime square'' here means the squared norm of a coherent sum over
terminal primes, not a restriction to integers \(p^2\).

The literal residual map of TPC-55 first keeps the terminal-prime
label and then forgets it \citep{WangTPC55}.  At one prescribed
\(h_0\ne0\), its physical output fiber has key
\[
 y=(\gamma,s,j),\qquad
 \cP_y=\{p:\ (\gamma,s,j,p)\text{ is active}\}.
\]
The terminal-prime coordinate is injective before the forgetful map.
Thus every \(p\in\cP_y\) has one inherited complex amplitude
\(b_{y,p}\), but all of them are coherently added at the output.
The static-mask and resolved-energy results upstream do not estimate
this final coherent sum \citep{WangTPC50,WangTPC55}.

This paper answers the coefficient-blind part of the question
completely.  It does not claim a bound for the literal amplitudes as
\(X\to\infty\).

\section{The literal block and its exact Gram operator}

\begin{definition}[Resolved and coherent energies]
For a nonempty finite fiber \(\cP_y\), put
\[
 k_y=|\cP_y|,\qquad
 \cD_y=\sum_{p\in\cP_y}|b_{y,p}|^2,\qquad
 \cG_y=\left|\sum_{p\in\cP_y}b_{y,p}\right|^2.
\]
Also write
\[
 a_{y,p}=|b_{y,p}|,\qquad
 R_y=\sum_pa_{y,p},\qquad
 a_{y,\max}=\max_pa_{y,p},
\]
and
\[
 d_y=(2a_{y,\max}-R_y)_+.
\]
The zero fiber is treated separately, with all quantities equal to
zero.
\end{definition}

\begin{proposition}[Exact coherent-prime Gram identity]
\label{prop:gram}
Order \(\cP_y=\{p_1,\dots,p_{k_y}\}\), put
\(b_y=(b_{y,p_i})_i\), and let
\(\mathbf J_{k_y}=\one\one^*\).  Then
\[
 \boxed{\cG_y=b_y^*\mathbf J_{k_y}b_y.}
\]
The spectrum of \(\mathbf J_{k_y}\) is
\(\{k_y,0,\dots,0\}\).  Hence
\[
 0\le\cG_y\le k_y\cD_y,
\]
and the upper constant \(k_y\) is optimal.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
The quadratic form expands to
\[
 b_y^*\one\one^*b_y
 =|\one^*b_y|^2
 =\left|\sum_pb_{y,p}\right|^2.
\]
The spectral statement is immediate.  Cauchy--Schwarz gives the
upper bound, and equality holds when all entries are equal.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}[Where the obstruction sits]
Prime-resolved injectivity controls the map before \(\mathbf J_k\)
is applied.  It does not diagonalize \(\mathbf J_k\).  Distinct
terminal primes are precisely the off-diagonal modes inside this
rank-one quadratic form.
\end{remark}

\section{Sharp phase saturation}

\begin{theorem}[Complete free-phase interval]
\label{thm:interval}
Fix a fiber and its moduli \((a_p)_{p\in\cP}\).  Allow the phases of
\(b_p=a_pe^{i\theta_p}\) to vary independently.  Then
\[
 \boxed{
 \left\{\left|\sum_pb_p\right|^2:
        \theta_p\in\mathbb R\right\}
 =
 [d^2,R^2],}
 \qquad
 d=(2a_{\max}-R)_+.
\]
Every point of the interval is attained.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
The possible lengths of a polygonal sum of segments of prescribed
lengths \(a_p\) form the interval
\([d,R]\).  The lower endpoint is the reverse triangle inequality,
attained by placing a longest segment opposite all others; when the
longest segment does not dominate, the segments close to a polygon
and the lower endpoint is zero.  Continuous rotation of one segment,
together with induction on the number of segments, fills the whole
interval.  Squaring gives the result.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[Sharp saturation dichotomy]
\label{cor:dichotomy}
The moduli alone force a positive coherent square if and only if
\[
 a_{\max}>\frac{R}{2}.
\]
In that case every phase assignment satisfies
\[
 \cG\ge(2a_{\max}-R)^2.
\]
If \(a_{\max}\le R/2\), the strongest universal modulus-only lower
bound is zero.
\end{corollary}

\begin{corollary}[Same-support nonidentifiability]
\label{cor:roots}
For every \(k\ge2\), there are two coefficient vectors on the same
\(k\)-prime support with the same unit moduli and the same diagonal
energy \(k\), but with coherent squares \(0\) and \(k^2\),
respectively.
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
Use the \(k\)-th roots of unity in the first vector and \(k\) copies
of \(1\) in the second.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}[Physical versus comparison phases]
The lower bound \(d_y^2\) applies to the literal fiber because it uses
its actual moduli.  Attainability in \cref{thm:interval} belongs to
the larger free-phase comparison class.  It does not say that the
arithmetic coefficient realizes a root-of-unity phase pattern.
\end{remark}

\section{Coefficient-specific certificates}

The finite geometry identifies exactly which additional statistic a
coherent-prime argument must control.

\begin{definition}[Alignment and cancellation ratios]
For \(\cD_y>0\), set
\[
 \alpha_y=\frac{\cG_y}{k_y\cD_y}\in[0,1],
 \qquad
 \beta_y=\frac{\cG_y}{R_y^2}\in[0,1].
\]
For a finite family \(\cB\) of disjoint fibers, define
\[
 \cD_{\cB}=\sum_{y\in\cB}\cD_y,\qquad
 \cG_{\cB}=\sum_{y\in\cB}\cG_y,\qquad
 K_{\cB}=\max_{y\in\cB}k_y.
\]
\end{definition}

\begin{proposition}[Exact block go certificate]
\label{prop:go}
For every nonzero block,
\[
 \frac{\cG_{\cB}}{K_{\cB}\cD_{\cB}}
 =
 \sum_{y\in\cB}
 \frac{k_y\cD_y}{K_{\cB}\cD_{\cB}}\,\alpha_y.
\]
In particular, a coefficient-specific improvement
\[
 \cG_{\cB}\le\varepsilon_X K_{\cB}\cD_{\cB}
\]
is equivalent to the displayed weighted alignment average being at
most \(\varepsilon_X\).
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Substitute \(\cG_y=k_y\cD_y\alpha_y\) and sum.
\end{proof}

\begin{proposition}[Dominant-prime lower certificate]
\label{prop:dominance}
Let \(\cB_\epsilon\) be the fibers satisfying
\[
 a_{y,\max}\ge\left(\frac12+\epsilon\right)R_y.
\]
Then
\[
 \boxed{
 \cG_{\cB}
 \ge4\epsilon^2
 \sum_{y\in\cB_\epsilon}R_y^2.}
\]
This bound is phase-independent and sharp at the single-fiber level.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
On such a fiber,
\(d_y=2a_{y,\max}-R_y\ge2\epsilon R_y\).
Apply \cref{thm:interval} and sum.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}[Upper saving and lower survival are different tasks]
A dispersion route may seek small \(\alpha_y\), while an
energy-survival route may seek a positive dominant share.  The same
unsigned occupancy statement cannot supply both.  The two directions
must be labeled separately.
\end{remark}

\section{Application to the TPC-109 queue item}

On the literal TPC-55 carrier, the amplitudes \(b_{y,p}\) include the
source coefficient, masks, terminal-prime weight, fixed \(h_0\), and
all retained profile factors.  Therefore
\cref{prop:gram,thm:interval,prop:go,prop:dominance} apply without
changing the physical output map.  This is the positive content of
the test.

The test also gives a rigorous stop certificate:

\begin{theorem}[TPC-109 route decision]
\label{thm:decision}
Terminal-prime injectivity, the exact active prime support,
subpower fiber multiplicity, and the resolved diagonal energy do not
imply any coefficient-blind improvement over
\[
 \cG_{\cB}\le K_{\cB}\cD_{\cB}.
\]
Nor do they imply a positive lower comparison
\(\cG_{\cB}\gg\cD_{\cB}\).
Any improvement in either direction must use one of:
\begin{enumerate}[label=(\roman*),leftmargin=2.2em]
\item the literal phase relation among the actual \(b_{y,p}\);
\item a proved dominant-prime share;
\item an arithmetic average that is subsequently localized back to
the same prescribed \(h_0\); or
\item cancellation across a larger physical reassembly that retains
all provenance.
\end{enumerate}
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
The upper bound and its sharpness are
\cref{prop:gram}.  The lower obstruction is
\cref{cor:roots}.  These examples retain support, moduli, diagonal
energy, and multiplicity.  Hence none of those unsigned invariants
can decide the coherent square.
\end{proof}

The coherent-prime corridor therefore remains open only as a
\emph{coefficient-specific} route.  TPC-110 should not recycle the
resolved diagonal as if it were the post-bin determinant energy.  It
must analyze the exact binning kernel and the angle of the literal
coefficient to that kernel.

\paragraph{Explicit exclusions.}
Nothing in this paper proves the generic affine M\"obius hypothesis
of TPC-MVP1, the determinant-energy bound, the distinguished
zero-mode bound, the compatibility
\(\lambda_D\le2\eta_Z\), the independent physical endpoint
\(\Lambda_{\rm phys}<1/400\), a Hardy--Littlewood asymptotic, or the
twin-prime conjecture.  No statement is specialized to \(h_0=2\).
