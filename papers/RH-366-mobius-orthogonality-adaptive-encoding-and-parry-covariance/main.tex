\begin{abstract}
On the certified local survivor of the area-preserving H\'enon map
$\Hmap(x,y)=(1-6x^2-y,x)$, we isolate a rigorous typical--exceptional
M\"obius dichotomy.  The survivor is conjugate to a primitive four-state
subshift whose scalar sign code forbids two plus signs at distance two.
Every fixed periodic point is M\"obius-orthogonal for every continuous
observable, and Parry-almost every point is simultaneously orthogonal for
all continuous observables.  In contrast, an explicitly M\"obius-adapted
point, chosen after the full positive-time arithmetic sequence is read, has
raw sign correlation $4/\pi^2$.  For the centered variance-one sign
observable we prove zero covariance at odd lags, covariance
$(-\varphi^{-2})^k$ at lag $2k$, the exact finite-prefix variance formula,
and the unconditional bound $V_N\leq\sqrt5N$.  The density limit
$V_N/N\to6/\pi^2$ remains conditional on ordinary Ces\`aro two-point
Chowla.  The finite-horizon adaptive capacity is exactly two path
maximum-weight-independent-set problems, computable in $O(N)$, with
$4/\pi^2\leq\liminf K_N/N\leq\limsup K_N/N\leq6/\pi^2$.
A frozen $N=2^{20}$ ordering audit returns $p=0.4111$, a scoped negative
finite result.  Adaptive encoding is not spontaneous arithmetic coupling,
an operator trace, or a zeta bridge.  Gates A--E remain false/open.
\end{abstract}

\section{Frozen object and claim boundary}

The RH-1--RH-361 foundation is preserved by the four-volume synthesis
\cite{WangRHMVP22026}.  RH-364 froze a certified local hyperbolic survivor
for the H\'enon map and audited a separate engineered prime lift
\cite{WangRH3642026}; RH-365 proved a return-bouquet height and radius theorem
\cite{WangRH3652026}.  Neither result supplies the M\"obius statements below,
which enter as an independent source-backed edge
\cite{WangHenonMobius2026}.  The physical route coordinate remains
\begin{equation}
 \texttt{actual\_same\_clock\_unnormalized\_head\_transport\_open}.
 \label{eq:physical-coordinate}
\end{equation}

Consider
\begin{equation}
 \Hmap(x,y)=(1-6x^2-y,x).
 \label{eq:henon}
\end{equation}
The frozen geometric input is a locally maximal mixing basic set $\Lam$
conjugate to the subshift $\Sigma_A$ with state order
$(--,-+,+-,++)$ and adjacency matrix
\begin{equation}
 A=
 \begin{pmatrix}
 1&0&1&0\\
 1&0&0&0\\
 0&1&0&1\\
 0&1&0&0
 \end{pmatrix}.
 \label{eq:adjacency}
\end{equation}
This is an inherited theorem, not a conclusion from finite orbit tables
\cite{WangHenonWeighted2026}.  It concerns the local survivor $\Lam$, not the
full nonwandering set of $\Hmap$.

Writing a state as $w_n=(\eps_n,\eps_{n-1})$, admissibility is equivalent to
\begin{equation}
 \eps_n\in\{-1,+1\},\qquad
 \text{there is no }n\text{ with }\eps_n=\eps_{n+2}=+1.
 \label{eq:distance-two}
\end{equation}
The continuous clipping observable
\begin{equation}
 s(x,y)=
 \begin{cases}
 -1,&x\leq-1/3,\\
 3x,&\abs{x}<1/3,\\
 +1,&x\geq1/3
 \end{cases}
 \label{eq:sign-observable}
\end{equation}
equals the sign code on $\Lam$.  Indeed the certified survivor lies strictly
inside $\abs{x}>1/3$ on its two branches.

For $z\in\Lam$ and $f\in C(\Lam)$ define
\begin{equation}
 \cN(z,f)=\frac1N\sum_{n=1}^{N}\Mob(n)f(\Hmap^n z).
 \label{eq:correlation}
\end{equation}
This is a time correlation along one orbit.  It is not a sum over an orbit
catalogue and is not an operator trace.

\section{Fixed periodic cancellation and an adaptive exceptional point}

\begin{theorem}[Every fixed periodic orbit is orthogonal]
\label{thm:periodic}
For every periodic $z\in\Lam$ and every $f\in C(\Lam)$,
\begin{equation}
 \cN(z,f)\longrightarrow0.
 \label{eq:periodic-zero}
\end{equation}
The point, and hence its period, is fixed before $N\to\infty$.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
If the period is $p$, then $b_n=f(\Hmap^n z)$ is a bounded $p$-periodic
sequence.  Its finite Fourier expansion is
\[
 b_n=\sum_{j=0}^{p-1}\widehat b_j e^{2\pi ijn/p}.
\]
Davenport's uniform estimate gives, for each fixed $A_0>0$,
\[
 \sup_{\theta\in\mathbb R}
 \abs{\sum_{n\leq N}\Mob(n)e^{2\pi i n\theta}}
 \ll_{A_0}N(\log N)^{-A_0}
\]
\cite{Davenport1937,IwaniecKowalski2004}.  Applying it at the finitely many
frequencies $j/p$ and dividing by $N$ proves the claim.
\end{proof}

Positive entropy nevertheless gives enough symbolic freedom to encode an
arithmetic sequence.  Define a bi-infinite sign word by $\eps_n=-1$ for
$n\leq0$, and for $n\geq1$ set
\begin{equation}
 \eps_n=+1
 \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
 \Mob(n)=1\ \text{ and }\ n\bmod4\in\{1,2\}.
 \label{eq:adaptive-word}
\end{equation}
Adding two maps the selected residue classes $1,2$ to the unselected classes
$3,0$.  Thus \eqref{eq:distance-two} holds, and the conjugacy supplies a
unique point $z_{\Mob}\in\Lam$ with this itinerary.

\begin{theorem}[Exact adaptive correlation]
\label{thm:exceptional}
For the point selected by \eqref{eq:adaptive-word},
\begin{equation}
 \lim_{N\to\infty}\cN(z_{\Mob},s)=\frac4{\pi^2}.
 \label{eq:four-over-pi2}
\end{equation}
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Let $M(N)=\sum_{n\leq N}\Mob(n)$.  From the definition,
\begin{equation}
 \sum_{n\leq N}\Mob(n)\eps_n
 =-M(N)+2\#\{n\leq N:\Mob(n)=1,\ n\bmod4\in\{1,2\}\}.
 \label{eq:exceptional-identity}
\end{equation}
For $r=1,2$ one has
\begin{equation}
 \frac1N\sum_{\substack{n\leq N\\n\equiv r\ (4)}}\Mob(n)\to0,
 \qquad
 \frac1N\sum_{\substack{n\leq N\\n\equiv r\ (4)}}\Mob(n)^2
 \to\frac2{\pi^2}.
 \label{eq:residue-density}
\end{equation}
For $r=1$, these are the fixed-progression prime number theorem and the
squarefree density.  For $r=2$, write $n=2m$ with $m$ odd.  Then
$\Mob(2m)=-\Mob(m)$, while odd squarefree integers have density $4/\pi^2$;
counting $m\leq N/2$ again gives $2/\pi^2$ after normalization by $N$
\cite{Mirsky1948}.  Since
\[
 \mathbf1_{\{\Mob=1\}}=\frac{\Mob^2+\Mob}{2},
\]
each selected residue class contributes density $1/\pi^2$.  Finally
$M(N)=o(N)$.  Divide \eqref{eq:exceptional-identity} by $N$.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}[The essential firewall]
The complete positive-time M\"obius sequence is read before $z_{\Mob}$ is
selected.  Theorem~\ref{thm:exceptional} proves offline encoding capacity.  It
does not prove a nonadaptive prediction rule, a dynamically preferred initial
condition, or spontaneous arithmetic coupling.  Moreover $\Lam$ has positive
entropy, so this is not a counterexample to Sarnak's zero-entropy conjecture
\cite{Sarnak2010}.  Positive-entropy systems can display either correlation or
uniform decorrelation, and entropy alone is not a switch
\cite{Karagulyan2017,DownarowiczSerafin2019}.
\end{remark}

\section{Parry covariance, variance, and typical orthogonality}

Let $\nu$ be the Parry measure on $\Sigma_A$, transported to $\Lam$.  Put
$\varphi=(1+\sqrt5)/2$.  In the state order of \eqref{eq:adjacency},
\begin{equation}
 \pi=\left(\frac{3+\sqrt5}{10},\frac15,\frac15,
            \frac{3-\sqrt5}{10}\right)
 \label{eq:parry-pi}
\end{equation}
and
\begin{equation}
 P=\begin{pmatrix}
 \varphi^{-1}&0&\varphi^{-2}&0\\
 1&0&0&0\\
 0&\varphi^{-1}&0&\varphi^{-2}\\
 0&1&0&0
 \end{pmatrix}.
 \label{eq:parry-P}
\end{equation}
The raw sign has mean $-1/\sqrt5$.  Hence
\begin{equation}
 F(w)=\frac{\sqrt5\,\eps(w)+1}{2}
 \label{eq:F}
\end{equation}
has mean zero and variance one.

\begin{proposition}[Exact covariance]
\label{prop:covariance}
For every $k\geq0$,
\begin{align}
 \Cov_\nu(F,F\circ\sigma^{2k+1})&=0,\label{eq:odd-cov}\\
 \Cov_\nu(F,F\circ\sigma^{2k})&=(-\varphi^{-2})^k.\label{eq:even-cov}
\end{align}
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
As a state vector,
\[
 F=(-\varphi^{-1},-\varphi^{-1},\varphi,\varphi)^{\mathsf T}.
\]
Direct multiplication gives
\begin{equation}
 P^2F=-\varphi^{-2}F,
 \qquad
 \pi\,\operatorname{diag}(F)PF=0.
 \label{eq:cov-algebra}
\end{equation}
Since
$\Cov_\nu(F,F\circ\sigma^\ell)=
 \pi\operatorname{diag}(F)P^\ell F$, the two identities follow.  The
executable artifact verifies this algebra exactly in $\mathbb Q(\sqrt5)$;
no floating eigendecomposition is used.
\end{proof}

For the fixed arithmetic weights define
\begin{equation}
 S_N(\omega)=\sum_{n=1}^{N}\Mob(n)F(\sigma^n\omega),
 \qquad V_N=\Var_\nu S_N.
 \label{eq:SN}
\end{equation}

\begin{corollary}[Exact finite-prefix variance]
\label{cor:variance}
For every $N\geq1$,
\begin{equation}
 V_N=\sum_{n\leq N}\Mob(n)^2
 +2\sum_{k\geq1}(-\varphi^{-2})^k
   \sum_{n\leq N-2k}\Mob(n)\Mob(n+2k),
 \label{eq:variance-formula}
\end{equation}
where terms with $2k\geq N$ vanish.  Unconditionally,
\begin{equation}
 0\leq V_N\leq\sqrt5\,N.
 \label{eq:sqrt5-bound}
\end{equation}
If ordinary Ces\`aro two-point Chowla holds for every fixed even shift, then
\begin{equation}
 \frac{V_N}{N}\longrightarrow\frac6{\pi^2}.
 \label{eq:conditional-limit}
\end{equation}
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
Expand \eqref{eq:SN}, group pairs by their lag, and insert
Proposition~\ref{prop:covariance}.  This gives \eqref{eq:variance-formula}.
Taking absolute values in the off-diagonal terms yields
\[
 V_N\leq N\left(1+2\sum_{k\geq1}\varphi^{-2k}\right)
 =N(1+2\varphi^{-1})=\sqrt5N.
\]
Variance is nonnegative by definition.  Under ordinary Chowla, for each
fixed $k$ the inner correlation in \eqref{eq:variance-formula} is $o(N)$.
After division by $N$ it is bounded by one, while the coefficients are
summable.  Dominated convergence therefore passes through the $k$-sum, and
$N^{-1}\sum_{n\leq N}\Mob(n)^2\to6/\pi^2$ proves
\eqref{eq:conditional-limit}.  The logarithmically averaged theorem does not
imply these ordinary Ces\`aro hypotheses \cite{Tao2016}.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Parry-almost-sure simultaneous orthogonality]
\label{thm:typical}
For $\nu$-almost every $z\in\Lam$, simultaneously for every
$f\in C(\Lam)$,
\begin{equation}
 \cN(z,f)\longrightarrow0.
 \label{eq:typical-zero}
\end{equation}
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Work first on $\Sigma_A$.  Let $g$ be a centered locally constant function.
Exponential mixing of the finite-state Parry chain gives constants
$C_g<\infty$ and $0<\rho_g<1$ with
\[
 \abs{\Cov_\nu(g,g\circ\sigma^h)}\leq C_g\rho_g^h.
\]
Because $\abs{\Mob(n)}\leq1$, the weighted partial sums
$T_N=\sum_{n\leq N}\Mob(n)g\circ\sigma^n$ satisfy
$\Var_\nu T_N=O_g(N)$.  Chebyshev's inequality at $N=k^2$ gives, for every
$\delta>0$,
\[
 \sum_{k\geq1}
 \nu\{\abs{T_{k^2}}>\delta k^2\}<\infty.
\]
Borel--Cantelli yields $T_{k^2}/k^2\to0$ almost surely.  Between $k^2$ and
$(k+1)^2$ there are at most $2k+1$ additional bounded summands, so convergence
holds for every $N$.

Choose a countable uniformly dense family of rational-valued locally
constant functions.  Intersect their full-measure convergence sets.  For a
noncentered function the constant component contributes a multiple of
$M(N)/N$, which vanishes by the prime number theorem.  Uniform approximation,
together with $N^{-1}\sum_{n\leq N}\abs{\Mob(n)}\leq1$, extends the conclusion
on this one full-measure set to every continuous function.  Transport through
the conjugacy.
\end{proof}

The quantifier is important: one full-measure set works for all continuous
observables, but the conclusion is not uniform over all $z\in\Lam$ because
Theorem~\ref{thm:exceptional} supplies an explicit exception.

\section{Finite-horizon adaptive capacity}

\begin{definition}
For $N\geq1$, define the open raw capacity
\begin{equation}
 K_N=\max_{\substack{\eps_1,\ldots,\eps_N\in\{-1,+1\}\\
              \eps_n=\eps_{n+2}=+1\ \mathrm{never}}}
 \abs{\sum_{n=1}^{N}\Mob(n)\eps_n}.
 \label{eq:capacity}
\end{equation}
The optimizing word may depend on $N$ and on the complete M\"obius prefix.
\end{definition}

\begin{theorem}[Linear-time exact optimization and asymptotic bracket]
\label{thm:capacity}
The value $K_N$ and a witnessing word can be computed exactly in $O(N)$ time.
Moreover
\begin{equation}
 \frac4{\pi^2}
 \leq\liminf_{N\to\infty}\frac{K_N}{N}
 \leq\limsup_{N\to\infty}\frac{K_N}{N}
 \leq\frac6{\pi^2}.
 \label{eq:capacity-bracket}
\end{equation}
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Start from the all-negative word.  If $I$ is the set of positions changed to
plus, then
\begin{equation}
 \sum_{n\leq N}\Mob(n)\eps_n=-M(N)+2\sum_{n\in I}\Mob(n).
 \label{eq:capacity-baseline}
\end{equation}
The constraint says exactly that $I$ is an independent set in the disjoint
union of the odd-index path and the even-index path.  For path weights $a_j$,
the maximum-weight-independent-set recurrence is
\begin{equation}
 D_j=\max\{D_{j-1},D_{j-2}+a_j\},
 \qquad D_0=0,\quad D_1=\max\{0,a_1\}.
 \label{eq:mwis}
\end{equation}
Run it on both paths with weights $\Mob(n)$ to maximize
\eqref{eq:capacity-baseline}, and with weights $-\Mob(n)$ to minimize it.
Traceback recovers witnesses.  The total work is linear.

Every prefix of the word in \eqref{eq:adaptive-word} is feasible, so
Theorem~\ref{thm:exceptional} gives the lower bound in
\eqref{eq:capacity-bracket}.  For every sign word,
\[
 \abs{\sum_{n\leq N}\Mob(n)\eps_n}
 \leq\sum_{n\leq N}\Mob(n)^2.
\]
The squarefree density gives the upper bound.
\end{proof}

No existence theorem for $\lim K_N/N$ follows.  The optimizer can change with
$N$, so a nonvanishing envelope also does not contradict
Theorem~\ref{thm:periodic}, which fixes one periodic point before taking the
limit.  Filling signs outside $[1,N]$ with $-1$ realizes each open optimizer
by an $N$-dependent point of $\Lam$.

\section{Conclusion and next exact questions}

One explicit positive-entropy H\'enon subsystem supports both rigorous
M\"obius cancellation and rigorous arithmetic tracking.  The distinction is
selection: fixed periodic and Parry-typical points cancel, while a point
chosen after reading the arithmetic sequence tracks it with correlation
$4/\pi^2$.  Exact covariance and linear-time capacity quantify the mechanism
without promoting it to a spectral or zeta construction.

The shortest new theorem problems are correspondingly narrow: determine
whether $K_N/N$ has a limit; classify nonadaptive invariant measures with
quantitative M\"obius cancellation; and generalize the graph-capacity theorem
to other mixing subshifts.  Independent breadth-first routes, including the
cyclic-Ulam structural audit and the RH-365 primitive-divisor/radius problems,
remain separate.  Repackaging adaptive encoding as intrinsic coupling would
not be a continuation.
