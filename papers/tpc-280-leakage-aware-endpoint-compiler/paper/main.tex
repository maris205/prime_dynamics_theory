\begin{abstract}
The preceding four-packet analysis identifies a normalized deficit of the
reassembled energy as the exact input needed for a power gain, but it leaves
open how a non-multiplicative error should be charged.  We prove a conditional
two-term compiler.  If $D\ge dX^a$ and
\[
 G\le B X^{-\gamma}D+\ell X^{a-\delta},
\]
then
\[
 \frac GD\le B X^{-\gamma}+\frac\ell dX^{-\delta}
 \quad\text{and}\quad
 \frac DG\ge\left(B X^{-\gamma}+\frac\ell dX^{-\delta}\right)^{-1}.
\]
The usual one-exponent summary has exponent
$\kappa=\min(\gamma,\delta)$ and constant $B+\ell/d$.
Combining this with the exact signed-margin identity gives the effective loss
$\max(0,\eta_D-\kappa/2)$ and the strict endpoint test
$\sigma-\eta_{\rm eff}>1/400$.  An equality family proves that the two-term
denominator is sharp under the stated hypotheses; in particular, a slower
additive leakage term cannot be silently assigned the main-term exponent.
Six exact rational budget fixtures, four margin fixtures, four endpoint
fixtures, and a twelve-row transfer from the TPC-279 parent are independently
certified.  No arithmetic $L^2$ estimate or twin-prime conclusion is claimed.
\end{abstract}

\section{The source interface}

Let $X\ge1$ be the scale, let $D>0$ denote the packet-diagonal source
energy, and let $G\ge0$ denote the energy after signed reassembly.  The
previous structural criterion uses the ratio $q=G/D$ and gain $r=D/G$
when $G>0$; see the exact four-packet identities in the TPC-279 release
\cite{tpc279}.  In an actual analytic estimate it is natural to separate a
main term, proportional to $D$, from a leakage term which is bounded only in
absolute scale.  We therefore assume
\begin{equation}
 D\ge dX^a,\qquad
 G\le B X^{-\gamma}D+\ell X^{a-\delta},
 \label{eq:raw}
\end{equation}
where $d>0$ and $B,\ell,\gamma,\delta\ge0$.  The exponent $a$ is deliberately
left symbolic; the intended source interface may have $a=5/3$.

The distinction between the two terms matters.  A multiplicative estimate
can be normalized by $D$ directly, whereas an additive estimate must first use
the lower bound on $D$.  The next theorem records exactly that operation.

\section{The two-term compiler}

\begin{theorem}[two-term additive-leakage compiler]
Assume \eqref{eq:raw} and set
\[
 c_\ell=\frac\ell d,\qquad \kappa=\min(\gamma,\delta),
 \qquad C=B+c_\ell.
\]
If $C>0$, then
\begin{align}
 q=\frac GD&\le B X^{-\gamma}+c_\ell X^{-\delta},
 \label{eq:two-term}\\
 q&\le C X^{-\kappa}.
 \label{eq:collapsed}
\end{align}
Consequently, if $G>0$,
\begin{align}
 r=\frac DG&\ge
 \left(B X^{-\gamma}+c_\ell X^{-\delta}\right)^{-1}
 \label{eq:gain-two}\\
 &\ge C^{-1}X^\kappa.
 \label{eq:gain-collapsed}
\end{align}
If $C=0$, the hypotheses force $G=0$.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Divide the second inequality in \eqref{eq:raw} by $D$ and use
$D^{-1}\le d^{-1}X^{-a}$ for the additive term.  This gives
\eqref{eq:two-term}.  Since $X\ge1$ and both exponents are at least
$\kappa$, $X^{-\gamma},X^{-\delta}\le X^{-\kappa}$, proving
\eqref{eq:collapsed}.  Positive reciprocal reversal gives
\eqref{eq:gain-two} and \eqref{eq:gain-collapsed}.  If $C=0$, both terms on
the right of \eqref{eq:raw} vanish.
\end{proof}

The two-term form is the useful one at finite scales: it retains the crossover
between the main and leakage lanes.  The collapsed form is the version that
can be inserted into an exponent ledger.  Notice that the slower decay wins:
if $\delta<\gamma$ and $\ell>0$, the leakage term has exponent $\delta$,
not $\gamma$.

\section{Margin and endpoint accounting}

The signed four-packet interface carries an exact identity
\begin{equation}
 m^2=\frac DG m_D^2,
 \label{eq:margin-identity}
\end{equation}
where $m_D$ is the diagonal margin and $m$ is the margin after reassembly.
Equation \eqref{eq:margin-identity} is an interface assumption for this
compiler, not a new arithmetic estimate.

\begin{corollary}[margin compiler]
Under the hypotheses of Theorem 1 and \eqref{eq:margin-identity},
\begin{equation}
 m^2\ge
 \frac{m_D^2}{B X^{-\gamma}+c_\ell X^{-\delta}}.
 \label{eq:margin-two}
\end{equation}
If, in addition, $m_D\ge cX^{-\eta_D-\varepsilon}$ with $c>0$, then
\begin{equation}
 m\ge c C^{-1/2}X^{-\eta_D+\kappa/2-\varepsilon}.
 \label{eq:margin-collapsed}
\end{equation}
In a ledger which records only nonnegative losses, one may use
\[
 \eta_{\rm eff}=\max(0,\eta_D-\kappa/2).
\]
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
Substitute the gain bound from Theorem 1 into \eqref{eq:margin-identity}.
Taking square roots after using \eqref{eq:collapsed} gives
\eqref{eq:margin-collapsed}.  If the exponent of $X$ is positive, replacing
the corresponding negative loss by zero weakens the lower bound for $X\ge1$.
\end{proof}

Suppose the remaining scalar lane has the inherited form
$|S|\le A X^{E_0-\sigma+\varepsilon}$.  The endpoint ledger then receives
the effective saving $\sigma-\eta_{\rm eff}$, so the strict target is
\begin{equation}
 \sigma-\eta_{\rm eff}>\frac1{400}.
 \label{eq:endpoint}
\end{equation}
The strict inequality is essential: equality is a borderline unpaid case.
The constant $C^{-1/2}$ affects constants, while $\kappa$ controls the power
lane.

\section{Sharpness and the leakage obstruction}

\begin{proposition}[sharpness under the information model]
For any admissible $X,d,B,\ell,a,\gamma,\delta$ with $C>0$, the formal
choices
\[
 D=dX^a,qquad
 G=B X^{-\gamma}D+\ell X^{a-\delta}
\]
satisfy the hypotheses and make \eqref{eq:two-term} and \eqref{eq:gain-two}
equalities.  If $\delta<\gamma$ and $\ell>0$, the gain has asymptotic order
$X^\delta$ up to constants, so a universal exponent $\gamma$ conclusion is
false under these inputs.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Substitution gives $D=dX^a$ and
$G/D=BX^{-\gamma}+c_\ell X^{-\delta}$ exactly.  When $\delta<\gamma$,
the ratio of the main term to the leakage term tends to zero, and the latter
determines the order of the sum.
\end{proof}

Thus the compiler identifies a precise failure mode for a tempting shortcut:
one may not normalize an additive remainder by an upper or nominal source
scale.  Its actual payment is determined by the available lower bound on $D$.
The equality family is an information-model adversary, not a claim that the
literal TPC source realizes equality.

As a separate provenance check, the twelve TPC-279 parent rows are copied only

eight positive-deficit and four negative-deficit rows.  This finite census
validates the coordinate interface; it does not assert that the literal source
has an additive leakage decomposition or a growing deficit.

\section{Route consequence and claim firewall}

The new result is a conditional compiler, not an arithmetic payment.  To use
it on the twin-prime route one still needs a literal estimate of the form
\eqref{eq:raw}, including a source lower bound and a typed identification of
the leakage term.  In particular, the theorem does not convert a finite table
of favorable gains into a power estimate.
