\begin{abstract}
RH-192 proves that the full Frobenius operator
$\cL_A:X\mapsto AX$ is $m$ copies of $A$ and therefore cannot have the
rank-one or rank-four ambient Riesz shells sought by the temporal packet.
This paper constructs the exact reduced state selected by one physical
matrix source:
\[
 \cK_S=\operatorname{span}\{S,AS,A^2S,\ldots\}.
\]
The space is $\cL_A$-invariant, cyclic, and has dimension
\[
 \dim\cK_S=\deg\mu_{A,S}\le\deg\mu_A\le n,
\]
independently of the source width $m$.  The restriction has minimal and
characteristic polynomial $\mu_{A,S}$.  Every right temporal packet built
from the source orbit lies exactly in $\cK_S$.

For an observation seed $O^*$ of the same matrix type, orthogonal restriction
to $\cK_S$ preserves every moment
$h_j=\langle O^*,A^jS\rangle_F$ and hence the complete scalar resolvent germ
at infinity.  A 140-case complex Arnoldi audit verifies invariance,
intertwining, packet inclusion, and moment preservation with zero failures.

The result removes the $m$-fold multiplicity obstruction at the correct
source-relative type.  It does not assert that $\dim\cK_S$ is uniformly
small in the physical limit.  The next task is to determine whether the
local length-four temporal roots match actual source-observable spectral
modes of the base operator.
\end{abstract}

\section{From an ambient no-go to a cyclic state}

Let $A\in\mathbb C^{n\times n}$, let
$S\in\mathbb C^{n\times m}$ be nonzero, and equip
$\cH_F=\mathbb C^{n\times m}$ with the Frobenius inner product.  Define
$\cL_A X=AX$.  RH-192 identifies $\cL_A$ with $I_m\otimes A$, so its full
Riesz multiplicities are multiples of $m$ \citep{WangRH192}.

The physical experiment, however, never initializes an arbitrary element
of $\cH_F$.  It initializes the single vector $S$ and observes its orbit.
The smallest invariant state containing that experiment is therefore a
cyclic subspace, not the complete direct sum.

\section{The source annihilator}

Define the ideal
\begin{equation}\label{eq:annihilator-ideal}
 \mathcal I_{A,S}=\{p\in\mathbb C[z]:p(A)S=0\}.
\end{equation}
It is nonzero because the characteristic polynomial of $A$ belongs to it.
Since $\mathbb C[z]$ is a principal ideal domain, there is a unique monic
generator, denoted $\mu_{A,S}$.

\begin{proposition}[Divisibility]\label{prop:divisibility}
The source minimal polynomial $\mu_{A,S}$ divides the ordinary minimal
polynomial $\mu_A$ and therefore
\begin{equation}\label{eq:degree-bound}
 \deg\mu_{A,S}\le\deg\mu_A\le n.
\end{equation}
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Since $\mu_A(A)=0$, one has $\mu_A(A)S=0$, so
$\mu_A\in\mathcal I_{A,S}=(\mu_{A,S})$.
\end{proof}

The polynomial depends on the complete matrix source.  Equivalently it is
the least common multiple of the vector minimal polynomials of the columns
of $S$.  The columns can activate more modes, but they do not create $m$
independent copies when the entire matrix is treated as one source vector.

\section{Exact cyclic restriction}

Set
\begin{equation}\label{eq:cyclic-space}
 \cK_S=\operatorname{span}\{A^jS:j\ge0\}\subset\cH_F.
\end{equation}

\begin{theorem}[Source-cyclic realization]\label{thm:cyclic-realization}
The space $\cK_S$ is invariant under $\cL_A$ and
\begin{equation}\label{eq:cyclic-dimension}
 \dim\cK_S=\deg\mu_{A,S}\le n.
\end{equation}
The restricted operator
$T_S=\cL_A|_{\cK_S}$ is cyclic with cyclic vector $S$.  Its minimal and
characteristic polynomials both equal $\mu_{A,S}$.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Invariance follows from
\[
 \cL_A(A^jS)=A^{j+1}S.
\]
If
$d=\deg\mu_{A,S}$, then
$S,AS,\ldots,A^{d-1}S$ are linearly independent; otherwise a polynomial of
degree below $d$ would annihilate $S$.  The relation
$\mu_{A,S}(A)S=0$ expresses every later power in their span, proving the
dimension statement.  The restriction is generated by $S$, so it is a
cyclic $d$-dimensional operator.  For a cyclic operator the minimal
polynomial has degree $d$ and equals the characteristic polynomial.
\end{proof}

The theorem is exact and contains no numerical-rank threshold.  In a
floating implementation, numerical cyclic dimension can be delicate because
long power sequences become ill-conditioned.  This affects computation, not
the algebraic state type.

\section{Coordinate realization}

Let
\begin{equation}\label{eq:isometry}
 Q:\mathbb C^d\longrightarrow\cK_S
\end{equation}
be any Frobenius isometry onto the cyclic space.  Define
\begin{equation}\label{eq:reduced-data}
 T=Q^*\cL_AQ,
 \qquad b=Q^*S.
\end{equation}

\begin{proposition}[Intertwining]\label{prop:intertwining}
One has
\begin{equation}\label{eq:intertwining}
 \cL_AQ=QT,
 \qquad
 A^jS=QT^jb
 \quad(j\ge0).
\end{equation}
The spectrum and algebraic multiplicities of $T$ are those of the
source-relative cyclic restriction, with no forced factor $m$.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Because $\operatorname{Ran}Q$ is invariant, $\cL_AQ$ lies in
$\operatorname{Ran}Q$ and equals $QQ^*\cL_AQ=QT$.  Induction gives the orbit
identity.
\end{proof}

If the source is cyclic for $A$ in the matrix sense, then $d=n$.  This still
reduces the ambient dimension from $nm$ to $n$, which is decisive at the
physical widths 64--256.

\section{Exact inclusion of temporal packets}

For indices $t_1,\ldots,t_L$, let the unnormalized temporal synthesis be
\begin{equation}\label{eq:synthesis}
 J=[A^{t_1}S,\ldots,A^{t_L}S].
\end{equation}
Column normalization, polar orthogonalization, and invertible right
coordinate changes do not alter its range.

\begin{corollary}[Packet inclusion]\label{cor:packet-inclusion}
Every right temporal subspace constructed from the source orbit is contained
in $\cK_S$.  In reduced coordinates,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:reduced-synthesis}
 J=Q[T^{t_1}b,\ldots,T^{t_L}b].
\end{equation}
\end{corollary}

This includes the RH-182 orthogonal packet and the right half of the RH-185
balanced bi-Krylov packet.  The left temporal vectors need not lie in the
same cyclic space, but they restrict to bounded coordinate functionals
$Q^*W$.  If $W^*V=I$ and $V\subset\cK_S$, then
\begin{equation}\label{eq:left-restriction}
 (Q^*W)^*(Q^*V)=W^*QQ^*V=W^*V=I.
\end{equation}
Thus biorthogonality is preserved when the left frame is restricted to the
source-cyclic state.

\section{Observation moments}

Let $O^*\in\cH_F$ be an observation seed and define the scalar moments
\begin{equation}\label{eq:moments}
 h_j=\langle O^*,A^jS\rangle_F.
\end{equation}
Write $c=Q^*O^*$.

\begin{theorem}[Exact moment realization]\label{thm:moments}
For every $j\ge0$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:moment-realization}
 h_j=c^*T^jb.
\end{equation}
For $|z|>\rho(T)$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:transfer}
 \langle O^*,(zI-\cL_A)^{-1}S\rangle_F
 =c^*(zI-T)^{-1}b
 =\sum_{j\ge0}\frac{h_j}{z^{j+1}}.
\end{equation}
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Insert $A^jS=QT^jb$ and use that $Q$ is an isometry.  The resolvent formula
follows from the convergent Neumann series.
\end{proof}

The source-cyclic restriction therefore loses no scalar input-output data.
Modes absent from $T$ are exactly modes not excited by the matrix source.
A further observability quotient can remove modes whose residues vanish.

\section{Riesz projectors in the cyclic state}

For a contour $\Gamma$ in the resolvent set of $T$, let
$P_\Gamma(T)$ be its Riesz projector.  Intertwining and holomorphic
functional calculus give
\begin{equation}\label{eq:riesz-intertwining}
 P_\Gamma(\cL_A)Q=QP_\Gamma(T).
\end{equation}
The rank on $\cK_S$ is therefore
$\rank P_\Gamma(T)$, not $m\rank P_\Gamma(A)$.  For one simple
source-excited base eigenvalue it can equal one.

There is no contradiction with RH-192.  The full ambient eigenspace has $m$
copies, while the cyclic orbit chooses at most one polynomially generated
direction through that eigenspace.  The remaining $m-1$ directions are not
reachable from the single matrix seed.

\section{Finite Arnoldi audit}

The implementation performs Frobenius Arnoldi on complex nonnormal matrices
of dimensions 2 through 8 and source widths 1 through 4.  Five independent
trials per pair give 140 cases.  Each matrix is similar to a diagonal matrix
with distinct eigenvalues, and each random source activates all modes.

The audit checks:
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=2em]
\item Frobenius orthogonality of the computed basis;
\item closure and the intertwining defect $\cL_AQ-QT$;
\item inclusion of the first temporal powers;
\item equality of $2n$ full and reduced moments for an independent
 observation seed.
\end{enumerate}
All 140 cases pass the declared tolerances.  This audit tests the
implementation of exact identities; it is not evidence for a uniform
physical numerical rank.

\section{What is and is not gained}

The gain is structural:
\begin{equation*}
 nm\text{-dimensional repeated spectrum}
 \quad\longrightarrow\quad
 d\le n\text{-dimensional source-cyclic spectrum}.
\end{equation*}
The temporal packet is now in the correct invariant state, and its left
observation functionals restrict without breaking biorthogonality.

What remains open is equally important.  The physical value of $d$ may grow
with refinement.  A four-vector temporal window does not imply $d=4$.
Moreover, the cyclic space is source-dependent.  Gate A eventually needs a
selection that is canonical enough to transport between levels and to feed
an intrinsic cloud ledger.

The immediate falsifiable question is finite: do the four temporal roots in
the surviving $\sigma=0.01$ windows lie near actual eigenvalues activated by
the source and seen by the observation?  RH-194 performs that audit.
