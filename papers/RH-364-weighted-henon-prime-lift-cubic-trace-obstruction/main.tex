\begin{abstract}
For the integral area-preserving H\'enon map
\(
H_6(x,y)=(1-6x^2-y,x)
\), a locked source constructs a local mixing hyperbolic survivor conjugate
to a four-state subshift.  We extract two all-order consequences.  First, if
\(L_o\) is the unstable multiplier modulus of a primitive survivor orbit,
the exact cone expansion \(L_o\ge(773/224)^{n_o}\), together with the
four-state orbit count, gives a certified zero-free disk and an explicit
primitive-period tail for every nonnegative multiplier weight.  The
Euler/flat correction has a larger analytic disk, but does not continue the
complete determinants or certify the reported finite-section root.

Second, we copy the same four-state matrix over all rational primes with one
common Dirichlet clock.  The resulting direct sum is in \(S_q\) exactly when
\(q\RePart s>1\), has an ordinary Fredholm determinant for \(\RePart s>1\),
and genuine regularized determinants on their exact Schatten half-planes.
Its inverse determinant factors into Riemann zeta functions, but its
prime-power trace weights begin
\(
(F_1,F_2,F_3)=(1,1,4)
\): primes and squares match von Mangoldt, while cubes have exact surplus
\(3\log p\).  Positive multiplier weights produce a fractional
non-meromorphic singularity at \(s=1\); the unique common scalar
normalization repairs orders one and two but still fails at cubes.

The local survivor is intrinsic, but copying it over prime labels is an
engineered functor rather than a finite-field reduction or canonical global
operator.  Gates A--E remain false/open.  No Hilbert--P\'olya operator,
Riemann-zero identification, completed-zeta divisor equality, or proof of the
Riemann Hypothesis is claimed.
\end{abstract}

\section{Symbolic counts and a certified weighted Euler disk}

Let
\(
F_n=\Tr(A^n)
\)
and let \(p_n\) be the number of primitive survivor orbits of period \(n\).

\begin{theorem}[Exact all-order symbolic ledger]
\label{thm:symbolic-ledger}
For every \(n\ge1\),
\begin{align}
 \det(\lambda I-A)
 &=(\lambda^2-\lambda-1)(\lambda^2+1),
 \label{eq:characteristic}\\
 F_n
 &=\mathsf L_n+2\cos\!\left(\frac{n\pi}{2}\right),
 \label{eq:trace-lucas}\\
 p_n
 &=\frac1n\sum_{d\mid n}\mu(d)F_{n/d},
 \qquad
 0\le p_n\le\frac{F_n}{n}\le\frac{4\varphi^n}{n},
 \label{eq:primitive-envelope}
\end{align}
where \(\mathsf L_n=\varphi^n+(-\varphi^{-1})^n\) is the \(n\)-th Lucas
number.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Direct expansion of \(\det(\lambda I-A)\) gives
\eqref{eq:characteristic}.  The eigenvalues are
\(
\varphi,-\varphi^{-1},i,-i
\), so taking power traces proves \eqref{eq:trace-lucas}.  The
period-preserving conjugacy and finite-state fixed-point identity give
\(
F_n=\sum_{d\mid n}d p_d
\); M\"obius inversion gives the first part of
\eqref{eq:primitive-envelope}.  Since the points on primitive period-\(n\)
orbits form a subset of \(\Fix(A^n)\), one has \(np_n\le F_n\).  Finally,
\(
|\mathsf L_n|\le\varphi^n+1
\)
and \(|2\cos(n\pi/2)|\le2\), which is at most \(4\varphi^n\).
\end{proof}

For a primitive survivor orbit \(o\), let \(n_o\) be its primitive period,
\(\sigma_o\in\{-1,1\}\) the sign of its unstable multiplier, and
\(L_o>1\) its modulus.  The cone certificate acts on the invariant unstable
line at every iterate and gives
\begin{equation}
 L_o\ge\kappa^{n_o}.
 \label{eq:multiplier-envelope}
\end{equation}
Here \(L_o\) is a monodromy multiplier of \(H_6\); it is not an eigenvalue of
the four-state matrix \(A\).

For real \(\beta\ge0\), define
\begin{equation}
 Z_\beta(z)=\prod_{o\in\cP_*}
 (1-z^{n_o}L_o^{-\beta})^{-1},
 \qquad
 D_{E,\beta}(z)=Z_\beta(z)^{-1}.
 \label{eq:weighted-euler}
\end{equation}

\begin{theorem}[Certified weighted disk and all-order tail]
\label{thm:weighted-disk}
For every real \(\beta\ge0\), both objects in
\eqref{eq:weighted-euler} converge normally, are holomorphic, and are
zero-free on
\begin{equation}
 |z|<R_\beta:=\frac{\kappa^\beta}{\varphi}.
 \label{eq:weighted-radius}
\end{equation}
Fix \(0<R<R_\beta\), put
\(
t=R\kappa^{-\beta}
\)
and \(q=\varphi t\).  If the logarithm is truncated after all primitive
periods at most \(N\), then
\begin{equation}
 \sup_{|z|\le R}
 \left|\log Z_\beta(z)-\log Z_\beta^{(\mathrm{prim}\le N)}(z)\right|
 \le
 \frac{4q^{N+1}}
 {(N+1)(1-q)(1-t^{N+1})}.
 \label{eq:euler-tail}
\end{equation}
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
For an orbit of period \(n\), equation \eqref{eq:multiplier-envelope}
bounds its logarithmic repetition sum by
\(
t^n/(1-t^n)
\)
on the closed disk.  Theorem~\ref{thm:symbolic-ledger} therefore gives
\begin{align*}
 \sum_{n>N}p_n\frac{t^n}{1-t^n}
 &\le
 \frac{4}{(N+1)(1-t^{N+1})}
 \sum_{n>N}(\varphi t)^n,
\end{align*}
which is \eqref{eq:euler-tail}.  Normal convergence supplies a canonical
holomorphic logarithm.  Exponentiating it and its negative proves the
zero-free statements.
\end{proof}

This is a certified disk, not a maximal-domain theorem.  In particular, for
\(\beta=1\),
\begin{equation}
 R_1=\frac{\kappa}{\varphi}=2.132769077248\ldots,
 \label{eq:euler-beta-one-radius}
\end{equation}
while the source reports a finite-section reciprocal root near
\(3.429\).  That numerical value lies outside the proved disk.

\subsection{The larger flat correction}

The periodic-point flat determinant has the formal factorization
\begin{equation}
 D_F(z)=\prod_{o\in\cP_*}\prod_{j\ge0}
 \left(1-\sigma_o^jL_o^{-(j+1)}z^{n_o}\right)^{j+1}.
 \label{eq:flat-factorization}
\end{equation}
Its \(j=0\) factor is \(D_{E,1}\).  Define the remaining correction
\begin{equation}
 C_F(z)=\prod_{o\in\cP_*}\prod_{j\ge1}
 \left(1-\sigma_o^jL_o^{-(j+1)}z^{n_o}\right)^{j+1}.
 \label{eq:flat-correction}
\end{equation}

\begin{theorem}[Analytic flat correction, with a strict firewall]
\label{thm:flat-correction}
The product \(C_F\) converges normally and is holomorphic and zero-free on
\begin{equation}
 |z|<R_F^{\rm corr}:=\frac{\kappa^2}{\varphi}
 =7.359957574612\ldots.
 \label{eq:flat-correction-radius}
\end{equation}
The analytic identity
\(
D_F=D_{E,1}C_F
\)
is directly licensed by the products only on the common smaller disk
\(|z|<\kappa/\varphi\).  The larger correction disk alone does not continue
either complete determinant.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
For \(0<x<1\),
\begin{equation}
 \sum_{j\ge1}(j+1)x^{j+1}
 =\frac{x^2(2-x)}{(1-x)^2}.
 \label{eq:flat-geometric}
\end{equation}
Use \(x=L_o^{-r}\), sum the logarithmic repetitions, and apply
\eqref{eq:multiplier-envelope}.  Uniformly in \(n_o\) and \(r\), the
right side of \eqref{eq:flat-geometric} is bounded by a constant times
\(\kappa^{-2rn_o}\).  The remaining orbit sum is dominated by
\(
\sum_n4\varphi^n u^n/(1-u^n)
\)
with \(u=|z|\kappa^{-2}\), which converges when \(\varphi u<1\).
Each factor is then nonzero, so the normal product is zero-free.  The final
scope statement follows because theorem~\ref{thm:weighted-disk} controls the
\(j=0\) Euler factor only to \(\kappa/\varphi\).
\end{proof}

\section{A common-clock prime-copy operator}

Let \(\ell\) denote a rational prime and use the real logarithm in
\(\ell^{-s}=e^{-s\log\ell}\).  On
\(
\mathcal H=\bigoplus_{\ell}\C^4
\), define
\begin{equation}
 \cT_s=\bigoplus_{\ell\ \mathrm{prime}}\ell^{-s}A.
 \label{eq:prime-copy-operator}
\end{equation}
This is a common-clock copy: every prime receives the same local matrix and
the same exponent \(s\).  It is not a reduction of \(H_6\) modulo \(\ell\).
We use the standard Schatten and regularized-determinant conventions of
\citet{Simon2005}.

\begin{theorem}[Exact bounded, compact, and Schatten regions]
\label{thm:schatten}
The operator \(\cT_s\) is bounded exactly for \(\RePart s\ge0\), and compact
exactly for \(\RePart s>0\).  For every finite \(q>0\),
\begin{equation}
 \|\cT_s\|_{S_q}^q
 =\|A\|_{S_q}^q\sum_{\ell}\ell^{-q\RePart s},
 \qquad
 \cT_s\in S_q\Longleftrightarrow q\RePart s>1.
 \label{eq:schatten-law}
\end{equation}
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
The norm of the \(\ell\)-block is
\(
\ell^{-\RePart s}\|A\|
\).  These block norms are uniformly bounded exactly for
\(\RePart s\ge0\), and tend to zero exactly for \(\RePart s>0\).  Each
singular value of \(A\) is multiplied by \(\ell^{-\RePart s}\), so summing
their \(q\)-powers gives \eqref{eq:schatten-law}.  The prime Dirichlet series
converges exactly when its real exponent exceeds one.
\end{proof}

The unweighted survivor determinant is
\begin{equation}
 D_{E,0}(z)=\det(I-zA)=1-z-z^3-z^4
 =(1+z^2)(1-z-z^2).
 \label{eq:unweighted-polynomial}
\end{equation}

\begin{theorem}[Fredholm identity and scalar zeta factorization]
\label{thm:fredholm}
For \(\RePart s>1\),
\begin{align}
 \cD_A(s):=\det_F(I-\cT_s)
 &=\prod_{\ell}
 \left(1-\ell^{-s}-\ell^{-3s}-\ell^{-4s}\right)
 \label{eq:fredholm-product}\\
 &=\prod_{n\ge1}\zeta(ns)^{-p_n}.
 \label{eq:fredholm-zeta}
\end{align}
Consequently
\begin{equation}
 \cZ_A(s):=\cD_A(s)^{-1}
 =\prod_{n\ge1}\zeta(ns)^{p_n}
 \label{eq:inverse-zeta-product}
\end{equation}
in the same region.  The scalar right sides continue meromorphically to
\begin{equation}
 \RePart s>\log_2\varphi,
 \label{eq:scalar-continuation-region}
\end{equation}
but below \(\RePart s>1\) they are not ordinary Fredholm determinants of
\(\cT_s\).
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Theorem~\ref{thm:schatten} makes \(\cT_s\) trace class for
\(\RePart s>1\).  The Fredholm determinant then factors normally over the
finite-dimensional blocks, and \eqref{eq:unweighted-polynomial} gives
\eqref{eq:fredholm-product}.  The primitive-orbit identity
\(
\det(I-zA)=\prod_n(1-z^n)^{p_n}
\)
and absolute convergence permit interchange of the prime and period products,
giving \eqref{eq:fredholm-zeta}.  The sign is essential: positive zeta
exponents belong to the inverse determinant.

For a compact set in \(\RePart s>\log_2\varphi\), the large-\(n\) estimate
\(
|\log\zeta(ns)|\ll2^{-n\RePart s}
\)
combines with \(p_n\le4\varphi^n/n\).  The tail product therefore converges
normally; finitely many initial zeta factors are meromorphic.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Exact regularized determinant regions]
\label{thm:regularized}
Let \(m\ge1\) be an integer.  On \(m\RePart s>1\), the regularized
determinant exists and has the branch-free block product
\begin{equation}
 \det_m(I-\cT_s)
 =\prod_{\ell}
 \left[
  \left(1-\ell^{-s}-\ell^{-3s}-\ell^{-4s}\right)
  \exp\!\left(
   \sum_{r=1}^{m-1}\frac{F_r}{r}\ell^{-rs}
  \right)
 \right].
 \label{eq:regularized-product}
\end{equation}
In particular \(\det_2(I-\cT_s)\) is a genuine regularized determinant on
\(\RePart s>1/2\).
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Theorem~\ref{thm:schatten} gives \(\cT_s\in S_m\) exactly in the stated
region.  In finite dimension,
\begin{equation}
 \det_m(I-zA)=\det(I-zA)
 \exp\!\left(\sum_{r=1}^{m-1}\frac{z^r\Tr(A^r)}r\right).
 \label{eq:finite-regularization}
\end{equation}
The defining regularized product converges normally because its local
remainder begins at order \(m\), and \(\sum_\ell\ell^{-m\RePart s}<\infty\).
Equations \eqref{eq:unweighted-polynomial} and
\eqref{eq:finite-regularization} give \eqref{eq:regularized-product}.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}
The power-trace logarithm
\(
-\sum_{r\ge m}F_r\sum_\ell\ell^{-rs}/r
\)
is directly absolutely convergent only after also imposing
\(\RePart s>\log_2\varphi\).  The block product
\eqref{eq:regularized-product}, not an unlicensed logarithm branch, is the
global statement on the full \(S_m\) half-plane.
\end{remark}

\begin{corollary}[An entropy-block zero of the regularized determinant]
\label{cor:entropy-zero}
At the positive real point
\begin{equation}
 s_2=\log_2\varphi=0.694241913630\ldots,
 \label{eq:entropy-zero}
\end{equation}
the operator belongs to \(S_2\) and \(\det_2(I-\cT_{s_2})=0\).
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
The \(\ell=2\) block has scalar \(2^{-s_2}=\varphi^{-1}\), and
\eqref{eq:unweighted-polynomial} vanishes at \(z=\varphi^{-1}\).  The
regularizing exponential is nonzero.  Also \(2s_2>1\), so theorem
\ref{thm:regularized} applies.
\end{proof}

This zero is the topological-entropy zero of one copied \(\ell=2\) block.  It
is not a Riemann zero, a self-adjoint eigenvalue, or evidence for Gate E.

\section{The first arithmetic defect is at prime cubes}

In \(\RePart s>1\), differentiate the inverse determinant logarithm:
\begin{equation}
 -\frac{\cZ_A'}{\cZ_A}(s)
 =\sum_{\ell}\sum_{r\ge1}
 F_r\log\ell\,\ell^{-rs}.
 \label{eq:trace-ledger}
\end{equation}
The classical von Mangoldt ledger would have coefficient one at every power
\(\ell^r\).

\begin{theorem}[Exact cubic trace obstruction]
\label{thm:cubic-obstruction}
The first symbolic ledgers are
\begin{equation}
 (F_1,F_2,F_3)=(1,1,4),
 \qquad
 (p_1,p_2,p_3)=(1,0,1).
 \label{eq:first-ledgers}
\end{equation}
Thus \eqref{eq:trace-ledger} gives the classical weight \(\log\ell\) at
primes and prime squares, but gives \(4\log\ell\) at prime cubes.  The first
surplus is exactly \(3\log\ell\).
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Theorem~\ref{thm:symbolic-ledger} gives \(F_1=1,F_2=1,F_3=4\).
M\"obius inversion gives
\(
p_1=1
\),
\(
p_2=(F_2-F_1)/2=0
\), and
\(
p_3=(F_3-F_1)/3=1
\).  Substitution in \eqref{eq:trace-ledger} proves the weight statement.
\end{proof}

Equivalently, the first zeta factors of \(\cZ_A\) are
\begin{equation}
 \cZ_A(s)=\zeta(s)\zeta(3s)\zeta(4s)^2\cdots.
 \label{eq:first-zeta-factors}
\end{equation}
The factor \(\zeta(s)\) comes from the unique survivor fixed orbit.  The first
chaotic surplus is the unique primitive period-three orbit and the factor
\(\zeta(3s)\).  The appearance of Riemann zeta here is forced by copying the
same finite-state orbit factor over the prime labels; it is not spectral
recovery of Riemann zeros.

\section{Positive weights and common scalar normalization}

The full real H\'enon map has two fixed points.  Only one belongs to the
certified survivor.

\begin{lemma}[The unique survivor fixed atom]
\label{lem:fixed-atom}
The unique fixed point in \(\Lam_*\) is
\begin{equation}
 P_*=(x_*,x_*),
 \qquad
 x_*=-\frac{1+\sqrt7}{6}.
 \label{eq:fixed-point}
\end{equation}
Its unstable multiplier modulus is
\begin{equation}
 L_*=1+\sqrt7+\sqrt{7+2\sqrt7}
 =7.151675243800\ldots.
 \label{eq:fixed-multiplier}
\end{equation}
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
The fixed-point equation is \(6x^2+2x-1=0\).  The negative root lies in the
negative state interval; the positive root does not lie in either certified
state interval.  At the negative root, the derivative has determinant one and
trace \(-12x_*=2+2\sqrt7\).  Solving its quadratic characteristic equation
gives \eqref{eq:fixed-multiplier}.
\end{proof}

For real \(\beta\ge0\) and \(c>0\), define, initially in a sufficiently far
right half-plane,
\begin{equation}
 \mathfrak Z_{\beta,c}(s)
 =\prod_{\ell}Z_\beta(c\ell^{-s}).
 \label{eq:weighted-prime-lift}
\end{equation}

\begin{theorem}[Natural weighted lift and a fractional singularity]
\label{thm:fractional}
For \(c=1\) and every real \(\beta\ge0\), the product
\eqref{eq:weighted-prime-lift} converges normally and is holomorphic and
zero-free on \(\RePart s>1\).  As real \(\sigma\downarrow1\),
\begin{equation}
 \log\mathfrak Z_{\beta,1}(\sigma)
 =L_*^{-\beta}\log\frac1{\sigma-1}+O(1).
 \label{eq:fractional-asymptotic}
\end{equation}
For \(\beta=0\), the singularity is an ordinary simple pole.  For every real
\(\beta>0\), the exponent lies strictly between zero and one, so
\(\mathfrak Z_{\beta,1}\) has no meromorphic continuation through \(s=1\).
Along the real boundary, its inverse has corresponding fractional-order
vanishing; this is not a holomorphic zero through \(s=1\).
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Theorem~\ref{thm:weighted-disk} gives
\(
R_\beta\ge1/\varphi>1/2
\).  Thus all local arguments \(\ell^{-s}\) stay in one compact subdisk for
\(\RePart s\ge1\), and
\begin{equation}
 \log Z_\beta(z)=L_*^{-\beta}z+O(z^2)
 \label{eq:local-fixed-expansion}
\end{equation}
uniformly there.  Summing the linear term over primes gives
\(
L_*^{-\beta}\sum_\ell\ell^{-s}
\), while the quadratic remainders converge normally near \(s=1\).  The
prime zeta asymptotic \citep{Tenenbaum2015}
\(
\sum_\ell\ell^{-\sigma}=\log(1/(\sigma-1))+O(1)
\)
proves \eqref{eq:fractional-asymptotic}.  A meromorphic function has an
integer zero or pole order, so a noninteger leading logarithmic coefficient
is impossible.
\end{proof}

To test whether one common scalar can repair the arithmetic trace weights,
put
\begin{equation}
 G_m(\beta)=\sum_{o:n_o\mid m}
 n_oL_o^{-\beta m/n_o},
 \qquad
 Q_m(\beta,c)=c^mG_m(\beta).
 \label{eq:weighted-ledger}
\end{equation}
Whenever \eqref{eq:weighted-prime-lift} is expanded in a common convergence
half-plane,
\begin{equation}
 -\frac{\mathfrak Z_{\beta,c}'}{\mathfrak Z_{\beta,c}}(s)
 =\sum_{\ell,m\ge1}Q_m(\beta,c)
 \log\ell\,\ell^{-ms}.
 \label{eq:weighted-trace-ledger}
\end{equation}

\begin{theorem}[Scalar normalization fails exactly at cubes]
\label{thm:scalar-obstruction}
Fix real \(\beta\ge0\).  If \(c>0\) is required to match the prime weight,
\(Q_1(\beta,c)=1\), then uniquely
\begin{equation}
 c=L_*^\beta.
 \label{eq:forced-scalar}
\end{equation}
For this scalar,
\begin{equation}
 Q_2(\beta,L_*^\beta)=1,
 \qquad
 Q_3(\beta,L_*^\beta)
 =1+3\left(\frac{L_*^3}{L_3}\right)^\beta>1,
 \label{eq:weighted-cubic-defect}
\end{equation}
where \(L_3\) is the unstable multiplier modulus of the unique primitive
period-three survivor orbit.  At \(\beta=0\), the cubic value is four.
Therefore no common positive scalar normalization produces von Mangoldt
weights at all prime powers.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Only the fixed orbit divides order one, so
\(
Q_1=cL_*^{-\beta}
\), proving \eqref{eq:forced-scalar}.  Since \(p_2=0\), order two contains
only the second repetition of the fixed orbit and hence
\(
Q_2=c^2L_*^{-2\beta}=1
\).  Since \(p_3=1\), order three contains the third fixed repetition and
one primitive period-three orbit, giving
\(
G_3=L_*^{-3\beta}+3L_3^{-\beta}
\).  Multiplication by \(c^3=L_*^{3\beta}\) gives
\eqref{eq:weighted-cubic-defect}.
\end{proof}

The coefficient obstruction is valid for every real \(\beta\ge0\).  The
analytic normalized product near \(s=1\) requires an additional strict
radius condition.

\begin{proposition}[Exact currently certified normalization threshold]
\label{prop:beta-threshold}
The all-order source bound proves that the normalized local arguments in
\eqref{eq:weighted-prime-lift}, with \(c=L_*^\beta\), lie strictly inside the
weighted Euler disk at \(s=1\) exactly when
\begin{equation}
 0\le\beta<\beta_0,
 \qquad
 \beta_0=
 \frac{\log(2/\varphi)}{\log(L_*/\kappa)}
 =0.290834898770\ldots.
 \label{eq:beta-threshold}
\end{equation}
In this range the normalized prime lift has a simple zeta pole times a
holomorphic nonzero factor near \(s=1\).  For \(\beta\ge\beta_0\), the exact
coefficient identities \eqref{eq:forced-scalar}--
\eqref{eq:weighted-cubic-defect} remain valid, but the present source does not
license the same near-\(s=1\) infinite-product statement.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
At \(s=1\), the largest local argument is the \(\ell=2\) argument
\(
L_*^\beta/2
\).  Theorem~\ref{thm:weighted-disk} places it strictly inside the certified
disk precisely when
\(
L_*^\beta/2<\kappa^\beta/\varphi
\), which is \eqref{eq:beta-threshold}.  In the strict range, the local
logarithm has first coefficient one and a uniformly summable quadratic
remainder, so the global product is \(\zeta(s)\) times a holomorphic nonzero
factor.  Equality or reversal of the radius inequality supplies no such
continuation theorem.
\end{proof}

\section{Conclusion}

The certified survivor supplies more than a finite cycle table: its exact
symbolic entropy and cone expansion give a rigorous weighted analytic domain.
Prime copying then yields unusually clean operator ideals and Fredholm
formulas.  Precisely because the construction is clean, its arithmetic
failure is visible at the first nontrivial primitive orbit: the unique
period-three orbit changes the cube trace weight from one to four.

This is useful Route A structure and a sharp Route B boundary.  The natural
next questions are a positive-radius theorem for the unweighted return-cycle
bouquet, a genuine primitive-divisor theorem for its coefficient anchors, or
an intrinsic globalization that does not add prime copies by hand.  The
original physical same-clock route remains open and unchanged.
