\section{Exact Gram compatibility}

We take Hilbert inner products to be linear in the first argument.

\begin{theorem}[Four-Gram criterion]
\label{thm:gram}
Let $T,T_0,T_1,T_2,T_3:\Hsrc\to\Hout$ be bounded linear operators.  Then
\begin{equation}
 F_{\boldsymbol T}(x,y)=\langle Tx,Ty\rangle
 \quad\text{for every }x,y\in\Hsrc
 \label{eq:target}
\end{equation}
if and only if
\begin{equation}
 T_j^*T_j=T^*T\qquad(0\leq j\leq3).
 \label{eq:four-gram}
\end{equation}
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Write $Q_j=T_j^*T_j$, $Q=T^*T$, and introduce the four-point operator Fourier
moments
\[
 A_k=\frac14\sum_{j=0}^3\ii^{kj}Q_j.
\]
Expanding the norm in \eqref{eq:packet-transform} gives
\begin{equation}
 F_{\boldsymbol T}(x,y)=
 \langle A_1x,x\rangle+\langle A_1y,y\rangle
 +\langle A_0x,y\rangle+\langle A_2y,x\rangle.
 \label{eq:dft-expansion}
\end{equation}
Assume \eqref{eq:target}.  Setting $y=0$ and polarizing the real and imaginary
Hermitian parts gives $A_1=0$.  Since $A_3=A_1^*$, also $A_3=0$.  We are left with
\begin{equation}
 \langle A_0x,y\rangle+\langle A_2y,x\rangle=\langle Qx,y\rangle.
 \label{eq:two-crosses}
\end{equation}
Replace $y$ by $\ii y$.  The first and target terms in
\eqref{eq:two-crosses} acquire the factor $-\ii$, while the second acquires $\ii$.
Combining the two equations gives $A_2=0$, and then $A_0=Q$.  Fourier inversion yields
$Q_j=Q$ for every $j$, proving necessity.

Conversely, \eqref{eq:four-gram} makes every quadratic form in
\eqref{eq:packet-transform} equal to the corresponding quadratic form for $T$.
The ordinary four-phase polarization identity then gives \eqref{eq:target}.
\end{proof}

For real finite matrices, Theorem~\ref{thm:gram} is checked by the exact conditions
\[
 A_0=Q,\quad
 \Re A_1=(Q_0-Q_2)/4=0,\quad
 \Im A_1=(Q_1-Q_3)/4=0,\quad
 A_2=(Q_0-Q_1+Q_2-Q_3)/4=0.
\]
These are equivalent to four separate target-Gram equalities, not merely to equality
among the packet Grams.
