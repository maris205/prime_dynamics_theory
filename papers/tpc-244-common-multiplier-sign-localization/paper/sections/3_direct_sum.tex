\section{Common-multiplier phase blindness}

\begin{theorem}[orthogonal common-multiplier identity]
For $b_h,w_h\in\cH_h$ and $C_h\in\C$, put
\[
B=\bigoplus_{h\in\cA}C_hb_h,
\qquad
W=\bigoplus_{h\in\cA}C_hw_h.
\]
Then
\begin{align}
\ip{W}{B}&=\sum_{h\in\cA}|C_h|^2\ip{w_h}{b_h},\label{eq:cov}\\
\norm{B}^2&=\sum_{h\in\cA}|C_h|^2\norm{b_h}^2,\qquad
\norm{W}^2=\sum_{h\in\cA}|C_h|^2\norm{w_h}^2.\label{eq:norms}
\end{align}
Consequently, replacing $C_h$ by $\eta_hC_h$ simultaneously in both lanes,
with $|\eta_h|=1$, changes none of these quantities.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Distinct direct-sum blocks are orthogonal.  On one block,
\[
\ip{C_hw_h}{C_hb_h}
=\overline{C_h}C_h\ip{w_h}{b_h}
=|C_h|^2\ip{w_h}{b_h}.
\]
Summing proves \eqref{eq:cov}; taking equal lane vectors proves
\eqref{eq:norms}.  A common unit phase has unit modulus and therefore leaves
every factor $|C_h|^2$ unchanged.
\end{proof}

The empty family and one-block family are included.  Zero multipliers simply
remove blocks.  The conclusion fails in general if the lanes carry different
multipliers: if $A_h$ multiplies $w_h$ and $D_h$ multiplies $b_h$, the local
factor is $\overline{A_h}D_h$ and its phase is visible.

For the literal real $C_h$, a simultaneous external sign flip is therefore
invisible.  By contrast, changing a Möbius sign inside the defining sum can
change $|C_h|$, so the theorem does not erase or estimate internal arithmetic
cancellation.
