\begin{abstract}
We audit the first parity-facing arithmetic gate in TPC-MVP1.
After the exact TPC-93 content extraction, a literal generic block
has two primitive affine forms
\[
 D(z)=d+sz,\qquad V(z)=u+az,\qquad su-ad=h_0\ne0,
\]
the extracted sign and coprimality mask, the actual smooth and
periodic weights, and an additive phase
\(\e(-\alpha z)\) satisfying \(N\|\alpha\|>1\).  We write this
block without suppressing any coefficient and prove the exact
\(TT^*\) identity
\[
 \left|\sum_{z\in I}A(z)\e(-\alpha z)\right|^2
 =
 \sum_{|h|<N}\e(-\alpha h)
 \sum_{\substack{z,z+h\in I}}
 A(z+h)\overline{A(z)}.
\]
The off-diagonal kernel contains four literal M\"obius values.
Both shifted affine pairs still have determinant \(h_0\), but no
local-support theorem makes this four-sign correlation small.

For a \(q\)-invertible phase slope we also prove the complete
frequency identity
\[
 \sum_{r\in\F_q}
 \left|\sum_{z\in I}A(z)e_q(-r\omega z)\right|^2
 =
 q\sum_{u\in\F_q}
 \left|\sum_{\substack{z\in I\\z\equiv u\;(\mathrm{mod}\ q)}}
 A(z)\right|^2.
\]
This is a rigorous average-frequency ceiling, with an explicit
aliasing term, but it does not select the literal low-frequency
filter or control coherent outer reassembly.

Two obstructions explain why the gate remains open.  Genericity
alone yields only a constant geometric-series gain and is saturated
by bounded phase-matched coefficients.  More seriously, a broad
prefix-uniform power-saving theorem for all generic twists of
\(\lambda(n)\lambda(n+h_0)\) would, by Abel summation, imply a
power-saving ordinary two-point Chowla estimate.  Existing
logarithmically averaged or shift-averaged results do not have the
literal growing-family quantifiers.

We therefore formulate a restricted hypothesis only for the actual
TPC block family and give an exact outer-loss ledger at the numerical
exponent \(1/200\); the physical \(TT^*\) crosswalk remains a
separate requirement.  The identities,
frequency theorem, and obstruction are \(\Lzero\); the literal
dictionary is \(\Lone\).  No new \(\Ltwo\) fixed-\(h_0\) M\"obius
estimate is proved, and H3 remains an explicit hypothesis.
\end{abstract}

\noindent\textbf{Keywords:} fixed-shift affine M\"obius
correlation; generic phase; \(TT^*\); frequency aliasing; Chowla
barrier; parity problem.

\medskip
\noindent\textbf{Audit convention.}
\(\Lzero\) means exact finite algebra or a model obstruction,
\(\Lone\) an exact crosswalk to the physical fixed-\(h_0\) carrier,
and \(\Ltwo\) only a growing arithmetic estimate on that carrier.
An average over frequencies, forms, shifts, or scales is not promoted
to a result for the prescribed physical coefficient.

\section{The H3 target and its literal block}

TPC-MVP1 isolates the working target
\begin{equation}
 \mathcal G_{b,\mathrm{gen}}^{\mathrm{signed}}
 \ll XQ_b\,X^{-1/200+o(1)}
\label{eq:h3-target}
\end{equation}
for every actual long generic packet, together with a separately
specified physical \(TT^*\) normalization that must yield the
\(1/400\) packet saving \citep{WangTPCMVP1}.  Neither part is known.

TPC-93 supplies the exact generic block.  Its resolved key
\(\xi=(\theta,c,\kappa,r)\) determines an interval
\(I_\xi\subset\mathbb Z\), \(N_\xi=|I_\xi|\), and
\begin{align}
 D_\xi(z)&=d_\xi+s_\xi z,&
 V_\xi(z)&=u_\xi+a_\xi z,
\label{eq:forms}\\
 s_\xi u_\xi-a_\xi d_\xi&=h_0\ne0,&
 \alpha_\xi&=
 \frac{r\varepsilon_\theta\Omega_\xi}{cq_X}
 \pmod1.
\label{eq:det-phase}
\end{align}
The generic condition is
\begin{equation}
 N_\xi\|\alpha_\xi\|_{\mathbb R/\mathbb Z}>1.
\label{eq:generic}
\end{equation}
The exact coefficient is
\begin{align}
 A_{\xi,X}(z)
 ={}&
 \mu(D_\xi(z))\mu(V_\xi(z))
 \one_{(B_\xi,V_\xi(z))=1}
\notag\\
 &\times
 \chi_\theta(\tau_\xi+B_\xi z)
 W_{\theta,X}(\tau_\xi+B_\xi z),
\label{eq:literal-coefficient}
\end{align}
and the block transform is
\begin{equation}
 S_{\xi,X}
 =
 \sum_{z\in I_\xi}
 A_{\xi,X}(z)\e(-\alpha_\xi z),
\qquad
 \e(t)=e^{2\pi it}.
\label{eq:block-transform}
\end{equation}
The complete outer coefficient retains
\[
 \mathfrak c_{\xi,X}
 =
 c_{\theta,X}\mu(\kappa)\mu(B_\xi)
 \mathfrak m_{K,X}(r)\zeta_{\xi,X}.
\]
The generic low window is exactly
\begin{equation}
 \operatorname{Low}_{\mathrm{Gen},X}
 =
 \sum_{\xi\in\Xi_{\mathrm{Gen}}}
 \mathfrak c_{\xi,X}S_{\xi,X}.
\label{eq:generic-low-window}
\end{equation}
These formulas retain both polarizations, all prefixes and masks,
the moving coefficients, and one prescribed \(h_0\)
\citep{WangTPC93}.

\section{Exact \texorpdfstring{\(TT^*\)}{TT-star} and the
four-sign kernel}

For \(h\in\mathbb Z\), define
\begin{equation}
 C_{\xi,X}(h)
 =
 \sum_{\substack{z\in I_\xi\\z+h\in I_\xi}}
 A_{\xi,X}(z+h)\overline{A_{\xi,X}(z)}.
\label{eq:shift-correlation}
\end{equation}

\begin{theorem}[Literal one-block \(TT^*\)]
\label{thm:block-ttstar}
For every resolved block,
\begin{equation}
 \boxed{
 |S_{\xi,X}|^2
 =
 \sum_{|h|<N_\xi}
 \e(-\alpha_\xi h)C_{\xi,X}(h).}
\label{eq:block-ttstar}
\end{equation}
The diagonal is
\[
 C_{\xi,X}(0)=\sum_{z\in I_\xi}|A_{\xi,X}(z)|^2.
\]
For \(h\ne0\), the arithmetic factor in
\(C_{\xi,X}(h)\) is
\begin{align}
 &\mu(D_\xi(z+h))\mu(V_\xi(z+h))
\mu(D_\xi(z))\mu(V_\xi(z)),
\label{eq:four-mobius}
\end{align}
times the two exact coprimality masks, the shifted periodic/local
factor
\[
 \chi_\theta(\tau_\xi+B_\xi(z+h))
 \overline{\chi_\theta(\tau_\xi+B_\xi z)},
\]
and the corresponding shifted physical weights.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Expand \(|S_{\xi,X}|^2\), set \(h=z-z'\), and group terms with the
same difference.
\end{proof}

\begin{proposition}[Fixed determinant survives the shift]
\label{prop:shifted-determinant}
For every integer \(h\), the shifted pair
\[
 D_{\xi,h}(z)=D_\xi(z+h),\qquad
 V_{\xi,h}(z)=V_\xi(z+h)
\]
has the same determinant \(h_0\):
\[
 s_\xi(u_\xi+a_\xi h)
 -a_\xi(d_\xi+s_\xi h)=h_0.
\]
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
The two \(a_\xi s_\xi h\) terms cancel.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}[Why \(TT^*\) is not yet a saving]
TPC-90 classifies the squarefree local support and writes the
remaining signs as a Liouville parity correlation
\citep{WangTPC90}.  In \eqref{eq:four-mobius}, \(TT^*\) raises the
correlation order from two signs to four.  A useful \(TT^*\)
argument must gain from frequency or outer averaging; the formal
identity alone does not bound the off-diagonal kernel.
\end{remark}

\section{A complete-frequency theorem and the aliasing ledger}

On the nonconstant \(q_X\)-invertible branch, literal provenance
gives \(c\mid\Omega_\xi\) and
\[
 \omega_\xi
 =
 \varepsilon_\theta\frac{\Omega_\xi}{c}
 \pmod{q_X}
 \in\F_{q_X}^\times.
\]
Thus the phase is \(e_{q_X}(-r\omega_\xi z)\).

\begin{theorem}[Exact full-frequency alias identity]
\label{thm:full-frequency}
Let \(q\) be prime, \(\omega\in\F_q^\times\), \(I\subset\mathbb Z\)
finite, and \(A:I\to\C\).  Put
\[
 S(r)=\sum_{z\in I}A(z)e_q(-r\omega z).
\]
Then
\begin{equation}
 \boxed{
 \sum_{r\in\F_q}|S(r)|^2
 =
 q\sum_{u\in\F_q}
 \left|
 \sum_{\substack{z\in I\\z\equiv u\;(\mathrm{mod}\ q)}}
 A(z)
 \right|^2.}
\label{eq:alias-parseval}
\end{equation}
If the residues of \(I\) modulo \(q\) are distinct, in particular
if \(I\) is an interval of length at most \(q\), then
\begin{equation}
 \sum_{r\in\F_q}|S(r)|^2
 =
 q\sum_{z\in I}|A(z)|^2.
\label{eq:no-wrap-parseval}
\end{equation}
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Expanding the left side and using additive orthogonality gives
\[
 \sum_{r\in\F_q}e_q(-r\omega(z-z'))
 =
 q\one_{\{z\equiv z'\pmod q\}},
\]
because \(\omega\ne0\).  Group the remaining pairs by their residue
class.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[Filtered one-block ceiling]
\label{cor:filtered-ceiling}
For any \(\mathcal R\subseteq\F_q\) and filter \(m\) supported on
\(\mathcal R\),
\begin{equation}
 \left|\sum_{r\in\mathcal R}m(r)S(r)\right|
 \le
 \|m\|_2\sqrt q
 \left(
 \sum_{u\in\F_q}
 \left|\sum_{\substack{z\in I\\z\equiv u\pmod q}}A(z)\right|^2
 \right)^{1/2}.
\label{eq:filtered-ceiling}
\end{equation}
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
Apply Cauchy--Schwarz on \(\mathcal R\), enlarge to all frequencies,
and use \cref{thm:full-frequency}.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}[Average frequency is not the literal gate]
\Cref{eq:filtered-ceiling} is unconditional, but it has a
\(\sqrt q\) cost and must still be summed with the physical outer
coefficients.  It does not prove \eqref{eq:h3-target}; nor may the
complete-frequency average be reported as control of one prescribed
low-window combination.
\end{remark}

\section{Two sharp barriers}

\subsection{Genericity alone gives no power}

\begin{proposition}[Geometric sum and coefficient-blind saturation]
\label{prop:generic-saturation}
For an interval \(I\) of length \(N\),
\[
 \left|\sum_{z\in I}\e(-\alpha z)\right|
 \le
 \min\left\{N,\frac1{2\|\alpha\|}\right\}.
\]
Hence \(N\|\alpha\|>1\) supplies at most a constant-factor conclusion
near the generic boundary.  Moreover, among coefficients satisfying
\(|A(z)|=1\), the choice \(A(z)=\e(\alpha z)\) gives
\[
 \left|\sum_{z\in I}A(z)\e(-\alpha z)\right|=N.
\]
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
The first statement is the standard finite geometric-series bound.
The second is immediate term by term.
\end{proof}

The saturation coefficient is not claimed to be the literal
M\"obius coefficient.  It proves that generic phase, bounded
weights, and interval length alone cannot imply a power saving.

\subsection{A broad generic theorem reaches ordinary Chowla}

\begin{lemma}[Twisted-prefix removal]
\label{lem:twist-removal}
Let \(|b_n|\le1\), \(0<\delta<1\), and
\(\alpha_N=N^{-1+\delta}\).  If
\[
 \max_{1\le T\le N}
 \left|\sum_{n\le T}b_n\e(\alpha_Nn)\right|
 \le C N^{1-\eta},
\]
then
\begin{equation}
 \left|\sum_{n\le N}b_n\right|
 \le C'(1+N^\delta)N^{1-\eta}
 \ll N^{1-\eta+\delta}.
\label{eq:abel-transfer}
\end{equation}
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Let
\(B(T)=\sum_{n\le T}b_n\e(\alpha_Nn)\).
Partial summation applied to
\(b_n=B(n)-B(n-1)\) after removal of the twist gives
\[
 \left|\sum_{n\le N}b_n\right|
 \le
 \max_{T\le N}|B(T)|
 \left(1+N|1-\e(-\alpha_N)|\right).
\]
Use \(|1-\e(-t)|\le2\pi\|t\|\).
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[Broad-family Chowla barrier]
\label{cor:chowla-barrier}
Let \(\lambda\) denote the Liouville function.  Fix \(h_0>0\).
Suppose a theorem gave, for some
\(\eta>0\), the preceding prefix bound with
\[
 b_n=\lambda(n)\lambda(n+h_0)
\]
for every sufficiently large \(N\) and every generic phase
\(\alpha_N=N^{-1+\delta}\), where \(0<\delta<\eta\).
Then
\[
 \sum_{n\le N}\lambda(n)\lambda(n+h_0)
 \ll N^{1-(\eta-\delta)},
\]
a power-saving ordinary two-point Chowla estimate.
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
Since \(N\|\alpha_N\|=N^\delta>1\), these twists are generic.  Apply
\cref{lem:twist-removal}.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}[Restricted does not mean equivalent]
The literal H3 family contains only the phases, forms, masks, and
weights generated by the TPC dictionary.  It is not asserted to
contain every pair \((n,n+h_0)\) and every
\(\alpha_N=N^{-1+\delta}\).  Therefore
\cref{cor:chowla-barrier} does not prove H3 equivalent to Chowla.
It explains why a convenient uniform theorem over arbitrary affine
forms and generic phases would be unexpectedly deep.
\end{remark}

\section{\texorpdfstring{\hspace{0.35em}}{}Why existing averaged theorems do not fill the gap}

The logarithmically averaged two-point Chowla theorem controls fixed
affine forms with reciprocal weight
\citep{Tao2016LogChowla}.  Shift-averaged results average the
prescribed displacement \citep{MatomakiRadziwillTao2015}.  The
literal TPC blocks instead require:
\begin{enumerate}[label=(\roman*),leftmargin=2.2em]
\item growing slopes and intercepts but one prescribed nonzero
determinant \(h_0\);
\item ordinary smooth physical weights and actual interval origins;
\item uniform control of every prefix needed by partial summation;
\item exact content and coprimality masks;
\item the specified low-frequency multiplier and both
polarizations; and
\item coherent outer reassembly at a quantitative power rate.
\end{enumerate}
TPC-96 proves that these quantifiers cannot be supplied by an
anchor-free Tauberian conversion \citep{WangTPC96}.  This comparison
is a quantifier audit, not a criticism of the averaged theorems.

\section{The restricted hypothesis and an exact loss ledger}

For a block, put
\[
 V_{\xi,X}=\sum_{z\in I_\xi}|A_{\xi,X}(z)|,
\qquad
 \Delta_{\xi,X}
 =
 \begin{cases}
 |S_{\xi,X}|/V_{\xi,X},&V_{\xi,X}>0,\\
 0,&V_{\xi,X}=0.
 \end{cases}
\]
Define the actual outer absolute mass
\begin{equation}
 \mathcal M_{\mathrm{Gen},X}
 =
 \sum_{\xi\in\Xi_{\mathrm{Gen}}}
 |\mathfrak c_{\xi,X}|V_{\xi,X}.
\label{eq:outer-mass}
\end{equation}

\begin{theorem}[Exact termwise exponent ledger]
\label{thm:outer-ledger}
If, for fixed exponents \(\eta,\ell_{\mathrm{out}}\ge0\),
\begin{align}
 \sup_{\xi\in\Xi_{\mathrm{Gen}}}\Delta_{\xi,X}
 &\le X^{-\eta+o(1)},
\label{eq:block-saving}\\
 \mathcal M_{\mathrm{Gen},X}
 &\le X^{\ell_{\mathrm{out}}+o(1)}XQ_X,
\label{eq:outer-loss}
\end{align}
then
\begin{equation}
 |\operatorname{Low}_{\mathrm{Gen},X}|
 \le XQ_X\,X^{-(\eta-\ell_{\mathrm{out}})+o(1)}.
\label{eq:net-saving}
\end{equation}
Thus this termwise route reaches the numerical exponent \(1/200\)
for the displayed linear generic window if
\(\eta-\ell_{\mathrm{out}}\ge1/200\).  Identifying that bound with the
normalized quadratic object in \eqref{eq:h3-target} still requires
the exact physical \(TT^*\) crosswalk and prefactor.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Apply the triangle inequality to
\eqref{eq:generic-low-window}, insert
\(|S_{\xi,X}|=\Delta_{\xi,X}V_{\xi,X}\), and use
\eqref{eq:block-saving}--\eqref{eq:outer-loss}.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}[Sufficient, deliberately not necessary]
\Cref{thm:outer-ledger} discards cancellation between blocks.  A
coherent \(TT^*\) theorem may be strictly stronger.  Conversely,
the scale \(XQ_X\) in \eqref{eq:outer-loss} is not asserted for the
physical coefficient; it is a separately auditable normalization
condition.
\end{remark}

\begin{hypothesis}[Restricted actual-family arithmetic gate]
\label{hyp:restricted-gate}
For every actual generic packet \(b\), the complete normalized
signed \(TT^*\) object built from
\eqref{eq:literal-coefficient}--\eqref{eq:generic-low-window},
including all prefixes, satisfies
\[
 \mathcal G_{b,\mathrm{gen}}^{\mathrm{signed}}
 \ll XQ_b\,X^{-1/200+o(1)}.
\]
The exact physical \(TT^*\) prefactor is recorded separately and
converts this quadratic saving to the packet target without an
unrecorded loss.
\end{hypothesis}

\begin{remark}
\Cref{hyp:restricted-gate} is H3 stated on the now explicit
block dictionary.  It is not proved in this paper.  The point is to
prevent its replacement by a theorem for arbitrary weights, fixed
forms, averaged shifts, or a complete-frequency mean.
\end{remark}

\section{Claim boundary and next gate}

\subsection*{Proved}

The literal generic block has an exact \(TT^*\) correlation kernel
with four M\"obius factors, and both shifted pairs preserve
determinant \(h_0\).  Complete-frequency averaging has the exact
alias formula \eqref{eq:alias-parseval}.  Genericity alone cannot
give a power saving, and a broad prefix-uniform generic-twist theorem
would imply a power-saving ordinary Chowla estimate.  The termwise
outer-loss law \eqref{eq:net-saving} is exact.  These results are
\(\Lzero\); their attachment to the imported TPC-93 coefficient is
\(\Lone\).

\subsection*{Not proved}

This paper does not prove:
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=2em]
\item \cref{hyp:restricted-gate} or
\eqref{eq:h3-target};
\item a growing fixed-\(h_0\) M\"obius or Liouville cancellation
estimate;
\item the physical outer-mass bound \eqref{eq:outer-loss};
\item conversion of a complete-frequency mean into the literal
low-window combination;
\item the separate \(1/400\) packet normalization, full-block
return, determinant/zero-mode compatibility, or endpoint budget;
or
\item a parity breakthrough, Hardy--Littlewood asymptotic,
prime-pair lower bound, or twin-prime theorem.
\end{itemize}
Nothing is specialized to \(h_0=2\).

\subsection*{Verdict}

TPC-108 advances the route by replacing the phrase ``generic affine
dispersion'' with one exact arithmetic object and two precise
obstructions.  It does not open H3.  The next rational move is
TPC-109's coherent prime-square block test: exploit special
structure of one actual residual block, or prove a
coefficient-blind saturation theorem.  Such a theorem stops only
the subroute based on support, moduli, occupancy, and diagonal
energy; it does not rule out gains from literal arithmetic phase
relations or a proved dominant-prime statistic.
