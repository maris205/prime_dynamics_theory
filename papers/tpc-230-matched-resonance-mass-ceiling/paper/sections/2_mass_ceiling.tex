\section{The sharp matched-mass ceiling}

Let $G$ be a matching on a finite set of Hilbert vectors $u_q$.  Write
\[
 D=\sum_q\|u_q\|^2,
 \qquad M=\sum_{q\ {m matched}}\|u_q\|^2,
 \qquad U=D-M.
\]

\begin{theorem}[Matched-mass ceiling]
\label{thm:ceiling}
The corresponding AP energy satisfies
\begin{equation}
 \EAP\ge D-M,
 \qquad D-\EAP\le M.
 \label{eq:ceiling}
\end{equation}
Both inequalities are sharp.  In particular,
\begin{equation}
 \EAP\le(1-\delta)D\quad\Longrightarrow\quad \frac MD\ge\delta.
 \label{eq:necessary-mass}
\end{equation}
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Matching gives the orthogonal decomposition
\[
 \EAP=\sum_{q\ {m unmatched}}\|u_q\|^2
 +\sum_{(p,r)\in E(G)}\|u_p+u_r\|^2.
\]
The first sum is $D-M$ and the second is nonnegative, proving
\eqref{eq:ceiling}.  Choosing $u_r=-u_p$ on every edge makes the second sum vanish,
so the bound is sharp.  Combining the first inequality with the hypothesis in
\eqref{eq:necessary-mass} proves the implication.
\end{proof}

The theorem is a ceiling even under perfect arithmetic signs.  No local estimate can
compensate for insufficient matched mass.
