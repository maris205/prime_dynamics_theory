\begin{abstract}
RH-161 reduced the first determinant gate to a packet-to-Riesz estimate, but
its abstract theorem places the reset projection and transfer operator on one
Hilbert space.  In the archived physical construction the reset packet is a
spectral subspace of a source-memory Gram matrix, while the moving cloud is a
Riesz subspace of a noisy two-step transfer operator.  Equal rank does not
identify these spaces.

We make the missing ambient realization explicit.  Let
$J:\cE\to\cH$ be an isometry, let $P$ reduce a source operator $M$, and put
$\widehat P=JPJ^*$.  For a transfer operator $A$ define the primal and
adjoint defects
\[
 D=AJ-JM,\qquad D^\sharp=A^*J-JM^*.
\]
We prove the directed bounds
\[
 \norm{(I-\widehat P)A\widehat P}\leq\norm{DP},\qquad
 \norm{\widehat PA(I-\widehat P)}\leq\norm{D^\sharp P}.
\]
Thus a type-correct realization supplies exactly the two feedback couplings
needed by a Schur resolvent argument.  A two-dimensional witness proves that
without $J$ the source packet gives no coupling information.  We also record
the polar repair and its commutator debt for a near-isometric map.  A 512-case
finite audit has zero bound failures.  No canonical prime-dynamics $J$ or
all-level defect estimate is constructed, so physical interface R and Gate A
remain open.
\end{abstract}

\section{Why the ambient space is part of the theorem}
The RH-160 reset construction diagonalizes positive memory Grams on a source
or clock space \citep{WangRH160}.  RH-80 and RH-161 require a reducing Riesz
cloud for a transfer operator.  Writing both outputs as a rank-$r$ projection
suppresses the domain and is therefore not type-correct.  A rank, a list of
eigenvalues, or a fitted frame does not define a functorial map between the
two spaces.

Let $\cE$ be the source-memory Hilbert space and $\cH$ the transfer space.
Let $M\in\mathcal B(\cE)$, $A\in\mathcal B(\cH)$, and let $P$ be a finite-rank
orthogonal projection satisfying $PM=MP$.  An \emph{ambient realization} is
an isometry $J:\cE\to\cH$.  Its realized packet is
\[
 \widehat P=JPJ^*,\qquad \widehat Q=I-\widehat P.
\]
The complement includes both $J(\operatorname{ran}(I-P))$ and any directions
outside $J\cE$; this is essential when the dimensions differ.

\section{The primal--adjoint realization theorem}
\begin{theorem}[Realization defects control both couplings]
\label{thm:realization}
With the notation above, define
\[
 D=AJ-JM,\qquad D^\sharp=A^*J-JM^*.
\]
Then
\begin{align}
 \norm{\widehat QA\widehat P}&\leq\norm{DP},\label{eq:out}\\
 \norm{\widehat PA\widehat Q}&\leq\norm{D^\sharp P}.\label{eq:in}
\end{align}
Consequently the two-way feedback product obeys
\[
 \norm{\widehat PA\widehat Q}\,
 \norm{\widehat QA\widehat P}
 \leq \norm{D^\sharp P}\,\norm{DP}.
\]
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
For $x\in\operatorname{ran}P$, reduction gives $MPx\in\operatorname{ran}P$,
and hence $JMPx\in\operatorname{ran}\widehat P$.  Therefore
\[
 \widehat QAJx=\widehat Q(JMx+Dx)=\widehat QDx.
\]
Since $J$ is isometric on $\operatorname{ran}P$, taking the supremum proves
\eqref{eq:out}.  Applying the same argument to $A^*$ and $M^*$ gives
$\norm{\widehat QA^*\widehat P}\leq\norm{D^\sharp P}$.  Taking adjoints
proves \eqref{eq:in}.  Multiplication gives the final inequality.
\end{proof}

The adjoint defect is not a cosmetic duplication.  Nonnormal dynamics can
have a small right residual and a large left residual.  Later Schur bounds
use their product, while directional graph bounds distinguish them.

\begin{corollary}[Exact intertwining]
If $AJ=JM$ and $A^*J=JM^*$ on $\operatorname{ran}P$, then $\widehat P$
reduces $A$.  Any contour separating the realized packet spectrum from its
complement already has $\widehat P$ as its Riesz projection.
\end{corollary}
\begin{proof}
Both off-diagonal blocks vanish by Theorem~\ref{thm:realization}.
\end{proof}

\section{No coupling bound without a realization}
\begin{proposition}[Two-dimensional non-identifiability witness]
Fix the one-dimensional source data $\cE=\mathbb C$, $M=0$, and $P=I$.
Let $A=\operatorname{diag}(0,1)$ on $\mathbb C^2$.  There are isometries
$J_0,J_1:\cE\to\mathbb C^2$ realizing the identical source packet for which
\[
 \norm{(I-J_0J_0^*)AJ_0J_0^*}=0,qquad
 \norm{(I-J_1J_1^*)AJ_1J_1^*}=\frac12.
\]
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Take $J_0(1)=e_1$ and $J_1(1)=2^{-1/2}(e_1+e_2)$.  The first range is an
eigenspace.  In the orthonormal basis
$2^{-1/2}(e_1+e_2),2^{-1/2}(e_1-e_2)$, the off-diagonal entry of $A$ has
absolute value $1/2$.
\end{proof}

Thus packet positivity, overlap floors, and source-space rank do not bound a
transfer-space coupling.  The map $J$ and its dynamical defects are genuine
physical inputs, not coordinate notation.

\section{Near-isometries and polar repair}
Suppose a proposed map $J_0$ obeys
\[
 \norm{J_0^*J_0-I}\leq\eta<1.
\]
Then $G=J_0^*J_0$ is positive invertible and
$J=J_0G^{-1/2}$ is an isometry.  Functional calculus gives
\[
 \norm{G^{-1/2}}\leq(1-\eta)^{-1/2},\qquad
 \norm{G^{-1/2}-I}\leq(1-\eta)^{-1/2}-1.
\]
However,
\begin{align*}
 AJ-JM={}&(AJ_0-J_0M)G^{-1/2}\\
 &+J_0\bigl(MG^{-1/2}-G^{-1/2}M\bigr).
\end{align*}
The second term vanishes only when the source dynamics commutes with the Gram
correction.  A numerical orthogonalization that omits this commutator does
not certify the physical intertwining defect \citep{StewartSun1990}.

\section{Gauge covariance and a canonicity test}
An ambient realization should depend on the source subspace, not on a chosen
basis.  The following elementary identity is useful in implementation.

\begin{proposition}[Source-gauge covariance]
Let $U$ be unitary on $\cE$ and transform the source data by
\[
 M'=UMU^*,\qquad P'=UPU^*,\qquad J'=JU^*.
\]
Then the realized packet and directed defect norms are unchanged:
\[
 J'P'J'^*=JPJ^*,
\]
and
\[
 \norm{(AJ'-J'M')P'}=\norm{(AJ-JM)P},
\]
with the analogous identity for the adjoint defect.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
The first identity follows by cancellation of adjacent $U^*U$ factors.
Also
\[
 (AJ'-J'M')P'=(AJ-JM)PU^*,
\]
and right multiplication by a unitary preserves the norm.  Apply the same
calculation to $A^*$ and $M^*$.
\end{proof}

This gives a direct falsifier for a proposed physical construction: if a
rotation of the reset frame changes the realized transfer packet, the map is
frame-fitted rather than packet-canonical.  Three further tests remain
independent: $J$ must not use target zero data; its normalization must remain
controlled as the dimensions grow; and its common-coordinate transports
must be compatible with refinement.  Gauge covariance is necessary but not
sufficient for those stronger requirements.

\section{Finite audit and physical frontier}
The companion audit samples 512 random triples $(A,M,J)$ with dimensions
$10$, $7$, and packet rank $3$.  It evaluates both sides of
\eqref{eq:out}--\eqref{eq:in}; no failure occurs.  This checks the
implementation, not the prime-dynamics hypotheses.

For the archived route, the new subinterface X consists of:
\begin{enumerate}
\item a target-independent ambient map $J_j$ from each reset-memory space to
the transfer/determinant space;
\item a normalization and polar repair with controlled commutator;
\item primal and adjoint packet-defect bounds;
\item compatibility of these maps across the moving cloud schedule.
\end{enumerate}
Only after X is supplied can RH-161's contour estimate be applied to the
physical reset packet.  RH-162 proves the type-correct implication and the
impossibility of deleting X from this architecture.  It does not construct
X, a Riesz cloud, a canonical determinant, a scattering completion, a
self-adjoint operator, a zeta identity, or the Riemann Hypothesis.
