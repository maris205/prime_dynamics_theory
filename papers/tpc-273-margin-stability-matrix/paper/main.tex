\begin{abstract}
TPC-272 identified the correlation margin \(m=|C_{\perp}|/R\) as a necessary
input for converting a signed residual estimate into an endpoint estimate.
We now test whether that margin is stable under the declared finite cutoff,
clock, and kernel choices, while keeping the literal V59 physical operator
fixed.  On a 32-row grid consisting of four scales, four comparison cutoffs,
and two kernel exponents, outward rational intervals classify 12 rows with
\(m<1/8\), 11 middle-band rows, and 9 rows with \(m>1/4\).  At fixed scale
and exponent, changing only the cutoff crosses from the middle band to the
high band at \(N=64\), and from the middle band to the low band at \(N=128\).
The same grid has 30 negative-real and two positive-real scalar phases.  This
is a certified finite stability obstruction for the declared interface, not
an asymptotic counterexample, a source-level theorem, or a twin-prime proof.
\end{abstract}

\section{Question and claim ceiling}

The residual route has a signed scalar \(C_{\perp}\) and a radius
\(R=\sqrt{W_{\perp}G_{\perp}}\).  TPC-272 proved that a scalar saving and a
quantitative lower bound on \(m=|C_{\perp}|/R\) combine as a
\(\sigma-\eta\) endpoint budget.  A finite phase label is not such a lower
bound.  The present paper makes the next minimal hostile test: hold the
physical operator, masks, shell, beta, and projection fixed, and vary only
declared local cutoff and kernel parameters.

The claim ceiling is
\[
 \texttt{NUMERICALLY\_CERTIFIED\_FINITE},\qquad
 \texttt{REFUTED\_SCOPED},
\]
for the registered matrix.  ``Scoped'' is part of the statement: the grid
does not represent a proved growing sequence.  Fixed-power credit,
arithmetic \(L^2\), full Gate B, and the twin-prime conclusion remain absent.

\section{Frozen finite object}

For each \(N\) we use the same finite literal operator as the parent
TPC-268 audit: prime shell \(Q<q\leq2Q\), outer weight \(q\), the two unit
masks, deleted diagonal, exact finite beta, and the four consecutive equal
blocks with their three Haar contrasts.  The physical scale triples are
\[
 (N,H,Q)=(64,15,4),(96,20,5),(128,24,5),(192,32,6).
\]
For a cutoff \(z\in\{2,3,4,5\}\) and exponent \(s\in\{1,2\}\), the kernel
is \(K_H(h)=(1+(h/H)^2)^{-s}\).  No source coefficient is replaced by a
synthetic random vector.

Let \(P_3\) be the declared rank-three block projection.  The parent engine
returns the interval for
\[
 \rho^2=\frac{|\Cperp|^2}{\|(I-P_3)w\|^2\|(I-P_3)g\|^2}.
\]
The residual norm intervals are positive and the scalar interval is separated
from zero on every registered row.

\begin{lemma}[margin transfer]
On every registered row,
\[
 \marg^2=\rho^2,\qquad \marg^6=(\rho^2)^3.
\]
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Both quantities are the same quotient after substituting
\(R^2=\|(I-P_3)w\|^2\|(I-P_3)g\|^2\).  Positivity makes squaring and cubing
monotone, so the parent outward interval transfers directly.  \(\square\)
\end{proof}

This elementary identity is useful operationally: the certificate never
extracts a floating-point square root to decide a margin band.

\section{The 32-row matrix}

The low and high tests are deliberately separated:
\[
 \rho^2<\frac1{64}\Longrightarrow m<\frac18,qquad
 \rho^2>\frac1{16}\Longrightarrow m>\frac14.
\]
Rows failing both tests are retained as the middle band.  The resulting
counts are
\[
 \begin{array}{c|ccc}
  &m<1/8&1/8\leq m\leq1/4&m>1/4\\ \hline
  \text{rows}&12&11&9.
 \end{array}
\]
The exact JSON stores rational endpoints; a few representative intervals are
shown below in decimal form.

\begin{theorem}[finite margin-stability obstruction]
The 32 registered rows have 12 low, 11 middle, and 9 high margin
classifications.  At fixed \((N,s)=(64,1)\), the cutoff-only transition
\(z=2\to5\) is middle-to-high.  At fixed \((N,s)=(128,1)\), the
cutoff-only transition \(z=2\to3\) is middle-to-low.  Each transition is
separated by its stored outward intervals.  Therefore uniform margin
stability for this declared finite parameter family is \texttt{REFUTED\_SCOPED}.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
The producer evaluates all 32 cases and applies the two rational threshold
tests above.  The independent checker recomputes each row through the frozen
parent engine and transfers the interval using Lemma 1.  For \(N=64\), the
\(z=2\) upper endpoint is below \(1/16\), whereas the \(z=5\) lower endpoint
is above \(1/16\).  For \(N=128\), the \(z=2\) row lies above \(1/64\),
whereas the \(z=3\) upper endpoint lies below \(1/64\).  Since only the
declared cutoff changes in each comparison, the asserted finite instability
follows.  \(\square\)
\end{proof}

The corresponding squared-margin ratios are approximately \(2.75\) and
\(0.969\), respectively.  They are finite transition diagnostics, not
exponents in \(N\).

\section{Phase census and kernel control}

The scalar phase census contains 30
\texttt{NEGATIVE\_REAL\_AXIS} rows and two
\texttt{POSITIVE\_REAL\_AXIS} rows.  The positive rows are exactly
\[
 (N,z,s)=(192,3,1),\qquad(192,4,1).
\]
They are retained in the matrix because deleting phase flips would make the
stability test circular.  At \(N=96,z=3\), changing only \(s=1\to2\) keeps
both rows in the high margin band; the associated squared-margin ratio is
enclosed near \(0.928\).  Thus the present obstruction is specifically driven
by cutoff sensitivity in the two displayed transitions, while the kernel
control provides a matched non-flip.

\begin{theorem}[finite phase census]
Within the declared grid, the phase counts are 30 negative-real, 2
positive-real, and 0 zero-crossing intervals.  This census does not imply an
eventual phase sector and does not imply a positive margin lower bound.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
The scalar intervals supplied by each parent-engine evaluation are separated
from zero.  Counting their signs gives the stated numbers; the independent
replay checks the same keys and signs.  The final sentence follows from the
two-dimensional sign-only construction already formalized in TPC-272, and is
also visible here from the coexistence of low-margin rows with either sign.
\end{proof}

\section{Interpretation and limits}

The new paper makes one narrow advancement: it converts the qualitative
``cutoff may matter'' warning into a threshold-separated margin matrix.  It
does not claim that the finite cutoff values are the source-level growing
rule, nor that a finite crossing supplies an asymptotic subsequence.  In
particular,

\begin{enumerate}
\item no row count is converted into a power saving or loss;
\item the two positive phase rows are not discarded or averaged away;
\item the parent operator and its exact beta are unchanged;
\item arithmetic \(L^2\), signed four-packet reassembly, and full Gate B are
      untouched.
\end{enumerate}

The next legitimate source-level question is uniformity on the registered
growing cutoff itself.  If that theorem fails, its failure must be stated in
the actual source family rather than inferred from this finite matrix.

\section{Conclusion}

TPC-273 supplies a hostile finite test of the new TPC-272 margin coordinate.
The same literal finite operator exhibits both low and high correlation bands
under cutoff-only changes, and it also exhibits two phase sign flips.  The
result is a useful obstruction to treating a declared finite interface as
automatically stable.  It leaves the source-level margin lower bound, the
strict \(1/400\) payment, arithmetic \(L^2\), and the twin-prime problem open.
