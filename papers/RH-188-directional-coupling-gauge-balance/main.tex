\title{Directional Coupling and Gauge Balance\\
\large A Surviving Schur Product Behind the Failed Oblique Maximum-Norm Gate}
\author{Liang Wang}
\date{July 2026}

\begin{abstract}
RH-186--187 show that maximum-residual oblique budgets and cross-Gram
clipping fail on every physical bi-Krylov window.  Those tests combine the two
directions too early.  A Schur complement instead depends on the product of
left and right couplings.  This paper isolates its gauge structure.

Under the scalar packet gauge $V\mapsto\alpha V$,
$W\mapsto\alpha^{-1}W$, the right coupling scales by $\alpha$ and the left
coupling by $\alpha^{-1}$.  Their product is invariant.  The maximum of the
two is minimized uniquely at
$\alpha=\sqrt{b/c}$, where both become $\sqrt{bc}$.  Thus scalar balancing
cannot improve the directed Schur product $adbc$, but it gives the optimal
symmetric representative for numerical work.

Replaying the 126 RH-185 windows, the absolute coupling product ranges from
$0.2486$ to $2.6847\times10^{10}$.  Eight windows have $bc<1$; all eight are
in the local $\sigma=0.01,L=4$ branch.  Twelve local windows have relative
coupling product below $0.01$.  Hence the failure of the coarse
$\chi\max(\epsilon_R,\epsilon_L)$ gate does not by itself kill the sharp
directional route.

The result is intentionally incomplete.  A Riesz theorem also requires the
packet and complement resolvent factors $a$ and $d$.  Neither is supplied
here.  The next paper constructs the exact oblique Feshbach factorization
that defines those quantities.
\end{abstract}

\section{Why products matter}

For a right/left packet pair $V,W$ with $W^*V=I$, the directed residuals
\begin{equation}\label{eq:residuals}
 C=(I-P)AV,
 \qquad
 B^*=(I-P^*)A^*W,
 \qquad P=VW^*,
\end{equation}
are the two off-diagonal couplings of the oblique block decomposition.  A
Schur--Feshbach feedback term has the schematic form
\begin{equation}\label{eq:feedback}
 \Sigma(z)=B(z-D)^{-1}C.
\end{equation}
A sufficient rank-preservation condition is therefore controlled by
\begin{equation}\label{eq:schur-product}
 a(z)d(z)\norm B\norm C<1,
\end{equation}
where $a$ bounds the packet resolvent and $d$ the complement resolvent
\citep{GohbergGoldbergKaashoek1990,WangRH171}.

The maximum-norm gate of RH-186 is useful but stronger than
\eqref{eq:schur-product}.  Before rejecting the route, the two coupling
directions should be balanced and their invariant product recorded.

\section{Scalar packet gauge}

Let $b=\norm B$ and $c=\norm C$.  For $\alpha>0$, apply
\begin{equation}\label{eq:scalar-gauge}
 V_\alpha=\alpha V,
 \qquad
 W_\alpha=\alpha^{-1}W.
\end{equation}
Biorthogonality and the oblique projector remain unchanged.  The compressed
operator is unchanged for a scalar gauge, while
\begin{equation}\label{eq:coupling-scale}
 b_\alpha=\alpha^{-1}b,
 \qquad
 c_\alpha=\alpha c.
\end{equation}

\begin{theorem}[Optimal scalar balance]\label{thm:balance}
If $b,c>0$, then
\begin{equation}\label{eq:product-invariant}
 b_\alpha c_\alpha=bc
\end{equation}
for every $\alpha>0$, and
\begin{equation}\label{eq:minimax}
 \min_{\alpha>0}\max(b_\alpha,c_\alpha)=\sqrt{bc}.
\end{equation}
The unique minimizer is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:optimal-alpha}
 \alpha_*=\sqrt{b/c},
\end{equation}
at which $b_{\alpha_*}=c_{\alpha_*}=\sqrt{bc}$.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Equation \eqref{eq:product-invariant} follows immediately from
\eqref{eq:coupling-scale}.  For any two nonnegative numbers with product
$bc$, their maximum is at least their geometric mean $\sqrt{bc}$, with
equality exactly when they are equal.  Solving
$\alpha^{-1}b=\alpha c$ gives \eqref{eq:optimal-alpha}.
\end{proof}

If one coupling vanishes, the product is zero and the remaining coupling can
be made arbitrarily small by a singular limit of the scalar gauge.  In
finite numerical work one would retain a bounded gauge, but the Schur
feedback is already zero.

The minimax statement has a useful weighted form.  It applies when two
directions are displayed in different coordinate norms.

\begin{proposition}[Weighted scalar balance]\label{prop:weighted-balance}
For $b,c,\mu,\nu>0$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:weighted-minimax}
 \min_{\alpha>0}
 \max\!\left(\mu\alpha^{-1}b,\nu\alpha c\right)
 =\sqrt{\mu\nu bc}.
\end{equation}
The unique minimizer is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:weighted-alpha}
 \alpha_*=\sqrt{\frac{\mu b}{\nu c}}.
\end{equation}
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
The product of the two displayed terms is the invariant
$\mu\nu bc$, so their maximum is at least its square root.  Equality holds
exactly when the two terms agree, which gives
\eqref{eq:weighted-alpha}.
\end{proof}

This weighted form can equalize outward left and right error bars, but it
still cannot change their product.  It is therefore a preconditioner for
validated arithmetic, not an additional source of Schur margin.

\section{Gauge-invariant Schur condition}

\begin{corollary}[Scalar-gauge invariance of feedback]\label{cor:feedback}
For fixed packet and complement resolvent bounds $a,d$, the sufficient Schur
product
\begin{equation}\label{eq:invariant-feedback}
 \kappa=adbc
\end{equation}
is invariant under \eqref{eq:scalar-gauge}.  Scalar balancing can improve the
largest displayed coupling but cannot turn a failing $\kappa$ into a passing
one.
\end{corollary}

This separates presentation from substance.  The balanced representative is
best for symmetric floating-point ranges; the product is the proof datum.

\section{Physical coupling definitions}

For each RH-185 record, the absolute couplings are
\begin{equation}\label{eq:physical-bc}
 c=\norm{AV-VK},
 \qquad
 b=\norm{A^*W-WK^*}.
\end{equation}
Their relative counterparts are
\begin{equation}\label{eq:relative-product}
 \widehat c=\frac c{\norm{AV}},
 \qquad
 \widehat b=\frac b{\norm{A^*W}},
 \qquad
 \widehat b\widehat c.
\end{equation}
The relative product is a scale-normalized diagnostic; the absolute product
is the quantity entering a fixed ambient Schur estimate.

\section{Physical audit}

Across all 126 windows:
\begin{center}
\begin{tabular}{lrrr}
\toprule
quantity & minimum & median & maximum\\
\midrule
$bc$ & $0.24862$ & $727.445$ & $2.6847\times10^{10}$\\
$\sqrt{bc}$ & $0.49862$ & $26.9711$ & $1.6385\times10^5$\\
$\widehat b\widehat c$ & $5.826\times10^{-4}$ & $3.5002$ & $4.0232\times10^5$\\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{center}

There are eight windows with
\begin{equation}\label{eq:bc-below-one}
 bc<1.
\end{equation}
All eight occur at $\sigma=0.01,L=4$.  There are twelve local length-four
windows with
\begin{equation}\label{eq:relative-small}
 \widehat b\widehat c<0.01,
\end{equation}
matching the twelve two-sided residual-gate windows from RH-185.

This is a genuine surviving signal.  The exact oblique condition number can
be hundreds while the product of the two actual coupling residuals is less
than one.  Therefore the negative maximum-residual result of RH-186 is not a
logical no-go for the directed Schur route \citep{WangRH186}.

The eight values below one should not be read as eight shell certificates.
They omit both resolvent factors and use nominal floating couplings.  Their
proper role is triage: they identify a small set of windows for which a
validated complement calculation is not ruled out before it starts.  The
remaining 118 windows already require a resolvent product below $1/(bc)$,
which can be extremely small.

\section{Why the Riesz certificate is still open}

The coupling product alone is not enough.  On a contour $\Gamma$ around a
candidate cycle root, one needs
\begin{equation}\label{eq:full-gate}
 \sup_{z\in\Gamma}
 \norm{(z-K)^{-1}}
 \norm{(z-D)^{-1}}
 bc<1.
\end{equation}
Even the minimum $bc=0.2486$ can fail if the two resolvent factors have
product larger than about four.  Near a packet root, the packet resolvent is
necessarily large on a small contour.  The complement factor is completely
unknown in the present physical realization.

The next requirement is therefore structural: define the complement block
$D$ in a type-correct oblique coordinate system and derive the exact
determinant/Feshbach identity.  Only then can a contour audit distinguish a
real complement obstruction from a crude norm artifact.

\section{Matrix gauges beyond scalars}

An invertible matrix gauge $G$ transforms
$V\mapsto VG$, $W\mapsto WG^{-*}$ and $K\mapsto G^{-1}KG$.  Such a gauge
can redistribute directions nonuniformly.  The exact optimization becomes a
matrix balancing problem and may improve computable upper bounds.  It cannot
change the underlying Schur self-energy as an ambient operator.  This paper
uses the scalar theorem because it is exact, transparent, and sufficient to
show that the directional branch has not been eliminated.

More explicitly, in packet/complement coordinates
\begin{equation}\label{eq:block-gauge}
 \begin{pmatrix}K&B\\C&D\end{pmatrix}
 \longmapsto
 \begin{pmatrix}G^{-1}KG&G^{-1}B\\CG&D\end{pmatrix}.
\end{equation}
Hence
\begin{equation}\label{eq:self-energy-gauge}
 B(z-D)^{-1}C
 \longmapsto
 G^{-1}B(z-D)^{-1}CG.
\end{equation}
The self-energy spectrum and reduced determinant are gauge invariant even
though a product of separate matrix norms need not be.  Scalar gauges are
special because both norms are multiplied by reciprocal positive scalars,
making $bc$ itself invariant.  A later matrix-balancing optimization must
therefore report the exact self-energy or a rigorously transformed norm
bound, not merely a smaller-looking pair of couplings.

\section{Requirements for the next certificate}

For each surviving window a complete finite certificate needs four outward
quantities on the same contour:
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=2em]
\item a packet inverse bound for $zI-K$;
\item a complement inverse bound for $zI-D$;
\item an upper bound for the left coupling $B$;
\item an upper bound for the right coupling $C$.
\end{enumerate}
The gauge used to compute them must be recorded, and the final product must
be invariant under any later change of packet coordinates.  RH-189 supplies
the exact block identity that makes these requirements type-correct.

\section{Boundary}

This paper proves scalar-gauge invariance and the optimal minimax balance,
and reports the physical coupling products.  It does not provide packet or
complement resolvent bounds, a continuous contour certificate, a physical
Riesz rank, all-level scale transport, R, Gate A, or a downstream
Hilbert--Polya/RH statement.
