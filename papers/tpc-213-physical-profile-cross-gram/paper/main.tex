\begin{abstract}
The transition-native twin-prime route contains one residual profile for every
squarefree divisor, but these profiles are generated by the same physical
sequence.  Treating them as an orthogonal direct sum therefore changes the
operator before the first Cauchy estimate.  This paper constructs the missing
finite coupling map.  A residue lift sends a common source on a finite support
to every divisor residue space; the reciprocal emitter is then pulled back to
that support.  We prove an exact affine pullback identity, an exact gcd/lcm
formula for cross-divisor residue lifts, and an exact frequency-intersection
formula for the Hermitian Gram of the pulled-back emitters.  In a complete
period, off-diagonal terms survive precisely when the rational frequencies
agree.  The fixture with divisors $5,7,35$, reciprocal primes $11,13,17$, and
$H=40$ gives cross-Gram values $560$ and $770$ for the nested pairs, while the
coprime pair has zero cross-Gram.  These identities establish a real structural
advance over a direct-sum emitter model, but they do not estimate the literal
smooth V46 kernel.  The prime shell, four-packet signs, asymptotic Gram bound,
Gate B, and arithmetic $L^2$ credit remain open.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}

The current analytic route to the twin-prime problem reduces a fixed-gap count
to a transition scalar indexed by squarefree divisors.  In the V46 formulation,
the transition residual has the schematic form
\begin{equation}
 \mathfrak R_x^{\mathrm{AP}}
 =-H\sum_{Y_0<d\leq U}\sum_{r\bmod d}
 A_d(r)\widehat{\mathcal R}_d(r),
 \label{eq:v46}
\end{equation}
where the reciprocal emitter is
\begin{equation}
 A_d(r)=\frac{\muM(d)\log d}{d}
 \sum_{q\in\mathcal Q}\sum_{0<|m|\leq dq/H}
 \psi\!\left(\frac{Hm}{dq}\right)
 \one_{r\equiv m\overline q\pmod d},
 \label{eq:emitter}
\end{equation}
and $\mathcal R_d$ is the arithmetic-progression profile after subtracting
the divisor-dependent Euler correction \cite{WangV46}.

TPC-212 isolated the divisor cut and the reciprocal occupancy map.  In the
natural direct sum of residue spaces, the emitter Gram is block diagonal, so
unit-weight residuals can align all blocks.  That is a valid interface
obstruction, but it deliberately leaves open whether the literal residuals
can be chosen independently \cite{WangTPC211,WangTPC212}.  The next question is therefore
more basic than an asymptotic estimate:

\begin{quote}
What is the exact operator obtained when all divisor profiles are generated
from one common physical sequence before Cauchy and before the outer absolute
value?
\end{quote}

This paper answers that operator question.  Let $\UU$ be a finite set of
physical indices.  For each divisor $d$, the residue lift aggregates a common
source by congruence classes.  The additive transform and the emitter then
pull the result back to $\UU$.  The total source term contains the sum of these
pulled-back kernels, not an orthogonal sum.  The cross-divisor Gram is governed
by shared rational frequencies and by the gcd of the moduli.

The results are structural and finite.  They make no claim about the shifted
prime sequence, and the certificate uses unit reciprocal weights only.  The
main contributions are:
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=2em]
 \item an exact common-source pullback identity that retains the divisor-
       dependent profile correction;
 \item an exact Chinese-remainder formula for the cross blocks of the residue
       lift;
 \item an exact frequency-intersection formula for the physical emitter Gram;
 \item a finite certificate showing nonzero nested-divisor cross terms and
       explicitly separating this structural result from an asymptotic saving.
\end{enumerate}

\section{The common-source physical operator}

\subsection{Residue lifts and transforms}

Let $\Dd$ be a finite family of positive moduli and let $\UU$ be a finite set
of integers.  For a source vector $f=(f(u))_{u\in\UU}$ define the residue lift
\begin{equation}
 (C_df)(a)=\sum_{\substack{u\in\UU\\u\equiv a\pmod d}} f(u),
 \qquad a\in\ZZ/d\ZZ.
 \label{eq:lift}
\end{equation}
The unnormalized additive transform is
\begin{equation}
 (\F_d g)(r)=\sum_{a\bmod d}g(a)\ee_d(ra),
 \qquad \ee_d(x)=\exp(2\pi i x/d).
 \label{eq:dft}
\end{equation}
The notation deliberately matches the unnormalized transform in the V46
transition artifact.

For each $d\in\Dd$, let $A_d(r)$ be any finite complex emitter row and define
its physical pullback by
\begin{equation}
 K_d(u)=\sum_{r\bmod d} A_d(r)\ee_d(ru),
 \qquad u\in\UU.
 \label{eq:pullback-kernel}
\end{equation}
The literal V46 emitter in \eqref{eq:emitter} is one instance.  No estimate is
assumed in the following identities.

The divisor-dependent profile correction is most conveniently written as a
source correction.  Let $v(u)$ be the common physical sequence and let
$b_d(u)$ be a correction that may depend on $d$ through the local Euler
profile.  Set
\begin{equation}
 R_d=C_d(v-b_d).
 \label{eq:residual-vector}
\end{equation}
Thus $R_d(a)$ is the residue-class sum of the same $v$ with a different
divisor correction, exactly as in the AP residual in V46.

\begin{theorem}[common-source pullback]
\label{thm:pullback}
For finite $\UU$, $\Dd$, source $v$, corrections $b_d$, and emitter rows
$A_d$, one has the exact bilinear identity
\begin{equation}
 \sum_{d\in\Dd}\sum_{r\bmod d} A_d(r)(\F_dR_d)(r)
 =\sum_{u\in\UU}v(u)K(u)
 -\sum_{d\in\Dd}\sum_{u\in\UU}b_d(u)K_d(u),
 \label{eq:pullback-identity}
\end{equation}
where
\begin{equation}
 K(u)=\sum_{d\in\Dd}K_d(u).
 \label{eq:joint-kernel}
\end{equation}
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
For a fixed $d$, substitute \eqref{eq:residual-vector} and \eqref{eq:dft}:
\begin{align*}
 \sum_r A_d(r)(\F_dR_d)(r)
 &=\sum_rA_d(r)\sum_a\sum_{\substack{u\in\UU\\u\equiv a\pmod d}}
       (v(u)-b_d(u))\ee_d(ra).
\end{align*}
Whenever $u\equiv a\pmod d$, one has $\ee_d(ra)=\ee_d(ru)$.
All sums are finite, so interchanging their order gives
\begin{equation*}
 \sum_r A_d(r)(\F_dR_d)(r)
 =\sum_{u\in\UU}(v(u)-b_d(u))
   \sum_rA_d(r)\ee_d(ru)
 =\sum_{u\in\UU}(v(u)-b_d(u))K_d(u).
\end{equation*}
Summing over $d$ and collecting the common $v(u)$ term proves
\eqref{eq:pullback-identity}.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}
The second term in \eqref{eq:pullback-identity} is essential.  It is the
finite analogue of the V46 $\Delta_{d,z}$ correction and cannot be silently
absorbed into $v$ because it changes with $d$.  The first term, however, uses
one common source and the joint kernel $K=\sum_dK_d$.
\end{remark}

\section{The residue-lift cross blocks}

The common-source statement already shows why a direct sum is not the physical
operator.  The next proposition identifies the lift cross blocks exactly.

\begin{proposition}[gcd/lcm residue aliasing]
\label{prop:crt}
Let $d,e\geq2$, let $L$ be a positive multiple of $\operatorname{lcm}(d,e)$,
and take $\UU=\{0,1,\ldots,L-1\}$.  Then
\begin{equation}
 (C_dC_e^*)(a,b)
 =\frac{L}{\operatorname{lcm}(d,e)}
   \one_{a\equiv b\pmod{\gcd(d,e)}}.
 \label{eq:crt-cross}
\end{equation}
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
The $(a,b)$ entry counts solutions in the complete interval to
$u\equiv a\pmod d$ and $u\equiv b\pmod e$.  The Chinese remainder theorem
says that the system is solvable if and only if the two residues agree modulo
$g=\gcd(d,e)$.  If it is solvable, its solutions form one class modulo
$L_0=\operatorname{lcm}(d,e)$.  There are exactly $L/L_0$ such solutions in
the chosen interval, which gives \eqref{eq:crt-cross}.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[the lift is generally not orthogonal]
\label{cor:lift-nonorthogonal}
If $\gcd(d,e)>1$, then $C_dC_e^*$ has nonzero entries on every compatible
residue pair.  Thus a common physical source produces cross-divisor residue
coupling before an emitter is applied.
\end{corollary}

The statement is elementary but important for the route.  TPC-212's direct sum
is a useful obstruction space; it is not the result of applying the literal
residue lifts to one common sequence.  In a finite family containing a maximal
modulus, the joint lift can also have fewer independent output coordinates than
the sum of the individual row counts.  The certificate below gives a concrete
instance with $5+7+35=47$ output rows but rank $35$.

\section{The emitter pullback Gram}

The residue lift is only one half of the coupling.  The emitter can create or
remove cross terms depending on its frequency support.

\begin{theorem}[finite-interval pullback Gram]
\label{thm:general-gram}
For any finite support $\UU$ and any $d,e\in\Dd$,
\begin{equation}
 \sum_{u\in\UU}K_d(u)\overline{K_e(u)}
 =\sum_{r\bmod d}\sum_{s\bmod e}
 A_d(r)\overline{A_e(s)}
 \sum_{u\in\UU}\ee\!\left(u\left(\frac rd-\frac se\right)\right).
 \label{eq:general-gram}
\end{equation}
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Insert \eqref{eq:pullback-kernel} for $K_d$ and the conjugate expansion for
$K_e$.  The resulting sum is finite, so the order of summation may be
interchanged.  The inner sum is precisely the finite exponential sum displayed
in \eqref{eq:general-gram}.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[complete-period frequency intersection]
\label{thm:frequency-gram}
Let $L=\operatorname{lcm}(d,e)$ and $\UU=\{0,\ldots,L-1\}$.  Then
\begin{equation}
 \sum_{u=0}^{L-1}K_d(u)\overline{K_e(u)}
 =L\sum_{\substack{r\bmod d,\ s\bmod e\\r/d\equiv s/e\pmod 1}}
 A_d(r)\overline{A_e(s)}.
 \label{eq:frequency-gram}
\end{equation}
In particular, the cross Gram is zero if the two emitter frequency supports
have no common rational frequency.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
For each pair $(r,s)$, the inner exponential sum in
\eqref{eq:general-gram} is a geometric series with ratio
$\ee(r/d-s/e)$.  Since $L(r/d-s/e)$ is an integer, the ratio is an $L$th root
of unity.  The sum over a complete period is $L$ if the ratio is one, which
is equivalent to $r/d-s/e\in\ZZ$, and is zero otherwise.  Substitution into
\eqref{eq:general-gram} proves the formula.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[the physical Gram is not block diagonal in general]
\label{cor:physical-gram}
Suppose there are $d\ne e$ for which the right-hand side of
\eqref{eq:frequency-gram} is nonzero.  Then the physical pullback Gram has a
nonzero $(d,e)$ entry.  Consequently, replacing the common-source family by
an orthogonal direct sum changes the quadratic form.
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
The right-hand side is exactly the $(d,e)$ Gram entry by
Theorem~\ref{thm:frequency-gram}.  A block-diagonal matrix has zero entries
between distinct divisor blocks, so a nonzero entry cannot be represented by
that direct sum without an additional projection or cancellation theorem.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}[what the theorem does not say]
The existence of an off-diagonal Gram entry is not itself a saving.  It can have
either sign after the $\muM(d)\log d$ coefficients and packet signs are
inserted, and it can increase a norm for an aligned vector.  The theorem only
identifies the exact object that a physical Gram estimate must control.
\end{remark}

\subsection{Interpretation}

The fixture supplies the missing interface theorem at the finite algebraic
level: the literal common-source map produces nontrivial nested-divisor Gram
entries.  It also prevents a stronger but unsupported conclusion.  Because the
cross terms are positive in the unit-weight fixture, coupling by itself does
not imply a saving.  In the physical problem the coefficients include
$\muM(d)\log d/d$, the smooth function $\psi$, and the signed packet
reassembly.  Those ingredients may create cancellation, but that is a new
estimate rather than a consequence of the lift identity.

Three limitations matter for the next step.  First, the certificate uses
$\psi=1$ and omits the logarithmic scalar in order to keep the finite Gram
integer-valued.  Second, complete-period orthogonality is not an asymptotic
estimate for an arbitrary interval; the exact finite-interval formula is
Theorem~\ref{thm:general-gram}.  Third, the profile correction remains an
affine term in \eqref{eq:pullback-identity}; it has not been absorbed into a
single source sequence.

The next natural problem is therefore to group the literal V46 kernel by shared
rational frequencies, retain the signs and smooth weights, and prove a bound
for the resulting joint clusters.  If such clusters remain positive, the
correct output is an obstruction rather than an advertised saving.  Either
outcome is more informative than applying a direct-sum Cauchy estimate before
the coupling map is constructed.

\section{Conclusion}

TPC-213 resolves the operator-level ambiguity left by TPC-212.  The physical
residuals are generated by one common source, and the corresponding emitter
pullbacks have an exact cross-divisor Gram.  Chinese-remainder compatibility
controls the residue lifts; rational-frequency intersections control the
complete-period emitter Gram.  The finite certificate exhibits the predicted
nested-divisor cross terms and verifies the profile invariants exactly.

The result is a structural bridge, not a twin-prime theorem.  It earns no
arithmetic $L^2$ credit and leaves the strict $1/400$ endpoint unpaid.  The
next theorem must estimate the joint pullback in the literal V46 range while
preserving the smooth emitter, packet signs, zero-axis normalization, and
prime-shell reassembly.
