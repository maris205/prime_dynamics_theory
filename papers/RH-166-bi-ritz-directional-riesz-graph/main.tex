\begin{abstract}
The Schur packet theorem uses two directed couplings, but a physical audit
starts from a candidate frame rather than exact block matrices.  Let $V$ be
an orthonormal packet frame, $P=VV^*$, and $H=V^*AV$.  We prove the exact
bi-Ritz identities
\[
 \norm{AV-VH}=\norm{(I-P)AP},\qquad
 \norm{A^*V-VH^*}=\norm{PA(I-P)}.
\]
Under the feedback gate $\kappa=adbc<1$, contour integration then gives
separate graph estimates.  The primal Riesz range is controlled directly by
the right residual $c$, while the dual Riesz range is controlled by the left
residual $b$.  The common packet-diagonal margin depends only on the product.
This directional theorem can certify a well-conditioned primal cloud even
when the dual graph is poor.  A 512-matrix audit verifies the residual
identities.  The archived reset atlas does not yet provide such residuals in
the transfer space, so physical interface R remains open.
\end{abstract}

\section{Frame residuals are the directed couplings}
Let $V:\mathbb C^r\to\mathcal H$ be an isometry, $P=VV^*$, $Q=I-P$, and
define the Rayleigh compression $H=V^*AV$.  Put
\[
 R_+=AV-VH,\qquad R_-=A^*V-VH^*.
\]

\begin{theorem}[Bi-Ritz identities]\label{thm:ritz}
The residuals satisfy
\[
 R_+=QAV,\qquad R_-=QA^*V,
\]
and hence
\[
 \norm{R_+}=\norm{QAP},\qquad
 \norm{R_-}=\norm{PAQ}.
\]
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Since $VH=VV^*AV=PAV$, subtraction gives $R_+=QAV$.  The adjoint formula is
identical.  Right multiplication by the coisometry $V^*$ does not change the
norm of an operator whose domain is $\operatorname{ran}V$, and taking the
adjoint converts $QA^*P$ to $PAQ$.
\end{proof}

Write $c=\norm{R_+}$ and $b=\norm{R_-}$.  Both are required for a general
nonnormal feedback audit.  They coincide only under additional symmetry.

\section{Directional block resolvent bounds}
Let $\Gamma$ separate the compressed packet block $A_P=PAP|_{P\mathcal H}$
from $A_Q=QAQ|_{Q\mathcal H}$, and suppose
\[
 \sup_\Gamma\norm{(z-A_P)^{-1}}\le a,qquad
 \sup_\Gamma\norm{(z-A_Q)^{-1}}\le d.
\]
Assume $\kappa=adbc<1$ and let $L=|\Gamma|$.  Denote the Riesz projection of
$A$ inside $\Gamma$ by $\Pi$.

\begin{theorem}[Primal directional graph]\label{thm:primal}
Define
\[
 \delta_P=\frac{L}{2\pi}\frac{a\kappa}{1-\kappa},
 \qquad
 \gamma_+=\frac{L}{2\pi}\frac{adc}{1-\kappa}.
\]
If $\delta_P<1$, then $\operatorname{ran}\Pi$ is a graph over
$\operatorname{ran}P$ with slope at most
\[
 \norm X_+\le\frac{\gamma_+}{1-\delta_P}.
\]
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
The Schur inverse formula from RH-163 gives, pointwise on $\Gamma$,
\[
 \norm{R_{11}(z)-(z-A_P)^{-1}}\le\frac{a\kappa}{1-\kappa},
 \qquad
 \norm{R_{21}(z)}\le\frac{adc}{1-\kappa}.
\]
After contour integration,
$\norm{P\Pi P-P}\le\delta_P$ and
$\norm{Q\Pi P}\le\gamma_+$.  Thus $P\Pi P$ is invertible on
$P\mathcal H$.  Rank preservation gives
\[
 \operatorname{ran}\Pi
 =\{x+X_+x:x\in P\mathcal H\},\qquad
 X_+=Q\Pi P(P\Pi P)^{-1},
\]
which proves the bound \citep{Kato1995,WangRH163}.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Dual directional graph]\label{thm:dual}
Under the same diagonal margin, the left Riesz space
$\operatorname{ran}\Pi^*$ is a graph over $P\mathcal H$ with slope
\[
 \norm X_-\le
 \frac{1}{1-\delta_P}\frac{L}{2\pi}
 \frac{adb}{1-\kappa}.
\]
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Apply Theorem~\ref{thm:primal} to $A^*$ and the conjugate contour.  The block
resolvent norms are unchanged, while the right residual of $A^*$ is the left
residual of $A$.
\end{proof}

\section{Asymmetry is information, not a defect}
If $c=0$, the packet is invariant: $AV=VH$.  Then $\kappa=0$ and
$\gamma_+=0$, so the primal Riesz range is exactly $P\mathcal H$ even if
$b$ is arbitrarily large.  The dual Riesz projection can nevertheless be
highly oblique.  Interchanging $b$ and $c$ reverses the roles.

This distinction matters for determinant assembly.  The cloud factor needs
the invariant primal range; coefficient extraction and dual trace pairings
may additionally require left conditioning.  A global
$\norm{\Pi-P}$ bound merges these two questions and can reject a usable
one-way bridge.

\section{Residuals from an approximate compression}
The exact Rayleigh matrix need not be formed before obtaining a safe residual
bound.  Let $\widehat H$ be any proposed $r\times r$ compression and put
\[
 \widehat R_+=AV-V\widehat H.
\]

\begin{proposition}[Candidate-compression residual transfer]
For $H=V^*AV$, one has
\[
 H-\widehat H=V^*\widehat R_+,
 \qquad R_+=Q\widehat R_+.
\]
Consequently
\[
 \norm{H-\widehat H}\le\norm{\widehat R_+},
 \qquad \norm{R_+}\le\norm{\widehat R_+}.
\]
The analogous statement holds for a candidate left compression.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Left multiplication by $V^*$ gives the first identity, since $V^*V=I$.
Left multiplication by $Q$ gives the second, since $QV=0$.  Orthogonal
projections are contractions.
\end{proof}

Thus an outward ball for the assembled action $AV$ and any candidate
compression already gives a safe directed coupling upper.  Replacing it by
the smaller floating residual after an unvalidated Rayleigh recomputation is
optional, not logically necessary.

\section{A quantitative asymmetry witness}
Take $a=d=1$, $L=2\pi$, $b=100$, and $c=10^{-4}$.  Then
$\kappa=10^{-2}$ and
\[
 \delta_P=\frac{10^{-2}}{0.99}\approx1.0101\times10^{-2}.
\]
The primal and dual cross bounds are respectively
\[
 \gamma_+=\frac{10^{-4}}{0.99}\approx1.0101\times10^{-4},
 \qquad
 \gamma_-=\frac{100}{0.99}\approx101.01.
\]
Thus the same rank certificate gives a primal graph slope below
$1.03\times10^{-4}$ while the displayed dual slope bound is above $102$.
Replacing $(b,c)$ by $(c,b)$ reverses the outcome without changing the
feedback product or the enclosed rank.

This family is an omission witness for any audit that stores only $bc$: the
product decides the rank homotopy, but it cannot recover which of the primal
or dual Riesz ranges is well conditioned.  Both directed residuals must
remain separately archived even after their product is formed.

\section{Audit and physical data contract}
The audit draws 512 random matrices and orthonormal packet frames.  It
compares the two residual norms with the corresponding block couplings; the
largest discrepancy is archived and no identity failure occurs.  Four
imbalanced examples record the separation between primal and dual graph
bounds.

For the prime-dynamics family, a physical R audit now needs an ambient frame
$V_j$, the compressed packet and complement resolvents, and both transfer-
space residuals.  The RH-160 memory-Gram residuals are not automatically
these quantities.  RH-166 proves the finite implication but no ambient map,
continuous contour bound, uniform scale law, Gate A, or downstream spectral
claim.
