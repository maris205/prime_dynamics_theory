# Roadmap after RH-MVP1

## Series handoff

RH-MVP1 is Volume I of the four-volume synthesis and covers exactly
RH-1--RH-160. Volume II starts with RH-161's typed packet-to-Riesz
relative-determinant assembly and continues through the RH-241 trace-envelope
frontier. Volumes III and IV cover RH-242--RH-281 and RH-282--RH-361,
respectively. This editorial partition does not close a gate, change a theorem
status, or make a later result a premise of Volume I.

problem, even though the analytic closure theorem after `E` is short.

## Immediate program: close or kill A

The first missing gate is `A`. Its next careful papers should separate four
jobs that RH-MVP1 intentionally bundles at macro scale:

1. **Eventual reset law.** Prove or refute one common all-level O/E/S path;
   add bounded-lag L only when the determinant assembly needs directional
   cross rank.
2. **Typed assembly.** State the exact input/output spaces by which a native
   or lagged seed controls the moving resonance cloud and complement.
3. **Moving-cloud quotient and pole ledger.** Divide by the actual finite
   reducing cloud factor, fill the removable cloud zeros, identify the
   deterministic pole divisor, and prove a normal residual family. RH-80
   rules out fixed scalar pole cancellation on disks crossing the pole circle.
4. **Canonicity.** Prove schedule, mesh, packet-gauge, reset, and cutoff
   independence, with a target-independent normalization.

Success requires a nontrivial meromorphic limit preserving the directed trace
data. Failure along an infinite scale sequence or inequivalent admissible
limits kills the present `A` route without erasing the fixed-noise theory.

## Parallel falsification track for B and C

Finite prototypes may test downstream viability before every constant in `A`
is optimized:

1. Construct candidate inner/scattering completions and test uniqueness under
   changes of dilation and auxiliary channels.
2. Check whether directed three/six-step information survives the completion;
   an orientation-blind completion fails `B`.
3. For any canonical candidate, derive its phase/counting law without fitting
   zeta ordinates. Bounded phase, logarithmic rank, power-law Weyl growth, or
   uncontrolled multiplicity fails `C`.

These prototypes are falsification experiments. They do not prove `B` or `C`.

## Arithmetic track after a viable spectral object

Gate `D` must derive, rather than insert, prime powers and von Mangoldt
weights. A TPC input is admissible only through an exact crosswalk matching
variables, test classes, weights, and counterterms. Gate `E` then accounts for
every spectral sector, gamma/pole term, zero-free factor, and multiplicity.
No zero ordinate may be an input to `A`--`D`.
