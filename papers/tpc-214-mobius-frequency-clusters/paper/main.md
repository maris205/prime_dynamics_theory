## Abstract

TPC-213 showed that the literal transition residual is generated by one common physical source and that its divisor cross terms are indexed by shared rational frequencies. This paper adds the actual Möbius-log coefficient to that frequency picture. For a common reciprocal prime set, common height, and smooth emitter, we prove an exact dilation covariance: if $h\mid d$ then the row at denominator $d$ restricted to frequencies with reduced denominator $h$ is exactly the row at $h$. Consequently the complete-period physical Gram factors through reduced-denominator clusters, with cluster coefficient $C_h=\sum_{h\mid d}\mu(d)\log(d)/d$. The zero axis vanishes when the prime shell lies below the Fourier height, and the same reduction commutes with the four-packet polarization used in the Gate-B route. Exact rational row data, followed by numerical logarithm evaluation, gives a weighted physical/direct-sum ratio $0.5963435557$ for $\{5,7,35\}$ and $1.2119952513$ for $\{3,5,7,105\}$. Thus the cluster reduction is a genuine analytic structure, but it has no universal favorable sign. No asymptotic saving, prime-shell reassembly, arithmetic $L^2$ credit, or twin-prime conclusion is claimed.

# Introduction

The transition-native twin-prime route contains one reciprocal emitter row for each squarefree divisor. In the V46 normalization the row has the schematic form $$A_d(r)=\frac{\mu(d)\log d}{d}
 \sum_{q\in\mathcal Q}\sum_{0<|m|\le dq/H}
 \psi\!\left(\frac{Hm}{dq}\right)
 \mathbf 1_{r\equiv m\overline q\pmod d}.
 \label{eq:v46-row}$$ This is the transition-native object frozen in the V46 research artifact `\cite{WangV46}`. TPC-212 isolated its truncated emitter boundary `\cite{WangTPC212}`, and TPC-213 supplied the common-source pullback and the pairwise frequency-intersection Gram `\cite{WangTPC213}`. The common-source pullback from TPC-213 showed that the corresponding physical kernel is not an orthogonal direct sum. On a complete period, only equal rational frequencies interact. The remaining question is whether the Möbius-log coefficients make those interactions cancel in a systematic way.

The answer has two parts. First, there is an exact algebraic collapse. Strip the coefficient from [\[eq:v46-row\]](main.tex#L77){reference-type="eqref" reference="eq:v46-row"} and call the resulting row $B_d$. Whenever $h\mid d$, the frequency $r=(d/h)a$ has the same amplitude as the reduced row $B_h(a)$. All divisors that are multiples of $h$ therefore share one row, and their coefficients add before squaring. This is stronger than a list of pairwise Gram entries: it identifies the correct reduced object.

Second, the coefficient tail is not sign-definite. The nested family $\{5,7,35\}$ produces a substantial finite reduction, while the family $\{3,5,7,105\}$ produces a finite enhancement. The examples do not model the full asymptotic problem, but they rule out a universal theorem whose only input is the existence of shared-frequency coupling.

The contributions are therefore:

1.  an exact dilation covariance for the literal reciprocal cutoff;

2.  an exact reduced-denominator factorization of the common-source Gram;

3.  an exact zero-axis and four-packet compatibility statement;

4.  two independently checked finite coefficient ledgers with opposite qualitative behavior.

# The literal emitter and its reduced frequencies

Let $\mathcal D$ be a finite family of squarefree positive integers. Let $\mathcal Q$ be a finite set of integers coprime to every member of $\mathcal D$, let $H\ge 1$, and let $\psi$ be any function on the finite set of arguments that occur below. Define the coefficient-free reciprocal emitter $$B_d(r)=\sum_{q\in\mathcal Q}\sum_{0<|m|\le dq/H}
 \psi\!\left(\frac{Hm}{dq}\right)
 \mathbf 1_{m\overline q\equiv r\pmod d},
 \qquad r\in\mathbb Z/d\mathbb Z.
 \label{eq:emitter}$$ The V46 coefficient is kept separately as $$c_d=\frac{\mu(d)\log d}{d}.
 \label{eq:coefficient}$$ For a common physical variable $u$, put $$K_d(u)=c_d\sum_{r\bmod d}B_d(r)\mathrm e\!\left(\frac{ru}{d}\right),
 \qquad K(u)=\sum_{d\in\mathcal D}K_d(u).
 \label{eq:kernel}$$

The cutoff is interpreted in the integer sense, so that $|m|\le\lfloor dq/H\rfloor$. This convention is important when the dilation argument changes variables from $m$ to $kn$.

> **Lemma: dilation covariance** <span id="lem:dilation" label="lem:dilation">\[lem:dilation\]</span> Suppose $h\mid d$ and write $d=kh$. For every $a\in\mathbb Z/h\mathbb Z$, $$B_d(ka)=B_h(a).
>  \label{eq:dilation}$$

> **Proof** In the $d$-row, the congruence $m\overline q\equiv ka\pmod{kh}$ is equivalent to $m=kn$ and $n\overline q\equiv a\pmod h$. Indeed, $q$ is a unit modulo $kh$, so the first congruence forces $k\mid m$. The range becomes $|n|\le hq/H$, and the smooth argument is unchanged: $$\frac{H(kn)}{(kh)q}=\frac{Hn}{hq}.$$ Term-by-term comparison with [\[eq:emitter\]](main.tex#L121){reference-type="eqref" reference="eq:emitter"} proves the identity.

> **Corollary: zero axis** <span id="cor:zero" label="cor:zero">\[cor:zero\]</span> If every $q\in\mathcal Q$ satisfies $q<H$, then $B_d(0)=0$ for every $d$. Hence the reduced denominator $h=1$ contributes no physical Gram energy.

> **Proof** The congruence for $r=0$ requires $d\mid m$. But every nonzero admissible $m$ satisfies $|m|\le dq/H<d$, which is impossible.

# The cluster factorization theorem

Let $$L=\operatorname{lcm}\{d:d\in\mathcal D\},
 \qquad
 \mathcal H_* =\{h\ge 2:h\mid d\text{ for some }d\in\mathcal D\}.
 \label{eq:period}$$ For $h\in\mathcal H_*$ define the Möbius-log tail $$C_h=\sum_{\substack{d\in\mathcal D\\h\mid d}}c_d.
 \label{eq:tail}$$

> **Theorem: reduced-denominator cluster factorization** <span id="thm:cluster" label="thm:cluster">\[thm:cluster\]</span> Assume the hypotheses of Lemma [\[lem:dilation\]](main.tex#L140){reference-type="ref" reference="lem:dilation"}. On one complete period, $$\sum_{u=0}^{L-1}|K(u)|^2
>  =L\sum_{h\in\mathcal H_*}|C_h|^2
>    \sum_{\substack{a\bmod h\\(a,h)=1}}|B_h(a)|^2.
>  \label{eq:cluster}$$ The formula remains valid for complex-valued $\psi$ after replacing the inner square by the Hermitian norm of the row.

> **Proof** Every nonzero rational frequency in the $d$-row has a unique reduced form $a/h$ with $h\mid d$ and $(a,h)=1$. Its representative in $\mathbb Z/d\mathbb Z$ is $r=(d/h)a$. Lemma [\[lem:dilation\]](main.tex#L140){reference-type="ref" reference="lem:dilation"} gives $$c_dB_d((d/h)a)=c_dB_h(a).$$ Collecting all divisors that contain the same reduced frequency therefore gives $$K(u)=\sum_{h\in\mathcal H_*}C_h
>  \sum_{\substack{a\bmod h\\(a,h)=1}}
>  B_h(a)\mathrm e(au/h),$$ where the zero-frequency term is absent by Corollary [\[cor:zero\]](main.tex#L161){reference-type="ref" reference="cor:zero"}. Distinct reduced fractions have distinct frequencies modulo one. Summing their products over $u=0,\ldots,L-1$ gives zero unless the fractions agree, in which case it gives $L$. The surviving terms are exactly [\[eq:cluster\]](main.tex#L193){reference-type="eqref" reference="eq:cluster"}.

> **Corollary: relation with the pairwise Gram** <span id="cor:pairwise" label="cor:pairwise">\[cor:pairwise\]</span> The pairwise cross-divisor Gram is not the fundamental object once the coefficient is restored. It is the expansion of the cluster sum: $$L\sum_{d,e\in\mathcal D}c_dc_e
>  \sum_{\substack{r\bmod d,\ s\bmod e\\r/d\equiv s/e\pmod 1}}
>  B_d(r)B_e(s)
>  =\text{right-hand side of \eqref{eq:cluster}}.
>  \label{eq:pairwise-expansion}$$ In particular, the physical period $L$ must be used consistently for every pair; replacing it by $\operatorname{lcm}(d,e)$ inside a family sum loses the number of repeated periods.

> **Proof** Expand the square in [\[eq:cluster\]](main.tex#L193){reference-type="eqref" reference="eq:cluster"}, use Lemma [\[lem:dilation\]](main.tex#L140){reference-type="ref" reference="lem:dilation"}, and apply complete-period orthogonality. The final sentence is the same identity with the common period written as a multiple of each pair period.

# Four-packet compatibility

The Gate-B route uses the complex polarization $a^{(j)}=\beta+i^jw$. The cluster theorem is linear in the row before taking the Hermitian square, so the polarization can be applied after the frequency reduction.

> **Proposition: polarization commutes with clustering** <span id="prop:polarization" label="prop:polarization">\[prop:polarization\]</span> For any $\beta,w\in\mathbb C$, $$\frac14\sum_{j=0}^3 i^j|\beta+i^jw|^2=\beta\overline w.
>  \label{eq:polarization}$$ If four physical kernels are formed from the rows $\beta+i^jw$, then each reduced-frequency cluster may be formed first and [\[eq:polarization\]](main.tex#L251){reference-type="eqref" reference="eq:polarization"} applied afterwards. The resulting polarized scalar is not positive in general.

> **Proof** Expand the square. The sums of $i^j$ and $i^{2j}$ vanish, while $i^j\overline{i^j}=1$. The remaining term is $\beta\overline w$. All maps used to form a cluster are linear, so the same calculation applies cluster by cluster.

> **Remark: scope of the zero axis** The zero-axis statement concerns the additive frequency $r=0$ in the reciprocal emitter. It does not delete the multiplicative principal character, a local Euler exception, or any other mode in a later character decomposition.

# Conclusion
