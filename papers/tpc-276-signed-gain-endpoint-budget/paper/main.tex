\begin{abstract}
The preceding TPC-275 audit retained the signs of four actual source-block
packets and found a finite diagonal-to-signed energy gain
\(r=D/G>1\).  This paper proves the exact margin identity
\(m^2=r m_D^2\), where \(m_D\) is the margin computed with the packet
diagonal and \(m\) is the margin computed with the signed output.  A conditional
endpoint compiler then shows that a uniform source-level gain
\(D/G\geq b x^\gamma\) contributes \(\gamma/2\) to the margin budget.  The
strict target condition is
\(\sigma-\max(0,\eta_D-\gamma/2)>1/400\).  Exact rational transfer of all
12 TPC-275 rows gives \(r>1\) everywhere, three signed rows above the quarter
margin threshold, and five above the eighth threshold.  The finite table is not
an asymptotic gain theorem and supplies zero fixed-power credit.
\end{abstract}

\section{Question and claim ceiling}

TPC-275 decomposed the projected literal V59 output into four actual
source-block packets \(V_j\), with packet diagonal energy
\[
 \Dpack=\sum_{j=0}^3\|V_j\|_2^2,
 \qquad \Gpack=\left\|\sum_{j=0}^3V_j\right\|_2^2.
\]
Its exact signed Gram audit found \(\Gpack<\Dpack\) on every registered row.
The present question is narrower and useful for the endpoint ledger: how does
the ratio \(r=\Dpack/\Gpack\) change the correlation margin, and what would a
growing lower bound for \(r\) actually buy?

The claim ceiling is
\[
 \texttt{PROVED\_CONDITIONAL}\;+
 \texttt{PROVED\_EXACT\_FINITE}\;+
 \texttt{NUMERICALLY\_CERTIFIED\_FINITE}.
\]
No finite ratio is promoted to a power saving, and no arithmetic \(L^2\) or
twin-prime conclusion is asserted.

\section{Exact margin recovery}

Let \(\Cperp\) be the signed residual scalar and \(\Wperp>0\) its source
norm lane.  Define
\[
 \md^2=\frac{|\Cperp|^2}{\Wperp\Dpack},
 \qquad
 m^2=\frac{|\Cperp|^2}{\Wperp\Gpack},
 \qquad r=\frac{\Dpack}{\Gpack}.
\]

\begin{theorem}[signed-gain margin identity]
Whenever \(\Dpack,\Gpack,\Wperp>0\),
\[
 m^2=r\,\md^2.
\]
In particular, \(r>1\) makes the diagonal margin proxy strictly
conservative.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Since \(\Gpack=\Dpack/r\), direct substitution gives
\[
 \frac{|\Cperp|^2}{\Wperp\Gpack}
 =\frac{|\Cperp|^2}{\Wperp(\Dpack/r)}
 =r\frac{|\Cperp|^2}{\Wperp\Dpack}.
\]
All denominators are positive, so this is an exact identity rather than an
interval or square-root approximation.
\end{proof}

\section{Conditional strict endpoint compiler}

Write the baseline and target exponents as
\[
 E_0=\frac53,\qquad E_*=\frac{1997}{1200},\qquad
 E_0-E_* = \frac1{400}.
\]

\begin{theorem}[signed-gain budget]
Suppose that for sufficiently large \(x\), with constants \(A,b,c>0\),
\(\gamma\geq0\), and arbitrarily small \(\epsilon>0\),
\[
 |\Cperp(x)|\leq A x^{E_0-\sigma+\epsilon},\qquad
 m_D(x)\geq c x^{-\eta_D-\epsilon},\qquad
 \frac{\Dpack(x)}{\Gpack(x)}\geq b x^\gamma.
\]
Set
\[
 \meff=\max\left(0,\eta_D-\frac\gamma2\right).
\]
Then, for sufficiently large \(x\),
\[
 |\Cperp(x)|+R(x)
 \leq A\left(1+\frac1{c\sqrt b}\right)
 x^{E_0-\sigma+\meff+2\epsilon},
 \qquad R=\frac{|\Cperp|}{m}.
\]
Consequently the endpoint is strictly beaten if
\[
 \sigma-\meff>\frac1{400}.
\]
In the non-overcompensated regime \(\gamma/2\leq\eta_D\), this is the
equivalent condition \(\sigma-\eta_D+\gamma/2>1/400\).
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
The identity in Theorem 1 and the two lower bounds imply
\[
 m=m_D\sqrt{\Dpack/\Gpack}
 \geq c\sqrt b\,x^{-\eta_D-\epsilon+\gamma/2}
 \geq c\sqrt b\,x^{-\meff-\epsilon}.
\]
Therefore
\[
 R\leq \frac{A}{c\sqrt b}
 x^{E_0-\sigma+\meff+2\epsilon}.
\]
Because \(\meff\geq0\), the assumed scalar bound is no larger than the same
power after increasing the constant.  Adding the two bounds proves the first
claim.  If \(\sigma-\meff>1/400\), choose \(\epsilon\) so small that
\(\sigma-\meff-2\epsilon>1/400\); the resulting exponent is strictly below
\(E_*\).  The gain enters with coefficient one half because it is a squared
margin identity.\end{proof}

\begin{proposition}[registered finite counts]
All 12 rows have \(r>1\), while the diagonal proxy is below \(1/16\) on all
12 rows.  Signed recovery places 3 rows above \(1/16\) and 5 rows above
\(1/64\), with no interval crossing either threshold.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
The producer and independent checker parse the parent rational intervals,
multiply by the exact positive ratio, and compare the resulting endpoints with
\(1/16\) and \(1/64\).  The stored theorem counts are respectively
\(12,12,3,5,0\), and the hostile mutation audit rejects changes to each of
these fields.\end{proof}

\section{Why the finite gain is not power credit}

The conditional theorem requires a quantified lower bound
\(\Dpack/\Gpack\geq b x^\gamma\) for all sufficiently large \(x\).  A finite
list of values at \(N=64,96,128,192,256,384\) has no such quantifier: outside
those points the gain can be extended by an arbitrary positive function.  Thus
the finite table proves a useful threshold recovery but assigns
\[
 \texttt{TPC276\_FIXED\_POWER\_CREDIT}=0.
\]
This is a scoped finite-to-asymptotic obstruction, not a negative theorem about
the literal growing source.  The missing result is a uniform source-level
signed-gain lower bound coupled to the margin lane.

\section{Conclusion and route evaluation}

TPC-276 adds one precise edge to the route map.  Signed packet cancellation is
not merely an output-energy curiosity: exactly, it multiplies the squared
margin by \(D/G\), and a genuine polynomial gain would enter the strict budget
with half its exponent.  The finite literal audit recovers the quarter margin
on three rows and the eighth margin on five rows, but it does not establish a
growing lower bound.  Therefore the source-level signed gain, arithmetic
\(L^2\), full Gate B, and the twin-prime conclusion remain open.
