\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:introduction}

The determinant-flatness route separates one signed coefficient into
three literal statistics:
\[
 \text{zero mode }Z,\qquad
 \text{energy }D,\qquad
 \text{actual support size }S.
\]
TPC-88 gives the exact mass--cancellation factorization, while
TPC-89 identifies
\[
 \rho=\frac{|Z|}{\sqrt{SD}}
\]
as the projective angle to the constant line
\citep{WangTPC88,WangTPC89}.  For a general same-frame surrogate
\(B\), TPC-89 proves a sharp worst-case fact: transferring the
critical target \(\rho\ll J^{-1}\) from only a relative
\(\ell^2\) approximation requires
\[
 \frac{\|A-B\|_2}{\|B\|_2}=O(J^{-1}).
\]
An \(o(1)\) approximation need not suffice.

That theorem treats the error as an arbitrary direction.  The
present paper asks whether a structured surrogate can avoid this
worst case.  The answer is exact.

Suppose \(B=PA\), where \(P\) is an orthogonal projection on the
\emph{same prescribed physical frame}.  If the constant vector lies
in \(\Ran P\), then the residual is automatically orthogonal to the
constant vector.  Hence
\[
 Z(PA)=Z(A)
\]
with no approximation, while Pythagoras gives
\[
 D(PA)\le D(A).
\]
The projected flatness is therefore at least the physical flatness.
This reverses the usual concern about surrogate optimism:
constant-preserving orthogonal coarsening is pessimistic in exactly
the direction needed for a one-sided upper certificate.

The observation is elementary, but three distinctions make its
literal use nontrivial.

\begin{enumerate}[label=(\roman*),leftmargin=2.2em]
\item The projection must act on the actual post-bin frame.  A
  compressed block vector must retain block multiplicities.
\item Preserving constants as an output, \(P\one=\one\), is enough
  here only because \(P=P^\ast\).  For a general linear surrogate
  the zero-mode condition is \(T^\ast\one=\one\).
\item A flat projected coefficient is a valid certificate only after
  its own arithmetic bound has been proved.  Projection monotonicity
  does not estimate that coefficient.
\end{enumerate}

If the projection misses the constant vector, the exact obstruction
is not the entire residual but one scalar pairing:
\[
 Z(PA)-Z(A)
 =
 \langle A,(P-I)\one\rangle.
\]
Writing \(q=(I-P)\one\), orthogonality sharpens this to
\[
 Z(PA)-Z(A)=-\langle (I-P)A,q\rangle.
\]
The resulting bound is governed by the product of two dimensionless
quantities:
\[
 \kappa(P)
 =\frac{\|q\|_2}{\sqrt S},
\qquad
 r(A;P)
 =\frac{\|(I-P)A\|_2}{\|PA\|_2}.
\]
The critical interface becomes
\[
 \kappa(P)r(A;P)\ll J^{-1}.
\]
This interpolates between the exact safe case \(\kappa=0\) and the
general TPC-89 barrier when \(\kappa\asymp1\).

\subsection{Main contributions}

The paper proves the following finite-dimensional statements.

\begin{enumerate}[label=(\arabic*),leftmargin=2.2em]
\item An exact zero-mode/energy ledger for every orthogonal
  projection, including the defect identity and its sharp equality
  cases.
\item Exact flatness monotonicity for every constant-preserving
  projection, with a multiplicative formula and equality
  classification.
\item A monotone hierarchy for nested coarse-grainings and a
  within-block variance formula for partition averages.
\item A weighted compression theorem and an explicit counterexample
  to unweighted block compression.
\item Sharp constant-defect bounds and the product criterion
  \(\kappa r=O(J^{-1})\).
\item The minimal rank-one constant completion of an unsafe
  projection.
\item An exact \(\Lone\) crosswalk to the literal fixed-\(h_0\)
  determinant coefficient and the TPC-92/TPC-93 continuation
  program \citep{WangTPC92,WangTPC93}.
\end{enumerate}

\subsection{Logical level}

The projection identities, sharpness constructions, partition laws,
and rank-one completion are unconditional \(\Lzero\) results.
Applying them to a fully specified operator on the literal
TPC determinant bins is an \(\Lone\) certificate interface.  No
operator is shown here to have a small projected zero-mode ratio for
the growing fixed-\(h_0\) arithmetic family.  Consequently there is
no new \(\Ltwo\) arithmetic estimate, no parity breakthrough, and no
prime-pair conclusion.
