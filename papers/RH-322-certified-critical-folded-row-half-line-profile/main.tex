\begin{abstract}
At the first-alias clock, a distinguished deterministic row can have endpoint
clearance of the same order as the Gaussian width.  We compute the exact
boundary rescaling of one physical folded row and prove that it converges in
total variation to a clearance-parameterized half-line Gaussian.  Both the
direct lobe and the complete folded row have the same exact error
\[
 2\,\overline\Phi(\sigma^{-1}-a)/\Phi(a)
\]
in $L^1$, while changing the clearance ratio from $a$ to $d$ costs at most
$|a-d|$.  We also give the limiting moment recurrence and prove that distinct
clearance phases produce distinct profiles.  This is a one-row probability
kernel theorem.  It does not combine the parity layer, neighboring shell, or
moving-order trace remainder, and is not a joint first-alias trace law.
\end{abstract}

\section{The exact physical row}

Let
\[
 \phi(t)=\frac{e^{-t^2/2}}{\sqrt{2\pi}},\qquad
 \Phi(t)=\int_{-\infty}^t\phi(s)\,ds,\qquad
 \overline\Phi=1-\Phi.
\]
Write $L=\sigma^{-1}$ and suppose that the Gaussian mean is
\[
 p_{\sigma,a}=1-\sigma a,\qquad 0\le a\le L.
\]
Thus $a$ is the clearance-to-noise ratio.  The physical folded transition
row on $[0,1]$ is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:physical-row}
 P_{\sigma,a}(y)=
 \frac{\phi_\sigma(y-p_{\sigma,a})+\phi_\sigma(y+p_{\sigma,a})}
 {Z_{\sigma,a}}\mathbf1_{[0,1]}(y),
 \qquad
 \phi_\sigma(t)=\sigma^{-1}\phi(t/\sigma).
\end{equation}
Direct integration gives
\begin{equation}\label{eq:folded-normalizer}
 Z_{\sigma,a}=\Phi(a)-\overline\Phi(2L-a).
\end{equation}
After the boundary change of variables $v=(1-y)/\sigma$, the pushed-forward
density is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:folded-density}
 \widetilde h_{\sigma,a}(v)=
 \frac{\phi(v-a)+\phi(2L-v-a)}
 {\Phi(a)-\overline\Phi(2L-a)}\mathbf1_{[0,L]}(v).
\end{equation}
The second term is the reflected lobe required by the exact folded kernel.
For comparison, retaining only the direct lobe gives
\begin{equation}\label{eq:direct-density}
 h^+_{\sigma,a}(v)=
 \frac{\phi(v-a)}{\Phi(a)-\overline\Phi(L-a)}
 \mathbf1_{[0,L]}(v).
\end{equation}
The half-line profile at fixed clearance ratio is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:limit-density}
 g_a(v)=\frac{\phi(v-a)}{\Phi(a)}\mathbf1_{[0,\infty)}(v).
\end{equation}
This is the upper-conditioned Gaussian row already implicit in the endpoint
geometry of RH-16.  The new point here is the exact finite-interval and full
folding transfer at critical clearance.

\section{Exact total variation and parameter stability}

We use $d_{\TV}(f,g)=\tfrac12\|f-g\|_{L^1}$.

\begin{theorem}[Critical folded-row half-line theorem]\label{thm:main}
For every $\sigma>0$ and $0\le a\le\sigma^{-1}$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:exact-tail}
 \|h^+_{\sigma,a}-g_a\|_1
 =\|\widetilde h_{\sigma,a}-g_a\|_1
 =\frac{2\overline\Phi(L-a)}{\Phi(a)}.
\end{equation}
For every $a,d\ge0$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:parameter-lipschitz}
 \|g_a-g_d\|_1\le |a-d|.
\end{equation}
Consequently
\begin{equation}\label{eq:combined-bound}
 \boxed{
 \|\widetilde h_{\sigma,a}-g_d\|_1
 \le |a-d|+
 \frac{2\overline\Phi(\sigma^{-1}-a)}{\Phi(a)}.}
\end{equation}
The same bound holds with $h^+_{\sigma,a}$ in place of the folded row.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
The direct row is $g_a$ conditioned on $v\le L$.  Its density exceeds $g_a$
on $[0,L]$ and vanishes beyond $L$, so its positive and negative $L^1$
parts both equal the discarded mass
\[
 \int_L^\infty g_a(v)\,dv
 =\frac{\overline\Phi(L-a)}{\Phi(a)}.
\]
For the folded row, the denominator in \eqref{eq:folded-density} is smaller
than $\Phi(a)$ and the numerator contains the direct nonnegative term.
Hence $\widetilde h_{\sigma,a}\ge g_a$ throughout $[0,L]$, while the folded
row again vanishes beyond $L$.  The same mass argument proves the second
identity in \eqref{eq:exact-tail}.

For parameter stability put $r(a)=\phi(a)/\Phi(a)$.  Differentiating
\eqref{eq:limit-density} gives
\[
 \partial_a g_a(v)=g_a(v)\{v-a-r(a)\}.
\]
Under $g_a$, the random variable $V-a$ has mean $r(a)$ and variance
\[
 1-a r(a)-r(a)^2\le1.
\]
Cauchy--Schwarz therefore gives
$\|\partial_a g_a\|_1\le1$.  Integrating along the segment from $d$ to $a$
proves \eqref{eq:parameter-lipschitz}; the triangle inequality proves
\eqref{eq:combined-bound}.
\end{proof}

The exact tail also records its true exponential scale.  If
$a,d\in K=[m,A]\subset[0,\infty)$ and $L>A$, Mills' inequality gives
\begin{equation}\label{eq:compact-mills}
 \|\widetilde h_{\sigma,a}-g_d\|_1
 \le |a-d|+
 \frac{2\phi(L-A)}{\Phi(m)(L-A)}.
\end{equation}
Uniformly for $a$ in a fixed compact set,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:tail-asymptotic}
 \frac{2\overline\Phi(L-a)}{\Phi(a)}
 =\frac{2\phi(L-a)}{\Phi(a)(L-a)}
 \bigl(1+O(\sigma^2)\bigr).
\end{equation}
Thus a bound $C_K e^{-c/\sigma^2}$ is valid after choosing any sufficiently
small fixed $c>0$.  One must not replace the exact exponent
$-(\sigma^{-1}-a)^2/2$ by $-1/(2\sigma^2)$ with a uniform fixed prefactor.

\begin{corollary}[Observable convergence]\label{cor:observables}
If $a_\sigma\to d\ge0$, then both finite rows converge to $g_d$ in total
variation.  Every bounded measurable observable $F$ satisfies
\[
 \left|\int F\widetilde h_{\sigma,a_\sigma}
       -\int Fg_d\right|
 \le \|F\|_\infty
 \left(|a_\sigma-d|+
 \frac{2\overline\Phi(\sigma^{-1}-a_\sigma)}{\Phi(a_\sigma)}\right).
\]
The direct row also converges in Wasserstein distance.  More precisely,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:wasserstein}
 W_1(h^+_{\sigma,a},g_a)=
 \frac{\phi(L-a)-r(a)\overline\Phi(L-a)}
 {\Phi(a)-\overline\Phi(L-a)},
 \qquad
 W_1(g_a,g_d)\le |a-d|.
\end{equation}
In particular,
\[
 W_1(h^+_{\sigma,a},g_d)\le |a-d|+
 \frac{\phi(L-a)-r(a)\overline\Phi(L-a)}
 {\Phi(a)-\overline\Phi(L-a)}.
\]
Hence all Lipschitz observables, including unbounded ones of at most linear
growth, obey the same quantitative convergence law.
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
The total-variation statement is \cref{thm:main}.  The direct finite row is
stochastically smaller than $g_a$, so its $W_1$ distance equals the difference
of their means; direct integration gives the first formula in
\eqref{eq:wasserstein}.  The family $g_a$ has monotone likelihood ratio in
$a$, hence is stochastically increasing.  Its mean is $a+r(a)$ and its
derivative is $1-a r(a)-r(a)^2\le1$, proving the second formula.
\end{proof}

\section{Moments and the clearance-phase obstruction}

Let $M_j(d)=\int_0^\infty v^j g_d(v)\,dv$.  Integration by parts gives
\begin{equation}\label{eq:moment-recurrence}
 M_0(d)=1,\qquad M_1(d)=d+r(d),\qquad
 M_{j+1}(d)=dM_j(d)+jM_{j-1}(d)\quad(j\ge1).
\end{equation}
In particular,
\begin{align}
 \mathbb E_d V&=d+r(d),\label{eq:mean}\\
 \mathbb E_d V^2&=d^2+1+d r(d),\label{eq:second}\\
 \operatorname{Var}_d(V)&=1-d r(d)-r(d)^2.\label{eq:variance}
\end{align}
The explicit Gaussian tails in \eqref{eq:folded-density} imply convergence of
every polynomial moment.  More quantitatively, for each $j$ and compact
$K\subset[0,\infty)$ there are $C_{j,K},c_K>0$ such that, whenever
$a,d\in K$ and $\sigma$ is sufficiently small with $\sigma^{-1}>\sup K$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:moment-bound}
 \left|\int v^j\widetilde h_{\sigma,a}(v)\,dv-M_j(d)\right|
 \le C_{j,K}|a-d|+C_{j,K}e^{-c_K/\sigma^2}.
\end{equation}

The parameter $d$ cannot be suppressed.  Differentiating the limiting mean
in \eqref{eq:mean} gives
\begin{equation}\label{eq:mean-derivative}
 \frac{d}{dd}\{d+r(d)\}
 =1-d r(d)-r(d)^2
 =\operatorname{Var}_d(V)>0.
\end{equation}
Thus $d\mapsto g_d$ is injective.  If $a_\sigma$ has two subsequential limits
$d_1\ne d_2$, the corresponding folded rows have two distinct total-variation
limits.  There is no phase-independent universal row profile without control
of the clearance ratio.

For the boundary cycles archived in RH-17 and RH-19, with
$\lambda=1.678573510428322\ldots$,
\[
 \delta_k=C_{\mathrm b}\lambda^{-2k}(1+o(1)).
\]
At the natural first-alias clock
$k_\sigma=\log(1/\sigma)/(2\log\lambda)+O(1)$, the ratio
$a_\sigma=\delta_{k_\sigma}/\sigma$ remains of order one, but the bounded
integer phase need not converge.  The theorem therefore supplies a complete
subsequential one-row dictionary, not a unique phase-free replacement.

\section{Protocol, scope, and next interface}

The reproduction code evaluates only the exact normalizers, Gaussian tails,
Wasserstein correction, moments, and pairwise profile separations.  No fitted
exponent or transfer-matrix diagonalization enters the proof.  The physical
folded formula is checked independently against the sum of its direct and
reflected masses.

At a critical source $x=\sqrt\sigma q$, RH-14 has
$u_c=1.543689012692076\ldots$, $a=u_cq^2$, and row normalizer
$\Phi(u_cq^2)+O(e^{-c/\sigma^2})$.
Equation \eqref{eq:folded-normalizer} recovers that local factor exactly and
shows why it cannot be replaced by one.  It does not replace the RH-14 signed
endpoint density, which integrates over all $q$, nor does it couple the
endpoint row to the repelling-boundary profile.

\begin{remark}[Route firewall]
RH-17 shows that the endpoint and first internal contact are simultaneously
critical, and RH-19 shows that the neighboring critical sibling has order-one
relative mass.  Neither contribution appears in a one-row theorem.  No
moving-order Duhamel remainder, parity cancellation, neighboring-shell
matching, full-trace replacement, or joint error $o(kR^{-2k})$ with
$R=1.4$ is proved
here.  Gates A--E remain false/open; the paper constructs no Hilbert--Polya
operator, identifies no Riemann zero, proves no von Mangoldt trace formula or
zeta-divisor equality, and does not imply RH.
\end{remark}

The next interface is a paired affine Gaussian chain retaining both critical
contacts with their common clearance phase.  Only after that pairing is
combined with curvature, normalization, parity, shell coupling, and a
moving-order remainder can it enter the first-alias trace equation.
