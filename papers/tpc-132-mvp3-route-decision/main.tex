\begin{abstract}
TPC-MVP2 separated exact interfaces from open arithmetic inputs but
returned a broad \textsc{not-testable} verdict.  TPC-121--131 refine
the determinant, zero-mode, archive, fixed-shift, arithmetic-reduction,
and end-to-end synthesis branches.  We give a stricter MVP3 decision
calculus with seven ordered, exhaustive route outcomes after manifest
validation:
\(\textsc{go}\), \(\textsc{architecture-infeasible}\),
\(\textsc{reroute}\), \(\textsc{stop-route}\),
\(\textsc{not-testable}\), \(\textsc{arithmetic-frontier}\), and
\(\textsc{open}\).  Contradictory route, scope, or stop records are
rejected before verdict selection.  The new arithmetic-frontier label is awarded only
after every structural, provenance, fixed-shift, and endpoint
interface is proved and every unresolved node is an explicitly typed
status-\(\textsc{open}\), positive-L2 growing
coefficient-specific arithmetic target.  Reductions,
finite regressions, and conditional interfaces cannot trigger it.
The audited snapshot has no complete actual-carrier native archive,
no complete theorem-backed endpoint registry, and no proved positive
L2 estimate.  Its first machine-readable missing record is H1's
native path archive, so the verdict remains
\(\textsc{not-testable}\).  This is not \(\textsc{go}\), not the
arithmetic frontier, and not an impossibility theorem for the
architecture or for twin primes.  We finish with certificate-specific
branches for repair, arithmetic attack, rerouting, and publication of
route obstructions.
\end{abstract}

\noindent\textbf{Keywords:} fixed shift; typed proof graph; route
decision; arithmetic frontier; first-missing certificate; parity
barrier.

\section{From MVP2 to MVP3}

TPC-MVP1 resolved a conditional fixed-shift proof program into gates
H1--H9 \citep{WangMVP1}.  TPC-MVP2 gave each gate a typed status and
returned \textsc{not-testable} for the TPC-103--119 snapshot
\citep{WangTPC120}.  The next papers sharpen two kinds of input.

First, TPC-121 and TPC-122 replace opaque determinant and zero-mode
symbols by explicit conditional exponent formulas
\citep{WangTPC121,WangTPC122}.  Second, TPC-123--130 specify a native
path archive, physical reassembly, prescribed-shift intertwining, a
literal affine block, and an exact route from determinant two to
masked shift-two Liouville correlations and a Fej\'er
four-Liouville target
\citep{WangTPC123,WangTPC124,WangTPC125,
WangTPC126,WangTPC127,WangTPC128,WangTPC129,WangTPC130}.  TPC-131
then states the general endpoint formula
\[
 |B_{h_0,\delta}(X)|
 \le o(X)+
 X^{1-\sigma_{\rm raw}+\Lambda_{\rm phys}+o(1)}
\]
under a proof-carrying acyclic certificate
\citep{WangTPC131}.

These advances make the missing inputs more precise.  They do not
prove those inputs.  MVP3 therefore needs a state between a generic
open program and a genuinely isolated arithmetic frontier, while
preserving route-specific stop certificates.

\section{Node status and evidence level are different}

\begin{definition}[Node status]
\label{def:node}
For one fully typed node:
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=2em]
\item \(\PROVED\): a theorem proves the declared statement;
\item \(\COND\): an exact implication is proved but a displayed
  premise remains unproved;
\item \(\OPEN\): the literal object and test are available but the
  target theorem is absent;
\item \(\NT\): an archive, type, exponent, or compatibility record
  needed to execute the test is absent; and
\item \(\REFUTED\): a theorem-backed witness contradicts that
  particular declared statement.
\end{itemize}
\end{definition}

\begin{definition}[Evidence level and direction]
\label{def:level}
An \(\Lzero\) record is an abstract finite identity or model
obstruction.  An \(\Lone\) record is an exact identity or executable
crosswalk on a literal physical carrier.  A conditional \(\Lone\)
record remains \(\Lone\).  A positive \(\Ltwo\) record is a proved,
uniform, growing, coefficient-specific power estimate at the
prescribed \(h_0\).  A negative \(\Ltwo\) record is an actual growing
counterfamily or lower obstruction in the same quantifier scope.
\end{definition}

Finite computation over many values of \(X\), a frozen-family
estimate, a density-one result, an averaged-shift theorem, and a
reduction to an unproved bilinear or four-sign form do not meet the
positive \(\Ltwo\) definition.

\begin{proposition}[No promotion by naming]
\label{prop:nopromotion}
A node labeled ``arithmetic,'' ``spectral,'' ``Liouville,''
``bilinear,'' or ``four-sign'' is not thereby \(\Ltwo\).  In
particular the exact
pullback and decompositions of TPC-127--130 remain \(\Lzero/\Lone\)
until their growing actual-family estimates are proved.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
The evidence level is determined by quantifiers, carrier, shift,
normalization, and the proved inequality, not by the formula's
vocabulary.  Exact transformations preserve a target but do not
supply its missing bound.
\end{proof}

\section{Seven ordered exhaustive route outcomes}

\begin{definition}[Verdict certificates]
\label{def:verdicts}
For a selected literal route and a declared route universe:
\begin{enumerate}[label=(\roman*),leftmargin=2.2em]
\item \(\GO\): every required node is \(\PROVED\) in one compatible
  scope and
  \(\sigma_{\rm raw}-\Lambda_{\rm phys}>0\).
\item \(\AINF\): a theorem-backed cover exhausts the declared route
  universe and every covered route has an unavoidable stop
  certificate.
\item \(\REROUTE\): the selected route is stopped and a typed
  alternative has been selected with a fresh registry.
\item \(\STOPR\): the selected route has a complete literal stop
  certificate, but no typed alternative has yet been selected.
\item \(\NT\): a required artifact or type is absent.
\item \(\AF\): every structural, provenance, fixed-shift, and endpoint
  node is \(\PROVED\), strict endpoint slack is certified, and every
  unresolved node has status \(\OPEN\) and is an explicitly typed
  positive-\(\Ltwo\) target.  A \(\COND\), \(\REFUTED\), \(\NT\), or
  scope-mismatched residual node blocks this outcome.
\item \(\OPEN\): every remaining case.
\end{enumerate}
\end{definition}

\begin{theorem}[Verdict precedence]
\label{thm:precedence}
Given a snapshot manifest passing the consistency checks of
\cref{def:snapshot}, apply \cref{def:verdicts} in the displayed order.
The first applicable test returns exactly one of the seven outcomes.
An inconsistent manifest returns no route verdict and must first be
repaired.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Manifest validation first removes malformed node tables, scopes,
route selections, stop covers, and reroute records.  On a valid
manifest, the displayed list is an ordered classifier: \(\GO\) is
tested first, the two global and selected-route stop branches are
tested next, and the first absent artifact then gives \(\NT\).  With
no earlier outcome, \(\AF\) requires strict structural closure and a
residual set consisting only of \(\OPEN\), positive-\(\Ltwo\) targets;
the final catch-all is \(\OPEN\).  Hence some test always applies and
precedence selects a unique first applicable outcome.  This does not
assert that the unprioritized raw predicates are intrinsically
pairwise disjoint.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}[Conditional synthesis is metadata]
The statement ``conditional synthesis available'' records that a
valid implication has been proved.  It is not a route verdict and is
not a weakened form of \(\GO\).
\end{remark}

\begin{remark}[Architecture-infeasible is scoped]
The route universe must itself have a proof-carrying cover.  Stopping
all currently fashionable examples is insufficient.  Even a valid
\(\AINF\) verdict excludes only the declared architecture; it does
not contradict the twin-prime conjecture.
\end{remark}

\section{Frozen snapshots and controlled changes}

A route verdict is meaningful only relative to a frozen object.  The
word ``current'' must therefore be backed by data rather than by the
latest paper number.

\begin{definition}[Snapshot manifest]
\label{def:snapshot}
An MVP3 snapshot manifest contains:
\begin{enumerate}[label=(\roman*),leftmargin=2em]
\item the audit date and repository commit or content hashes;
\item the declared route universe and the selected route;
\item every node statement, scope, carrier hash, and evidence source;
\item the proof-DAG edges and one topological order;
\item the physical occurrence registry and every allowed joint-token
  replacement; and
\item the endpoint threshold, parameter cells, and exact certificate
  status.
\end{enumerate}
Changing any field creates a new snapshot rather than silently
updating the old verdict.
\end{definition}

Before verdict selection, the validator requires exact agreement
between the node table, the DAG node set, and the supplied topological
order; checks acyclicity and parent-before-child order; validates every
status, evidence tag, and scope; and requires the selected route to
belong to the declared universe.  Each stop record must carry a
source, carrier, and scope.  An \(\AINF\) claim must cover the route
universe exactly.  A reroute alternative must be distinct, declared,
and not already stopped, while the selected route must be stopped.
An endpoint certificate must name its source and route cell.
Contradictions are rejected rather than mapped silently to a verdict.

The dated v2 manifest used here records content hashes for TPC-120 and
TPC-123--132, with a joint hash for TPC-121--122; the route universe
\[
\begin{split}
\mathcal R=\{&
\texttt{signed\_native},\ \texttt{positive\_resonance},\\
&\texttt{coherent\_prime\_square}\};
\end{split}
\]
the selected route \(\texttt{signed\_native}\); the explicit H1--H9
DAG and its
topological order; an \(\texttt{INCOMPLETE}\) occurrence registry; and
an endpoint record with both \(\texttt{proved=false}\) and
\(\texttt{strict\_slack=false}\).  A source change changes its content
hash and therefore creates a new snapshot.

\begin{proposition}[What is and is not monotone]
\label{prop:monotone}
Fix the node statements, route universe, carrier, and physical event
registry.
\begin{enumerate}[label=(\alph*),leftmargin=2em]
\item Replacing a missing artifact by a valid typed artifact can move
  the first-missing pointer downstream, but does not by itself prove
  the target at that node.
\item Adding a redundant proof to an already complete \(\GO\)
  certificate preserves \(\GO\).
\item A route-specific stop certificate remains valid when unrelated
  alternatives are added.
\item An \(\AINF\) certificate need not survive enlargement of the
  declared route universe.
\item Revealing the value of a formerly unknown nonnegative endpoint
  token may lead either to a strict pass or to a stop; evidence
  completion is not numerical improvement.
\end{enumerate}
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
For (a), the topological scan proceeds to the next absent record, but
the new artifact may have status \(\OPEN\) or \(\COND\).  Item (b)
follows because neither a premise nor the strict inequality changes.
A witness for one fixed route does not depend on the existence of a
different route, proving (c).  By contrast, \(\AINF\) has a universal
quantifier over the declared route universe; adding an uncovered
route invalidates that quantifier and proves (d).  Finally, an
unknown token is not zero.  Its theorem-backed value can lie on
either side of the strict threshold, which proves (e).
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}[The first missing item is not the hardest theorem]
The topological first-missing report identifies the earliest point at
which the present certificate cannot be executed.  It does not claim
that this item is analytically deepest.  H3 may remain the parity-facing
gate even while H1 is the first absent artifact.
\end{remark}

\begin{remark}[Rerouting is a new certificate]
Changing from an averaged frame to direct native-\(h_0\) dispersion,
or from a positive majorant to a signed four-sign route, changes maps,
scopes, and event occurrences.  The old endpoint optimizer is not a
warm-start proof: the alternative requires a new manifest and a new
canonical registry.
\end{remark}

\begin{definition}[First-missing report]
\label{def:first}
The machine manifest stores an explicit gate-certificate DAG and a
candidate topological order.  It first rejects cycles, missing or
extra node IDs, and any order that fails exact node coverage or
parent-before-child precedence.  It then scans the validated order.
The first-missing report is
\[
 (\mathrm{node},\mathrm{gate},\mathrm{status},
  \mathrm{evidence},\mathrm{required\ artifact})
\]
for the earliest required node having status \(\NT\).
\end{definition}

The v2 DAG makes H1 the root; H2--H7 depend on H1; H8 depends on H1,
H6, and H7; and H9 depends on H2--H8.  Its validated machine scan
therefore returns
\[
\boxed{
\begin{aligned}
\mathrm{node}&=\mathsf{H1.archive},\\
\mathrm{artifact}&=\text{complete actual-carrier native path archive}.
\end{aligned}}
\]

The report is stable under downstream speculation: a later
conditional theorem cannot make this earlier archive executable.

\section{MVP3 snapshot verdict}

\begin{theorem}[MVP3 decision]
\label{thm:decision}
For the declared TPC-MVP1 architecture and the TPC-121--131
certificate bundle audited on 27 July 2026,
\[
 \boxed{\operatorname{Verdict}_{121:131}=\NT.}
\]
The snapshot is neither \(\GO\), nor \(\AF\), nor \(\AINF\).
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
H1's complete actual-carrier archive is absent, so
\cref{thm:precedence} reaches \(\NT\) before either
\(\AF\) or \(\OPEN\).  The \(\GO\) premise fails additionally at the
open H2--H4 arithmetic and tail targets and the not-testable
H5--H9 records.  The arithmetic-frontier premise fails because several
structural nodes are not proved and no strict actual endpoint is
certified.  Finally, the stop records cover only
\(\texttt{positive\_resonance}\) and
\(\texttt{coherent\_prime\_square}\); the selected
\(\texttt{signed\_native}\) route has no stop certificate.  Hence no
exact cover of the declared route universe exists and \(\AINF\) is
unavailable.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[No positive L2 progress claim]
The TPC-121--131 bundle may sharpen arithmetic targets and exact
crosswalks, but it contains no theorem that can be entered as a
positive \(\Ltwo\) gate in \cref{tab:gates}.
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
Each row of the paper audit either proves a finite identity,
conditional implication, reduction, or quantifier obstruction, or
states a growing estimate as the missing item.  None proves the
required uniform coefficient-specific power estimate.
\end{proof}

\section{Machine regression and claim boundary}

The standard-library script
\texttt{experiments/tpc132\_mvp3\_route\_audit.py} encodes the gate
records and verifies:
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=2em]
\item reachability and precedence of all seven ordered outcomes after
manifest validation;
\item rejection of arithmetic-frontier labels with a conditional or
refuted residual node;
\item refusal to return \(\AINF\) without a complete route-universe
certificate and rejection of contradictory manifests;
\item presence of the content-hash manifest, explicit DAG, and valid
topological order;
\item absence of a proved positive-\(\Ltwo\) record; and
\item the DAG-checked machine-readable first-missing report for H1.
\end{enumerate}
The script is a decision-logic regression.  It does not inspect
primes or establish any growing analytic estimate.

\paragraph{Established.}
The verdict precedence, certificate requirements, first-missing
format, and snapshot classification are \(\Lzero/\Lone\) audit
results.  They prevent circular implication, false route closure,
and false promotion to the arithmetic frontier.

\paragraph{Not established.}
No complete actual archive, H1--H9 conjunction, positive fixed-shift
\(\Ltwo\) theorem, strict physical endpoint, hard-packet
\(o(X)\) estimate, Hardy--Littlewood asymptotic, prime-pair lower
bound, or twin-prime theorem is proved.

\section{Conclusion}

MVP3 turns ``we are near the arithmetic core'' into a checkable
statement rather than a rhetorical one.  The current program has a
sharper map and several exact transformations, but its first missing
object is still structural.  The correct verdict is therefore
\(\NT\), not \(\AF\).  If the archive, reassembly, fixed-shift, and
endpoint certificates are eventually closed, the same decision
calculus will promote the route to the arithmetic frontier only when
every remaining unresolved node is \(\OPEN\), positive \(\Ltwo\), and
scope-compatible, without calling a conditional interface or
reduction a theorem.  If a literal stop appears first, the
calculus records the stopped route and the permitted alternative
without implying anything negative about twin primes.
