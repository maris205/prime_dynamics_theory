\begin{abstract}
We continue the open distance-two capacity problem from RH-366.  Positive
signs may not occur two places apart in the raw Mobius prefix.  The two path
components of this constraint admit an exact finite reduction: even nonzero
vertices are isolated, while every odd same-sign run has length at most eight
because nine step-two odd positions contain a multiple of nine.  The resulting
eight alternating run frequencies give the exact capacity for every prefix.
This is an all-$N$ identity, not a numerical fit.  A separate period-18
ternary construction has the same complete ordered three-symbol pair ledger
at every cyclic lag for two words, but capacities $10q$ and $12q$ after $q$
repetitions.  The construction is a scoped negative for pair data as a
sufficient statistic; it is not a Mobius sequence and does not prove that the
Mobius capacity limit fails.  No canonical operator, prime trace,
Hilbert--Polya construction, or proof of RH is claimed.
\end{abstract}

\section{Scope and frozen source}

RH-366 fixes the open raw capacity
\begin{equation}
 K_N=\max_{\substack{\varepsilon_1,\ldots,\varepsilon_N\in\{-1,+1\}\\
 \varepsilon_n=\varepsilon_{n+2}=+1\ \text{never}}}
 \left|\sum_{n\leq N}\Mob(n)\varepsilon_n\right|.
 \label{eq:capacity}
\end{equation}
The optimizer is allowed to depend on the complete prefix.  The frozen
source gives a two-path maximum-weight-independent-set (MWIS) algorithm and
the bracket
\begin{equation}
 \frac4{\pi^2}\leq\liminf_{N\to\infty}\frac{K_N}{N}
 \leq\limsup_{N\to\infty}\frac{K_N}{N}\leq\frac6{\pi^2},
 \label{eq:oldbracket}
\end{equation}
but no existence theorem for the limit \cite{rh366,capacitysource}.

Put $M_N=\sum_{n\leq N}\Mob(n)$.  Starting from the all-negative word,
changing a position to $+1$ adds $2\Mob(n)$.  Thus the positive positions
form independent sets on the odd and even step-two paths.  All claims below
are in this scalar adaptive-capacity data type.  They are not trace or
determinant statements.

\begin{remark}[Claim firewall]
Finite endpoint rows reproduce an exact calculation only.  The periodic
words used later are synthetic ternary words, not values of $\Mob$.  Their
cyclic pair ledger is not an open-prefix ledger.  The physical route
coordinate remains the frozen RH-366 same-clock coordinate.
\end{remark}

\section{Exact eight-run reduction}

\begin{definition}[Run counts]
For $\sigma\in\{-1,+1\}$ and $1\leq k\leq8$, define
\begin{equation}
 C_{\sigma,k}(N)=\#\left\{\begin{array}{l}
 n\leq N-2(k-1),\ n\text{ odd},\\
 \Mob(n+2j)=\sigma\text{ for }0\leq j<k
 \end{array}\right\}.
 \label{eq:C}
\end{equation}
Also define the isolated even count
\begin{equation}
 E_\sigma(N)=\#\{n\leq N:n\equiv2\pmod4,\ \Mob(n)=\sigma\}.
 \label{eq:E}
\end{equation}
\end{definition}

\begin{lemma}[Finite run cutoff]
Every odd step-two run of nonzero Mobius values having one fixed sign has
length at most eight.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
The nine integers $n,n+2,\ldots,n+16$ represent every residue class modulo
9, since $2$ is invertible modulo 9.  One is divisible by 9 and hence has
Mobius value zero.  Such a zero interrupts every fixed-sign nonzero run.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[All-prefix run formula]
Let $W_\sigma(N)$ be the sum of the positive weights in the two path MWIS
problems with weights $\sigma\Mob(n)$.  Then for every $N\geq1$,
\begin{equation}
 W_\sigma(N)=E_\sigma(N)+
 \sum_{k=1}^{8}(-1)^{k+1}C_{\sigma,k}(N).
 \label{eq:runformula}
\end{equation}
Consequently,
\begin{equation}
 S_N^{\max}=-M_N+2W_{+1}(N),\qquad
 S_N^{\min}=-M_N-2W_{-1}(N),
 \label{eq:extremes}
\end{equation}
and
\begin{equation}
 K_N=\max\bigl\{|S_N^{\max}|,|S_N^{\min}|\bigr\}.
 \label{eq:Kformula}
\end{equation}
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
If $n$ is even and $\Mob(n)\ne0$, then $n\equiv2\pmod4$; the intervening
multiple of four has Mobius value zero.  Hence the even path contributes
exactly $E_\sigma(N)$ to the MWIS for weights $\sigma\Mob$.  On the odd path,
discard zero and opposite-sign vertices and consider one fixed-sign run of
length $L$.  Its MWIS contribution is $\lceil L/2\rceil$.  The elementary
identity
\begin{equation}
 \left\lceil\frac L2\right\rceil
 =\sum_{k=1}^{L}(-1)^{k+1}(L-k+1)
 \label{eq:ceilidentity}
\end{equation}
counts the all-one intervals in that run.  Summing over all runs turns the
right side into the alternating interval counts in \eqref{eq:C}; the lemma
truncates it at $k=8$.  The baseline identity
\[
 \sum_{n\leq N}\Mob(n)\varepsilon_n
 =-M_N+2\sum_{n:\,\varepsilon_n=+1}\Mob(n)
\]
then gives \eqref{eq:extremes} and \eqref{eq:Kformula}.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[Exact convergence criterion]
Set
\begin{equation}
 R_\sigma(N)=E_\sigma(N)+
 \sum_{k=1}^{8}(-1)^{k+1}C_{\sigma,k}(N).
 \label{eq:R}
\end{equation}
Then
\begin{equation}
 \left|\frac{K_N}{N}-2\max_{\sigma\in\{-1,+1\}}
 \frac{R_\sigma(N)}{N}\right|\leq\frac{|M_N|}{N}.
 \label{eq:criterionbound}
\end{equation}
Since $M_N=o(N)$, the limit $K_N/N$ exists if and only if
\begin{equation}
 \max\{R_{+1}(N),R_{-1}(N)\}/N
 \label{eq:maxcriterion}
\end{equation}
converges.  Existence of each individual $C_{\sigma,k}(N)/N$ is sufficient,
but is not necessary.
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
For each nonnegative $R$, the reverse triangle inequality gives
\[
 \bigl||-M+2R|-2R\bigr|\leq|M|,
 \qquad
 \bigl||-M-2R|-2R\bigr|\leq|M|.
\]
Take maxima and use \eqref{eq:extremes}.  The prime number theorem gives
$M_N=o(N)$, proving the equivalence.
\end{proof}

The elementary squarefree sieve gives
$\#\{m\leq X:m\text{ odd},\Mob(m)^2=1\}\sim4X/\pi^2$.  Writing $n=2m$
therefore shows that $E_{+1}(N)+E_{-1}(N)\sim2N/\pi^2$.  If
$S_{\rm odd}(X)=\sum_{m\leq X,\,m\text{ odd}}\Mob(m)$, then
$M_X=S_{\rm odd}(X)-S_{\rm odd}(X/2)$.  Iterating this relation and using
$M_X=o(X)$ gives $S_{\rm odd}(X)=o(X)$, so the two signs split equally:
\begin{equation}
 \frac{E_{+1}(N)}N,\frac{E_{-1}(N)}N\longrightarrow\frac1{\pi^2}.
 \label{eq:Edensity}
\end{equation}
Thus the open part can also be written as the convergence of the maximum of
the two alternating eight-run combinations after subtracting their common
baseline $1/\pi^2$.  No source here proves those combinations converge.

\section{A cyclic pair-ledger obstruction}

Consider the period-18 words
\begin{equation}
 u=\texttt{+++++-++0--+-----0},\qquad
 v=\texttt{+++++---0--+---++0}.
 \label{eq:words}
\end{equation}
Both have eight plus signs, eight minus signs, and zeros at positions 9 and
18.  For any finite ternary weight word $w=(w_n)$, write
\begin{equation}
 K_N(w)=\max_{\varepsilon_n=\varepsilon_{n+2}=+1\ \mathrm{never}}
 \left|\sum_{n=1}^{N}w_n\varepsilon_n\right|.
 \label{eq:generalcapacity}
\end{equation}
Thus \eqref{eq:capacity} is the special case $w_n=\Mob(n)$.  For a ternary
word $w$ let $B_{ab,w}(d)$ count ordered pairs
$(w_j,w_{j+d})=(a,b)$ with indices modulo 18.  The complete cyclic ledger is
the nine-vector $(B_{++},B_{+-},B_{+0},B_{-+},B_{--},B_{-0},B_{0+},B_{0-},B_{00})$
for each $d\in\mathbb Z/18\mathbb Z$.

\begin{theorem}[Cyclic pair data do not determine capacity]
The words in \eqref{eq:words} have identical cyclic ordered pair ledgers at
all 18 lags.  Nevertheless, for every integer $q\geq1$,
\begin{equation}
 K_{18q}(u^q)=10q,\qquad K_{18q}(v^q)=12q.
 \label{eq:periodcapacity}
\end{equation}
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Work in $\mathbb Z[x]/(x^{18}-1)$ and let $^*$ send $x$ to $x^{-1}$.  The plus
indicator polynomials and the common zero indicator are
\begin{align*}
 A&=1+x+x^2+x^3+x^4+x^6+x^7+x^{11},\\
 B&=1+x+x^2+x^3+x^4+x^{11}+x^{15}+x^{16},\\
 Z&=x^8+x^{17}.
\end{align*}
One has $A-B=x^6(1+x)(1-x^9)$ and
\begin{equation}
 (A-B)Z^*=0\pmod{x^{18}-1}.
 \label{eq:crosscert}
\end{equation}
Direct expansion gives the second finite group-ring certificate
\begin{equation}
 AA^*-BB^*=0\pmod{x^{18}-1}.
 \label{eq:autocert}
\end{equation}
Representing each $x^{-j}$ by $x^{18-j}$, the resulting unreduced
polynomial difference is $(x^{18}-1)$ times
\[
 -x^2(x-1)(x+1)^2(x^2+1)(x^2+x+1)(x^6+x^3+1).
\]
Let $J=1+x+\cdots+x^{17}$.  Cyclic multiplication by $J^*$ records only the
common plus count, while \eqref{eq:crosscert} records the plus-zero cross
terms.  Since the minus indicators are $J-Z-A$ and $J-Z-B$, these identities
and \eqref{eq:autocert} show that every cross-correlation among plus, minus,
and zero indicators agrees.
This is exactly equality of all nine ordered pair counts at every cyclic lag.

For the capacity, split each repeated word into its odd and even paths and
use the run formula for a ternary word.  The four parity strings and their
positive/negative MWIS totals are
\begin{center}
\begin{tabular}{@{}lccc@{}}
\toprule
word/path & repeated block & $W_+$ & $W_-$\\
\midrule
$u$, odd & \texttt{++++0----} & $2q$ & $2q$\\
$u$, even & \texttt{++-+-+--0} & $3q$ & $3q$\\
$v$, odd & \texttt{+++-0---+} & $2q+1$ & $3q$\\
$v$, even & \texttt{++---+-+0} & $3q$ & $3q$\\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{center}
Both words have total weight zero.  Therefore $u^q$ has capacity $2(5q)=10q$;
for $v^q$ the two extrema are $2(5q+1)=10q+2$ and $2(6q)=12q$, and the latter
is at least the former for $q\geq1$.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}[Open-prefix boundary]
The ledger equality is cyclic only.  At open lag two, the $++$ count is 4
for $u$ and 3 for $v$ (and the $-+$ count is 2 versus 3).  The periodic
words are not a Mobius sequence and do not prove that the Mobius capacity
limit fails.  They show only that a general periodic ternary two-point law is
not a sufficient statistic for this nonlinear capacity.
\end{remark}

\section{Conclusion}

The distance-two capacity is not an uncontrolled infinite run problem: its
finite-prefix value is exactly an eight-frequency arithmetic expression.  The
remaining obstacle is a genuine higher-order Mobius pattern theorem, not
another finite fit.  Cyclic pair data alone cannot replace that theorem in a
general ternary model.  The next route must either control the alternating
eight-run envelope in the Mobius data type or supply a distinct source-backed
edge.
