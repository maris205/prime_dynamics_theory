\begin{abstract}
The exact rectangular history maps of RH-173 suggest a square completion on
an infinite memory space.  Freezing the normalized first-block dynamics at
$B=rA$ and writing $q=\sqrt\eta>0$, the canonical completion is
\[
 \mathcal L(y_0,y_1,y_2,\ldots)
 =(By_0,qy_0,qy_1,qy_2,\ldots)
\]
on $\ell^2(\mathbb N_0;\mathcal Y)$.  We determine its spectrum and trace-
ideal status exactly.

If $\mathcal Y$ is finite dimensional, then
\[
 \sigma(\mathcal L)=\sigma(B)\cup\{z:|z|\le q\},
 \qquad
 \sigmae(\mathcal L)=\{z:|z|=q\}.
\]
The tail is a weighted unilateral shift.  Therefore $\mathcal L$ is not
compact, belongs to no Schatten class, and no positive power
$\mathcal L^m$ is compact or Schatten.  The ordinary Fredholm determinants
$\det(I-z\mathcal L^m)$ required by the moving-cloud route are consequently
undefined.

Finite truncations conceal this obstruction: their shift tails are nilpotent
and have spectrum only at zero.  Nevertheless, for $0<|z|<q$ their resolvent
satisfies the exact vector lower bound
\[
 \left\|(zI-qS_L)^{-1}e_0\right\|
 =\frac1{|z|}\left(\sum_{k=0}^{L-1}(q/|z|)^{2k}\right)^{1/2},
\]
which grows exponentially with $L$.  A 15-case audit verifies this formula;
at $L=64$ the largest recorded lower bound exceeds $10^{39.9}$ although every
finite shift eigenvalue is zero.

This is a rigorous no-go theorem for the direct canonical infinite-history
shift as a Fredholm-determinant operator.  It does not reject finite cyclic
closures, weighted differences that cancel the shift, scattering objects, or
direct transfer-space constructions.  The natural next branch is finite
cyclic memory.
\end{abstract}

\section{Why square the rectangular cocycle?}

RH-173 constructs exact maps $T_t:\cK_t\to\cK_{t+1}$, but a standard
operator determinant requires a square operator on one fixed Hilbert space
\citep{WangRH173}.  The most literal square completion stores the entire past
in
\[
 \cH=\ell^2(\mathbb N_0;\cY)
 =\cY\oplus\cY\oplus\cdots.
\]
If the time-dependent normalization ratio is frozen at $r>0$, the current
block evolves by $B=rA$ and every older block shifts one position with weight
$q=\sqrt\eta$.  The resulting operator is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:L}
 \mathcal L(y_0,y_1,y_2,\ldots)
 =(By_0,qy_0,qy_1,qy_2,\ldots).
\end{equation}

The frozen model is already sufficient to decide the determinant question.
If even one positive constant $q$ forces a noncompact shift tail, changing
$B$ or fitting a finite packet cannot make the literal completion trace
class.

\section{Block form and invariant tail}

Let $S$ be the unilateral shift on $\ell^2(\mathbb N;\cY)$,
\[
 S(y_1,y_2,\ldots)=(0,y_1,y_2,\ldots),
\]
and let $E:\cY\to\ell^2(\mathbb N;\cY)$ inject into the first tail
coordinate.  Relative to
$\cH=\cY\oplus\ell^2(\mathbb N;\cY)$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:block}
 \mathcal L=
 \begin{pmatrix}
 B&0\\
 qE&qS
 \end{pmatrix}.
\end{equation}
The tail subspace $\{0\}\oplus\ell^2(\mathbb N;\cY)$ is invariant and the
restriction is $qS$.  The coupling $qE$ has rank $\dim\cY$.

\section{Exact spectrum}

\begin{theorem}[Spectral disk of the history completion]\label{thm:spectrum}
Suppose $\dim\cY<\infty$ and $q>0$.  Then
\begin{equation}\label{eq:spectrum}
 \sigma(\mathcal L)=\sigma(B)\cup\overline{D(0,q)}.
\end{equation}
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
If $z\notin\sigma(B)\cup\overline{D(0,q)}$, both $z-B$ and $z-qS$ are
invertible.  The triangular inverse formula applied to \eqref{eq:block}
shows that $z-\mathcal L$ is invertible.  This proves one inclusion.

Let $|z|=q$.  The unilateral shift has approximate eigenvectors at every
point of its boundary circle.  Embedding those vectors in the invariant tail
gives approximate eigenvectors for $\mathcal L$, so
$z\in\sigma(\mathcal L)$.

Now let $|z|<q$.  If $z-B$ is not surjective, neither is
$z-\mathcal L$, because the first output coordinate is $(z-B)y_0$.
If $z-B$ is invertible, use the adjoint.  For any nonzero $v\in\cY$, the
tail vector
\[
 h_v=(v,(\overline z/q)v,(\overline z/q)^2v,\ldots)
\]
belongs to $\ell^2$ and satisfies $qS^*h_v=\overline z h_v$.  Choose
$x\in\cY$ solving
\[
 (B^*-\overline z)x=-qv.
\]
Then $(x,h_v)$ is a nonzero eigenvector of $\mathcal L^*$ with eigenvalue
$\overline z$.  Hence $z\in\sigma(\mathcal L)$.  Finally, every point of
$\sigma(B)$ makes the first-coordinate equation nonsurjective in finite
dimension, so it also belongs to the spectrum.
\end{proof}

The disk is independent of the detailed prime-dynamics block $B$.  Its radius
is fixed solely by the memory parameter.  For the archived value
$\eta=1/512$,
\[
 q=\frac1{\sqrt{512}}=0.0441941738\ldots.
\]
Small radius does not mean trace class: infinitely many directions carry the
same nonzero shift weight.

\section{Essential spectrum and Schatten obstruction}

\begin{theorem}[No compact or Schatten power]\label{thm:schatten}
For $q>0$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:essential}
 \sigmae(\mathcal L)=\{z:|z|=q\}.
\end{equation}
Moreover, for every integer $m\ge1$, $\mathcal L^m$ is not compact and does
not belong to $\cS_p$ for any $1\le p<\infty$.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
The difference between $\mathcal L$ and $B\oplus qS$ is the finite-rank
operator with only block $qE$.  Essential spectrum is invariant under compact
perturbations, the finite matrix $B$ has empty essential spectrum, and the
Fredholm essential spectrum of the unilateral shift is the unit circle
\citep{Conway1990}.  This proves \eqref{eq:essential}.

The invariant tail restriction of $\mathcal L^m$ is $q^mS^m$.  The vectors
$q^mS^me_n$ form an orthogonal sequence of constant norm $q^m$, so the image
of the unit ball is not relatively compact.  Thus $\mathcal L^m$ is not
compact.  Every Schatten operator is compact, completing the proof
\citep{Simon2005}.
\end{proof}

\section{Which memory weights could evade the obstruction?}

The failure is caused by a constant tail weight, not by the mere existence of
history coordinates.  This can be stated exactly for general weighted shifts.
Let $(q_n)_{n\ge0}$ be nonnegative and define
\[
 W(y_0,y_1,\ldots)=(0,q_0y_0,q_1y_1,\ldots)
\]
on $\ell^2(\mathbb N_0;\cY)$, where $d=\dim\cY<\infty$.

\begin{theorem}[Compactness and Schatten criterion for age weights]
\label{thm:weighted}
The weighted history shift $W$ is compact if and only if $q_n\to0$.  For
$1\le p<\infty$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:weighted-schatten}
 W\in\cS_p
 \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
 \sum_{n\ge0}q_n^p<\infty,
\end{equation}
and in that case
\begin{equation}\label{eq:weighted-norm}
 \norm{W}_p^p=d\sum_{n\ge0}q_n^p.
\end{equation}
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
The positive operator $W^*W$ is block diagonal with blocks $q_n^2I_\cY$.
Hence the singular values of $W$ are precisely the numbers $q_n$, each
repeated $d$ times.  They tend to zero exactly when $W$ is compact, and their
$p$th powers are summable exactly under \eqref{eq:weighted-schatten}.
\end{proof}

The archived memory recursion has the same shift factor
$q_n\equiv\sqrt\eta$, so it lies at the opposite extreme: none of the
singular values decays with age.  A trace-class redesign would require, for
example, age weights in $\ell^1$; a Hilbert--Schmidt redesign would require
weights in $\ell^2$.  Such a redesign changes the original recursive memory
and must be justified physically rather than inserted after the fact.

\section{Explicit exterior resolvent}

Outside the spectral union in Theorem~\ref{thm:spectrum}, the triangular
inverse is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:triangular-inverse}
 (z-\mathcal L)^{-1}=
 \begin{pmatrix}
 (z-B)^{-1}&0\\
 (z-qS)^{-1}qE(z-B)^{-1}&(z-qS)^{-1}
 \end{pmatrix}.
\end{equation}
Consequently, with
$b(z)=\norm{(z-B)^{-1}}$ and
$s(z)=\norm{(z-qS)^{-1}}$, a simple block-row estimate gives
\begin{equation}\label{eq:exterior-bound}
 \norm{(z-\mathcal L)^{-1}}
 \le b(z)+s(z)+q\,b(z)s(z).
\end{equation}
For $|z|>q$, $s(z)\le(|z|-q)^{-1}$.  Formula
\eqref{eq:triangular-inverse} makes clear that the first-block spectrum and
the shift circle are the only exterior singularities; the finite-rank coupling
creates no additional spectral component outside their union.

\begin{corollary}[Direct Fredholm-determinant route is unavailable]
Neither $\det(I-z\mathcal L)$ nor $\det(I-z\mathcal L^m)$ is an ordinary
Fredholm determinant for any $m\ge1$.  In particular, squaring does not remove
the history-tail obstruction.
\end{corollary}

One could seek a relative determinant against the shift background, but that
would be a scattering construction with a specified reference operator.  It
is not the direct moving-cloud determinant required in RH-80
\citep{WangRH80}.

\section{Why finite spectra are misleading}

Let $S_L$ be the nilpotent unilateral shift on $\mathbb C^L$,
$S_Le_j=e_{j+1}$ for $j<L-1$ and $S_Le_{L-1}=0$.  Its spectrum is
$\{0\}$ for every $L$, so eigenvalue plots cannot reveal the limiting disk.

\begin{proposition}[Exact interior resolvent growth]\label{prop:resolvent}
For $z\ne0$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:finite-resolvent}
 (zI-qS_L)^{-1}e_0
 =\sum_{k=0}^{L-1}\frac{q^k}{z^{k+1}}e_k,
\end{equation}
and hence
\begin{equation}\label{eq:lower-bound}
 \norm{(zI-qS_L)^{-1}}
 \ge\frac1{|z|}
 \left(\sum_{k=0}^{L-1}(q/|z|)^{2k}\right)^{1/2}.
\end{equation}
For every fixed $0<|z|<q$, the right side grows exponentially in $L$.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Because $S_L^L=0$, the finite Neumann expansion is exact:
\[
 (zI-qS_L)^{-1}
 =z^{-1}\sum_{k=0}^{L-1}(qS_L/z)^k.
\]
Applying it to $e_0$ gives \eqref{eq:finite-resolvent}.  The basis vectors are
orthonormal, so taking the norm gives the displayed lower bound.
\end{proof}

This is a standard finite-section manifestation of nonnormal spectral
pollution: the eigenvalues remain at zero while the pseudospectrum expands
toward the shift disk \citep{TrefethenEmbree2005}.

\section{Finite-section audit}

The audit uses $L=4,8,16,32,64$ and points
$|z|/q=0.25,0.50,0.75$, for 15 resolvent cases.  Direct triangular solves
agree with \eqref{eq:lower-bound} to relative error below $10^{-12}$.  Every
finite shift spectrum is exactly zero.  At $L=64$ the largest recorded
resolvent-vector lower bound has
\[
 \log_{10}\norm{(zI-qS_L)^{-1}e_0}=39.9005.
\]

The same audit forms square block truncations of \eqref{eq:L} with
$B=0.8\operatorname{diag}(0.25,-0.40)$.  Exactly
$2(L-1)$ singular values remain at least $q$ in every truncation, matching
the growing shift multiplicity.  This finite singular floor is the trace-
ideal obstruction in elementary matrix form.

\section{Scope of the no-go theorem}

Theorem~\ref{thm:schatten} rejects one precise branch:
\[
 \text{literal infinite normalized history shift}
 \Longrightarrow
 \text{direct trace-ideal determinant}.
\]
It does not reject:
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=2em]
\item a finite cyclic closure, whose wrap edge replaces the unilateral tail;
\item a relative or scattering determinant with $qS$ as reference;
\item a difference of two histories in which the shift cancels;
\item a compactly weighted memory with weights tending to zero by age;
\item a direct physical transfer-space Riesz construction.
\end{enumerate}
The next paper develops the finite cyclic branch because it preserves a
direct finite determinant and, unexpectedly, reproduces the geometric cloud
polynomial exactly.

No physical cycle identification, all-level Riesz projection, complement
limit, self-adjoint operator, zeta divisor identity, or Riemann Hypothesis is
proved.
