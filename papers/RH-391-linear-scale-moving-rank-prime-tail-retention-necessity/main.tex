\begin{abstract}
Maynard's bounded-gap theorem contains an infinite subsequence of
consecutive-prime edges with one fixed repeated gap $h_*\le600$.  On
those edges, let the same exact rank $r$ be used at both endpoints, with
$r\to\infty$ and $r\le Cx$ for a fixed $C>0$.  We compare the strict
prime tail $P_r$ with the power and prime-density integrals $I_{2r}$ and
$J_r$, and lift both comparisons through the frozen seven-channel
square-clock endpoint.  If $q=x+h_*$ and
\[
 a=\left(\frac{x^2}{q^2-1}\right)^r,
 \qquad \rho=\left(\frac{x}{q}\right)^{2r},
\]
then every scalar and endpoint edge jump, under its natural
normalization, equals $a+o(1)$.  The two endpoint scales are unequal;
retaining the factor $\rho$ gives the sharp pair mechanism.  If
$r/x\to\lambda<\infty$, each natural pair maximum has lower profile
$e^{-2\lambda h_*}/(1+e^{-2\lambda h_*})$.  Without convergence of
$r/x$, the linear bound gives $e^{-1200C}/2$; for $r=o(x)$ it gives
$1/2$.  The pair errors also dominate $P_{r+1}$ by an unbounded factor.
The proof uses elementary integer-tail bounds, a uniform all-rank
endpoint coefficient, and Taylor control.  It does not use a
linear-rank asymptotic $P_r\sim K_r$.  A 60-row exact artifact records
the finite algebra and source closure but is not an analytic proof.
\end{abstract}

\noindent\textbf{Keywords:} moving rank; strict prime tails; bounded
consecutive-prime gaps; endpoint retention; square-clock gap.

\section{Definitions and main theorem}

Let $3=p_1<p_2<\cdots$ be the odd primes.  At the endpoint $x=p_y$ and
for every exact integer $j\ge1$, define the strict and smooth tails
\begin{equation}
 P_j(y)=\sum_{p>x}(p^2-1)^{-j},\qquad
 I_{2j}(x)=\int_x^\infty\frac{t^{-2j}}{\log t}\,dt,
 \qquad
 J_j(x)=\int_x^\infty\frac{dt}{(t^2-1)^j\log t}.
 \label{eq:tails}
\end{equation}
The endpoint prime is excluded from $P_j(y)$.  For the rank $r$ under
consideration, put
\begin{equation}
 \EI_r(y)=P_r(y)-I_{2r}(x),\qquad
 \EJ_r(y)=P_r(y)-J_r(x).
 \label{eq:scalar-errors}
\end{equation}

We recall only the endpoint data needed below.  For $2\le m\le8$, set
\begin{equation}
 u_m=\prod_{p\ \mathrm{odd}}\frac{1-m/p^2}{1-1/p^2},\quad
 (\alpha_m)=(-2,2,-2,2,-2,2,-2),\quad
 (\beta_m)=(1,-2,2,-2,2,-2,2).
 \label{eq:endpoint-data}
\end{equation}
For $z=(z_1,\ldots,z_7)$ write
\[
 Z_m(z)=u_me^{z_{m-1}},\qquad
 C(Z)=1+\sum_{m=2}^8\alpha_mZ_m,\qquad
 W(Z)=\sum_{m=2}^8\beta_mZ_m,
\]
and
\begin{equation}
 F(z)=2\{C(u)-C(Z(z))\}-4W(Z(z))(1-e^{-z_1}).
 \label{eq:endpoint-map}
\end{equation}
RH-383 proves the exact normal form
$\GapP(y)=F(\PhiP(y))/\pi^2$, where
\begin{equation}
 \PhiP_c(y)=\sum_{j\ge1}\frac{c^jP_j(y)}j,
 \qquad 1\le c\le7.
 \label{eq:actual-coordinate}
\end{equation}
The two retained-head surrogates use the same exact rank $r$:
\begin{align}
 \PhiI_c(y)&=\sum_{j<r}\frac{c^jP_j(y)}j
      +\sum_{j\ge r}\frac{c^jI_{2j}(x)}j,
 \label{eq:I-coordinate}\\
 \PhiJ_c(y)&=\sum_{j<r}\frac{c^jP_j(y)}j
      +\sum_{j\ge r}\frac{c^jJ_j(x)}j.
 \label{eq:J-coordinate}
\end{align}
Define $\GapI=F(\PhiI)/\pi^2$, $\GapJ=F(\PhiJ)/\pi^2$, and the
unscaled endpoint discrepancies
\begin{equation}
 \DeltaI_r(y)=\pi^2\{\GapP(y)-\GapI(y)\},\qquad
 \DeltaJ_r(y)=\pi^2\{\GapP(y)-\GapJ(y)\}.
 \label{eq:endpoint-errors}
\end{equation}

For $r\ge1$, let
\begin{align}
 v_r&=\left(\frac{c^r}{r}\right)_{c=1}^7,
 &\gamma_r&=\nabla F(0)\mathbin{\cdot}v_r \notag\\
 &=\frac4r\{3^ru_4-2^ru_3+5^ru_6-4^ru_5 \notag\\
 &\hspace{3.2em}+7^ru_8-6^ru_7
   +2(u_3-u_4+u_5-u_6+u_7-u_8)\}.
 \label{eq:gamma}
\end{align}
Let $\underline u_8$ denote the exact outward rational lower endpoint
for $u_8$ frozen in RH-384, and set
\begin{equation}
 \kappa_\gamma=\frac{4\underline u_8}{7}
 >0.0347017856545.
 \label{eq:kappa}
\end{equation}

\begin{theorem}[Moving-rank retention along bounded consecutive-prime gaps]
\label{thm:main}
There is a fixed positive integer $h_*\le600$ and there are infinitely many
consecutive-prime edges
\begin{equation}
 x=p_y,\qquad q=p_{y+1}=x+h_*.
 \label{eq:fixed-edges}
\end{equation}
Fix $C>0$.  Along any sequence of these edges choose exact integers
$r=r_y$ such that
\begin{equation}
 r\longrightarrow\infty,\qquad r\le Cx,
 \label{eq:linear-rank}
\end{equation}
and use this same $r$ at both endpoints.  Put
\begin{equation}
 a=\left(\frac{x^2}{q^2-1}\right)^r,\qquad
 \rho=\left(\frac{x}{q}\right)^{2r}.
 \label{eq:a-rho}
\end{equation}
Then $\rho/a=(1-q^{-2})^r\to1$, and
\begin{align}
 x^{2r}\{\EI_r(y)-\EI_r(y+1)\}&=a+o(1),
 &x^{2r}\{\EJ_r(y)-\EJ_r(y+1)\}&=a+o(1),
 \label{eq:scalar-jumps}\\
 \frac{x^{2r}}{\gamma_r}\{\DeltaI_r(y)-\DeltaI_r(y+1)\}&=a+o(1),
 &\frac{x^{2r}}{\gamma_r}\{\DeltaJ_r(y)-\DeltaJ_r(y+1)\}&=a+o(1).
 \label{eq:endpoint-jumps}
\end{align}
Here $\gamma_r\ge\kappa_\gamma7^r/r>0$ once $r\ge7$.

For each of
\begin{equation}
 Q_r\in\left\{\EI_r,\ \EJ_r,\ \DeltaI_r/\gamma_r,\qquad
                         \DeltaJ_r/\gamma_r\right\},
 \label{eq:four-errors}
\end{equation}
the natural two-scale maximum satisfies
\begin{equation}
 \liminf_{y\to\infty}\frac{1+\rho}{a}
 \max\{x^{2r}|Q_r(y)|,q^{2r}|Q_r(y+1)|\}\ge1.
 \label{eq:natural-profile}
\end{equation}
If, in addition to \eqref{eq:linear-rank},
$r/x\to\lambda\in[0,\infty)$, then with
$a_0=e^{-2\lambda h_*}$,
\begin{equation}
 \liminf_y\max\{x^{2r}|Q_r(y)|,q^{2r}|Q_r(y+1)|\}
 \ge\frac{a_0}{1+a_0}.
 \label{eq:lambda-profile}
\end{equation}
Without assuming that $r/x$ converges, the right side may be replaced by
$e^{-1200C}/2$.  In particular, $r=o(x)$ gives the lower bound $1/2$.
Finally,
\begin{equation}
 \frac{\max\{|Q_r(y)|,|Q_r(y+1)|\}}{P_{r+1}(y)}
 \longrightarrow\infty
 \qquad\text{for every $Q_r$ in \eqref{eq:four-errors}.}
 \label{eq:next-rank-divergence}
\end{equation}
\end{theorem}

The optional hypothesis $r/x\to\lambda$ is used only for
\eqref{eq:lambda-profile}.  The jump identities, the natural profile,
and the coarse $C$-bound allow $r/x$ to oscillate.

\section{Uniform edge and endpoint estimates}

Maynard's unconditional Theorem~1.3, printed page~385 (PDF page~3), states
\begin{equation}
 \liminf_{n\to\infty}(p_{n+1}-p_n)\le600
 \label{eq:Maynard}
\end{equation}
for consecutive primes \cite{Maynard2015}.  Hence there are infinitely
many consecutive gaps at most $600$.  Only finitely many positive
integer values can occur there, so one value $h_*\le600$ repeats
infinitely often.  This proves the extraction in \eqref{eq:fixed-edges};
no nonconsecutive prime-pair statement is substituted for it.

The strict endpoint gives the exact successor identity
\begin{equation}
 P_j(y)-P_j(y+1)=(q^2-1)^{-j}.
 \label{eq:successor}
\end{equation}
Using the same rank $r$ at $x$ and $q$ in \eqref{eq:scalar-errors},
\begin{align}
 \EI_r(y)-\EI_r(y+1)
 &=\frac1{(q^2-1)^r}-\int_x^q\frac{t^{-2r}}{\log t}\,dt,
 \label{eq:I-edge}\\
 \EJ_r(y)-\EJ_r(y+1)
 &=\frac1{(q^2-1)^r}-\int_x^q
       \frac{dt}{(t^2-1)^r\log t}.
 \label{eq:J-edge}
\end{align}
The atom becomes exactly $a$ after multiplication by $x^{2r}$.  The
two smooth terms obey
\begin{align}
 0\le x^{2r}\int_x^q\frac{t^{-2r}}{\log t}\,dt
 &\le\frac{h_*}{\log x},
 \label{eq:I-smooth}\\
 0\le x^{2r}\int_x^q\frac{dt}{(t^2-1)^r\log t}
 &\le\frac{h_*}{\log x}(1-x^{-2})^{-r}=o(1).
 \label{eq:J-smooth}
\end{align}
Indeed $r\le Cx$ makes
$r\{-\log(1-x^{-2})\}=O_C(x^{-1})$.  Equations
\eqref{eq:I-edge}--\eqref{eq:J-smooth} prove
\eqref{eq:scalar-jumps} uniformly in the declared rank regime.

The two edge factors are close but not interchangeable:
\begin{equation}
 \frac{\rho}{a}=(1-q^{-2})^r\longrightarrow1,
 \qquad
 \log a=-r\log\left(1+\frac{2h_*}{x}
                       +\frac{h_*^2-1}{x^2}\right).
 \label{eq:edge-factor-identities}
\end{equation}
Thus $r/x\to\lambda$ implies $a,\rho\to e^{-2\lambda h_*}$.
For the general linear regime, $\log(1+u)\le u$ gives
\begin{equation}
 \liminf a\ge e^{-2Ch_*}\ge e^{-1200C}.
 \label{eq:coarse-a}
\end{equation}

The endpoint lift requires estimates uniform in $r$.  They are recorded
next in the form used in the proof.  All bounds are elementary
integer-tail or integral comparisons; their threshold $x_0(C)$ need not
be effective here.

\begin{lemma}[Integer tails and the $J/I$ bridge]
\label{lem:tails}
Fix $C>0$.  For $x\ge x_0(C)$, $r\ge7$, $r\le Cx$, and
$1\le c\le7$,
\begin{align}
 P_{r+1}(y)&\le\frac{4x^{-2r-1}}{2r+1},
 \label{eq:next-P-upper}\\
 \sum_{j>r}\frac{c^jP_j(y)}j
 &\le\frac{4c^{r+1}x^{-2r-1}}{(r+1)(2r+1)},
 \label{eq:P-integer-tail}\\
 \sum_{j>r}\frac{c^jI_{2j}(x)}j
 &\le\frac{2c^{r+1}x^{-2r-1}}
 {(r+1)(2r+1)\log x},
 \label{eq:I-integer-tail}\\
 0\le\sum_{j\ge r}\frac{c^j\{J_j(x)-I_{2j}(x)\}}j
 &\le\frac{4c^rx^{-2r-1}}{(2r+1)\log x}.
 \label{eq:JI-bridge}
\end{align}
Also, if $H_c=\sum_{j<r}c^jP_j(y)/j$, then
\begin{equation}
 \|H\|_\infty\le\frac{14}{x}.
 \label{eq:head-bound}
\end{equation}
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Replace the odd primes beyond $x$ by the larger set of integers.  If
$S_P$ denotes the left side of \eqref{eq:P-integer-tail}, Tonelli and
$1/j\le1/(r+1)$ give
\begin{align}
 S_P
 &\le\frac{c^{r+1}}{r+1}\sum_{n>x}
 \frac{(n^2-1)^{-r-1}}{1-c/(n^2-1)} \notag\\
 &\le\frac{4c^{r+1}x^{-2r-1}}{(r+1)(2r+1)}.
 \label{eq:SP-payment}
\end{align}
Indeed, the geometric denominator is at least $1/2$ eventually, the
summand is decreasing, and
\begin{equation}
 \sum_{n>x}(n^2-1)^{-r-1}
 \le\int_x^\infty(t^2-1)^{-r-1}\,dt
 \le\frac{2x^{-2r-1}}{2r+1}.
 \label{eq:integer-payment}
\end{equation}
The last factor $2$ follows from
$(1-x^{-2})^{-r-1}\le2$ once $x\ge x_0(C)$.  The same comparison without
the geometric sum proves \eqref{eq:next-P-upper}.  For the integral tail,
\begin{equation}
 \sum_{j>r}\frac{c^jI_{2j}(x)}j
 \le\frac{c^{r+1}}{r+1}\int_x^\infty
 \frac{t^{-2r-2}}{\log t\{1-c/t^2\}}\,dt
 \le\frac{2c^{r+1}x^{-2r-1}}
 {(r+1)(2r+1)\log x},
 \label{eq:SI-payment}
\end{equation}
which proves \eqref{eq:I-integer-tail}.

For \eqref{eq:JI-bridge}, the mean-value inequality
\[
 (1-u)^{-j}-1\le ju(1-u)^{-j-1}\qquad(0\le u<1)
\]
cancels the coordinate divisor $j$.  Summing the remaining geometric
series under the integral yields
\begin{align}
 \sum_{j\ge r}\frac{c^j\{J_j-I_{2j}\}}j
 &\le\int_x^\infty\frac{c^r\,dt}
 {(t^2-1)^{r+1}\{1-c/(t^2-1)\}\log t} \notag\\
 &\le\frac{4c^rx^{-2r-1}}{(2r+1)\log x},
 \label{eq:JI-payment}
\end{align}
where the geometric denominator and
$(1-x^{-2})^{-r-1}$ each cost at most $2$.

Finally, positivity permits extension of the head to all ranks, and
the logarithmic series and an exact telescoping sum give
\begin{align}
 H_c
 &\le\sum_{n>x}-\log\left(1-\frac{c}{n^2-1}\right)
 \le2c\sum_{n>x}\frac1{n^2-1} \notag\\
 &=c\left(\frac1x+\frac1{x+1}\right)\le\frac{14}{x}.
 \label{eq:H-payment}
\end{align}
No prime number theorem estimate and no asymptotic formula for $P_r$
enter these comparisons.
\end{proof}

The endpoint coefficient has enough growth to absorb all seven
channels.

\begin{lemma}[Uniform endpoint direction]
\label{lem:gamma}
For every exact integer $r\ge7$,
\begin{equation}
 \gamma_r\ge\kappa_\gamma\frac{7^r}{r}>0.
 \label{eq:gamma-lower}
\end{equation}
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
The exact outward intervals of RH-384 give, by directed rational cross
multiplication,
\begin{equation}
 \frac{u_4}{u_3}>\frac23,
 \qquad \frac{u_6}{u_5}>\left(\frac45\right)^2,
 \qquad \frac{u_8}{u_7}>\left(\frac67\right)^6,
 \label{eq:ratio-locks}
\end{equation}
and $u_3>u_4$, $u_5>u_6$, $u_7>u_8$.  Hence the first two power
differences and the memory term in \eqref{eq:gamma} are positive for
$r\ge7$.  The last ratio in \eqref{eq:ratio-locks} yields
\[
 7^ru_8-6^ru_7\ge\frac17\,7^r\underline u_8.
\]
Substitution into \eqref{eq:gamma} proves \eqref{eq:gamma-lower}.
The endpoint formula is inherited from RH-383, while the exact directed
intervals are those of RH-384 \cite{RH383,RH384}.
\end{proof}

Put
\begin{align*}
 D^I_c(y)&=(\PhiP_c-\PhiI_c)(y),&
 D^J_c(y)&=(\PhiP_c-\PhiJ_c)(y).
\end{align*}
The rank-$r$ term in \eqref{eq:successor}, the smooth bounds
\eqref{eq:I-smooth}--\eqref{eq:J-smooth}, and
Lemma~\ref{lem:tails} give, for $S\in\{I,J\}$,
\begin{equation}
 x^{2r}\{D^S(y)-D^S(y+1)\}=a v_r+\eta^S,
 \qquad
 \frac{r}{7^r}\|\eta^S\|_\infty\longrightarrow0.
 \label{eq:coordinate-jump}
\end{equation}
The $J$ statement uses \eqref{eq:JI-bridge}; it is not inferred from a
fixed-rank limit.

For completeness, the Taylor step can be kept quantitative.  On the
eventual cube $[0,1/2]^7$, the frozen endpoint ledger gives
\begin{equation}
 \sum_{i,j=1}^7|\partial_{ij}F(z)|<224.
 \label{eq:Hessian}
\end{equation}
If $A$ is the actual tail from rank $r$, $B$ is either smooth tail, and
$H$ is their common exact head, then
\begin{align}
 |F(H+A)-F(H+B)-\nabla F(0)\mathbin{\cdot}(A-B)|
 &\le224\|H\|_\infty\|A-B\|_\infty \notag\\
 &\quad+112(\|A\|_\infty^2+\|B\|_\infty^2).
 \label{eq:Taylor}
\end{align}
Direct versions of Lemma~\ref{lem:tails} give
\begin{align}
 \|A\|_\infty&\le
 \frac{4\,7^rx^{1-2r}}{r(2r-1)},
 &\|B\|_\infty&\le
 \frac{2\,7^rx^{1-2r}}{r(2r-1)\log x}
 \label{eq:tail-norms}
\end{align}
for the $I$ tail, with \eqref{eq:JI-bridge} handling the $J$ tail.  After
division by $\gamma_rx^{-2r}$, the cross and square remainders in
\eqref{eq:Taylor} are at most
\begin{equation}
 \frac{18816}{\kappa_\gamma(2r-1)},
 \qquad
 \frac{2240}{\kappa_\gamma}
 \frac{7^rx^{2-2r}}{r(2r-1)^2},
 \label{eq:Taylor-remainders}
\end{equation}
respectively.  Both tend to zero.  Apply \eqref{eq:Taylor} separately at
$x$ and $q$, subtract, and combine \eqref{eq:coordinate-jump} with
$\nabla F(0)\cdot v_r=\gamma_r$.  This proves
\eqref{eq:endpoint-jumps}.

\section{Two-endpoint profile and next-rank separation}

The retention constant is pairwise because an edge jump alone does not
select one endpoint.  Let $Q_r$ be any error in \eqref{eq:four-errors}
and set
\begin{equation}
 L_y=x^{2r}|Q_r(y)|,\qquad
 R_y=q^{2r}|Q_r(y+1)|.
 \label{eq:L-R}
\end{equation}
The corresponding jump formula and the triangle inequality give
\begin{equation}
 a+o(1)\le L_y+\rho R_y
 \le(1+\rho)\max\{L_y,R_y\}.
 \label{eq:pair-inequality}
\end{equation}
Because \eqref{eq:coarse-a} keeps $a$ away from zero, division by $a$
proves \eqref{eq:natural-profile}.  If $r/x\to\lambda$, then
$a,\rho\to a_0=e^{-2\lambda h_*}$, which proves
\eqref{eq:lambda-profile}.  In the general linear regime, $\rho<1$ and
\eqref{eq:coarse-a} give $e^{-1200C}/2$.  Taking $\lambda=0$ gives the
sublinear constant $1/2$.

It remains to compare with the next prime-tail rank.  The coarse pair
bound and $q/x=1+h_*/x$ show that, for some constant depending only on
$C$, at least one raw endpoint error is bounded below by a positive
multiple of $q^{-2r}$.  Lemma~\ref{lem:tails} gives
\[
 P_{r+1}(y)\le\frac{4x^{-2r-1}}{2r+1}.
\]
Moreover $(x/q)^{2r}=\rho$ has the positive lower limit bound inherited
from \eqref{eq:coarse-a}.  The quotient is therefore bounded below by a
positive multiple of $x(2r+1)$ and tends to infinity.  This proves
\eqref{eq:next-rank-divergence}.  The argument uses a one-sided integer
upper bound for $P_{r+1}$, not $P_{r+1}\sim K_{r+1}$.

\section{Relation to prior work and scope}

The exact endpoint normal form and its gradient are supplied by RH-383.
RH-384 supplies the directed $u_m$ intervals and establishes fixed-rank
prime-tail scale separation.  Its fixed-rank asymptotic is background
only: it is not used when $r$ grows linearly with $x$.  RH-388 proved the
rank-one bounded-gap/Taylor prototype, and RH-390 extended endpoint
positivity to all fixed ranks while proving a growing-rank sufficiency
filtration and fixed-rank necessity \cite{RH383,RH384,RH388,RH390}.
The present theorem is the same-rank pair obstruction through the full
linear scale $r=O(x)$, including the exact finite-slope profile.  It is
not obtained by inserting a moving rank into a fixed-rank asymptotic.

The quantifiers are part of the claim.  The rank is identical at the two
consecutive-prime endpoints and tends to infinity.  Necessity is for a
pair maximum in the frozen $P/J/I$ hierarchy.  No arbitrary
single-vertex rank schedule, arbitrary surrogate, or regime
$r/x\to\infty$ is covered.  The theorem has no dependency on RH-389,
TPC-137, or the Tao active-log source chain.  It makes no claim about
ordinary Ces\`aro limits, growing clocks, complex channels, operators,
traces, zeta zeros, or the Riemann hypothesis.  Gates A--E remain false.
