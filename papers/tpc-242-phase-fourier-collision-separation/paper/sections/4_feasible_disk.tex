\section{The sharp feasible disk and phase defect}\label{sec:disk}

The trivial mode controls the selected mode only through a sharp disk.

\begin{theorem}[Exact fixed-energy feasible set]\label{thm:disk}
Let $\Hc$ be nonzero and fix $S\geq0$.  Over all pairs $X,Y\in\Hc$ satisfying
$\norm X^2+\norm Y^2=S$, the set of possible values of
$F_1=\ip YX$ is exactly
\begin{equation}\label{eq:disk}
 \left\{z\in\C:\abs z\leq\frac S2\right\}.
\end{equation}
For $S=0$, this disk is the singleton $\{0\}$.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Cauchy--Schwarz followed by the arithmetic--geometric mean inequality gives
\[
 \abs{\ip YX}\leq\norm X\norm Y
 \leq\frac{\norm X^2+\norm Y^2}{2}=\frac S2.
\]
This proves one inclusion.

For the converse, suppose first that $S>0$ and choose any $z$ with
$\rho:=\abs z\leq S/2$.  Put
\[
 D=\sqrt{S^2-4\rho^2},\qquad
 a=\frac{S+D}{2},\qquad b=\frac{S-D}{2}.
\]
Then $a>0$, $a+b=S$, and $ab=\rho^2$.  Choose a unit vector $e\in\Hc$ and
define
\begin{equation}\label{eq:disk-witness}
 X=\sqrt a\,e,\qquad
 Y=\frac{\overline z}{\sqrt a}\,e.
\end{equation}
Consequently,
\[
 \norm X^2=a,\qquad
 \norm Y^2=\frac{\abs z^2}{a}=b,
 \qquad
 \ip YX=z.
\]
Thus every point in~\eqref{eq:disk} is attained.  If $S=0$, both nonnegative
squared norms vanish, so $X=Y=0$ and $F_1=0$.
\end{proof}

The construction realizes the center, every interior point, and every
boundary phase.  Consequently even an exact value of $F_0$ gives no positive
lower bound for $\abs{F_1}$ and no preferred argument of $F_1$.  For instance,
at $S=2$, orthogonal unit vectors give $F_1=0$, whereas $X=(1,0)$ and
$Y=(-\ii,0)$ give $F_1=\ii$ on the boundary.

The distance from the boundary has a canonical two-part decomposition.

\begin{theorem}[Exact phase-defect identity]\label{thm:defect}
Let $S=\norm X^2+\norm Y^2$ and $F_1=\ip YX$.  Then
\begin{equation}\label{eq:defect}
 S^2-4\abs{F_1}^2
 =\bigl(\norm X^2-\norm Y^2\bigr)^2
  +4\left(\norm X^2\norm Y^2-\abs{\ip YX}^2\right).
\end{equation}
Both terms on the right are nonnegative.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Set $a=\norm X^2$ and $b=\norm Y^2$.  Expanding and inserting $4ab$ gives
\[
 (a+b)^2-4\abs{\ip YX}^2
 =(a-b)^2+4\left(ab-\abs{\ip YX}^2\right).
\]
The final parenthesis is the determinant of the Gram matrix of $X$ and $Y$,
so Cauchy--Schwarz makes it nonnegative.
\end{proof}

Equality $\abs{F_1}=S/2$ holds precisely when the norms agree and the Gram
determinant vanishes.  Away from the boundary, equation~\eqref{eq:defect}
distinguishes two mechanisms: imbalance between packet energies and angular
decorrelation between the packet vectors.  Any future strict signed saving
must create a quantitative defect of one of these forms on the attached
physical object.
