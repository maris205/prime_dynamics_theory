\begin{abstract}
The preceding physical Gram experiments found a late-shell regime with
strong positive cross-prime coherence and an isolated early sign-flip
regime.  This paper asks what the corresponding two-component cancellation
directions actually cost.  For two nonzero physical prime-component vectors
$g_i,g_j$, with $d_k=\norm{g_k}^2$ and
$G_{ij}=\ip{g_i}{g_j}$, we prove the exact projection identity
\[
 \min_{\rho\in\RR}\frac{\norm{g_i-\rho g_j}^2}{d_i}
 =1-\Gamma_{ij},\qquad
 \Gamma_{ij}=\frac{G_{ij}^2}{d_i d_j},
\]
and the normalized signed two-vector Rayleigh minimum
$1-\sqrt{\Gamma_{ij}}$.  The sign of the optimal projection coefficient
then gives an explicit distinction between opposite-sign cancellation for a
positive cross term and same-sign cancellation for a negative one.  An
exact-rational replay of the 18-row TPC-289 grid evaluates all 1,380 pairs:
1,377 are positive and 3 are negative, while 1,074, 852, and 477 pairs have
Schur residual at most $1/2$, $1/4$, and $1/10$.  The best tested pair has
$\Gamma\approx0.984876$ and residual $\approx0.015124$.  These results
quantify a genuine pairwise cancellation mechanism, but do not establish a
multi-prime signed null direction, an arithmetic $L^2$ estimate, or a
twin-prime theorem.
\end{abstract}

\section{Position on the twin-prime route}

This release stays inside the same literal prime-shell dynamical family.
TPC-287 separated the physical deleted-diagonal output into prime
components, and TPC-288 showed that retaining those full output vectors
produces a positive-semidefinite source-output Gram object whose finite
energy can increase even when a scalar readout is small
\cite{tpc287,tpc288}.  TPC-289 made the cross terms explicit: most tested
pairs were positive and a late block had large normalized coherence, but
three negative pairs appeared at the early crossover row
\cite{tpc289}.  TPC-290 then proved that diffuse nonnegative shell weights
cannot cross an all-positive coherence wall \cite{tpc290}.

The next natural question is therefore narrower than ``find a good weight'':
when a pair is coherent, what is the exact best two-prime cancellation, and
does its coefficient sign agree with the available shell mechanism?  The
answer is a clean local compiler.  It also identifies the next bridge
problem: assemble many such pair directions while preserving the literal
prime-source arithmetic and the required normalization.

\section{Frozen physical model}

Let $I=I_N$ be the finite integer interval and let $\beta$ be the frozen
rational source vector inherited from the parent certificate.  For an odd
prime $q$, height $H$, and kernel exponent $s$, write
\[
 K_{H,s}(h)=\frac{H^{2s}}{(H^2+h^2)^s},\qquad
 B_q(u,t)=\mathbf 1_{q\nmid u}\mathbf 1_{q\nmid t}
 \left(\mathbf 1_{u\equiv t\pmod q}-\frac1{q-1}\right).
\]
The physical deleted-diagonal component is the vector $g_q=A_q\beta$, where
\[
 (A_q)_{u,t}=qK_{H,s}(u-t)\mathbf 1_{u\ne t}B_q(u,t).
 \label{eq:model}
\]
For a shell $S$ of primes, define
\[
 d_q=\norm{g_q}^2,\qquad G_{q,r}=\ip{g_q}{g_r},
 \qquad \Gamma_{q,r}=\frac{G_{q,r}^2}{d_qd_r}.
 \label{eq:gram}
\]
The certificate verifies $d_q>0$ on every declared row.  By
Cauchy--Schwarz, $0\leq\Gamma_{q,r}\leq1$; the square deliberately keeps
the magnitude of coherence separate from its sign.

\section{Exact two-prime cancellation compiler}

We orient a pair as ``target minus coefficient times reference''.  Thus the
quantity below is $\norm{g_i-\rho g_j}^2$, with $j$ the reference component.

\begin{lemma}[Schur projection identity]
For nonzero real vectors $g_i,g_j$, the unique minimizer of
$\norm{g_i-\rho g_j}^2$ is
\[
 \rho_* = \frac{G_{ij}}{d_j},
 \]
and
\begin{equation}
 \min_{\rho\in\RR}\frac{\norm{g_i-\rho g_j}^2}{d_i}
 =1-\frac{G_{ij}^2}{d_i d_j}=1-\Gamma_{ij}.
 \label{eq:schur}
\end{equation}
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Expanding the square gives
\[
 \norm{g_i-\rho g_j}^2=d_i-2\rho G_{ij}+\rho^2d_j.
\]
Since $d_j>0$, completing the square gives the unique minimizer
$\rho_*=G_{ij}/d_j$.  Substitution yields
$d_i-G_{ij}^2/d_j=d_i(1-\Gamma_{ij})$.
\end{proof}

\begin{proposition}[Signed two-vector Rayleigh minimum]
For real $(a,b)\ne(0,0)$,
\begin{equation}
 \inf_{(a,b)\ne(0,0)}
 \frac{\norm{a g_i+b g_j}^2}{a^2d_i+b^2d_j}
 =1-\sqrt{\Gamma_{ij}}.
 \label{eq:rayleigh}
\end{equation}
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Conjugating the two-by-two Gram matrix by
$\operatorname{diag}(d_i^{-1/2},d_j^{-1/2})$ gives
\[
 \begin{pmatrix}1&c\\c&1\end{pmatrix},
 \qquad c=\frac{G_{ij}}{\sqrt{d_id_j}}.
\]
Its eigenvalues are $1+c$ and $1-c$, so the smaller one is
$1-|c|=1-\sqrt{\Gamma_{ij}}$.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[Sign cost]
In the orientation of Lemma~1, $\operatorname{sign}(\rho_*)=
\operatorname{sign}(G_{ij})$.  A positive cross term therefore uses
opposite signs in $g_i-\rho_*g_j$, whereas a negative cross term uses
same-sign coefficients after the subtraction is expanded.
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
The denominator $d_j$ is positive.  The statement follows directly from
the formula for $\rho_*$.  Equivalently, the lower eigenvector in
\eqref{eq:rayleigh} is $(1,-1)$ when $c>0$ and $(1,1)$ when $c<0$.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}
Equation~\eqref{eq:schur} is a two-dimensional statement.  It does not say
that the individually optimal directions for different pairs are compatible,
nor that their coefficients are produced by the literal prime source.  This
compatibility distinction is the central firewall of the present paper.
\end{remark}

\section{Finite protocol}

The certificate replays eight $s=2$ growth anchors, four $s=1$ crossover
rows, and six $s=2$ source-control rows, for 18 rows in total.  Each row
uses the same interval, rational source, kernel, deleted diagonal, and prime
shell as TPC-289.  Every unordered pair in every shell is evaluated with
exact rational arithmetic.  The producer records the cross term, its sign,
$\Gamma$, the exact residual $1-\Gamma$, the projection coefficient, and the
sign-cost label.  The independent checker reconstructs the physical outputs
in reverse column-first order.  A ten-mutation hostile audit tests theorem
fields, threshold counts, sign labels, row deletion, and provenance locks.

The finite thresholds are $1/2$, $1/4$, and $1/10$ for the residual, and
$9/25$ and $3/4$ for squared coherence.  These are audit thresholds, not
claimed universal constants.  The parent TPC-290 code, result, and frozen
TPC-268 engine are SHA-256 locked by the producer.

\subsection{The exceptional sign-flip row}

The only negative pairs occur at the crossover row
$(N,H,Q,z,s)=(256,38,27,5,1)$.  They are
\[
 (29,53),\qquad(31,53),\qquad(41,53).
\]
For equal two-prime support, the physical ratio is
\[
 \frac{\norm{g_i+g_j}^2}{d_i+d_j}
 =1+\frac{2G_{ij}}{d_i+d_j}.
\]
Consequently these three negative pairs are precisely the finite
same-sign subunit witnesses seen by TPC-290.  They do not occur in the
late positive block, where the low-residual directions require signed
subtraction.  The apparent ``escape'' is therefore sparse and localized;
it does not yet provide a diffuse or growing-shell mechanism.

\section{Interpretation and claim firewall}

The results make the local geometry of the map more precise:

\begin{enumerate}
\item High cross-prime coherence converts exactly into a small two-prime
      Schur residual.  This is a reusable analytic bridge from the Gram
      phase diagram to a cancellation direction.
\item The sign of the cross term is not cosmetic.  In the dominant positive
      block, cancellation requires signed coefficients; the three same-sign
      opportunities are confined to one early row.
\item Pairwise optimality does not compose automatically.  A multi-prime
      construction must control the compatibility of projection directions,
      coefficient signs, and the literal source normalization.
\end{enumerate}

Accordingly, the strongest exact claim is the two-prime projection and
Rayleigh compiler.  The strongest finite numerical claim is the complete
1,380-pair coherence-to-cancellation atlas.  The following remain open:
multi-prime signed reassembly, a growing-shell theorem, a source-native
arithmetic $L^2$ estimate, fixed-power credit, and the full Gate-B bridge.
No Route-A or Route-B evaluator pass is claimed here because the
Session-named evaluator files are absent from this checkout; the local
proof, exact certificate, independent replay, stress audit, and Bridge-B
checker are the fail-closed validation package.

\section{Reproducibility and conclusion}

From the project directory, the producer and independent replay can be run
with:
\begin{verbatim}
export PYTHONDONTWRITEBYTECODE=1
python -B code/tpc291_signed_schur_cancellation_certificate.py --check
python -B experiments/tpc291_independent_checker.py
python -B experiments/tpc291_schur_stress.py
\end{verbatim}
The canonical result is
\texttt{results/tpc291\_certificate.json}; normal and optimized runs are
required to have empty standard error and byte-identical output.

TPC-291 closes one local uncertainty on the broad twin-prime map.  It shows
that the late coherent island is not missing a two-prime cancellation
formula; the formula is exact.  The unresolved bridge is higher-dimensional:
whether compatible signed directions can be assembled from the actual
prime-shell source while paying the physical $L^2$ and arithmetic budgets.
That is the next meaningful question, rather than another unstructured
weight scan.
