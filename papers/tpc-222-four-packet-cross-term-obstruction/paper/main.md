Thus PSD and trace information alone cannot certify signed reassembly. The result is a structural obstruction and a precise specification of the missing polarized cross-correlation theorem; no arithmetic $L^2$ advance is claimed.

# Four packet interface

Let $V_0,V_1,V_2,V_3$ be vectors in a complex Hilbert space. We use the convention $$\langle x,y\rangle=\sum_k\overline{x_k}y_k,
 \qquad
 \mathsf G_{j\ell}=\langle V_j,V_\ell\rangle.
 \tag{1}$$ The packet index is the four-sign interface inherited from the prime-shell lift. For a coefficient vector $c=(c_0,c_1,c_2,c_3)$, define the reassembled vector $$V(c)=\sum_{j=0}^3c_jV_j.$$ The desired signed estimate concerns $\|V(c)\|^2$, not just the four diagonal masses $\|V_j\|^2$.

> **Theorem: PSD packet Gram** The matrix $\mathsf G$ is Hermitian positive semidefinite and $$\|V(c)\|^2=c^*\mathsf Gc\geq0.
>  \tag{2}$$

> **Proof** Hermitian symmetry follows from conjugate symmetry of the inner product. Expanding the norm of $V(c)$ gives (2), and positivity is immediate from the norm square.

The diagonal of $\mathsf G$ records the four individual packet energies, while the off-diagonal entries are the signed cross-terms. TPC-221’s Schur result controls an absolute collision envelope, but does not determine these phases.

# Exact four-point polarization

> **Theorem: Four-point polarization** For all vectors $x,y$, $$\langle x,y\rangle
>  =\frac14\sum_{r=0}^3 i^{-r}\,\|x+i^r y\|^2.
>  \tag{3}$$

> **Proof** Expand $$\|x+i^r y\|^2=\|x\|^2+\|y\|^2
>  +i^r\langle x,y\rangle+i^{-r}\langle y,x\rangle.$$ Multiplication by $i^{-r}$ and summation over $r=0,1,2,3$ kills the diagonal terms and the $\langle y,x\rangle$ term, because $\sum_r i^{-r}=\sum_r i^{-2r}=0$. The remaining term is $4\langle x,y\rangle$.

Identity (3) is an exact compiler, but it carries four phase-labelled energies. Replacing those energies by one unsigned majorant discards precisely the information needed for the cross-term.

> **Corollary: Trace envelope** For every coefficient vector $c$, $$0\leq c^*\mathsf Gc\leq \operatorname{tr}(\mathsf G)\,\|c\|_2^2.
>  \tag{4}$$ The constant is sharp.

> **Proof** The eigenvalues of a PSD matrix are nonnegative and their maximum is at most their sum. Apply the Rayleigh quotient bound to (2). A rank-one Gram with one nonzero eigenvalue attains equality.

# Same trace, different signed reassembly

> **Theorem: Scoped cross-term obstruction** Let $u=(1,0)$ and define two four-packet families by $$V_j^+=u,\qquad V_j^-=(-1)^j u,\qquad 0\leq j\leq3.$$ Their Gram matrices are PSD and have the same diagonal $(1,1,1,1)$ and trace $4$. For $c=(1,1,1,1)$, however, $$\|V^+(c)\|^2=16,\qquad \|V^-(c)\|^2=0.
>  \tag{5}$$

> **Proof** Both families are rank one, so their Gram matrices are respectively $J_4$ and $s s^*$ with $s=(1,-1,1,-1)$. Each diagonal entry is one. The plus sum is $4u$, while the minus sum is zero, proving (5). Formula (3) holds exactly for every pair in both families, so the difference is genuine signed phase information rather than an algebraic inconsistency.

The plus fixture saturates (4): $\operatorname{tr}(\mathsf G)\|c\|^2=4\cdot4=16$. The minus fixture has the same trace but complete cancellation. Therefore no theorem whose inputs are only packet diagonals, trace, or an unsigned PSD envelope can identify the signed reassembly. This is a finite scoped obstruction; it is not an assertion that these two abstract phase patterns are both generated by every growing literal prime shell.
