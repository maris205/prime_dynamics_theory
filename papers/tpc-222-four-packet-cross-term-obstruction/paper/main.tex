\begin{abstract}
The prime-shell route carries four packet coordinates whose signed reassembly is not
determined by their separate energies.  We isolate this interface in exact Hilbert-space
form.  The four-packet Gram is positive semidefinite, its coefficient energy is a Rayleigh
quotient, and a four-point polarization identity reconstructs every cross-term from four
phase-labelled energies.  A trace bound gives a sharp universal envelope.  We then give two
rank-one packet fixtures with identical diagonal and trace data but target signed energies
$16$ and $0$.  Thus PSD and trace information alone cannot certify signed reassembly.  The
result is a structural obstruction and a precise specification of the missing polarized
cross-correlation theorem; no arithmetic $L^2$ advance is claimed.
\end{abstract}

\section{Four packet interface}

Let $V_0,V_1,V_2,V_3$ be vectors in a complex Hilbert space.  We use the convention
\[
 \langle x,y\rangle=\sum_k\overline{x_k}y_k,
 \qquad
 \G_{j\ell}=\langle V_j,V_\ell\rangle.
 \tag{1}
\]
The packet index is the four-sign interface inherited from the prime-shell lift.  For a
coefficient vector $c=(c_0,c_1,c_2,c_3)$, define the reassembled vector
\[
 V(c)=\sum_{j=0}^3c_jV_j.
\]
The desired signed estimate concerns $\|V(c)\|^2$, not just the four diagonal masses
$\|V_j\|^2$.

\begin{theorem}[PSD packet Gram]
The matrix $\G$ is Hermitian positive semidefinite and
\[
 \|V(c)\|^2=c^*\G c\geq0.
 \tag{2}
\]
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Hermitian symmetry follows from conjugate symmetry of the inner product.  Expanding the
norm of $V(c)$ gives (2), and positivity is immediate from the norm square.
\end{proof}

The diagonal of $\G$ records the four individual packet energies, while the off-diagonal
entries are the signed cross-terms.  TPC-221's Schur result controls an absolute collision
envelope, but does not determine these phases.

\section{Exact four-point polarization}

\begin{theorem}[Four-point polarization]
For all vectors $x,y$,
\[
 \langle x,y\rangle
 =\frac14\sum_{r=0}^3 i^{-r}\,\|x+i^r y\|^2.
 \tag{3}
\]
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Expand
\[
 \|x+i^r y\|^2=\|x\|^2+\|y\|^2
 +i^r\langle x,y\rangle+i^{-r}\langle y,x\rangle.
\]
Multiplication by $i^{-r}$ and summation over $r=0,1,2,3$ kills the diagonal terms and
the $\langle y,x\rangle$ term, because $\sum_r i^{-r}=\sum_r i^{-2r}=0$.  The remaining
term is $4\langle x,y\rangle$.
\end{proof}

Identity (3) is an exact compiler, but it carries four phase-labelled energies.  Replacing
those energies by one unsigned majorant discards precisely the information needed for the
cross-term.

\begin{corollary}[Trace envelope]
For every coefficient vector $c$,
\[
 0\leq c^*\G c\leq \operatorname{tr}(\G)\,\|c\|_2^2.
 \tag{4}
\]
The constant is sharp.
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
The eigenvalues of a PSD matrix are nonnegative and their maximum is at most their sum.
Apply the Rayleigh quotient bound to (2).  A rank-one Gram with one nonzero eigenvalue
attains equality.
\end{proof}

\section{Same trace, different signed reassembly}

\begin{theorem}[Scoped cross-term obstruction]
Let $u=(1,0)$ and define two four-packet families by
\[
 V_j^+=u,\qquad V_j^-=(-1)^j u,\qquad 0\leq j\leq3.
\]
Their Gram matrices are PSD and have the same diagonal $(1,1,1,1)$ and trace $4$.  For
$c=(1,1,1,1)$, however,
\[
 \|V^+(c)\|^2=16,\qquad \|V^-(c)\|^2=0.
 \tag{5}
\]
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Both families are rank one, so their Gram matrices are respectively
$J_4$ and $s s^*$ with $s=(1,-1,1,-1)$.  Each diagonal entry is one.  The plus sum is
$4u$, while the minus sum is zero, proving (5).  Formula (3) holds exactly for every
pair in both families, so the difference is genuine signed phase information rather than
an algebraic inconsistency.
\end{proof}

The plus fixture saturates (4): $\operatorname{tr}(\G)\|c\|^2=4\cdot4=16$.  The minus
fixture has the same trace but complete cancellation.  Therefore no theorem whose inputs
are only packet diagonals, trace, or an unsigned PSD envelope can identify the signed
reassembly.  This is a finite scoped obstruction; it is not an assertion that these two
abstract phase patterns are both generated by every growing literal prime shell.
