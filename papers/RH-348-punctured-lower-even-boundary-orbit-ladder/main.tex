\begin{abstract}
After removing the selected orders $2k$ and $2k-2$, the one-alias prefix
still contains a complete ladder of physical boundary orbits at
$n=2m$, $m_\star\le m\le k-2$.  We extract all of them simultaneously.
Writing
\[
 G_m=\frac{r_H^{-2m}}{1+|M_m|},\qquad
 F_m^{\rm orb}=2mG_m,
\]
the exact direct coefficient is
\[
 p_{\sigma,k,2m}=Y_{k,m}+\cP_{\sigma,2m}-S_{k,m},
 \qquad
 S_{k,m}=F_m^{\rm orb}+\cA_{k,2m}.
\]
Let $x=(\beta R)^2>1$.  The weighted complete-orbit ladder obeys
\[
 \cL_k^{\rm orb}
 :=\sum_{m=m_\star}^{k-2}G_mR^{2m}
 =\frac{x^{k-1}}{C_M(x-1)}\{1+o(1)\}.
\]
The absolute radial correction over the whole ladder is only
$O(k^{-1})\cL_k^{\rm orb}$, without any sign assumption.  Consequently the
absolute combined deterministic demand has mass
$\cL_k^{\rm orb}\{1+o(1)\}\to\infty$.

An exact reverse-triangle argument then shows that vanishing of the direct
punctured lower-even subprefix would force the actual signed
remainder-plus-parity supply to carry asymptotically at least this full
divergent mass.  The repository has no moving-order estimate for that supply,
so neither closure nor nonclosure follows.  Odd orders, upper off-alias
orders, determinant gluing, and Gates A--E remain open.  No
Riemann-hypothesis conclusion is made.
\end{abstract}

\section{The punctured lower-even coefficient family}

Work on the same physical clock
\begin{equation}\label{eq:clock}
 k=\frac{\log(1/\sigma)}{2\log\lambda}+O(1),
 \qquad R=\frac75,
\end{equation}
and choose once and for all an integer $m_\star$ beyond the eventual
multiplier-sign threshold in RH-17.  For sufficiently large $k$, put
\begin{equation}\label{eq:index}
 I_k=\{m_\star,m_\star+1,\ldots,k-2\}.
\end{equation}
Every $m\in I_k$ gives an even order $n=2m<2k-2$.  Thus these orders exclude
the critical order $2k$ and the first lower sideband $2k-2$, and they belong
to every strict one-alias prefix $2k<h_\sigma\le4k$
\citep{WangSynchronizedPrefix2026,WangLowerBalance2026}.

For each $m\in I_k$, let $p_{2m}$ be the primitive period-$2m$ boundary
point, and define
\begin{equation}\label{eq:orbit}
 \Gamma_m=\{|f^j(p_{2m})|:0\le j<2m\},
 \qquad
 M_m=(f^{2m})'(p_{2m}).
\end{equation}
RH-17 gives $2m$ distinct folded marked points and
\begin{equation}\label{eq:multiplier}
 M_m=-C_M\lambda^m\{1+o(1)\},
 \qquad C_M>0
\end{equation}
\citep{WangBoundaryMonodromy2026}.  Hence, after increasing $m_\star$ if
needed,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:FG}
 G_m=\frac{r_H^{-2m}}{1+|M_m|}>0,
 \qquad F_m^{\rm orb}=2mG_m.
\end{equation}

The all-order physical observation identity and deterministic numerator
anchor are already fixed in RH-326 and RH-334
\citep{WangFirstAlias2026,WangObservation2026}.  At every non-alias even
order $2m<2k$, the radial counterloop term is exactly
\begin{equation}\label{eq:radial}
 \cA_{k,2m}=2(\beta^{2m}-\beta_k^{2m}),
 \qquad
 \beta_k=\frac{|M_k|^{-1/(2k)}}{r_H},
 \qquad
 \beta=\frac1{r_H\sqrt\lambda}.
\end{equation}
No sign for \eqref{eq:radial} is assumed.

Remove the finite set $\Gamma_m$ from the raw trace partition at order
$2m$, and call the resulting quantity $\cT_{k,m}^{\rm rest}$.  A finite set
has zero localized noisy trace, while each deterministic marked point has
weight $(1+|M_m|)^{-1}$.

\begin{theorem}[Simultaneous complete-orbit ladder]\label{thm:ladder}
For every sufficiently large $k$ and every $m\in I_k$,
\begin{align}
 \cT_{\sigma,2m}
 &=\cT_{k,m}^{\rm rest}-F_m^{\rm orb},
 \label{eq:raw}\\
 q_{\sigma,k,2m}
 &=\cT_{k,m}^{\rm rest}+\cP_{\sigma,2m}
   -\cA_{k,2m}-F_m^{\rm orb},
 \label{eq:q}\\
 p_{\sigma,k,2m}
 &=Y_{k,m}+\cP_{\sigma,2m}-S_{k,m},
 \label{eq:p}
\end{align}
where
\begin{equation}\label{eq:YS}
 Y_{k,m}=\cT_{k,m}^{\rm rest}-d_{\sigma,k,2m},
 \qquad
 S_{k,m}=F_m^{\rm orb}+\cA_{k,2m}.
\end{equation}
The coefficient $q$ is the Hardy full-trace constituent, whereas $p=q-d$
is the direct coefficient.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
The proof of the complete extraction in RH-346 depends only on the
period-$2m$ orbit, the folding bijection, and the zero noisy trace of a finite
set, not on the special relation $m=k-1$
\citep{WangCompleteLower2026}.  It therefore gives \eqref{eq:raw} for every
$m\in I_k$.  Substitute \eqref{eq:raw} into the all-order five-slot identity
to obtain \eqref{eq:q}; then use $p=q-d$ and \eqref{eq:YS}.
\end{proof}

This is a simultaneous coefficient family, not a sum of orbit weights taken
from different data types.  The same physical $k$ and the same deterministic
numerator anchor are retained at every order.

\section{Exact weighted orbit ladder}

Give each coefficient its prefix weight
\begin{equation}\label{eq:weight}
 w_m=\frac{R^{2m}}{2m}.
\end{equation}
By \eqref{eq:FG}, the complete atom has the exact weighted identity
\begin{equation}\label{eq:weighted-identity}
 F_m^{\rm orb}w_m=G_mR^{2m}.
\end{equation}
Define
\begin{equation}\label{eq:L-orb}
 \cL_k^{\rm orb}
 =\sum_{m\in I_k}F_m^{\rm orb}w_m
 =\sum_{m=m_\star}^{k-2}G_mR^{2m},
 \qquad x=(\beta R)^2.
\end{equation}
The strict inequality $x>1$ is source-certified by RH-336
\citep{WangProjectorMass2026}.

\begin{theorem}[Sharp punctured orbit-ladder mass]\label{thm:orbit-mass}
As $k\to\infty$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:orbit-asymptotic}
 \boxed{
 \cL_k^{\rm orb}
 =\frac{x^{k-1}}{C_M(x-1)}\{1+o(1)\}.}
\end{equation}
In particular, $\cL_k^{\rm orb}\to\infty$ exponentially.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Equations \eqref{eq:multiplier} and \eqref{eq:FG} give
\[
 G_mR^{2m}
 =\frac1{C_M}(\beta R)^{2m}\{1+o(1)\}
 =\frac1{C_M}x^m\{1+o(1)\}.
\]
For a sequence asymptotic to $C_M^{-1}x^m$ with $x>1$, summing from a fixed
lower endpoint through $k-2$ preserves the geometric-tail asymptotic.  More
explicitly, split the sum at a fixed large $M$, bound the relative error by
an arbitrary $\varepsilon$ on $m\ge M$, and note that the finite initial sum
is $o(x^{k-1})$.  Since
\[
 \sum_{m=M}^{k-2}x^m
 =\frac{x^{k-1}-x^M}{x-1},
\]
letting $\varepsilon\downarrow0$ proves \eqref{eq:orbit-asymptotic}.
\end{proof}

Thus the two previously selected orders do not exhaust the deterministic
boundary-orbit demand.  Even after deleting them, the remaining lower-even
orbit ladder has an explicit divergent weighted mass.

\section{The radial ladder is aggregate lower order}

The finite counterloop radius satisfies
\begin{equation}\label{eq:beta-expansion}
 \beta_k
 =\beta\exp\left[-\frac{\log C_M}{2k}+o(k^{-1})\right].
\end{equation}
Set $\varepsilon_k=\log(\beta_k/\beta)$.  Then
$|\varepsilon_k|\le C/k$ for all large $k$.  Uniformly for $m\le k$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:exp-bound}
 |e^{2m\varepsilon_k}-1|
 \le 2m|\varepsilon_k|e^{2m|\varepsilon_k|}
 \le C_1\frac{m}{k}.
\end{equation}
Using \eqref{eq:radial}, define the absolute radial mass
\begin{equation}\label{eq:Lrad}
 \cL_k^{\rm rad,abs}
 =\sum_{m\in I_k}|\cA_{k,2m}|w_m.
\end{equation}

\begin{theorem}[Aggregate radial suppression]\label{thm:radial}
There is a constant $C_2$ such that
\begin{equation}\label{eq:radial-bound}
 \cL_k^{\rm rad,abs}
 \le \frac{C_2}{k}\sum_{m=m_\star}^{k-2}x^m
 =O(k^{-1})\cL_k^{\rm orb}.
\end{equation}
Consequently, with
\begin{equation}\label{eq:demand-masses}
 \cL_k^{S}=\sum_{m\in I_k}S_{k,m}w_m,
 \qquad
 \cL_k^{S,\rm abs}=\sum_{m\in I_k}|S_{k,m}|w_m,
\end{equation}
one has
\begin{equation}\label{eq:demand-asymptotic}
 \cL_k^{S}=\cL_k^{\rm orb}\{1+O(k^{-1})\},
 \qquad
 \boxed{\cL_k^{S,\rm abs}=\cL_k^{\rm orb}\{1+O(k^{-1})\}.}
\end{equation}
In particular, both are eventually positive and diverge with the asymptotic
in \eqref{eq:orbit-asymptotic}.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Equations \eqref{eq:radial} and \eqref{eq:exp-bound} imply
\[
 |\cA_{k,2m}|
 =2\beta^{2m}|1-e^{2m\varepsilon_k}|
 \le C_3\frac{m}{k}\beta^{2m}.
\]
After multiplication by $w_m$, this is at most $C_4x^m/k$.  Summation and
\cref{thm:orbit-mass} give \eqref{eq:radial-bound}.  Finally,
\[
 |\cL_k^S-\cL_k^{\rm orb}|
 \le\cL_k^{\rm rad,abs},
 \qquad
 \bigl|\cL_k^{S,\rm abs}-\cL_k^{\rm orb}\bigr|
 \le\cL_k^{\rm rad,abs},
\]
which proves \eqref{eq:demand-asymptotic}.
\end{proof}

The theorem uses an absolute estimate only to compare two deterministic
demand ledgers.  It does not use separate absolute bounds to decide the
signed physical residual.

\section{Necessary aggregate compensation}

For the direct coefficient, collect the actual signed supply as
\begin{equation}\label{eq:Z}
 Z_{k,m}=Y_{k,m}+\cP_{\sigma,2m}.
\end{equation}
Then \eqref{eq:p} reads $p_{\sigma,k,2m}=Z_{k,m}-S_{k,m}$.  Define the
punctured lower-even residual and supply masses
\begin{align}
 \cE_k^{\rm low}
 &=\sum_{m\in I_k}|p_{\sigma,k,2m}|w_m,
 \label{eq:E-low}\\
 \cC_k^{\rm low}
 &=\sum_{m\in I_k}|Z_{k,m}|w_m.
 \label{eq:C-low}
\end{align}
Both are formed in the direct coefficient data type on the common physical
clock.

\begin{theorem}[Punctured ladder compensation demand]\label{thm:compensation}
For every sufficiently large $k$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:reverse-triangle}
 \boxed{
 \cC_k^{\rm low}+\cE_k^{\rm low}
 \ge \cL_k^{S,\rm abs}.}
\end{equation}
Consequently, if
\begin{equation}\label{eq:subprefix-close}
 \cE_k^{\rm low}\longrightarrow0,
\end{equation}
then
\begin{equation}\label{eq:supply-liminf}
 \liminf_{k\to\infty}
 \frac{\cC_k^{\rm low}}{\cL_k^{\rm orb}}\ge1,
 \qquad
 \cC_k^{\rm low}\longrightarrow\infty.
\end{equation}
Thus vanishing of the punctured lower-even direct subprefix requires actual
signed supply with the full leading orbit-ladder mass.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
For each $m\in I_k$, the reverse triangle inequality gives
\[
 |Z_{k,m}|+|Z_{k,m}-S_{k,m}|\ge|S_{k,m}|.
\]
Multiply by $w_m$ and sum to obtain \eqref{eq:reverse-triangle}.  If
\eqref{eq:subprefix-close} holds, then
$\cE_k^{\rm low}/\cL_k^{\rm orb}\to0$ because
$\cL_k^{\rm orb}\to\infty$.  Divide \eqref{eq:reverse-triangle} by
$\cL_k^{\rm orb}$ and use \eqref{eq:demand-asymptotic}.
\end{proof}

The full-trace analogue omits $d_{\sigma,k,2m}$ from $Y_{k,m}$ and is proved
identically.  Neither version supplies an upper bound for the actual signed
supply.  A divergent deterministic demand may, in principle, be cancelled
coefficient by coefficient.

\begin{remark}[Why this does not close $E_{\rm off}$]
The lower-even ladder is only one subfamily of the punctured prefix.  Odd
orders and orders above the first alias are absent.  More importantly,
\cref{thm:compensation} is a necessary mass law, not a proof that the actual
$Z_{k,m}$ fails to meet it.  The repository contains no moving-order signed
theorem for $\cT_{k,m}^{\rm rest}$, $d_{\sigma,k,2m}$, and
$\cP_{\sigma,2m}$ jointly.  Hence both lower-even closure and lower-even
nonclosure remain open, as does the full $E_{\rm off}$ aggregate.
\end{remark}

\section{Executable protocol and next route}

The artifact reconstructs the complete boundary multipliers and exact
finite ladder at $k=8,12,16,20$.  It checks the exact identity
$F_m^{\rm orb}w_m=G_mR^{2m}$, the geometric orbit reference, the absolute
radial and combined-demand ratios, and a rational reverse-triangle fixture.
All finite rows are formula-reproduction checks only.  They are not interval
certificates, asymptotic evidence, or observations of an actual noisy
operator.

RH-348 proves an exact punctured lower-even physical coefficient ladder, its
sharp aggregate deterministic demand, and a necessary aggregate
compensation mass law.  It proves no signed supply estimate and no prefix
closure or nonclosure.

RH-349 may now compare the first two still-punctured sidebands
$m=k-2$ and $m=k-3$.  Their scalar parity/demand ratios differ by the fixed
factor $\lambda$, so the minimax identity
\[
 \inf_{a>0}\max\{|a-1|,|a/\lambda-1|\}
 =\frac{\lambda-1}{\lambda+1}>0
\]
can yield a conditional two-order obstruction under explicit
target-negligibility hypotheses for both actual signed remainders.  The
missing hypotheses may not be inferred from the present demand theorem.

RH-288 remains inactive.  Gates A--E remain false/open.  This paper
constructs no Hilbert--Polya operator, identifies no Riemann zero, proves no
von Mangoldt prime-power trace, proves no completed-zeta divisor equality,
and does not prove the Riemann Hypothesis.
