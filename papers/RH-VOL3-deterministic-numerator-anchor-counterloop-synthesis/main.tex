\begin{abstract}
This volume synthesizes RH-242--RH-281, the forty-paper interval in which the
prime-dynamics program separates an exact deterministic target from the
still-unidentified moving noisy cloud.  The deterministic side advances from
graded superloop identities and coefficient dictionaries to a certified
all-order envelope
\[
 |a_n|<48q_*^n,\qquad q_*=0.7008752258547757\ldots,qquad n\ge2,
\]
the sharp limit $a_n/q_*^n\to1$, the exact radius
$\rho_*=1.4267874838640739\ldots$, and a resolution-clocked monodromy
counterloop with a sharp minimal-rank theorem.

The volume records two complementary negative lessons.  First, several
frozen, idempotent, fractional, and unit-cap selector classes fail in their
declared finite algebras.  Second, a complete root-of-unity shell can erase
any fixed finite moment prefix while retaining a freely scalable next moment.
Thus finite coefficient matching does not imply a moving all-order cloud
envelope, even when the deterministic target envelope is sharp.

We prove an executable provenance statement, a deterministic closure theorem
relative to the upstream sources, and a finite-head separation theorem.  The
actual cloud identification remains open: no source supplies a legal
operator-derived head, a cloud-to-anchor coefficient bridge, aggregate
noisy-cloud transport, or an instantiated variable-rank quotient theorem.
Gates A--E remain false/open.  This synthesis does not construct a
Hilbert--Polya operator, identify Riemann zeros, prove a von Mangoldt trace,
prove completed-zeta divisor equality, or prove the Riemann Hypothesis.
\end{abstract}

\noindent\textbf{Keywords:} deterministic numerator; spectral anchors;
regularized determinants; analytic tails; counterloops; coefficient
envelopes; provenance audit.

\section{Scope, data types, and status words}

Volume III occupies the interval between the physical-cloud frontier of
Volume II and the actual noisy-head analysis of Volume IV.  The position is
important: a deterministic target can be solved exactly without being
identified with an actual noisy operator.

The numbered papers remain atomic sources.  We label a statement
\textsc{proved} only in its source scope, \textsc{certified} when it is a
finite exact or outward computation, \textsc{conditional} when its implication
has open physical hypotheses, and \textsc{scoped negative} when it excludes
one named class rather than every possible selector or operator.

The four phase endpoints are RH-251, RH-261, RH-271, and RH-281.  Each reviews
the preceding ten-paper block and fixes its route coordinate.  No finite
ledger count is promoted to an all-order theorem unless an analytic source
paper supplies that theorem.

\section{RH-242--RH-251: superloops, quotients, and frozen anchors}

RH-242 gives a graded periodic-superloop representation.  RH-243 supplies a
deterministic numerator coefficient dictionary.  The early block then asks
whether a finite cloud can be selected so that its moments realize the
required anchored prefix while the quotient tail remains controlled.

Several exact structural tools survive the audit.  Orthogonal quotient
grouping gives a trace identity; a block-power theorem gives a sufficient
uniform-envelope criterion; and finite-head/analytic-tail gluing isolates the
necessary obligations.  On the negative side, separately majorizing physical
and atomic sectors incurs a superunit rate.  The complete single-use frozen
shell zonotope has zero passing endpoints in the archived class, with dual
certificates.  Unbounded nonnegative weights create formal fits only by using
pathological magnitudes and do not define legal spectral multiplicities.

RH-251 therefore records an honest mixed verdict: exact superloop and
quotient algebra, but no legal anchored head and no complete current
certificate.  This is a scoped obstruction to the frozen class, not a proof
that every invariant selector fails.

\section{RH-252--RH-261: analytic tails and selector legality}

This phase adds a genuinely analytic target-side input.  The deterministic
numerator has an exact Cauchy tail interface on every disk
\[
 0\le R<S<r_H\lambda,
\]
where $r_H\lambda=1.42678748386407\ldots>1$.  At this point the boundary
supremum was not yet certified numerically; later papers close it.

The deterministic anchor atlas reaches order $28$, but its fitted root rate
is descriptive finite data.  Enlarging every resolved spectral window by
sixteen roots still produces no legal anchored head in the archived
single-use box.  Real conjugate-closed polynomial idempotents collapse to
binary generalized-root-space masks.  Arbitrary fractional signed fits have
nontrivial monodromy unless their combined exponents are integral, and the
first monodromy-legal lattice $\{-1,0,1\}$ again fails in the archived class.

The quotient diagnostic finds contractive twelfth powers at the tested
endpoints but lacks nine endpoints and a continuum bridge.  Its finite worst
root rate worsens rather than stabilizes.  The RH-261 review accordingly
separates one proved analytic obligation from four unproved physical or
uniform obligations.

\section{RH-262--RH-271: deterministic all-order closure}

The next block closes the target side.  It certifies the boundary constant,
proves an exact all-order parity anchor, controls the direct order-$29$ tail,
and constructs a finite ladder leading to the global estimate
\begin{equation}\label{eq:envelope}
 |a_n|<48q_*^n,qquad n\ge2,qquad
 q_*=0.7008752258547757\ldots.
\end{equation}
The coefficient base is sharp:
\begin{equation}\label{eq:sharp}
 \frac{a_n}{q_*^n}\longrightarrow1,
 \qquad
 \rho_*=q_*^{-1}=1.4267874838640739\ldots.
\end{equation}

\begin{theorem}[Deterministic closure theorem]\label{thm:deterministic}
In the deterministic numerator normalization fixed by RH-262--RH-268, the
all-order parity anchor, envelope~\eqref{eq:envelope}, sharp asymptotic
law~\eqref{eq:sharp}, and exact radius $\rho_*$ are proved.  These conclusions
require neither a finite-order fit nor an extrapolated noisy spectrum.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
The statement is the conjunction of the exact source theorems bound by the
RH-271 review: the certified boundary constant supplies the analytic input,
the parity anchor fixes all coefficient orders, the factorwise tail closes
the finite transition, and the sharp base theorem supplies the limit and
radius.  The inventory binds those sources.  No actual-cloud identification
is included in the hypotheses or conclusion.
\end{proof}

The phase also proves why this closure is not enough.  A complete shell of
$(N+1)$-st roots of unity has trace moments zero through order $N$ while its
moment at order $N+1$ may be scaled.  This exact separation survives any
fixed finite prefix.

\begin{theorem}[Finite-head separation theorem]\label{thm:separation}
For every finite order bound $N$, equality of two spectral moment lists
through order $N$ does not determine a uniform all-order envelope or the
next moment without an additional structural hypothesis.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Adjoin a complete root-of-unity shell of size $N+1$.  Orthogonality makes its
power sums vanish for orders $1,\ldots,N$, while the $(N+1)$-st power sum is
nonzero and scales with the shell amplitude or multiplicity allowed by the
information class.  The finite prefix is unchanged and the next coefficient
is free.  This is a logical counterexample; it does not claim that the
archived noisy quotient contains the shell.
\end{proof}

\section{RH-272--RH-281: counterloops and quotient limitations}

RH-272 constructs a resolution-clocked monodromy counterloop giving an exact
all-order coefficientwise deterministic bridge.  RH-273 proves its minimal
rank and equality-case uniqueness.  RH-274 identifies aggregate Fourier
control as the correct noisy-cloud input and gives a maximum-phase
counterexample to weaker coordinatewise reasoning.  The seven-row RH-275
audit does not certify that aggregate input.

The operator branch supplies complementary negative and positive facts.
RH-276 proves that raw Hilbert--Schmidt mass diverges like
$\sigma^{-1/2}$.  RH-277 excludes fixed-rank Calkin contraction in the
natural stationary $L^2$ geometry at zero noise.  RH-278 shows that
positive-noise local shell charts can activate the earlier quotient theorem.
RH-279 proves a variable-rank block-power tail criterion, but its physical
hypotheses are uninstantiated.  RH-280 and RH-281 keep the deterministic and
spectral ledgers separate.

\begin{proposition}[Counterloop type boundary]
The resolution-clocked counterloop is an exact deterministic all-order
object.  Its moments cannot be substituted for an actual noisy head unless a
separate theorem proves aggregate noisy-cloud transport and canonical
identification on the same clock.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
The source construction of the counterloop uses a graded monodromy model,
whereas the open ledger asks for an actual noisy spectral aggregate.  Equality
of notation or coefficient length does not supply a map between these data
types.  RH-274 identifies the missing aggregate Fourier hypothesis and
RH-275 does not certify it.
\end{proof}

\section{Selector and quotient firewalls}

The volume supports several precise stopping rules.
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=2.2em]
\item Do not rerun the same frozen shell window with unbounded weights and
call the result a legal spectral head.
\item Do not interpret complex polynomial coefficients as fractional
spectral multiplicities; idempotence and monodromy impose exact algebraic
constraints.
\item Do not promote a finite list of contractive block powers to a uniform
small-noise theorem.
\item Do not infer a moving coefficient bridge from any fixed finite anchor,
even when the deterministic target envelope is sharp.
\item Do not use raw Hilbert--Schmidt compactness at zero noise; its mass has
the proved divergent scale.
\item Do not extend the fixed-rank Calkin no-go to every variable-rank or
adapted geometry.
\end{enumerate}

These rules preserve both sides of the result.  The deterministic theorem is
stronger than a fit, while each negative conclusion is narrower than global
nonexistence.

\section{The surviving physical obligations}

A complete physical bridge requires at least:
\begin{enumerate}[label=(\roman*),leftmargin=2.2em]
\item a legal operator-derived anchored head;
\item an exact cloud-to-deterministic coefficient bridge;
\item aggregate noisy-cloud transport into the monodromy shell;
\item a common-space ideal estimate and moving isolating contour; and
\item an instantiated contractive power for the variable-rank quotient.
\end{enumerate}

The all-order target tail and deterministic counterloop do not replace any
of (i)--(v).  Conversely, the scoped selector failures do not prove that a
new invariant quotient or physical construction cannot satisfy them.  The
current coordinate is
\[
 \boxed{\texttt{deterministic\_all\_order\_closed\_actual\_cloud\_identification\_open}}.
\]

source papers.  Finite selector counts remain finite certificates.  The

zeros, prove a von Mangoldt trace, prove equality with the completed-zeta
