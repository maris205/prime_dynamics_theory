\section{Audit protocol and invariant frame}
\label{sec:audit-protocol}

\subsection{Proof levels}

\begin{definition}[Three proof levels]
\label{def:proof-levels}
We use the following noninterchangeable labels.
\begin{description}[leftmargin=2.5em,style=nextline]
\item[\(\Lzero\): finite model or abstract theorem.]
An exact algebraic, geometric, Fourier, convex, or Tauberian theorem,
including a finite or structure-free countermodel.

\item[\(\Lone\): literal interface theorem.]
An exact transfer that identifies the \(\Lzero\) quantity on the
physical carrier while preserving every required key, mask, support,
coefficient, and normalization.  Its hypotheses may still include an
unproved arithmetic estimate.

\item[\(\Ltwo\): growing arithmetic estimate.]
A new uniform bound for the actual growing fixed-\(h_0\) physical
coefficient, with all prefixes, exceptional sectors, polarizations,
and outer reassembly returned.
\end{description}
\end{definition}

\begin{remark}
An averaged shift, almost-all scale, fixed-form, frozen-family,
support-only, or positive-square estimate is recorded at its native
scope.  It is not promoted to \(\Ltwo\) fixed-\(h_0\) progress without
a proved literal transfer.
\end{remark}

\subsection{The literal invariant tuple}

\begin{definition}[Literal invariant frame]
\label{def:literal-frame}
At scale \(X\), the physical frame consists of:
\[
 \mathfrak I_X=
 (h_0,\Omega_X,\mathcal K_X,\mathcal W_X,\mathcal N_X).
\]
Here \(h_0\ne0\) is the prescribed shift, \(\Omega_X\) is the actual
active atom or determinant support, \(\mathcal K_X\) is the complete
native-key and provenance list, \(\mathcal W_X\) contains all masks,
weights, prefixes, interval origins, and polarizations, and
\(\mathcal N_X\) is the single global post-aggregation normalizer.
\end{definition}

A transfer is literal only when it gives an exact map between the
source and target frames.  Equal numerical determinants do not
justify merging distinct native atoms.  Zero padding, changing a
fiber mass, or replacing the post-bin mass by a raw majorant changes
the relevant statistic and requires a separate theorem.

\begin{proposition}[Promotion rule]
\label{prop:promotion-rule}
Within this audit, an \(\Lzero\) or conditional \(\Lone\) statement is
promoted to an \(\Ltwo\) statement only after a theorem verifies:
\begin{enumerate}[label=(\alph*),leftmargin=2.2em]
\item the same literal frame \(\mathfrak I_X\);
\item uniformity in every growing physical coefficient and prefix;
\item return of every exceptional, resonant, short-fiber, and
  outer-reassembly sector; and
\item the exact endpoint loss in the correct ledger.
\end{enumerate}
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
These items are part of the definition of the audited \(\Ltwo\)
target.  Omitting one changes either the coefficient, its
quantifiers, or its normalization, so it proves a different
statement.
\end{proof}

\subsection{What counts as forward progress}

An audited step is forward if it does at least one of the following:
\begin{enumerate}[label=(\roman*),leftmargin=2.2em]
\item proves a new literal fixed-power or logarithmic estimate;
\item closes a necessary exact interface in the dependency graph;
\item reduces a required estimate to a strictly smaller literal
  family with controlled return; or
\item rules out a proposed route by an exact physical counterexample
  or dual obstruction.
\end{enumerate}
Numerical stability, a pilot census, or a phase-free upper bound may
guide the next experiment, but is not by itself any of these four
items.
