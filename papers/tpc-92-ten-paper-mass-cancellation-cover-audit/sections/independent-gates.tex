\section{The remaining independent gates}
\label{sec:independent-gates}

\subsection{Current-route completion vector}

The exact papers now permit the remaining hypotheses to be stated
without substitution.  Fix exponent parameters
\(\lambda_D,\eta_Z\ge0\).  On the current route, write:
\begin{align*}
\mathsf G_{\rm mass}:\quad&
D_X\ge
X^{-\lambda_D+o(1)}Q_X^3J_X^2,
\\
\mathsf G_{\rm zero}:\quad&
|Z_X|\le X^{-\eta_Z+o(1)}Q_X^2,
\\
\mathsf G_{\rm reasm}:\quad&
w_X=B_Xc_X+r_X,\quad c_X\in\mathscr C_X^{\rm up},\quad
\mathcal T_X(r_X)=o(X),
\\
\mathsf G_{\rm DD}:\quad&
\text{the TPC-18 primitive determinant estimate holds on every
returned block},
\\
\mathsf G_{\rm phys}:\quad&
\Lambda_{\rm phys}<\frac1{400}.
\end{align*}
Here \(\mathscr C_X^{\rm up}\) is the coefficient rule actually
derived upstream; in the original unit-coefficient packet it is not
replaceable by an arbitrary signed solution.  The reassembly gate
includes coefficient amplification and the quantitative residual
rate required by the physical ledger, and \(\mathcal T_X\) is the
original literal physical reassembly functional.  The exact cover
\(B_Xc_X=w_X\) is the special sufficient case \(r_X=0\).
The determinant-dispersion gate includes every prefix and exceptional
block required by TPC-18.

\begin{definition}[Audited completion conjunction]
\label{def:completion-conjunction}
The current route has an \emph{audited completion certificate} only
when all five gates above hold in one literal frame and the recorded
mass/zero-mode bounds satisfy
\begin{equation}
 \lambda_D\le2\eta_Z.
 \label{eq:determinant-compatibility}
\end{equation}
\end{definition}

This definition does not assert that the gates have been proved.  It
records the conjunction required by the present chain.  A genuinely
different proof may bypass a gate, but must then replace the
corresponding dependency by another literal theorem.

\subsection{Determinant and physical ledgers}

The determinant compatibility
\eqref{eq:determinant-compatibility} compares the recorded lower
bound for \(D_X\) with the recorded upper bound for \(Z_X\).  At the
critical row it is the sufficient flatness ledger derived in
TPC-82 and reproduced exactly by TPC-88
\citep{WangTPC82,WangTPC88}.  If
\(\lambda_D>2\eta_Z\), those particular recorded bounds do not
certify the target.  This failure need not disprove a sharper bound
for the actual coefficient.

The physical ledger records fixed-power degradation elsewhere in the
literal interface and downstream comparison:
\begin{equation}
 \boxed{\Lambda_{\rm phys}<\frac1{400}.}
 \label{eq:strict-physical-ledger}
\end{equation}
It is strict.  Equality or excess stops the route.  A polynomial
reassembly-amplification or physical-residual loss enters this ledger
once if it is not already present in the block error.  A determinant-energy loss already
represented by \(\lambda_D\), or a zero-mode gain represented by
\(\eta_Z\), is not charged again.

\begin{theorem}[Ledger separation]
\label{thm:ledger-separation}
The audited chain requires the conjunction
\[
 \lambda_D\le2\eta_Z
 \qquad\text{and}\qquad
 \Lambda_{\rm phys}<1/400.
\]
It supplies no theorem that adds these inequalities into one budget,
converts one into the other, or permits the same loss to be entered
twice.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
The first inequality compares two statistics of the same literal
post-bin coefficient.  The second counts losses in distinct physical
maps and reassembly steps, excluding losses already represented in
the first comparison.  They have different definitions and inputs.
TPC-87--TPC-91 explicitly preserve this separation
\citep{WangTPC87,WangTPC88,WangTPC89,WangTPC90,WangTPC91}.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}[Equality]
Equality \(\lambda_D=2\eta_Z\) is compatible with the recorded
flatness exponent but leaves no determinant-ledger reserve.
Equality \(\Lambda_{\rm phys}=1/400\) violates the strict physical
rule and is a stop.  These two equality cases must not be conflated.
\end{remark}

\subsection{Reassembly and determinant dispersion}

\begin{proposition}[TPC-18 gate independence]
\label{prop:cover-dd-independence}
Within the audited theorem chain:
\begin{enumerate}[label=(\roman*),leftmargin=2.2em]
\item an exact allowed cover \(B_Xc_X=w_X\) is a sufficient
  zero-residual reassembly certificate, but does not prove the
  primitive determinant estimate on any column;
\item a determinant estimate on every candidate column does not prove
  either exact coverage or a physical bound for the residual;
\item a nonzero coefficient residual does not by itself fail the
  TPC-18 reassembly gate, but closes it only after
  \(\mathcal T_X(r_X)\) is bounded at the required rate.
\end{enumerate}
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Zero-residual coverage is a constrained coefficient identity.
Determinant dispersion is an arithmetic bound on the column
statistics.  TPC-91 gives finite systems with bounded overlap but
positive cover defect, while the TPC-18 estimate is stated
conditionally for already admissible blocks
\citep{WangTPC18,WangTPC91}.  If
\(w_X=B_Xc_X+r_X\), reassembly contains the additional term
\(\mathcal T_X(r_X)\); Euclidean smallness alone does not estimate it.
\end{proof}

\subsection{Local support and sign arithmetic}

The TPC-90 product
\[
 \prod_p(1-\nu_{p,2}/p^2)>0
\]
certifies that primitive affine squarefree support has no local
annihilation.  It is not a saving in \(\eta_Z\), a lower bound for
\(D_X\), a TPC-18 determinant estimate, or a reduction of
\(\Lambda_{\rm phys}\).  The remaining Liouville product is a signed
arithmetic gate and must be estimated on its literal growing family.
