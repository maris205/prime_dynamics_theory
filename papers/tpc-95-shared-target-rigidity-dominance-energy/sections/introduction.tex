\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:introduction}

Let \(A_X(n)\) be the literal signed normalized-determinant coefficient
obtained after the three TPC-32 matched cutoff channels and all native
contributions in each determinant fiber have been aggregated
\citep{WangTPC32}.  TPC-83 proves, using the actual support and
singleton envelopes, that natural post-bin mass and natural
determinant energy are equivalent:
\[
 \|A_X\|_1\ge X^{-o(1)}N_{0,X}
 \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
 \|A_X\|_2^2
 \ge X^{-o(1)}\frac{N_{0,X}^2}{Q_X}.
\]
This identifies the correct variable but supplies neither lower bound
\citep{WangTPC83}.

TPC-84 then opens the coefficient into canonical native determinant
fibers.  It proves the sharp resultant interval from the native
moduli and the exact identity
\[
 D=E_{\rm trip}+C_{\eqc}.
\]
Since \(E_{\rm trip}\) lies at most on the loss-one scale, a future
energy lower bound with fixed loss below one must be carried by
positive off-diagonal equal-determinant coherence
\citep{WangTPC84}.  The paper lists exact duplicates,
determinant-preserving symmetries, shared targets or divisors, and an
unpaired remainder as candidate classes, while deliberately assigning
no unproved sign or size to them.

The present paper performs the next two exact steps.

\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=2.2em]
\item We square the sharp fiber resultant before summing.  This gives
  the optimal modulus-only energy certificate
  \[
   D_{\dom}=\sum_n(2r_{\max,n}-R_n)_+^2.
  \]
  It is never weaker than first summing the defects and then applying
  Cauchy--Schwarz with the entire support, and it can be strictly
  stronger when the surviving defect is concentrated.
\item We show that a same-determinant shared-target graph is too
  sparse to supply the required coherence.  The key is not a
  heuristic independence claim.  It is an exact parametrization:
  fixing a determinant and one affine target leaves at most a
  divisor-bounded number of canonical parent keys.  This yields a
  subpower degree and hence a loss-one absolute bound for the whole
  shared-target class.
\end{enumerate}

The second result is stronger than saying that no positive
shared-target main term has been found.  It proves that even perfect
phase alignment inside that declared class cannot reach any
determinant-energy scale with fixed loss below one.  Thus this
particular door is closed.  The conclusion is class-local: sharing a
selected divisor without sharing a complete target is a different
relation and remains open.

\subsection{Main results}

The finite geometry gives the exact free-phase range
\[
 D\in
 \left[
 \sum_nd_n^2,\ \sum_nR_n^2
 \right].
\]
Subtracting the fixed triplet diagonal gives the exact free-phase
range of \(C_{\eqc}\).  A dominant share of the raw mass therefore
supplies a deterministic energy lower bound with an explicit exponent
ledger.

For the arithmetic rigidity, write one canonical parent as
\[
 t=(\alpha,\gamma,j),\qquad
 x_t=m_\alpha j+h_0,\qquad
 y_t=m_\gamma j+h_0,
\]
and
\[
 \nu(t)=
 \frac{m_\alpha-m_\gamma}{(x_t,y_t)}.
\]
The physical orbit has \(j>0\), and the row mask has
\(m_\alpha\ne m_\gamma\).  We prove:

\begin{enumerate}[label=(\roman*),leftmargin=2.2em]
\item the composite key \((\nu(t),x_t,y_t)\) identifies \(t\);
\item two distinct equal-determinant keys share at most one target,
  even if left--right cross-sharing is allowed;
\item the target-sharing correlation is an exact sum of compressed
  left, right, and cross group statistics;
\item every target group has divisor-bounded cardinality; and
\item the resulting coherence is at most the loss-one scale.
\end{enumerate}

The target-group formula also provides an audit invariant for a future
native-key census.  It requires only sorting by exact integer keys and
does not create a quadratic pair table.

\subsection{Claim discipline}

The sharp phase results are \(\Lzero\) finite algebra.  Their placement
on the literal post-bin coefficient and the target-collision bound are
\(\Lone\) results because they preserve the fixed nonzero \(h_0\),
canonical parent keys, active target support, and global normalization.
There is no new \(\Ltwo\) theorem: no lower bound for raw mass,
post-bin mass, determinant energy, or the distinguished zero mode is
proved.  In particular, no sieve-parity, prime-pair, or twin-prime
conclusion is claimed.
