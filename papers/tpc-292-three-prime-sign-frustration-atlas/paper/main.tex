\begin{abstract}
TPC-291 identified an exact low-residual signed direction for a pair of
physical prime components.  This paper tests the next compatibility question:
can three pairwise cancellation preferences be realized simultaneously?  We
prove a triangle parity criterion: for three nonzero Gram cross terms,
coefficient signs can make all three pair contributions nonpositive if and
only if the product of their signs is negative.  We also prove the exact
three-vector Schur projection formula
\[
 \min_{\alpha,\beta}\frac{\norm{g_i-\alpha g_j-\beta g_k}^2}{\norm{g_i}^2}
 =\frac{\det G_{ijk}}{G_{ii}\det G_{(j,k)}}.
\]
An exact-rational replay of the inherited 18-row literal grid evaluates all
5,727 prime triples: 5,718 are sign-frustrated and 9 are anti-alignable,
with positive normalized Gram volume in all 5,727 cases.  The finite atlas
turns pairwise cancellation into a concrete multi-prime obstruction.  It
does not establish a growing-shell compatibility theorem, an arithmetic
$L^2$ estimate, or a twin-prime result.
\end{abstract}

\section{Position on the twin-prime route}

This release remains inside the same literal prime-shell dynamical family.
The earlier route separated physical prime components and measured their
cross-prime Gram geometry \cite{tpc287,tpc288,tpc289}.  Diffuse nonnegative
weights were shown to face an all-positive coherence wall \cite{tpc290},
while TPC-291 proved that an individual coherent pair has an exact signed
Schur cancellation direction \cite{tpc291}.  Those facts leave a precise
question: are the preferred signs for many pair directions mutually
compatible?

Three components are the smallest setting in which compatibility can fail by
cycle parity.  We therefore use the parent physical output without changing
the source, interval, kernel, or prime-shell convention.  The result is a
structural probe on the road map, not a claim that the finite grid represents
an asymptotic prime distribution.

\section{Physical Gram model}

Let $I=I_N$ be the frozen integer interval and let $\beta$ be the frozen
rational source vector.  For an odd prime $q$, height $H$, and exponent $s$,
write
\[
 K_{H,s}(h)=\frac{H^{2s}}{(H^2+h^2)^s},\qquad
 B_q(u,t)=\mathbf 1_{q\nmid u}\mathbf 1_{q\nmid t}
 \left(\mathbf 1_{u\equiv t\pmod q}-\frac1{q-1}\right).
\]
The physical deleted-diagonal component is $g_q=A_q\beta$, with
\[
 (A_q)_{u,t}=qK_{H,s}(u-t)\mathbf 1_{u\ne t}B_q(u,t).
 \label{eq:model}
\]
For a shell $S$, define
\[
 d_q=\norm{g_q}^2,\qquad G_{q,r}=\ip{g_q}{g_r}.
 \label{eq:gram}
\]
All computations below use exact rational arithmetic.  Thus a displayed
decimal is only a convenient rendering of an exact fraction in the JSON
certificate.

\section{The compatibility theorem}

\begin{theorem}[Triangle sign parity]
Let $G_{12},G_{13},G_{23}$ be nonzero real numbers.  There exist signs
$a_1,a_2,a_3\in\{-1,+1\}$ such that
\[
 a_i a_j\operatorname{sign}(G_{ij})=-1
 \quad ((i,j)=(1,2),(1,3),(2,3))
 \]
if and only if
\[
 \operatorname{sign}(G_{12}G_{13}G_{23})=-1.
\]
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Multiplying the three required equations makes each $a_i$ occur twice, so
the left coefficient factor is $1$ and the product of the edge signs must be
$(-1)^3=-1$.  Conversely, if the product is $-1$, choose
$a_1=1$, $a_2=-\operatorname{sign}(G_{12})$, and
$a_3=-\operatorname{sign}(G_{13})$.  The first two edges have the desired
sign, and the third has
\[
 a_2a_3\operatorname{sign}(G_{23})
 =\operatorname{sign}(G_{12}G_{13}G_{23})=-1.
\]
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}
We call the product $+1$ case \emph{sign-frustrated}.  It means that every
coefficient-sign assignment leaves at least one positive pair contribution.
The theorem is a statement about a signed triangle; it does not optimize
coefficient magnitudes or assert that all edges are equally important.
\end{remark}

\section{Three-vector Schur residual}

\begin{proposition}[Three-vector projection identity]
Let $g_i,g_j,g_k$ have Gram matrix $G$, and suppose the two-vector block
$M=G_{(j,k),(j,k)}$ is positive definite.  Then
\[
 \min_{\alpha,\beta\in\RR}
 \frac{\norm{g_i-\alpha g_j-\beta g_k}^2}{G_{ii}}
 =\frac{\det G}{G_{ii}\det M}.
 \label{eq:schur}
\]
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Put $d_i=G_{ii}$ and $v=(G_{ij},G_{ik})^T$.  Expanding the square gives
$d_i-2v^Tc+c^TMc$, where $c=(\alpha,\beta)^T$.  The unique minimizer is
$c=M^{-1}v$, and the minimum is $d_i-v^TM^{-1}v$.  The block determinant
identity gives
$\det G=\det M\,(d_i-v^TM^{-1}v)$; division by $d_i\det M$ proves the
formula.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[Normalized volume]
For three vectors, $\det G/(d_i d_j d_k)$ is the squared volume after
normalizing each vector to unit length.  It is nonnegative by positive
semidefiniteness of a Gram matrix, and is positive exactly when the three
vectors are linearly independent.
\end{corollary}

The projection formula complements the parity theorem: a small Schur
residual says that one vector is close to the span of two others, but it does
not say that the signs of all pairwise cross terms can be chosen to lower the
same quadratic form.

\section{Exact finite protocol}

The certificate inherits eight $s=2$ growth anchors, four $s=1$ exponent
crossover rows, and six $s=2$ source-control rows, for 18 rows.  The prime
shells have cardinalities ranging from 3 to 17.  Every unordered triple is
evaluated, giving
\[
 \sum_{\text{rows}}\binom{|S|}{3}=5727.
\]
For each triple the producer records the edge-sign pattern in the canonical
order $(1,2),(1,3),(2,3)$, the sign product, all three projection
coefficients, the minimum target residual, and the normalized volume.  The
independent checker reconstructs the physical output in reverse
column-first order, rather than calling the producer's row builder.  Parent
code and result hashes, and the frozen TPC-268 engine hash, are locked.

\section{Interpretation and claim firewall}

The finite experiment closes one uncertainty and exposes another.

\begin{enumerate}
\item The exact pairwise cancellation directions from TPC-291 do not compose
      automatically: cycle parity is already an obstruction at three primes.
\item The obstruction is strongest in the late coherent block, where all
      tested triangle edges are positive.  A signed subtraction can lower a
      selected pair, but no choice of three coefficient signs lowers all
      cross terms simultaneously.
\item The nine anti-alignable triangles are localized finite exceptions.  A
      useful next experiment must test whether they form a shell-level
      signed-graph structure or are merely isolated artifacts of the
      crossover row.
\end{enumerate}

The exact claims are Theorem 1, Proposition 2, and Gram positivity.  The
numerical claim is the complete finite atlas on the declared grid.  A
growing-shell compatibility theorem, source-restricted coefficient image,
arithmetic $L^2$ estimate, fixed-power credit, full Gate B, and a twin-prime
conclusion remain open.  The Session-named Route-A/Route-B evaluator files
are absent from this checkout, so no official evaluator pass is claimed; the
local proof, certificate, independent replay, stress test, and Bridge-B
checker form the fail-closed validation package.

\section{Reproducibility and conclusion}

From the project directory run:
\begin{verbatim}
export PYTHONDONTWRITEBYTECODE=1
python -B code/tpc292_three_prime_sign_frustration_certificate.py --check
python -B experiments/tpc292_independent_checker.py
python -B experiments/tpc292_frustration_stress.py
\end{verbatim}
The canonical result is
\texttt{results/tpc292\_certificate.json}.  Normal and optimized runs are
required to emit empty standard error and identical standard output.

TPC-292 advances the route map from pairwise cancellation to a precise
multi-prime compatibility test.  Its main reusable object is the signed
Gram graph: cycle parity should be checked before spending effort on
coefficient magnitudes or arithmetic endpoint estimates.
