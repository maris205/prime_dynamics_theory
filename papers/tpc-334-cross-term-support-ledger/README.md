# TPC-334 — Cross-term support: twins versus the odd background

**Author:** Liang Wang
**Affiliation:** School of Mathematics and Statistics, Huazhong University of Science and Technology (HUST), Wuhan, China

## One-line result

TPC-334 takes the substantial source cross term found in TPC-333 and partitions
it by exact finite support:

```text
<Lambda,b> = twin-prime mass
             + non-twin prime-shift mass
             + prime-power-shift mass.
```

On the six parent-locked windows, the twin-prime share is only
`5.4296754369378503%`--`7.1734300218214184%`, while the non-twin
prime-shift share is `92.826569978178597%`--`94.41957197913976%`.
Prime-power shifts contribute at most `0.28651911963981512%`.
This is a finite support obstruction: the polarization cross term is mostly
generated by odd composite predecessors of a prime `t+2`, not by pairs in
which both entries are prime.  It does not prove a density theorem or refute
the twin-prime conjecture.

## New contribution

TPC-333 measured the cross-term magnitude but did not attribute it.  TPC-334
proves the exact support partition for the declared source model and certifies
all six category ledgers independently.  The result gives a concrete reason
not to treat the source polarization cross term as a twin-prime proxy.

## Frozen finite object

The parent is TPC-333, locked by normalized LF SHA-256.  We retain

```text
origins       = {42001, 44001}
scales        = {2048, 4096, 8192}
source counts = {1024, 2048, 4096}
cutoff        = 50000
```

For each coordinate, the producer labels the cross contribution
`Lambda(t+2)b(t)` as:

1. `twin_prime`: both `t` and `t+2` prime;
2. `non_twin_prime_shift`: `t+2` prime but `t` not prime;
3. `prime_power_shift`: `t+2=p^k`, `k>=2`;
4. `zero_support`: the coordinate contributes zero.

The first three masses sum to the full cross term; the fourth category has
zero mass but records the remaining coordinates and their counts.

## Certified finite readout

| category | coordinate-count range | cross-mass share range |
|---|---:|---:|
| twin prime | 11--43 | 0.054296754369378503--0.071734300218214184 |
| non-twin prime shift | 87--334 | 0.92826569978178597--0.94419571979139760 |
| prime-power shift | 0--1 | 0--0.0028651911963981512 |
| zero support | 919--3721 | 0 |

All six rows have twin share below `0.10` and non-twin share above `0.90`.
The exact partition error is below the recorded float64 replay tolerance and
the independent checker uses a separate trial sieve and reverse factorization.

claim that composite background dominates every possible twin-prime proof.

## Next clue

If a twin-specific signal is desired, it must be isolated explicitly rather
than inferred from the raw polarization cross term.  The next project will
construct a twin-isolated source and compare its norm and response with the
full source and its non-twin background.
