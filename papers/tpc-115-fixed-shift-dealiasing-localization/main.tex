\begin{abstract}
A frozen-parameter or frequency-averaged estimate is not yet an
estimate at one prescribed physical shift \(h_0\).  The missing
operation is evaluation on the actual shift-profile subspace.  We
give its exact finite-dimensional cost.

Let \(A:E\to\C^I\) be the declared observation map, let
\(\Om=\operatorname{diag}(p_i)\) with \(p_i\ge0\), and let
\(\ell(x)=b_0^*x\) be the target functional.  With
\(G=A^*\Om A\), a finite localization inequality
\[
 |\ell(x)|^2\le K\,\|Ax\|_{\Om}^2
 \qquad(x\in E)
\]
exists if and only if \(b_0\in\operatorname{ran}G\), equivalently
\(\ell\) annihilates \(\ker A_{\Om}\), where
\(A_{\Om}=\Om^{1/2}A\).  In that case the sharp constant is
\[
 \boxed{\qquad K=b_0^*G^\dagger b_0.\qquad}
\]
For a profile space \(V=\operatorname{ran}B\), this becomes the
Christoffel evaluation cost
\[
 K_V(h_0)=b(h_0)^*
  (B^*\Om B)^\dagger b(h_0).
\]

The formula separates three regimes.  The unrestricted profile space
costs exactly \(1/p_{h_0}\), reproducing the positivity-only
localization loss.  Constants cost \(1\).  A \(d\)-character subspace
under uniform averaging costs exactly \(d\), independent of the
ambient number of shifts.  Conversely, if the target does not
annihilate the observation kernel, no finite averaged estimate can
recover it.

This is a sharp conditional bridge, not a fixed-shift arithmetic
estimate.  It advances the interface only after the actual TPC shift
profile is proved to lie in a controlled subspace.  We prove no such
regularity, no \(\Ltwo\) fixed-power saving, no parity breakthrough,
and no prime-pair or twin-prime theorem.
\end{abstract}

\noindent\textbf{Keywords:}
prescribed shift; localization; Christoffel cost; pseudoinverse;

\section{The missing return from averages}

TPC-46 exhibits the sharp positivity cost
\(\sum_h\omega_h/\omega_{h_0}\) for recovering one shift from a
weighted average with no regularity \citep{WangTPC46}.  Later frozen
and spectral routes improve averaged or model estimates, but
TPC-MVP1 requires an actual prescribed physical shift
\citep{WangMVP1}.  TPC-113 fixes physical conditioning, and TPC-114
identifies the residual/native Gram and its cancellation kernel
\citep{WangTPC113,WangTPC114}.  The present paper isolates the exact
evaluation norm after those maps.

Let \(E\) be a finite-dimensional Hilbert space and
\[
 A:E\to\C^I,\qquad
 \Om=\operatorname{diag}(p_i:i\in I),\qquad p_i\ge0.
\]
Write
\[
 \|z\|_{\Om}^2=z^*\Om z,\qquad
 A_{\Om}=\Om^{1/2}A,\qquad G=A^*\Om A=A_{\Om}^*A_{\Om}.
\]
The target is \(\ell(x)=b_0^*x\).

\section{The exact localization theorem}

\begin{theorem}[Sharp evaluation through a semidefinite observation]
\label{thm:sharp}
There exists a finite \(K\) such that
\[
 |b_0^*x|^2\le K\,x^*Gx\qquad(x\in E)
\]
if and only if
\[
 b_0\perp\ker G,
 \qquad\text{equivalently}\qquad
 b_0\in\operatorname{ran}G.
\]
When this holds, the least possible constant is
\[
 \boxed{\quad K_{\rm loc}(A,\Om;b_0)
 =b_0^*G^\dagger b_0.\quad}
\]
If it fails, the sharp constant is \(+\infty\).
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Since \(G=A_{\Om}^*A_{\Om}\),
\(\ker G=\ker A_{\Om}\).  If \(b_0\) does not annihilate this kernel,
some \(x\in\ker G\) has \(b_0^*x\ne0\), so no finite inequality is
possible.

Assume \(b_0\in\operatorname{ran}G\).  On
\((\ker G)^\perp\), put \(y=G^{1/2}x\).  Then
\[
 b_0^*x=(G^{\dagger/2}b_0)^*y.
\]
Cauchy--Schwarz gives
\[
 |b_0^*x|^2
 \le\|G^{\dagger/2}b_0\|^2\|G^{1/2}x\|^2
 =(b_0^*G^\dagger b_0)(x^*Gx).
\]
Equality holds for
\(y=G^{\dagger/2}b_0\), equivalently along
\(x=G^\dagger b_0\), unless \(b_0=0\), when both sides vanish.
Thus the constant is sharp.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}[Kernel condition, not invertibility]
The observation need not be injective on all of \(E\).  Only the
target must vanish on its invisible directions.  Requiring a global
inverse can therefore overstate the cost, while ignoring the kernel
can assert an impossible de-aliasing.
\end{remark}

\begin{proposition}[Hilbert-valued extension]
\label{prop:vector}
Let \(\mathcal H\) be any Hilbert space and replace \(A\) by
\(A\otimes I_{\mathcal H}\), and \(b_0^*\) by
\(b_0^*\otimes I_{\mathcal H}\).  Under the condition of
\cref{thm:sharp},
\[
 \|(b_0^*\otimes I_{\mathcal H})X\|_{\mathcal H}^2
 \le K_{\rm loc}
 \|(A_{\Om}\otimes I_{\mathcal H})X\|^2,
\]
with the same sharp constant.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Apply the scalar inequality to the scalar products of the
\(\mathcal H\)-valued coefficients against arbitrary unit vectors,
or diagonalize \(G\) and sum the componentwise inequalities.
Sharpness follows by tensoring a scalar extremizer with a unit vector.
\end{proof}

\section{Profile spaces and Christoffel cost}

Let \(H\) be the finite shift set, let \(p_h\ge0\) with
\(\sum_hp_h=1\), and let \(B:\C^d\to\C^H\) describe a declared
profile space:
\[
 (Bc)(h)=b(h)^*c.
\]
Evaluation at \(h_0\) is \(b(h_0)^*c\).

\begin{corollary}[Exact profile-space localization]
\label{cor:profile}
Put
\[
 G_V=B^*\Om B,\qquad
 \Om=\operatorname{diag}(p_h:h\in H).
\]
If \(b(h_0)\in\operatorname{ran}G_V\), then
\[
 |(Bc)(h_0)|^2
 \le K_V(h_0)\sum_{h\in H}p_h|(Bc)(h)|^2,
\]
where the sharp constant is
\[
 \boxed{\quad
 K_V(h_0)=b(h_0)^*G_V^\dagger b(h_0).
 \quad}
\]
If \(b(h_0)\notin\operatorname{ran}G_V\), no finite constant exists.
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
Apply \cref{thm:sharp} with \(A=B\) and \(b_0=b(h_0)\).
\end{proof}

\begin{proposition}[Three exact regimes]
\label{prop:regimes}
The following constants are sharp.
\begin{enumerate}[label=(\roman*),leftmargin=2em]
\item For the unrestricted profile space \(\C^H\),
\[
 K_{\C^H}(h_0)=\frac1{p_{h_0}}
 \]
when \(p_{h_0}>0\); it is infinite when \(p_{h_0}=0\).
\item For the constant profile space, \(K_{\rm const}(h_0)=1\).
\item Let \(H=\mathbb Z/N\mathbb Z\), use uniform weights \(1/N\),
and let \(V\) be the span of any \(d\) distinct characters.  Then
\[
 K_V(h_0)=d
 \qquad\text{for every }h_0.
\]
\end{enumerate}
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
For (i), \(B=I\), \(G_V=\Om\), and the target is \(e_{h_0}\).
For (ii), \(B=\one\), so \(G_V=\sum_hp_h=1\).
For (iii), character orthogonality gives
\(B^*(N^{-1}I)B=I_d\), while the evaluation vector \(b(h_0)\)
has \(d\) unit-modulus entries.  Hence
\(b(h_0)^*b(h_0)=d\).
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}[The positivity cost is genuinely sharp]
Writing \(p_h=\omega_h/\sum_t\omega_t\), part (i) gives
\[
 K_{\C^H}(h_0)=\frac{\sum_t\omega_t}{\omega_{h_0}},
\]
exactly the TPC-46 positivity cost.  It cannot be improved from an
averaged inequality alone.  Any improvement must prove that the
actual profile lies in a smaller declared space, as in parts (ii) or
(iii), or establish another explicit regularity inequality.
\end{remark}

\section{Conditional fixed-shift transfer}

\begin{theorem}[What an averaged arithmetic estimate would imply]
\label{thm:conditional}
Let \(F_X(h)\in\mathcal H_X\) be the literal physical shift profile.
Assume:
\begin{enumerate}[label=(\alph*),leftmargin=2em]
\item \(F_X\) belongs to a declared Hilbert-valued profile space
  \(V_X\otimes\mathcal H_X\);
\item the averaged estimate
  \[
   \sum_h p_{X,h}\|F_X(h)\|_{\mathcal H_X}^2\le E_X
  \]
  holds on the same active support and normalization; and
\item the target evaluation annihilates the observation kernel.
\end{enumerate}
Then
\[
 \boxed{\qquad
 \|F_X(h_0)\|_{\mathcal H_X}^2
 \le K_{V_X}(h_0)E_X.
 \qquad}
\]
If \(K_{V_X}(h_0)=X^{\lambda+o(1)}\), localization spends
\(\lambda\) in an energy ledger and \(\lambda/2\) in the corresponding
amplitude ledger.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Apply \cref{prop:vector,cor:profile}.  The exponent statement follows
by taking square roots when converting squared norms to amplitudes.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}[Strict endpoint accounting]
The localization cost is charged exactly once, after the native Gram
and before the prescribed-shift conclusion.  If the route uses the
amplitude-normalized physical ledger of TPC-MVP1, the accumulated
loss, including \(\lambda/2\), must remain strictly below \(1/400\).
An endpoint equality is not a positive reserve.
\end{remark}

\section{Sharp invisibility obstruction}

\begin{proposition}[Indistinguishable profiles]
\label{prop:invisible}
If \(x_1-x_2\in\ker A_{\Om}\) but
\(\ell(x_1)\ne\ell(x_2)\), then no procedure using only the weighted
observation \(A_{\Om}x\) can recover the target on both profiles.  In
particular, no finite localization inequality exists.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
The observations of \(x_1\) and \(x_2\) coincide, whereas their target
values differ.  Their difference is the kernel witness in
\cref{thm:sharp}.
\end{proof}

For example, \(A=(1\ \ 1)\) observes only \(x_1+x_2\).  Evaluation of
the first coordinate does not annihilate
\(\ker A=\operatorname{span}\{(1,-1)\}\), so it is impossible.
Evaluation of the observed sum itself has sharp cost \(1\).

\section{Finite exact certificate and verdict}

The script
\texttt{experiments/tpc115\_localization\_certificate.py} checks with
exact rational arithmetic:
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=2em]
\item unrestricted weighted costs \(6,3,2\) for weights
  \(1/6,2/6,3/6\);
\item constant-profile cost \(1\);
\item Walsh-character subspace costs \(d=1,2,4,8\) on eight shifts;
  and
\item the invisible and visible functionals for \(A=(1\ \ 1)\).
\end{enumerate}
The certificate validates the finite formulas, not an arithmetic
profile hypothesis.

\paragraph{Established.}
\Cref{thm:sharp,prop:vector,cor:profile,prop:regimes,thm:conditional,prop:invisible}
give the exact evaluation norm, its
Hilbert-valued extension, and a sharp obstruction.  These are
\(\Lzero\) finite-dimensional theorems.  Attaching them to the
literal fixed-shift profile is an explicit \(\Lone\) conditional
interface.

\paragraph{Not established.}
We do not prove that the actual TPC profile lies in a low-dimensional
character or regularity space.  We prove no bound for its
Christoffel cost, no new averaged arithmetic estimate, and no
prescribed-shift saving.  There is no \(\Ltwo\) advance, parity
breakthrough, Hardy--Littlewood asymptotic, prime-pair lower bound, or
twin-prime theorem.  Nothing is specialized to \(h_0=2\).
