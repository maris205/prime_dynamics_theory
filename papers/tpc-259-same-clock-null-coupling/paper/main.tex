\begin{abstract}
TPC-258 constructed a source-frozen transverse unit vector
\(\znull\) for which the leading literal V59 adjoint diagonal cancels:
\(\ip{\znull}{A_x\beta}=o(x^{7/6}/\log^3x)\).  We now place that
direction on the same clock as the physical hybrid residual
\(w(u)=\Lambda(u+2)-b_x^{(Z_x)}(u)\).  A source-backed maximal-interval
Type-I theorem controls each of the four consecutive Haar blocks and gives,
for every fixed admissible \(K\) and every fixed \(M>0\),
\[
 |\ip{\znull}{w}|\ll_{M,K}\frac{x^{1/2}}{(\log x)^M}.
\]
The exact Hilbert decomposition
\[
 \ip{w}{A_x\beta}
 =\overline{\ip{\znull}{w}}\ip{\znull}{A_x\beta}
   +\ip{\wperp}{A_x\beta}
\]
therefore makes the first rank-one channel
\(o(x^{5/3}/\log^{M+3}x)\).  This is a source-backed advance for one
literal signed-coupling channel, not for the full scalar.  The orthogonal
residual remains open, and an exact zero-diagonal finite witness shows that
it can carry the entire scalar when the null channel vanishes.  No fixed
power saving, arithmetic \(L^2\), full Gate B, strict global \(1/400\)
payment, or twin-prime conclusion is claimed.
\end{abstract}

\section{Problem and claim boundary}

The Route-B scalar needed downstream is not merely a product of two unrelated
norms.  On the literal V59 clock it has the signed form
\begin{equation}
 C_x=\ip{w}{\Ax\beta}.                                      \label{eq:fullscalar}
\end{equation}
The preceding papers separately supplied a strong hybrid mean for \(w\)
and a source-frozen adjoint null direction \cite{tpc254,tpc258}.  The smallest
honest question is whether these facts suppress a precisely identified
piece of \eqref{eq:fullscalar} on one and the same clock.

The answer is yes for one rank-one channel and no as a formal inference for
the full scalar.  We keep the residual in every identity.  In particular,
the phrase ``null channel'' below refers to the projection onto one fixed
Haar direction; it never means that \(C_x\) itself is zero or small.

Throughout,
\[
 \ip{f}{g}=\sum_u\overline{f(u)}g(u)
\]
is conjugate-linear in the first slot.  The literal \(w\) and the Haar
directions are real, while \(\Ax\beta\) may be complex.

\section{One clock, four blocks, and the frozen null direction}

Let \(x\to\infty\) through real values and put
\begin{equation}
 a=\lfloor x/2\rfloor,\qquad b=\lfloor x\rfloor,
 \qquad I_x=\{a+1,\ldots,b\},\qquad N=b-a.              \label{eq:clock}
\end{equation}
Write \(\ell=\lfloor N/2\rfloor\), \(r=N-\ell\), and split the two
ordered children into four consecutive blocks \(B_1,\ldots,B_4\), of sizes
\begin{equation}
 s_1=\lfloor\ell/2\rfloor,\quad s_2=\ell-s_1,
 \qquad s_3=\lfloor r/2\rfloor,\quad s_4=r-s_3.          \label{eq:sizes}
\end{equation}
Thus the blocks cover \(I_x\) exactly for every admissible real clock, and
\(s_j=x/8+O(1)\).

For adjacent blocks of sizes \(p,q\), define the normalized contrast
\[
 h(A,B)=\left(\frac{pq}{p+q}\right)^{1/2}
 \left(\frac{\one_A}{p}-\frac{\one_B}{q}\right).
\]
The two transverse descendants are
\begin{equation}
 z_1=h(B_1,B_2),\qquad z_2=h(B_3,B_4),                  \label{eq:descendants}
\end{equation}
with normalizations
\(\rho_1^2=s_1s_2/(s_1+s_2)\) and
\(\rho_2^2=s_3s_4/(s_3+s_4)\).

Set
\begin{equation}
 L_1=\log(3456/3125),\qquad L_2=\log(884736/823543),
 \qquad L_T=(L_1^2+L_2^2)^{1/2},                       \label{eq:logs}
\end{equation}
and freeze
\begin{equation}
 \znull=\frac{L_2z_1-L_1z_2}{L_T}.                    \label{eq:null}
\end{equation}

\begin{lemma}[Exact source geometry]\label{lem:geometry}
For every sufficiently large real \(x\), the vectors \(z_1,z_2\) are
exactly orthonormal.  Hence \(\znull\) is an exact unit vector.  It depends
only on the ordered source coordinates and the two fixed interval constants;
it is independent of \(w\), \(\beta\), and the observed output.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
For a contrast on blocks of sizes \(p,q\), direct counting gives
\[
 \|h(A,B)\|_2^2=\frac{pq}{p+q}\left(\frac1p+\frac1q\right)=1.
\]
The supports of \(z_1\) and \(z_2\) are disjoint, so their inner product is
zero.  Equation \eqref{eq:null} then has squared norm
\((L_2^2+L_1^2)/L_T^2=1\).  Every quantity in its definition is fixed before
either literal coefficient is inspected.
\end{proof}

TPC-258 proved, with
\begin{equation}
 S_x=\frac{x^{7/6}}{\log^3x},                           \label{eq:scale}
\end{equation}
the source-backed cancellation
\begin{equation}
 \ip{\znull}{\Ax\beta}=o(S_x).                         \label{eq:tpc258}
\end{equation}
It arises because the two leading curvatures are \(L_1/2,L_2/2\), so their
cross combination cancels exactly.  Equation \eqref{eq:tpc258} is an
\(o(1)\) statement and is not promoted here to a fixed-power estimate.

\section{Four-block control of the literal hybrid residual}

Fix a finite admissible \(K\) and write
\begin{equation}
 Z_x=(\log x)^K,\qquad
 w(u)=\Lambda(u+2)-b_x^{(Z_x)}(u).                     \label{eq:w}
\end{equation}
The source-locked hybrid maximal Type-I theorem, through its nonnegative
\(m=1\) row, gives for every fixed \(M>0\)
\begin{equation}
 \max_{\substack{J\subset I_x\\J\ \mathrm{consecutive}}}
 \left|\sum_{u\in J}w(u)\right|
 \ll_{M,K}\frac{x}{(\log x)^M}.                       \label{eq:maximal}
\end{equation}
We record the consequence for the frozen transverse direction.

\begin{theorem}[Source-backed null moment]\label{thm:w-null}
For every fixed admissible \(K\) and every fixed \(M>0\),
\begin{equation}
 \boxed{\displaystyle
 |\ip{\znull}{w}|\ll_{M,K}\frac{x^{1/2}}{(\log x)^M}.} \label{eq:w-null}
\end{equation}
The implied constant is not uniform in \(M\) or \(K\).
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Let \(W_j=\sum_{u\in B_j}w(u)\).  Each \(B_j\) is one of the consecutive
intervals admitted in \eqref{eq:maximal}, so
\begin{equation}
 |W_j|\ll_{M,K}\frac{x}{(\log x)^M},\qquad 1\le j\le4. \label{eq:blocksum}
\end{equation}
The exact Haar formulas are
\[
 \ip{z_1}{w}=\rho_1\left(\frac{W_1}{s_1}-\frac{W_2}{s_2}\right),
 \qquad
 \ip{z_2}{w}=\rho_2\left(\frac{W_3}{s_3}-\frac{W_4}{s_4}\right).
\]
Here \(s_j=x/8+O(1)\), while
\(\rho_i\le\sqrt{s_{2i-1}+s_{2i}}/2\ll\sqrt{x}\).  Dividing
\eqref{eq:blocksum} by the block sizes therefore gives
\[
 |\ip{z_1}{w}|+|\ip{z_2}{w}|
 \ll_{M,K}\frac{x^{1/2}}{(\log x)^M}.
\]
Finally, \eqref{eq:null} is a fixed linear combination whose coefficient
\(\ell^1\)-norm is less than two.  This proves \eqref{eq:w-null}.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}[Quantifiers]
The theorem means: first freeze finite \(K\), then choose fixed \(M\), then
take \(x\ge x_0(M,K)\).  Arbitrary fixed logarithmic saving does not imply
\(x^{1/2-\delta}\) for any fixed \(\delta>0\).
\end{remark}

\section{Exact signed-coupling split}

Put
\begin{equation}
 c_x=\ip{\znull}{w},\qquad
 w_{\parallel}=c_x\znull,\qquad
 \wperp=w-c_x\znull.                                  \label{eq:projection}
\end{equation}
Since \(\znull\) is unit, \(\ip{\znull}{\wperp}=0\).  The next identity
is purely finite-dimensional and requires no symmetry of \(A_x\).

\begin{proposition}[Rank-one/residual identity]\label{prop:split}
For every finite clock,
\begin{equation}
 \boxed{\displaystyle
 \ip{w}{\Ax\beta}
 =\overline{c_x}\ip{\znull}{\Ax\beta}
  +\ip{\wperp}{\Ax\beta}.}                            \label{eq:split}
\end{equation}
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Equation \eqref{eq:projection} gives \(w=c_x\znull+\wperp\).  By
conjugate-linearity in the first slot,
\[
 \ip{c_x\znull}{\Ax\beta}
 =\overline{c_x}\ip{\znull}{\Ax\beta}.
\]
Adding the residual term proves \eqref{eq:split}.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Same-clock null-channel suppression]\label{thm:channel}
For every fixed admissible \(K\) and every fixed \(M>0\), the first term in
\eqref{eq:split} satisfies
\begin{equation}
 \boxed{\displaystyle
 \overline{\ip{\znull}{w}}\ip{\znull}{\Ax\beta}
 =o\!\left(\frac{x^{5/3}}{\log^{M+3}x}\right).}       \label{eq:main}
\end{equation}
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Write \(\ip{\znull}{\Ax\beta}=S_xe(x)\), where \(e(x)\to0\) by
\eqref{eq:tpc258}.  Theorem~\ref{thm:w-null} gives
\[
 \left|\overline{c_x}\ip{\znull}{\Ax\beta}\right|
 \ll_{M,K}\frac{x^{1/2}}{\log^Mx}\,
              \frac{x^{7/6}}{\log^3x}|e(x)|.
\]
Since \(1/2+7/6=5/3\), this is exactly \eqref{eq:main}.
\end{proof}

The theorem controls one explicitly identified piece of the literal scalar
\eqref{eq:fullscalar}.  It says nothing yet about
\begin{equation}
 R_x=\ip{\wperp}{\Ax\beta}.                            \label{eq:residual}
\end{equation}
This term is not discarded, bounded by wishful orthogonality, or absorbed
into the little-oh notation.

\section{Conditional rate and the residual obstruction}

TPC-258 isolated the following stronger rate as conditional: if its two
scalar remainders satisfy
\[
 \ip{\znull}{\Ax\beta}
 \ll_{\psi,\e}\frac{S_x}{\log x}+x^{55/48+\e},
\]
then Theorem~\ref{thm:w-null} gives
\begin{equation}
 |\overline{c_x}\ip{\znull}{\Ax\beta}|
 \ll_{M,K,\psi,\e}
 \frac{x^{5/3}}{\log^{M+4}x}
 +\frac{x^{79/48+\e}}{\log^Mx}.                       \label{eq:conditional}
\end{equation}
Indeed, \(1/2+55/48=79/48\), while \(5/3=80/48\); the boundary exponent
has a \(1/48\) gap before \(\e\).  Equation \eqref{eq:conditional} is a
\texttt{CONDITIONAL\_THEOREM}, not an unconditional fixed-power claim.

The open residual cannot be controlled by projection algebra alone.

\begin{proposition}[Exact synthetic non-promotion witness]\label{prop:witness}
There exist a real unit vector \(z\), real vectors \(w,\beta\), and a real
zero-diagonal matrix \(A\) such that
\[
 \ip{z}{w}=0,\qquad
 \overline{\ip{z}{w}}\ip{z}{A\beta}=0,\qquad
 \ip{w}{A\beta}\ne0.
\]
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
In \(\mathbb R^2\), choose
\[
 z=(1,0),\qquad w=(0,1),\qquad \beta=(1,0),\qquad
 A=\begin{pmatrix}0&0\\ \lambda&0\end{pmatrix}
\]
with \(\lambda\ne0\).  Then \(A\beta=(0,\lambda)\), so
\(\ip{z}{w}=\ip{z}{A\beta}=0\), while
\(\ip{w}{A\beta}=\lambda\).  Here \(w_\perp=w\), and the residual carries
the entire scalar.
\end{proof}

This witness is structural.  It is not claimed to arise from the literal
prime shell, and it does not refute any arithmetic estimate.  It refutes only
the attempted implication from null-channel suppression to full-scalar
suppression without an additional theorem.

\section{Route evaluation}

The strongest positive result is the source-backed estimate
\eqref{eq:main} for one same-clock signed channel.  The strongest obstruction
is exact: the perpendicular residual \eqref{eq:residual} may carry all of a
general zero-diagonal scalar.  The next open theorem is therefore to estimate
\(R_x\) for the literal V59 operator, or to reassemble all four signed
packets while retaining that residual explicitly.

The reusable chain is
\[
 \text{same clock}\to\text{four-block Haar null}
 \to\text{maximal-interval }w\text{ moment}
 \to\text{exact rank-one split}
 \to\text{residual firewall}.
\]
No step supplies arithmetic \(L^2\), a full Gate-B upper estimate, the
strict global \(1/400\) endpoint, a fixed atom, or a twin-prime theorem.
