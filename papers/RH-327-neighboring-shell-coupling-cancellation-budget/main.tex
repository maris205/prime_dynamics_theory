\begin{abstract}
The critical sibling cannot be discarded as a Gaussian tail, but its equal
forward mass does not determine a signed trace contribution.  We first
define the missing object directly at the cyclic-trace level.  A
basepoint partition into the boundary window, its neighboring critical
sibling, and the far complement gives an exact Hardy-scaled decomposition
of the actual raw trace packet into boundary, shell, and remainder slots.
No smallness of the remainder or sign of the shell is inferred.  We then
construct an exchange-symmetric two-channel Markov completion retaining the
clearance parameter and the oriented affine payload of RH-323.  Every
branch-blind power compression is independent of the exchange contrast,
whereas the trace power equals $1+c^m$.  Thus the archived local probability
data do not identify the shell trace.  For a fixed reference contrast
$c_0$, the even-order signed defect has the exact interval
$[-L|c_0|^{2k},L(1-|c_0|^{2k})]$, yielding a sharp cancellation-distance
formula.  A fixed nonzero fraction of the scale forces at least one contrast
to approach unit modulus at rate $1-O(k^{-1})$.  These are exact localized
and synthetic-model theorems, not an identification of the physical shell,
a joint first-alias matching theorem, or a full-trace replacement.
\end{abstract}

\section{Actual localized trace slots}

Let $u_c\in(1,2)$ be the root of
$u^3-2u^2+2u-2=0$, put $f(x)=1-u_cx^2$, and let $K_\sigma$ be the
row-normalized noisy Markov operator with smooth kernel $p_\sigma(x,y)$ on
$[0,1]$.  Define the deterministic flat trace by
\begin{equation}
 P_n=\sum_{f^n(x)=x}\frac1{|1-(f^n)'(x)|}
 \label{eq:flat-trace}
\end{equation}
Put $r_H=0.85$.  The raw Hardy packet is
\begin{equation}
 \cT_{\sigma,n}=r_H^{-n}\{\Tr K_\sigma^n-P_n\}.
 \label{eq:raw-packet}
\end{equation}

Put
\begin{equation}
 \lambda=2u_c(u_c-1),
 \qquad
 \beta=(r_H\sqrt\lambda)^{-1},
 \qquad
 R=1.4.
 \label{eq:route-constants}
\end{equation}
All first-alias asymptotics below are taken as $\sigma\to0$ along
\begin{equation}
 k=k_\sigma
 =\frac{\log(1/\sigma)}{2\log\lambda}+O(1),
 \qquad
 \eta_\sigma=k_\sigma-\frac{\log(1/\sigma)}{2\log\lambda},
 \label{eq:natural-clock}
\end{equation}
with bounded $\eta_\sigma$.
Passing to a fixed-phase subsequence when needed, we write
$\eta_\sigma\to\eta$.

We retain the first-alias clearance rather than suppressing its integer
phase.  Let $p_{2k}$ be the distinguished deterministic boundary periodic
point, set
\begin{align}
 \Delta_k^{\rm clr}
 &=1-p_{2k}=C_{\rm b}\lambda^{-2k}\{1+o(1)\},
 & C_{\rm b}&>0,
 \label{eq:clearance-source}
 \\
 h(x)&=\sqrt{\frac{1-\sqrt{(1-x)/u_c}}{u_c}},
 & s_{\sigma,k}&=h(p_{2k})
\end{align}
and write
\begin{equation}
 d_{\sigma,k}=\frac{\Delta_k^{\rm clr}}\sigma,
 \qquad
 b=u_c^{-1/2},
 \qquad
 q_{{\rm b},\sigma,k}=\frac{b-s_{\sigma,k}}{\sqrt\sigma},
 \qquad
 q_{\sigma,k}(x)=\frac{x-s_{\sigma,k}}{\sqrt\sigma},
 \label{eq:sibling-coordinates}
\end{equation}
where $s_{\sigma,k}$ is the final source point.  The archived inverse-branch
expansion gives
\begin{equation}
 q_{{\rm b},\sigma,k}
 =\frac{\sqrt{d_{\sigma,k}}}{2u_c}
  +O(\sqrt\sigma\,d_{\sigma,k}).
 \label{eq:partition-asymptotic}
\end{equation}
Moreover, the natural clock gives
\begin{equation}
 d_{\sigma,k}=C_{\rm b}\lambda^{-2\eta_\sigma}\{1+o(1)\}
 \longrightarrow C_{\rm b}\lambda^{-2\eta}
 \label{eq:clearance-phase}
\end{equation}
along a fixed-phase subsequence.
For a fixed window radius $A>0$, choose the half-open physical sibling
windows
\begin{align}
 J^-_{\sigma,k,A}
 &=\{x\in[0,1]:q_{{\rm b},\sigma,k}-A
     \le q_{\sigma,k}(x)<q_{{\rm b},\sigma,k}\},
 \label{eq:left-window}\\
 J^+_{\sigma,k,A}
 &=\{x\in[0,1]:q_{{\rm b},\sigma,k}
     \le q_{\sigma,k}(x)\le q_{{\rm b},\sigma,k}+A\},
 \label{eq:right-window}
\end{align}
and let $F_{\sigma,k,A}$ be their complement.  The labels $-$ and $+$ here
refer to the two RH-19 physical branches.  They are not the signs in the
RH-323 coordinate orientation $(+,-,+)$.

For a measurable set $J\subset[0,1]$, let $M_J$ be multiplication by its
indicator and define
\begin{align}
 L_{\sigma,n}(J)&=\Tr(M_JK_\sigma^n),
 \label{eq:localized-noisy}\\
 P_n(J)&=\sum_{\substack{f^n(x)=x\\x\in J}}
       \frac1{|1-(f^n)'(x)|}.
 \label{eq:localized-flat}
\end{align}

\begin{theorem}[Basepoint-localized cyclic trace partition]
\label{thm:localized-partition}
For every $n\ge2$ for which $K_\sigma^n$ is trace class,
\begin{equation}
 L_{\sigma,n}(J)
 =\int_{x_0\in J}\int_{[0,1]^{n-1}}
   \prod_{j=0}^{n-1}p_\sigma(x_j,x_{j+1})
   \,dx_1\cdots dx_{n-1}\,dx_0,
 \qquad x_n=x_0.
 \label{eq:closed-loop-observation}
\end{equation}
With the partition in \eqref{eq:left-window}--\eqref{eq:right-window}, define
\begin{align}
 \cB_{\sigma,k,n}
 &=r_H^{-n}\{L_{\sigma,n}(J^-_{\sigma,k,A})
                   -P_n(J^-_{\sigma,k,A})\},
 \label{eq:boundary-slot}\\
 \cS_{\sigma,k,n}
 &=r_H^{-n}\{L_{\sigma,n}(J^+_{\sigma,k,A})
                   -P_n(J^+_{\sigma,k,A})\},
 \label{eq:shell-slot}\\
 \cR_{\sigma,k,n}
 &=r_H^{-n}\{L_{\sigma,n}(F_{\sigma,k,A})
                   -P_n(F_{\sigma,k,A})\}.
 \label{eq:far-slot}
\end{align}
Then the actual raw trace packet has the exact signed decomposition
\begin{equation}
 \boxed{\cT_{\sigma,n}
 =\cB_{\sigma,k,n}+\cS_{\sigma,k,n}+\cR_{\sigma,k,n}.}
 \label{eq:raw-decomposition}
\end{equation}
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Equation \eqref{eq:closed-loop-observation} is the diagonal-kernel trace of
$M_JK_\sigma^n$.  The three indicators sum to one, so linearity of the trace
splits $\Tr K_\sigma^n$.  The fixed points in \eqref{eq:flat-trace} are
partitioned by the same three sets.  Subtract and multiply by $r_H^{-n}$.
\end{proof}

This theorem constructs an actual cyclic observation and an actual signed
shell slot.  It does not identify \eqref{eq:shell-slot} with the RH-19
one-pullback $L^2$ mass or with the RH-322--RH-324 forward probability laws.
The localization marks a closed-loop basepoint; the older branch-return
operators mark a prescribed time-labeled path.  No estimate here proves that
$\cR_{\sigma,k,2k}$ is small.

\section{The exact first-alias budget}

Write the peripheral parity eigenvalue as
\begin{equation}
 \lambda_-(\sigma)=-(1-\delta_\sigma),
 \qquad
 \delta_\sigma=C_*\sqrt\sigma+o(\sqrt\sigma),
 \qquad C_*>0,
 \label{eq:parity-type}
\end{equation}
and, for the archived boundary-cycle multiplier $M_k$, type the finite
counterloop radius by
\begin{equation}
 \beta_k=\frac{|M_k|^{-1/(2k)}}{r_H}
 =\beta\exp\left[-\frac{\log C_M}{2k}+o(k^{-1})\right],
 \qquad C_M>0.
 \label{eq:counterloop-type}
\end{equation}
The even parity packet and first-alias counterloop defect are
\begin{align}
 \cP_{\sigma,2k}
 &=r_H^{-2k}\{1-\lambda_-(\sigma)^{2k}\}>0,
 \label{eq:parity-packet}\\
 \cA_{k,2k}
 &=(2k-2)\beta_k^{2k}+2\beta^{2k}>0.
 \label{eq:alias-defect}
\end{align}
Define the RH-326 coefficient residual at $n=2k$ by the exact ledger
\begin{equation}
 e_{\sigma,k,2k}
 =\cB_{\sigma,k,2k}+\cS_{\sigma,k,2k}
  +\cR_{\sigma,k,2k}+\cP_{\sigma,2k}-\cA_{k,2k},
 \label{eq:joint-ledger}
\end{equation}
Thus the positive packets occur with signs $+\cP-\cA$.  No rounded decimal
inequality for $C_*$ or $C_M$ is used.
Let
\begin{equation}
 H_k=kR^{-2k}.
 \label{eq:target}
\end{equation}

\begin{proposition}[Necessary signed cancellation precision]
\label{prop:necessary-budget}
If $e_{\sigma,k,2k}=o(H_k)$ and
$\cR_{\sigma,k,2k}=o(H_k)$, then necessarily
\begin{equation}
 \cB_{\sigma,k,2k}+\cS_{\sigma,k,2k}
 =\cA_{k,2k}-\cP_{\sigma,2k}+o(H_k).
 \label{eq:necessary-match}
\end{equation}
The archived counterloop asymptotic gives
\begin{equation}
 \cA_{k,2k}=\frac{2k}{C_M}\beta^{2k}\{1+o(1)\},
 \qquad
 \frac{H_k}{\cA_{k,2k}}
 =\frac{C_M}{2}(\beta R)^{-2k}\{1+o(1)\}.
 \label{eq:relative-target}
\end{equation}
Hence \eqref{eq:necessary-match} is equivalently the exact-centered demand
\begin{equation}
 \frac{\cB+\cS}{\cA}
 -\left(1-\frac{\cP}{\cA}\right)
 =o\bigl((\beta R)^{-2k}\bigr).
 \label{eq:relative-demand}
\end{equation}
Along a fixed phase subsequence,
$\cP/\cA=C_*C_M\lambda^\eta+o(1)$, but that leading phase law alone does
not supply the much finer error in \eqref{eq:relative-demand}.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Rearrange \eqref{eq:joint-ledger}.  Equation
\eqref{eq:relative-target} is the RH-326 multiplier asymptotic divided into
\eqref{eq:target}.  Division of \eqref{eq:necessary-match} by $\cA$ proves
\eqref{eq:relative-demand}.
\end{proof}

No sign is assigned to the required quantity in
\eqref{eq:necessary-match}.  In particular, the repository does not contain
an interval certificate for $C_*C_M\lambda<1$.

\section{A branch-blind exchange completion}

The localized trace slots are exact, but their asymptotics remain unknown.
We now isolate the missing information with a synthetic two-channel model.
Let
\begin{equation}
 \Pi_{\rm s}=\frac12\begin{pmatrix}1&1\\1&1\end{pmatrix},
 \qquad
 \Pi_{\rm a}=\frac12\begin{pmatrix}1&-1\\-1&1\end{pmatrix},
 \qquad
 \bK_c=\Pi_{\rm s}+c\Pi_{\rm a},\quad |c|\le1.
 \label{eq:exchange-matrix}
\end{equation}
Write $E_-=\operatorname{diag}(1,0)$ and
$E_+=\operatorname{diag}(0,1)$ for the two branch-coordinate projectors.
This is a nonnegative symmetric Markov matrix.  Its branch labels are the
physical sibling coordinate; the sign of $c$ is neither a parity sign nor a
sign of an individual branch mass.

To retain the RH-323 payload, define
\begin{equation}
 \phi(t)=\frac{e^{-t^2/2}}{\sqrt{2\pi}},
 \qquad
 \Phi(t)=\int_{-\infty}^t\phi(s)\,ds,
 \qquad
 g_d(v)=\frac{\phi(v-d)}{\Phi(d)}\mathbf1_{[0,\infty)}(v),
 \quad d\ge0,
 \label{eq:affine-ingredients}
\end{equation}
and let $J_d$ be the probability law
\begin{equation}
 dJ_d(v,u,w)
 =g_d(v)\phi(u+\alpha v)\phi(w+\lambda u)\,dv\,du\,dw,
 \label{eq:affine-law}
\end{equation}
where $\alpha=2u_c$ and $\kappa_{\rm aff}=\alpha\lambda$.  It has orientation
$(V,U,W)=(+,-,+)$ and its output is compared after the shift
$W-\kappa_{\rm aff}d$.  On $L^2(J_d)$ define the rank-one Markov reset
\begin{equation}
 G_df=\left(\int f\,dJ_d\right)\mathbf1,
 \label{eq:reset}
\end{equation}
so $G_d$ is a rank-one trace-class projection with $\Tr G_d=1$.
Put $Q_{d,c}=G_d\otimes\bK_c$.  This is an explicit synthetic
completion, not the physical noisy kernel.

\begin{theorem}[All-power branch-blind nonidentifiability]
\label{thm:nonidentifiability}
Let $e_{\rm s}=2^{-1/2}(1,1)^T$ and let
$I_{\rm s}h=h\otimes e_{\rm s}$.  For every $m\ge1$,
\begin{align}
 \bK_c^m&=\Pi_{\rm s}+c^m\Pi_{\rm a},
 \label{eq:power-law}\\
 I_{\rm s}^*Q_{d,c}^mI_{\rm s}&=G_d,
 \label{eq:blind-compression}\\
 \Tr Q_{d,c}^m&=1+c^m.
 \label{eq:trace-contrast}
\end{align}
Moreover, the two branch-localized traces are exactly equal:
\begin{equation}
 \Tr\{(I\otimes E_\pm)Q_{d,c}^m\}
 =\frac{1+c^m}{2}.
 \label{eq:equal-localized-traces}
\end{equation}
Thus the clearance, affine law, orientation, output shift, and every
symmetric power compression are identical for all $c$, while the trace
power varies with $c$.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
The two projectors in \eqref{eq:exchange-matrix} are complementary and
orthogonal, proving \eqref{eq:power-law}.  Since $G_d^m=G_d$ and
$\Pi_{\rm s}e_{\rm s}=e_{\rm s}$ while
$\Pi_{\rm a}e_{\rm s}=0$, tensor compression gives
\eqref{eq:blind-compression}.  Both $G_d$ and $\Pi_{\rm s}$ have trace one,
and $\Pi_{\rm a}$ has trace one, proving \eqref{eq:trace-contrast}.
The diagonal entries of \eqref{eq:power-law} are equal to
$(1+c^m)/2$, proving \eqref{eq:equal-localized-traces}.
\end{proof}

The theorem is a closure-observation no-go: branch-blind probability data
do not identify the antisymmetric loop contrast.  It does not say that the
actual Gaussian shell is small, large, or represented by any $Q_{d,c}$.

\section{Fixed-reference shell interval and sharp residual}

Let $L>0$ be a typed shell scale and let $c_0\in[-1,1]$ be a fixed
deterministic reference contrast.  At the even first alias define the
signed exchange defect
\begin{equation}
 X_{k,L}(c;c_0)=L\{c^{2k}-c_0^{2k}\}.
 \label{eq:exchange-defect}
\end{equation}
Its sign comes only from noisy-minus-reference subtraction.  A single
positive Markov completion has no signed shell defect by itself.

\begin{theorem}[Fixed-reference cancellation interval]
\label{thm:fixed-budget}
For fixed $c_0$, the exact image of \eqref{eq:exchange-defect} is
\begin{equation}
 \boxed{
 I_{k,L,c_0}
 =[-L|c_0|^{2k},\,L(1-|c_0|^{2k})].}
 \label{eq:fixed-interval}
\end{equation}
For any demanded shell value $D\in\mathbb R$,
\begin{equation}
 \inf_{|c|\le1}|X_{k,L}(c;c_0)-D|
 =\dist(D,I_{k,L,c_0}).
 \label{eq:sharp-distance}
\end{equation}
If $D$ lies in the interval, a realizing nonnegative contrast is
\begin{equation}
 c=\left(\frac DL+|c_0|^{2k}\right)^{1/(2k)}.
 \label{eq:realizing-contrast}
\end{equation}
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
As $|c|\le1$, the even power $c^{2k}$ fills $[0,1]$.  Translation by
$-|c_0|^{2k}$ and multiplication by $L$ gives
\eqref{eq:fixed-interval}.  The closest attainable scalar is the projection
of $D$ onto this closed interval, proving \eqref{eq:sharp-distance}.
Solving \eqref{eq:exchange-defect} for $c$ gives
\eqref{eq:realizing-contrast}.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[Free-pair information-class budget]
\label{cor:free-budget}
If both $c$ and $c_0$ are allowed to vary over the unidentified completion
class, the union of defect values is $[-L,L]$ and the sharp residual is
\begin{equation}
 (|D|-L)_+.
 \label{eq:free-distance}
\end{equation}
This is an information-class result.  It is not permissible to optimize the
physical deterministic reference when testing an actual matching equation.
\end{corollary}

\begin{proposition}[Same-order contrast must approach the unit edge]
\label{prop:edge-law}
If $|X_{k,L}(c;c_0)|\ge\delta L$ for a fixed $0<\delta\le1$, then
\begin{equation}
 \max\{|c|,|c_0|\}\ge\delta^{1/(2k)}.
 \label{eq:edge-necessity}
\end{equation}
Consequently at least one contrast obeys
\begin{equation}
 1-|c_*|\le\frac{\log(1/\delta)}{2k}+O(k^{-2}).
 \label{eq:edge-gap}
\end{equation}
If both contrasts are bounded by $1-\varepsilon$, then
$|X_{k,L}|/L\le(1-\varepsilon)^{2k}$ and the exchange defect is not a
fixed nonzero fraction of $L$.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Put $r=\max\{|c|,|c_0|\}$.  Both even powers lie in $[0,r^{2k}]$, so their
difference has modulus at most $r^{2k}$.  This proves
\eqref{eq:edge-necessity}.  Expanding
$\delta^{1/(2k)}=\exp\{\log\delta/(2k)\}$ gives
\eqref{eq:edge-gap}.  The uniform-gap statement is immediate.
\end{proof}

For example, with $2k=8$ and $c_0=0.8$, the unit-scale interval is
\begin{equation}
 [-0.16777216,\,0.83222784].
 \label{eq:numeric-interval}
\end{equation}
Demands $-0.25$ and $0.9$ therefore leave exact residual fractions
$0.08222784$ and $0.06777216$, respectively.  These are ordinary evaluations
of exact formulas for a synthetic model, not physical shell measurements.

\section{RH-328 interface and claim boundary}

For the actual first-alias ledger, put
\begin{equation}
 D_{\sigma,k}=\cA_{k,2k}-\cP_{\sigma,2k}
               -\cB_{\sigma,k,2k}.
 \label{eq:actual-demand}
\end{equation}
If an RH-328 model proposes to replace the actual localized shell by
$X_{k,L}(c;c_0)$, then its best leading residual before $\cR$ is exactly
\begin{equation}
 \dist(D_{\sigma,k},I_{k,L,c_0}).
 \label{eq:handoff-distance}
\end{equation}
This distance optimizes over every noisy contrast in the synthetic class.
It is only a best-case reachability obstruction and does not show that the
physical contrast attains the closest point.  A necessary pre-screen is
\begin{equation}
 \dist(D_{\sigma,k},I_{k,L,c_0})=o(kR^{-2k}).
 \label{eq:reachability-screen}
\end{equation}
Actual closure through this model requires an identified contrast
$c_{\sigma,k}^{\rm phys}$ satisfying
\begin{equation}
 |X_{k,L}(c_{\sigma,k}^{\rm phys};c_0)-D_{\sigma,k}|
 =o(kR^{-2k}),
 \qquad
 \cR_{\sigma,k,2k}=o(kR^{-2k}),
 \label{eq:handoff-requirements}
\end{equation}
together with a theorem identifying the physical trace observation, shell
scale $L$, noisy contrast $c$, and fixed reference $c_0$.  None of those
identifications is supplied by the reset completion.

The typed handoff retains $\sigma$, $k$, period $2k$, phase $\eta$,
clearance $d$, the coordinates $(V,U,W)$ with orientation $(+,-,+)$, the
shift $\kappa_{\rm aff}d$, Hardy normalization $r_H^{-2k}$, and the exact
sign ledger
\begin{equation}
 e=\cB+\cS+\cR+\cP-\cA.
 \label{eq:typed-ledger}
\end{equation}
RH-325's operator Duhamel weights must still include the trace-observation
norm and every prefix/suffix product norm.  Markov $L^1$ contraction does not
prove either requirement in \eqref{eq:handoff-requirements}.

\begin{remark}[Claim boundary]
The paper proves an actual basepoint-localized raw-trace partition and exact
theorems for a synthetic exchange completion.  It does not control the
magnitude or sign of the actual shell slot, identify the RH-323 probability
law with the localized cyclic trace, prove a physical exchange parameter,
bound the far trace remainder, control the second physical leg, or establish
the joint alias/parity/shell matching equation.  No full-trace replacement
or actual divergence theorem follows.  Gates A--E remain false/open.  This
paper constructs no Hilbert--Polya operator, identifies no Riemann zero,
proves no von Mangoldt trace formula or completed-zeta divisor equality, and
does not imply RH.
\end{remark}

RH-328 may now test a fixed-reference joint matching equation only after it
supplies the physical localized observation estimates and the shell
parameters required in
\eqref{eq:reachability-screen}--\eqref{eq:handoff-requirements}.
