\begin{abstract}
TPC-278 showed that a favorable signed packet cross term can change sign under
a nearby finite shell or clock choice.  This paper isolates the exact
finite-dimensional input that a growing source theorem would have to provide.
For four vectors, let $D$ be the packet-diagonal energy, $G$ the energy of
their sum, and $E$ the net real cross term.  We prove the exact deficit
identity $\Delta=1-G/D=-2E/D$ and show that a power gain is equivalent to an
upper bound on $G/D$, or equivalently to $\Delta$ being within the required
power scale of one.  We also prove the sharp pairwise-coherence envelope
$G/D\leq\min(4,1+3\mu)$, together with its reciprocal gain floor.  Orthogonal
packets attain the no-gain endpoint, while explicit near-canceling packets
attain arbitrarily large gain.  An exact interval transfer of the twelve
TPC-278 source rows validates the coordinate change but supplies no
asymptotic or arithmetic credit.  The result is a reusable structural
theorem and a precise firewall: pairwise absolute coherence alone cannot
pay a positive power; a schedule-specific aggregate deficit estimate is the
minimal missing hypothesis.
\end{abstract}

\section{Motivation and notation}

The four-packet source interface in TPC-277 and TPC-278 produces vectors
$V_0,V_1,V_2,V_3$ in a real or complex Hilbert space.  The quantities relevant
to signed reassembly are
\[
 D=\sum_{j=0}^3\|V_j\|^2,\qquad
 G=\left\|\sum_{j=0}^3V_j\right\|^2,qquad
 E=\sum_{0\leq j<k\leq3}\operatorname{Re}\langle V_j,V_k\rangle.
\]
TPC-277 proved the universal floor $G\leq4D$ and found a favorable sign on
an eight-row finite source scan.  TPC-278 then varied only a declared shell
endpoint or clock and found four finite sign flips.  Thus the next useful
question is not whether a sign happens on one table, but what exact source
inequality is equivalent to the desired gain.

Throughout, assume $D>0$.  When $G>0$ write
\[
 r=\frac DG,\qquad q=\frac GD,\qquad
 \Delta=1-q=\frac{D-G}{D}.
\]

\section{The exact deficit criterion}

\begin{theorem}[minimal four-packet criterion]
For any four Hilbert-space vectors with $D>0$,
\[
 0\leq q\leq4,\qquad G-D=2E,\qquad \Delta=-\frac{2E}{D}.
\]
If $G>0$, then
\[
 r=\frac1q=\frac1{1-\Delta}.
\]
Consequently, for $b>0$, $\gamma\geq0$, and $X\geq1$,
\[
 r\geq bX^\gamma
 \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
 q\leq b^{-1}X^{-\gamma}
 \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
 \Delta\geq1-b^{-1}X^{-\gamma}.
\]
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Expanding the squared norm gives
\[
 G=D+2\sum_{j<k}\operatorname{Re}\langle V_j,V_k\rangle=D+2E.
\]
The lower bound $G\geq0$ is immediate, while Cauchy--Schwarz gives
\[
 G\leq\left(\sum_j\|V_j\|\right)^2
 \leq4\sum_j\|V_j\|^2=4D.
\]
Division by $D$ yields the first two identities and $q\in[0,4]$.
For $G>0$, reciprocal division gives $r=1/q=(1-\Delta)^{-1}$, and taking
positive reciprocals proves the three equivalent inequalities.
\end{proof}

The theorem is an equivalence, rather than merely a sufficient estimate.
In particular, the sign condition $E\leq0$ gives only $q\leq1$ and hence
$r\geq1$.  It says nothing about how close $q$ is to zero.  A power gain
requires the stronger near-cancellation scale
$q\ll X^{-\gamma}$, or the equivalent deficit condition in the theorem.
If $G=0$, the sum packet is exactly zero and the same conclusion holds in
the extended convention $r=+\infty$; the displayed reciprocal formula is
used only for $G>0$.

\begin{corollary}[sign is not a power estimate]
No assertion of the form $E\leq0$ alone can imply
$r\geq bX^\gamma$ for any fixed $b>0$ and $\gamma>0$ on an unbounded scale.
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
Take four mutually orthogonal unit vectors.  Then $D=G=4$, $E=0$, and
$r=1$ at every scale.  This also gives $\Delta=0$ in the exact criterion.
\end{proof}

\section{What pairwise coherence can and cannot prove}

For nonzero packet norms define
\[
 \mu=\max_{j<k}
 \frac{|\langle V_j,V_k\rangle|}{\|V_j\|\,\|V_k\|}\in[0,1],
\]
ignoring pairs with a zero norm (their contribution is zero).  This is an
absolute, phase-blind statistic.

\begin{theorem}[sharp pairwise-coherence envelope]
For four packets with $D>0$,
\[
 G\leq D+2\mu\sum_{j<k}\|V_j\|\,\|V_k\|
 \leq(1+3\mu)D,
\]
and therefore
\[
 \frac GD\leq\min\{4,1+3\mu\}.
\]
If $G>0$, then
\[
 r\geq\max\left\{\frac14,\frac1{1+3\mu}\right\}.
\]
The constants are sharp for every $\mu\in[0,1]$.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Put $a_j=\|V_j\|$.  The definition of $\mu$ and the triangle inequality
give
\[
 G=D+2E\leq D+2\mu\sum_{j<k}a_ja_k
 =D+\mu\left(\left(\sum_ja_j\right)^2-D\right).
\]
By Cauchy--Schwarz, $(\sum_ja_j)^2\leq4D$, proving the second inequality.
Combining it with the universal estimate $G\leq4D$ gives the minimum.
Taking reciprocals gives the gain floor when $G>0$.

For sharpness, let
\[
 \Gamma_\mu=(1-\mu)I_4+\mu\mathbf1\mathbf1^*.
\]
Its eigenvalues are $1-\mu$ (multiplicity three) and $1+3\mu$, so it is a
Gram matrix for every $\mu\in[0,1]$.  Four unit vectors realizing this Gram
matrix have $D=4$, $G=4(1+3\mu)$, and pairwise coherence exactly $\mu$.
Thus equality holds in the coherence envelope.  At $\mu=1$ it also attains
the universal floor $r=1/4$.
\end{proof}

The envelope is useful as a ceiling, but it is not a signed source theorem.
At $\mu=0$ it gives $r\geq1$, not a positive power.  More generally, any
uniform bound on $\mu$ leaves $1/(1+3\mu)$ at constant scale.  The missing
quantity is the signed aggregate deficit $\Delta$, not an absolute pairwise
coherence bound.

\section{Sharp adversaries and near cancellation}

The equicorrelated Gram family contains two instructive endpoints.  At
$\mu=0$ the packets are orthogonal and $r=1$, so no positive power can be
inferred from coherence information.  At $\mu=1$ they are aligned and
$r=1/4$, showing that the four-packet universal floor is exact.

The opposite phenomenon, arbitrarily large gain, is also explicit.  For
$0<\varepsilon<1$, take scalar packets
\[
 V_0=V_1=V_2=1,\qquad V_3=-(3-\varepsilon).
\]
Then
\[
 D=3+(3-\varepsilon)^2,\qquad G=\varepsilon^2,qquad
 r=\frac{3+(3-\varepsilon)^2}{\varepsilon^2},
\qquad
 \Delta=1-\frac{\varepsilon^2}{3+(3-\varepsilon)^2}.
\]
As $\varepsilon\downarrow0$, the gain diverges, but $\mu=1$ throughout.
Thus large gain is compatible with maximal absolute coherence and is
controlled only by the aggregate cancellation scale.  The certificate uses
$\varepsilon=1/10$, for which $D=1141/100$, $G=1/100$, and $r=1141$.

\section{Route consequence and claim firewall}

The exact criterion identifies the next source-level theorem without leaving
room for a hidden normalization step.  To obtain a gain
$r\geq bX^\gamma$, one must prove
\[
 \frac{G}{D}\leq b^{-1}X^{-\gamma}
 \quad\text{or equivalently}\quad
 \Delta\geq1-b^{-1}X^{-\gamma}
\]
on the precisely declared shell, clock, masks, and packet schedule.  A
negative cross-term sign is strictly weaker, and pairwise absolute coherence
is phase-blind.  The source-level deficit estimate is therefore the minimal
missing hypothesis for the signed-gain branch.

This paper proves a finite-dimensional theorem and certifies a coordinate
transfer only.  It does not estimate the arithmetic coefficient, prove an
$L^2$ bound, pay the strict $1/400$ endpoint, or imply a twin-prime result.
The Session-named Route-A/Route-B evaluator files are absent in this checkout;
the local proof package, exact certificate, independent checker, stress audit,
and Bridge-B checker supply the fail-closed scoped evaluation.
