\begin{abstract}
The fixed shift stored on a cut record and the fixed-shift selector
after physical grouping are different mathematical objects.  We
formalize their relation through a commuting square.  For an exact
grouping matrix \(G\) with source and target shift selectors,
\[
 P_{\rm phys}G=GP_{\rm src}
\]
if and only if every aggregate cross-selector block vanishes.  This
operator identity is still not a pathwise provenance certificate:
opposite cross-shift occurrence edges can cancel in the same matrix
entry.  On the row-separated occurrence lift, commutation is
equivalent to every nonzero occurrence edge preserving membership in
the prescribed-\(h_0\) slice.  We prove this exact criterion and a
sharp cancellation counterexample.  Applying it to the current
archive gives one positive structural result,
\(P_{h_0}^{\rm cut}=I\), already supported by cut metadata
intertwining.  The physical grouping \(G\), later stage matrices and
downstream selector are absent, so their totality and commuting
square remain \textsc{not-testable}.  Treating the cut selector as a
downstream selector, or aggregate cancellation as shift provenance,
stops only those declared implementations.  No physical cover or
positive arithmetic saving is obtained.
\end{abstract}

\section{Cut selection versus downstream selection}

Fix a prescribed nonzero shift \(h_0\).  TPC-136 proves that every
nonzero edge through the complete cut archive retains the native
shift tag \citep{WangTPC136}.  If \(E_{\rm cut}\) is its row space,
define the cut coordinate selector by
\[
 P_{h_0}^{\rm cut}e_c
 =
 \begin{cases}
 e_c,&h(c)=h_0,\\
 0,&h(c)\ne h_0.
 \end{cases}
\]
Every row in the fixed-\(h_0\) archive has the first case, whence
\begin{equation}
 \boxed{P_{h_0}^{\rm cut}=I_{E_{\rm cut}}.}
 \label{eq:cutidentity}
\end{equation}
This is a literal \(\Lone\) fact, not a localization estimate.

TPC-125's downstream selector is defined on later shift-tagged record
spaces and must intertwine every later stage \citep{WangTPC125}.
Those stages can split, aggregate or leak between shift labels.
Equation \eqref{eq:cutidentity} says nothing about an unarchived
stage.

\begin{definition}[Two selector namespaces]
We write \(P_{h_0}^{\rm cut}\) only for the proved cut selector and
\(P_{h_0}^{\rm down}\) only for a selector on a completed downstream
archive.  Equality of their names or numerical \(h_0\)-values is not
an intertwining theorem.
\end{definition}

\section{Physical occurrence grouping}

Let \(\mathcal O\) be a fully typed source occurrence set and
\(\mathcal P\) a physical occurrence set.  An exact physical
grouping matrix
\[
 G:\mathbb C^{\mathcal O}\longrightarrow\mathbb C^{\mathcal P}
\]
must store, on every nonzero edge:
\begin{enumerate}[label=(\roman*),leftmargin=2.2em]
\item source occurrence, physical occurrence and physical group IDs;
\item exact reconstruction multiplier;
\item physical and computational multiplicities and any
inverse-aggregation relation;
\item masks, cover class and reconnection destination;
\item source and target shift tags; and
\item the one inherited physical normalization.
\end{enumerate}

The physical group key is not determined by a determinant label or
by equality of two numerical coefficients.  TPC-124 shows why a
physical aggregate can hide a native residual, while TPC-117 reports
that the actual growing physical map is unavailable
\citep{WangTPC117,WangTPC124}.

Let \(P_{\rm src}\) and \(P_{\rm phys}\) be the coordinate
projections onto records tagged by \(h_0\) in the source and target,
respectively.

\begin{theorem}[Aggregate block criterion]
\label{thm:aggregate}
The operator square
\begin{equation}
 P_{\rm phys}G=GP_{\rm src}
 \label{eq:square}
\end{equation}
commutes if and only if
\[
 G_{p,o}=0
\]
whenever exactly one of \(h(p),h(o)\) equals \(h_0\).
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
On a matrix entry \((p,o)\), the left side of \eqref{eq:square} is
\[
 \mathbf1_{h(p)=h_0}G_{p,o},
\]
while the right side is
\[
 G_{p,o}\mathbf1_{h(o)=h_0}.
\]
They agree on same-selector blocks.  On a cross-selector block they
agree precisely when the entry vanishes.
\end{proof}

\section{Why aggregate commutation is not lineage}

A grouping matrix records the sum of all transformation paths between
one source and one target.  Let
\[
 \widehat{\mathcal E}
 =
 \{e:o(e)\to p(e)\}
\]
be the row-separated set of individual occurrence edges, with exact
nonzero multipliers \(g_e\).  The corresponding path lift
\(\widehat G\) has one row for each \(e\), so two edges never cancel
inside one row.

\begin{definition}[Occurrence-level shift preservation]
The grouping is pathwise shift preserving at \(h_0\) when every edge
with \(g_e\ne0\) satisfies
\[
 \mathbf1_{h(o(e))=h_0}
 =
 \mathbf1_{h(p(e))=h_0}.
\]
\end{definition}

\begin{theorem}[Row-separated commuting-square criterion]
\label{thm:pathwise}
For the row-separated occurrence lift, selector commutation is
equivalent to occurrence-level shift preservation.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
The row associated with \(e\) contains one source entry \(g_e\).
The corresponding selector defect is
\[
 \left(
 \mathbf1_{h(p(e))=h_0}
 -
 \mathbf1_{h(o(e))=h_0}
 \right)g_e.
\]
Because rows are separate, a defect in one occurrence cannot cancel
a defect in another.  The full defect vanishes exactly when every
displayed scalar vanishes.
\end{proof}

\begin{proposition}[Sharp hidden-leakage example]
\label{prop:hidden}
Aggregate commutation does not imply the pathwise condition.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Take a source \(o\) tagged \(h_0\) and a target \(p\) tagged
\(h_1\ne h_0\).  Add two occurrence edges \(o\to p\), with
multipliers \(1\) and \(-1\).  Their aggregate matrix entry is zero,
so the aggregate square commutes.  Each row-separated edge crosses
the selector boundary, so both fail the pathwise condition.
\end{proof}

This is the grouping analogue of the distinction between a scalar
zero defect and the row-separated matrix defect in TPC-123 and
TPC-136 \citep{WangTPC123,WangTPC136}.  A route that claims literal
shift provenance must use the pathwise statement.

\section{The current field boundary}

The TPC-143 occurrence obligations require a one-to-many
row-separated lift from every nonsoft cut path
\citep{WangTPC143}.  The required domain is
\[
 \mathcal C_X^{\mathsf{ETO}}\sqcup
 \mathcal C_X^{\mathsf{FUM}}.
\]
No finite sample census can replace that typed domain.

For \(G\), the current records lack physical occurrence IDs, group
keys, reconstruction multipliers, multiplicity rules, cover classes
and reconnection destinations.  For \(P_{h_0}^{\rm down}\), they lack
every later source/target shift pair and stage matrix.  Consequently
neither matrix in \eqref{eq:square} can be formed.

\begin{corollary}[Current map statuses]
\label{cor:status}
The exact cut selector and the two downstream statuses are
\[
\boxed{
\begin{aligned}
\mathsf{H1.cut\_Ph0}&=\PROVED_{\Lone},\\
\mathsf{H1.frontier\_G\_totality}&=\NT,\\
\mathsf{H1.frontier\_Ph0\_downstream\_totality}&=\NT.
\end{aligned}}
\]
The occurrence-level \(G/P_{h_0}\) commuting square is also
\(\NT\).
\end{corollary}

\section{Executable regression}

The standard-library script
\texttt{experiments/tpc145\_group\_shift\_audit.py} imports the
TPC-143 proof of \eqref{eq:cutidentity}.  Its actual manifest contains
no physical or downstream stage edge.  Separate synthetic fixtures
verify \cref{thm:aggregate,thm:pathwise,prop:hidden} exactly using
rational multipliers.

\begin{center}
\small
\begin{tabularx}{\textwidth}{@{}P{0.41\textwidth}P{0.20\textwidth}Y@{}}
\toprule
Statement & Status & Interpretation\\
\midrule
Cut selector \(P_{h_0}^{\rm cut}\)
& \(\PROVED_{\Lone}\) & Identity on the current cut rows.\\
Aggregate block criterion
& \(\PROVED_{\Lzero}\) & Exact finite matrix theorem.\\
Row-separated edge criterion
& \(\PROVED_{\Lzero}\) & Exact path-provenance theorem.\\

& \(\NT\) & Occurrence and stage edges are absent.\\
\bottomrule
\end{tabularx}
\end{center}

The audit rejects a frontier-only domain, promotion of the cut
selector, fabricated physical or stage edges, and promotion of
aggregate cancellation to pathwise provenance.  The two stopped
implementations are therefore precisely:
\[
\begin{split}
&\text{cut metadata as a downstream selector},\\
&\text{aggregate cancellation as shift provenance}.
\end{split}
\]
Both are scoped \(\STOP\) outcomes.  They do not stop a future
occurrence-augmented map.

\section{Claim boundary}

\begin{theorem}[Audited physical-shift conclusion]
The cut-stage identity \eqref{eq:cutidentity} is proved, but the
current source artifacts do not define a literal physical grouping
or downstream fixed-shift map.  Thus the selected augmented route
remains open at the occurrence-lift node and is not
architecture-infeasible.
\end{theorem}

This paper proves no physical cover, quotient observability bound,
frontier cancellation, localization cost, positive fixed-shift
\(\Ltwo\) estimate, endpoint below \(1/400\), parity advance,
prime-pair lower bound or twin-prime theorem.  Determinant reserve is
not charged as a physical grouping gain.  The only positive
application to the current archive is the exact and deliberately
stage-limited identity \(P_{h_0}^{\rm cut}=I\).
