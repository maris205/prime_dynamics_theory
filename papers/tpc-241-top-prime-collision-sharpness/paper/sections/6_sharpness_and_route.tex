\section{Fixed-power sharpness and route closure}\label{sec:sharpness}

\begin{corollary}[No fixed-power improvement]\label{cor:no_power}
Fix an admissible profile $\psi$, a number $\delta>0$, and a real number $A$.
There is no constant $C_{\psi,\delta,A}$ for which
\begin{equation}\label{eq:false_upper}
 \meanIx |K_\psi(n)|^2
 \leq C_{\psi,\delta,A}
 x^{1/48-\delta}(\log x)^A
\end{equation}
holds for all sufficiently large $x$.
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
Theorem~\ref{thm:window} has a positive liminf constant.  Dividing its lower
scale by the right side of \eqref{eq:false_upper} produces, up to a positive
constant,
\[
 \frac{x^\delta}{(\log x)^{A+1}},
\]
which tends to infinity for every fixed real $A$.  This contradicts
\eqref{eq:false_upper}.
\end{proof}

Corollary~\ref{cor:no_power} closes a specific route, not the whole twin-prime
program.  It excludes further fixed-power progress from a coefficient-blind
unsigned envelope on the same fixed-profile common-source kernel.  Three
features remain outside the theorem:

\begin{enumerate}
\item The sign of $C_h$ is erased by the absolute square.  Even on top primes,
      the identity $C_p=-\log p/p$ contributes only $|C_p|^2$.
\item The four literal polarized packets are not projected before the square.
      Their signed combination could cancel, attenuate, or annihilate the
      positive collision mode proved here.
\item The theorem supplies no arithmetic $L^2$ saving, no strict $1/400$
      payment, and no fixed-atom credit.
\end{enumerate}

The most economical next question is therefore sign-sensitive: project the
top-prime collision mode through the actual four-packet polarization before any
absolute square, or retain the literal $C_h$ signs across denominator bands.
Continuing to optimize unsigned bucket multiplicities cannot change the
fixed-power exponent.

The phrase ``sharp up to logarithms'' is used only in this fixed-power sense.
The present lower bound has one negative logarithm, while known structural
upper bounds have positive logarithmic factors.  Matching those logarithms is
an open refinement and is not needed for Corollary~\ref{cor:no_power}.
