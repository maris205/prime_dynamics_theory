\begin{abstract}
The preceding finite cross-panel audit found that one nuisance coefficient
vector does not fit two protocol-compatible twin-prime response panels.  We
test the smallest structured relaxation that still shares coefficients within
each panel: one signed panel-contrast column is added for each of three
nuisance categories.  The resulting six-column basis is exactly equivalent to
one shared nuisance vector per panel.  On the locked six-row finite panel, its
raw-energy residual retention is $0.2962189247$, just below the inherited
$0.30$ guard.  The same basis retains $0.3186506700$ after equal-row
normalization, and four cross-fit predictions retain
$0.3759486734$--$0.6342934197$.  Thus the raw crossing is a finite,
weighting-sensitive partial repair rather than a stable canonical law.  The
18 contrast holdout records retain $0.6372238668$--$0.9128543547$.  No
arithmetic, asymptotic, Route-A/Route-B, or twin-prime conclusion follows.
\end{abstract}

\section{Question and scope}

TPC-343 showed that a single nuisance coefficient vector across its TPC-341
and TPC-342 panels fails an in-sample residual guard under both raw and
equal-row weighting.  The next minimal question is whether the mismatch is
explained by a panel-level contrast while retaining a common law inside each
panel.  This paper makes that relaxation explicit and audits it independently.

All objects below are finite vectors generated by the repository's locked
source/operator model.  The words \textquotedblleft twin\textquotedblright{}
and \textquotedblleft nuisance\textquotedblright{} name masks and response
vectors; they do not assert an arithmetic noise decomposition.  In
particular, a finite fit is not a source-uniform estimate and does not pay any
Route-B loss budget.

\section{Frozen finite protocol}

The two panels are inherited without modification:
\begin{align*}
\mathcal P_{341}&:\ [48097,48608],\ [48609,49120],\
 [49217,49728],\\
\mathcal P_{342}&:\ [40097,40608],\ [40609,41120],\
 [41121,41632].
\end{align*}
Each row has source length $512$ and scale $1024$.  The operator is the
all-plus deleted-diagonal operator with $Q=54$, kernel exponent one, and
height $H=66$.  The nine controls are the identity, reversal, and seven fixed
affine coordinate bijections.  There are four source masks: twin-prime,
non-twin-prime shift, prime-power shift, and zero support.

For row $r$, write $y_r\in\R^{512}$ for the nine-control mean of the twin
output and $n_{r,j}\in\R^{512}$ for the mean of nuisance category $j$, where
$j$ ranges over the three nuisance categories.  The six rows are ordered with
the three TPC-341 rows first.  Define
\[
b_j=(n_{1,j},n_{2,j},n_{3,j},n_{4,j},n_{5,j},n_{6,j})^T,
\qquad
d_j=(n_{1,j},n_{2,j},n_{3,j},-n_{4,j},-n_{5,j},-n_{6,j})^T.
\]
The sign vector $(+1,-1)$ is fixed before the numerical readout.  The
baseline shared model uses the three columns $b_j$; the contrast model uses
the six columns $b_j,d_j$.

For equal-row weighting, each $y_r$ and all three $n_{r,j}$ in that row are
divided by $\norm{y_r}$.  This is a declared sensitivity transform, not a
canonical normalization.  The inherited in-sample decision guard is residual
retention $<0.30$; the hostile leave-one-control-out guard is retention
$>0.40$.

\section{Exact finite structure}

\begin{proposition}[contrast reparameterization]
\label{prop:contrast}
For every nuisance category $j$, let
\[
u_{1,j}=\frac{b_j+d_j}{2},\qquad
u_{2,j}=\frac{b_j-d_j}{2}.
\]
Then $u_{1,j}$ is supported on the TPC-341 block, $u_{2,j}$ is supported on
the TPC-342 block, and
\[
\operatorname{span}\{b_j,d_j\}
=\operatorname{span}\{u_{1,j},u_{2,j}\}.
\]
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
The definitions give
\[
u_{1,j}=(n_{1,j},n_{2,j},n_{3,j},0,0,0)^T,\qquad
u_{2,j}=(0,0,0,n_{4,j},n_{5,j},n_{6,j})^T.
\]
Thus both $u_{1,j}$ and $u_{2,j}$ are linear combinations of $b_j,d_j$.
Conversely $b_j=u_{1,j}+u_{2,j}$ and
$d_j=u_{1,j}-u_{2,j}$.  Each span contains the other.
\end{proof}

The proposition is a finite change of coordinates.  It says exactly what the
new model permits: separate nuisance coefficients for the two panels, with
the coefficient vector still shared over the three rows inside each panel.
It does not say that the two coefficient vectors are canonical or persist for
new source windows.

For any finite nuisance matrix $N$, let $P_N$ be the Euclidean orthogonal
projector onto its column space.  We use the exact identity
\[
\norm{Y}^2=\norm{P_NY}^2+\norm{(I-P_N)Y}^2.                 \tag{1}
\]
The implementation constructs $P_N$ from the positive singular vectors and
checks the decomposition gap.  A rational anchor, independent of the source
calculation, uses
$b=(1,0,0,1)^T$, $d=(1,0,0,-1)^T$.  The two reparameterized columns are
$u_1=(1,0,0,0)^T$ and $u_2=(0,0,0,1)^T$; projecting
$Y=(1,1,1,1)^T$ has energy $2$ and residual energy $2$.

The raw contrast result is a narrow crossing: its margin below $0.30$ is

The exact span identity in Proposition~\ref{prop:contrast} was also evaluated
by constructing the equivalent panel-supported columns directly.  The raw
and equal-row residual retentions agree with the contrast construction to the
declared numerical tolerance.  This validates the reparameterization but does
not turn the model into a universal basis.

\section{Holdout and cross-fit tests}

For each omitted control, nuisance columns are formed from the other eight
controls, and the six held-out twin outputs are projected onto the contrast
span.  Across the nine omissions, the raw retention range is
$0.6372238668$--$0.9039334258$ and the equal-row range is
$0.6880962075$--$0.9128543547$.  Hence all 18 hostile holdout values exceed
the $0.40$ guard.  This is a finite out-of-sample diagnostic for the declared
control orbit, not a probability statement.

As a separate transfer test, least-squares coefficients are fitted to the
three row means of one panel and applied without refitting to the other.  The
four prediction retentions are
\[
0.5156705854,\quad 0.3759486734
\quad\text{(raw, two directions)}
\]
and
\[
0.6342934197,\quad 0.4261160161
\quad\text{(equal-row, two directions)}.
\]
All four exceed $0.30$, so the low-residual cross-panel transfer criterion is
refuted on this finite pair.  These are prediction residuals, not orthogonal
projection residuals; equation (1) is not asserted for them.

\section{Independent implementation and claim firewall}

The producer checks the TPC-343 parent certificate and then uses the
hash-locked TPC-340 source and operator implementation.  The independent checker
does not import the producer: it uses the separately hash-locked reverse-shell
engine, rebuilds the source masks and all nine controls, and reimplements the
projection, contrast, holdout, and cross-fit calculations.  A nine-mutation
stress suite rejects changes to row geometry, panel signs, guard values,
provenance semantics, rank, and the exact anchor.  Normal and optimized
executions are required to have empty standard error and byte-identical
standard output.

\section{Conclusion and next question}

The panel-contrast basis gives a useful but sharply delimited result.  It
explains enough of the cross-panel mismatch to move the raw pooled residual
from $0.3198$ to $0.2962$, while its equal-row value $0.3187$ and the cross-fit
diagnostics prevent a robustness claim.  The reusable structure is
\[
\text{shared span}\ \longrightarrow\ \text{signed contrast}\ \longrightarrow\
\text{panel-adaptive span}\ \longrightarrow\ \text{weight and transfer audit}.
\]
The next natural finite question is geometric: compute principal angles
between the two panel nuisance subspaces and test whether the apparent
improvement is a stable Grassmannian relation or a coordinate/weighting
artifact.  Until a source-uniform arithmetic theorem is supplied, all Route-B
and twin-prime gates remain open.
