\title{The Exact Normalized History Cocycle\\
\large Rectangular Time Evolution, Singular Values, and a Reset-Invariance Counterexample}
\author{Liang Wang}
\date{July 2026}

\begin{document}
\maketitle

\begin{abstract}
RH-172 realized every positive reset-memory packet canonically in a finite
normalized history space.  The next question is whether these spaces and
packets possess an intrinsic time evolution.  We derive that evolution
exactly.  If $S_{t+1}=AS_t$, $\alpha_t=\norm{S_t}_F$, and
$r_t=\alpha_t/\alpha_{t+1}$, then the history factors satisfy
\[
 F_{t+1}=T_tF_t,
 \qquad
 T_t(y_0,\ldots,y_t)=
 (r_tAy_0,\sqrt\eta y_0,\ldots,\sqrt\eta y_t).
\]
Thus the memory recursion is generated by an exact rectangular cocycle
$T_t:\cK_t\to\cK_{t+1}$.

We compute its adjoint and full singular-value structure:
\[
 T_t^*T_t=
 \operatorname{diag}(r_t^2A^*A+\eta I,\eta I,\ldots,\eta I).
\]
For $t\ge1$, $T_t$ is bounded below by $\sqrt\eta$ and
$\norm{T_t}=\sqrt{r_t^2\norm A^2+\eta}$.  These formulas are exact and give a
well-conditioned finite-time injection even when $A$ is singular.

However, exact factor evolution does not imply that independently selected
top-Gram packets form an invariant cocycle bundle.  We construct a two-column
diagonal example in which the top reset packet rotates by ninety degrees in
one step, so the transported and reset history ranges have projection
distance one.  A 160-case audit verifies the action, adjoint, and singular
value formulas to $1.5\times10^{-15}$ and reproduces the exact counterexample.
The result closes finite history kinematics, not a transfer-space
intertwining or a physical Riesz cloud.
\end{abstract}

\section{From a static realization to time evolution}

Let $A\in\mathcal B(\cY)$, let $S_0:\cX\to\cY$, and set
$S_t=A^tS_0\ne0$.  With $\alpha_t=\norm{S_t}_F$ and
$\widehat S_t=S_t/\alpha_t$, RH-172 defines
\[
 F_tx=(\widehat S_tx,\sqrt\eta\widehat S_{t-1}x,
 \ldots,\eta^{t/2}\widehat S_0x)
 \in\cK_t:=\cY^{\oplus(t+1)}
\]
and proves $M_t=F_t^*F_t$ \citep{WangRH172}.  Since $\cK_t$ changes with
$t$, the update is not an ordinary power of one finite square matrix.  It is
a nonautonomous rectangular cocycle.

The normalization matters.  Although $S_{t+1}=AS_t$, generally
$\widehat S_{t+1}\ne A\widehat S_t$.  The missing scalar is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:ratio}
 r_t=\frac{\alpha_t}{\alpha_{t+1}},
 \qquad
 \widehat S_{t+1}=r_tA\widehat S_t.
\end{equation}

\section{The rectangular history cocycle}

Define $T_t:\cK_t\to\cK_{t+1}$ by
\begin{equation}\label{eq:cocycle}
 T_t(y_0,y_1,\ldots,y_t)
 =\bigl(r_tAy_0,\sqrt\eta y_0,\sqrt\eta y_1,
 \ldots,\sqrt\eta y_t\bigr).
\end{equation}

\begin{theorem}[Exact normalized history evolution]\label{thm:factor-cocycle}
For every $t\ge0$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:exact-intertwining}
 F_{t+1}=T_tF_t.
\end{equation}
Consequently
\begin{equation}\label{eq:memory-from-cocycle}
 M_{t+1}=F_t^*T_t^*T_tF_t.
\end{equation}
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
The first block of $T_tF_tx$ is
$r_tA\widehat S_tx=\widehat S_{t+1}x$ by
\eqref{eq:ratio}.  Every remaining block is the corresponding block of
$F_t x$ multiplied by $\sqrt\eta$.  This is exactly the ordered definition
of $F_{t+1}x$.  Taking adjoints gives \eqref{eq:memory-from-cocycle}.
\end{proof}

The theorem does more than restate the scalar memory recursion.  It identifies
the full analysis operator that transports every source vector, including
phase and cross-coordinate information discarded by $M_t$ alone.

\section{Adjoint, norm, and lower bound}

For $z=(z_0,\ldots,z_{t+1})\in\cK_{t+1}$, direct calculation gives
\begin{equation}\label{eq:adjoint}
 T_t^*z=
 \bigl(r_tA^*z_0+\sqrt\eta z_1,
 \sqrt\eta z_2,\ldots,\sqrt\eta z_{t+1}\bigr).
\end{equation}

\begin{theorem}[Complete singular-value formula]\label{thm:singular}
Relative to $\cK_t=\cY\oplus\cY^{\oplus t}$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:tstar-t}
 T_t^*T_t=
 \operatorname{diag}\bigl(r_t^2A^*A+\eta I,
 \eta I,\ldots,\eta I\bigr).
\end{equation}
Hence
\begin{equation}\label{eq:norm}
 \norm{T_t}=\sqrt{r_t^2\norm A^2+\eta}.
\end{equation}
If $t\ge1$, then
\begin{equation}\label{eq:lower}
 \norm{T_ty}\ge\sqrt\eta\norm y
 \quad\text{for every }y\in\cK_t.
\end{equation}
For $t=0$, the least singular value is
$\sqrt{r_t^2s_{\min}(A)^2+\eta}$.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Using \eqref{eq:cocycle},
\[
 \norm{T_ty}^2
 =r_t^2\norm{Ay_0}^2+\eta\sum_{j=0}^t\norm{y_j}^2.
\]
There are no cross terms because the outputs occupy distinct direct-sum
coordinates.  This proves \eqref{eq:tstar-t}.  Its largest eigenvalue is
$r_t^2\norm A^2+\eta$.  When $t\ge1$, the later diagonal blocks have
eigenvalue $\eta$, while the first block is bounded below by $\eta I$.
The stated formulas follow.
\end{proof}

Thus every positive forgetting weight regularizes the finite rectangular
map: $T_t$ is injective with closed range even when $A$ has a kernel.  This
finite-time fact should not be confused with compactness of an infinite
history completion; RH-175 will show that the latter contains a unilateral
shift obstruction.

\section{Transported packets and two directional defects}

Let $P_t$ be a rank-$r$ reset packet for $M_t$, and let
$J_t:\Ran P_t\to\cK_t$ be its canonical polar realization.  Choose
orthonormal frames $V_t$ and $V_{t+1}$ for the realized ranges.  Define the
reduced cocycle
\begin{equation}\label{eq:reduced}
 B_t=V_{t+1}^*T_tV_t.
\end{equation}
The two natural residuals are
\begin{align}
 R_t^{\rm pr}&=(I-V_{t+1}V_{t+1}^*)T_tV_t,
 \label{eq:primal}\\
 R_t^{\rm ad}&=(I-V_tV_t^*)T_t^*V_{t+1}.
 \label{eq:adjoint-residual}
\end{align}
The first asks whether the old packet transports into the new packet.  The
second asks whether the new packet pulls back into the old packet.  For a
nonnormal or rectangular map these are independent.  A small primal defect
does not imply a small adjoint defect.

\begin{proposition}[Exact reduction criterion]\label{prop:reduction}
The identities
\[
 T_tV_t=V_{t+1}B_t,
 \qquad
 T_t^*V_{t+1}=V_tB_t^*
\]
hold simultaneously if and only if
$R_t^{\rm pr}=R_t^{\rm ad}=0$.  In that case the two realized packet ranges
form a reducing pair for the rectangular cocycle.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Orthogonal decomposition of $T_tV_t$ into the range of $V_{t+1}$ and its
orthogonal complement gives the first equivalence.  The coefficient of the
range component is $V_{t+1}^*T_tV_t=B_t$.  Applying the same argument to the
adjoint gives the second equivalence and coefficient $B_t^*$.
\end{proof}

The Gram recursion alone does not imply either residual vanishes.  At time
$t+1$, the reset packet optimizes the new quadratic form $M_{t+1}$; it is not
defined as the image of the old packet under $T_t$.

\section{An exact ninety-degree reset counterexample}

Take $\cX=\cY=\mathbb C^2$, choose $0<\varepsilon<1$, and set
\[
 S_0=\begin{pmatrix}1&0\\0&\varepsilon\end{pmatrix},
 \qquad
 A=\begin{pmatrix}\varepsilon&0\\0&\varepsilon^{-1}\end{pmatrix}.
\]
Then
\[
 S_1=AS_0=\begin{pmatrix}\varepsilon&0\\0&1\end{pmatrix}.
\]
For every $0\le\eta<1$, the rank-one top packet of
$M_0=\widehat S_0^*\widehat S_0$ is $\mathbb Ce_1$, while the rank-one top
packet of
$M_1=\widehat S_1^*\widehat S_1+\eta M_0$ is $\mathbb Ce_2$.

\begin{theorem}[Gram optimality does not imply cocycle invariance]
In the preceding example, the canonical realized range of the old top packet
after transport by $T_0$ is orthogonal to the canonical realized range of the
new top packet.  Therefore
\[
 \norm{P_{T_0V_0}-P_{V_1}}=1.
\]
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
All matrices are diagonal.  The image of the first source coordinate occupies
only first-coordinate output axes in both history blocks, whereas the image
of the second source coordinate occupies only second-coordinate axes.  The
two history columns are orthogonal.  Polar normalization changes their
lengths but not their ranges.  Orthogonal rank-one projections have operator
distance one.
\end{proof}

The counterexample is not a statement about the physical frozen models.  It
shows that no theorem can deduce packet invariance solely from positivity,
the memory recursion, and exact factor evolution.  Additional dynamical
structure is indispensable.

\section{Block-time cocycle and an explicit long-step formula}

The one-step maps compose without ambiguity even though their domains change.
For integers $s<t$, define
\[
 T_{t:s}=T_{t-1}T_{t-2}\cdots T_s:\cK_s\to\cK_t.
\]

\begin{proposition}[Exact block-time evolution]\label{prop:block-time}
For every $0\le s<t$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:block-factor}
 F_t=T_{t:s}F_s.
\end{equation}
Writing $n=t-s$, $q=\sqrt\eta$, and
$y=(y_0,y_1,\ldots,y_s)$, the first $n+1$ output blocks of $T_{t:s}y$ are
\begin{equation}\label{eq:block-formula}
 q^j\frac{\alpha_s}{\alpha_{t-j}}A^{n-j}y_0,
 \qquad 0\le j\le n,
\end{equation}
and the remaining blocks are
\begin{equation}\label{eq:old-tail}
 q^ny_1,q^ny_2,\ldots,q^ny_s.
\end{equation}
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Equation \eqref{eq:block-factor} follows by iterating
Theorem~\ref{thm:factor-cocycle}.  For the explicit blocks, induction shows
that the $j$th newly generated block contains $j$ shifts and $n-j$ first-block
updates.  The product of normalization ratios telescopes:
\[
 r_s r_{s+1}\cdots r_{t-j-1}
 =\frac{\alpha_s}{\alpha_{t-j}}.
\]
Every old tail block is shifted exactly $n$ times, giving
\eqref{eq:old-tail}.
\end{proof}

The formula separates two mechanisms: physical propagation by powers of
$A$, and memory aging by powers of $q$.  It also gives a type-correct starting
point for lagged or block packets.  Such packets need not inherit the severe
one-step adjoint defect observed later, because their reduction target can be
a whole product $T_{t:s}$ rather than one $T_s$.

\section{From primal residual to subspace distance}

Let $W=T_tV_t$ and assume $W$ has full column rank.  Its polar frame is
$\widehat W=W(W^*W)^{-1/2}$.  The projection distance from the transported
range to the new packet is
\[
 d_t=\norm{(I-V_{t+1}V_{t+1}^*)\widehat W}.
\]

\begin{proposition}[Conditioned primal-distance bound]\label{prop:distance}
With $R_t^{\rm pr}$ from \eqref{eq:primal},
\begin{equation}\label{eq:distance-bound}
 d_t\le
 \frac{\norm{R_t^{\rm pr}}}{s_{\min}(W)}
 =\epsilon_t^{\rm pr}\,\kappa(W),
\end{equation}
where
$\epsilon_t^{\rm pr}=\norm{R_t^{\rm pr}}/\norm W$ and
$\kappa(W)=\norm W/s_{\min}(W)$.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Since $\widehat W=W(W^*W)^{-1/2}$,
\[
 (I-V_{t+1}V_{t+1}^*)\widehat W
 =R_t^{\rm pr}(W^*W)^{-1/2}.
\]
The inverse positive factor has norm $s_{\min}(W)^{-1}$.  Taking norms gives
the first inequality; multiplying and dividing by $\norm W$ gives the second.
\end{proof}

Thus a small normalized primal residual need not force a small subspace
distance unless the transported packet is also well conditioned.  Conversely,
the adjoint residual is not controlled by \eqref{eq:distance-bound} at all.
This quantitative separation motivates recording all three diagnostics in
the physical audit rather than treating one as a proxy for the others.

\section{Finite identity audit}

The implementation audit uses dimensions $2,3,5,8$, input history lengths
$1,2,4,7$, and ten random trials per configuration, for 160 cases.  It
compares explicit rectangular matrices with blockwise action and adjoint
formulas, and compares numerical singular values with
Theorem~\ref{thm:singular}.
\begin{center}
\begin{tabular}{lr}
\toprule
quantity & maximum residual\\
\midrule
matrix action & $1.48\times10^{-15}$\\
adjoint action & $4.45\times10^{-16}$\\
largest singular value & $1.34\times10^{-15}$\\
smallest singular value & $5.00\times10^{-16}$\\
counterexample projection distance & $1.000000000000$\\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{center}
These are implementation checks, not physical interval certificates.

\section{Route consequence and boundary}

RH-172 and RH-173 now provide exact finite history kinematics:
\[
 M_t=F_t^*F_t,
 \qquad F_{t+1}=T_tF_t,
 \qquad T_t^*T_t\text{ explicit}.
\]
The next executable question is empirical but type-correct: on the 130
archived physical reset snapshots, how large are the transported packet
distance and the two directional residuals?  That is the task of RH-174.

Nothing here maps $\cK_t$ into the RH-80 transfer space.  The maps $T_t$ are
rectangular and time dependent, so they do not themselves possess a standard
Fredholm determinant.  No all-level autonomous completion, Riesz cloud,
Schatten limit, self-adjoint generator, zeta divisor, or Riemann-hypothesis
claim is made.
