\section{Cancellation margin and phase sector}

\begin{corollary}[Exact cancellation criteria]\label{cor:zero}
If $m\ge2$, then
\[
 \min_{z\in\mathcal S}|z|=\max\{|c|-r,0\},
 \qquad 0\in\mathcal S\iff |c|\le r.
\]
If $m=1$ and $r>0$, the minimum is $\bigl||c|-r\bigr|$ and zero is feasible
if and only if $|c|=r$.  Every realizable branch obeys
$|\ip{W}{B}|\ge\max\{|c|-r,0\}$.
\end{corollary}

This is the exact distance from the origin to the translated disk, circle, or
singleton.  It separates ``cancellation is permitted by the moment data'' from
``cancellation occurs for the physical vectors.''  Only the first statement is
part of the present theorem.

\begin{corollary}[Sharp phase cone]\label{cor:phase}
Suppose $0\le r<|c|$.  Every feasible nonzero covariance $z$ satisfies
\[
 \left|\operatorname{Arg}(z\overline c)\right|
 \le \arcsin(r/|c|).
\]
The bound is sharp for the disk and for the nondegenerate one-dimensional
circle.
\end{corollary}

To prove the claim, rotate so that $c=a>0$ and write
$z=\rho e^{i\theta}$.  The disk inequality becomes
\[
 |z-a|^2=(\rho-a\cos\theta)^2+a^2\sin^2\theta\le r^2.
\]
Because the disk misses zero, the principal angle is acute, and hence
$|\theta|\le\arcsin(r/a)$.  Equality occurs at either tangent point from the
origin; those points lie on the boundary circle.
