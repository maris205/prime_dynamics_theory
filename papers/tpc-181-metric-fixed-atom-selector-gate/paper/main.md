## Abstract

TPC-170 proves a fixed-$X$-power, all-prefix packet theorem for Lebesgue-almost every fixed phase on a prescribed determinant-two schedule. TPC-180 shows that the frozen source corpus does not yet contain the value-bearing production phase registry required to name the physical atom. We isolate the remaining selector question even conditionally on a future positive registry.

For the schedule-dependent bad sets $E_n$, Borel–Cantelli proves $\operatorname{meas}(\limsup E_n)=0$. It does not prove that a prespecified singleton $\alpha_\star$ lies outside that limsup: the null set may be exactly $\{\alpha_\star\}$. We formulate the minimal source-backed bridge contract, whose load-bearing theorem is the schedule-specific assertion $\alpha_\star\notin\limsup E_n$. No artifact currently supplies this assertion. Therefore $\mathsf{H2.metric\_fixed\_atom\_crosswalk}$ remains $\mathsf{NOT\_TESTABLE}$, and only the uncontrolled atomic promotion method receives a scoped stop. The pointwise bad-endpoint and direct additive-twist routes remain parent-ready and open. This is a rigorous $\mathrm{L1}$ quantifier obstruction, not a program-positive $\mathrm{L2}$ estimate.

# The imported metric theorem

At scale $n$, let $\mathcal P_n$ be the packet list prescribed before its exceptional phases are read. For $p\in\mathcal P_n$, write $$G_{n,p}(\alpha)
 =
 \frac{q_{n,p}}{T_{n,p}}
 \max_{k\le L_{n,p}}|S_{n,p,k}(\alpha)|.
\tag{1}\label{eq:G}$$ TPC-169 proves $$\int_{\mathbb T}G_{n,p}(\alpha)^2\,d\alpha\le V_{n,p},
\tag{2}\label{eq:energy}$$ where $$V_{n,p}
 =
 D_{n,p}^2\|\rho_{n,p}\|_\infty^2
 \left(\frac{q_{n,p}}{T_{n,p}}
       +\frac{q_{n,p}^2}{T_{n,p}^2}\right)
\tag{3}\label{eq:V}$$ `\citep{WangTPC169}`. For thresholds $\lambda_n>0$, define $$E_n
 =
 \left\{\alpha\in\mathbb T:
 \max_{p\in\mathcal P_n}G_{n,p}(\alpha)>\lambda_n
 \right\}.
\tag{4}\label{eq:En}$$ TPC-170 proves that $$\sum_n\lambda_n^{-2}
 \sum_{p\in\mathcal P_n}V_{n,p}<\infty
\tag{5}\label{eq:summability}$$ implies $$\operatorname{meas}\!\left(\limsup_{n\to\infty}E_n\right)=0.
\tag{6}\label{eq:limsup}$$ Equivalently, for Lebesgue-almost every fixed $\alpha$, every declared packet and every prefix is eventually at most $\lambda_n$ `\citep{WangTPC170}`.

Under the dyadic, polylogarithmic packet envelope, one may choose $$\lambda_n=X_n^{-\delta},\qquad \delta<\frac14.
\tag{7}\label{eq:power}$$ The exact imported signature is $$\begin{array}{c|c}

\hline
\text{carrier}&\mathsf{EXPLICIT\_PACKET\_CORRIDOR}\\

\end{array}
\tag{8}\label{eq:metric-signature}$$ The word “fixed” in the phase row means that the same $\alpha$ is followed across $n$. It does not change the almost-everywhere quantifier to evaluation at a named atom.

# The singleton obstruction

> **Proposition: Full measure does not select a singleton** <span id="prop:singleton" label="prop:singleton">\[prop:singleton\]</span> Let $\alpha_\star\in\mathbb T$ be prescribed. From $\operatorname{meas}(G)=1$ alone one cannot infer $\alpha_\star\in G$.

> **Proof** Take $$N=\{\alpha_\star\},\qquad G=\mathbb T\setminus N.$$ Then $\operatorname{meas}(N)=0$ and $\operatorname{meas}(G)=1$, but $\alpha_\star\notin G$.

> **Corollary: Borel–Cantelli selector nonimplication** <span id="cor:BC" label="cor:BC">\[cor:BC\]</span> Equation [\[eq:limsup\]](../main.tex#L131){reference-type="eqref" reference="eq:limsup"}, without an independent theorem about $\alpha_\star$, does not imply [\[eq:avoid\]](../main.tex#L178){reference-type="eqref" reference="eq:avoid"}.

> **Proof** Apply [\[prop:singleton\]](../main.tex#L208){reference-type="ref" reference="prop:singleton"} with $$N=\limsup E_n,\qquad G=\mathbb T\setminus\limsup E_n.$$ The metric theorem controls the measure of $N$, not membership of a prespecified singleton.

TPC-170 also realizes the logical sharpness with constant coefficients at phase zero. That example is not asserted to be the literal Möbius packet. The general obstruction in [\[prop:singleton\]](../main.tex#L208){reference-type="ref" reference="prop:singleton"} is a quantifier nonimplication, not a coefficient lower bound.

> **Theorem: Exact scoped stop** <span id="thm:stop" label="thm:stop">\[thm:stop\]</span> The implication $$\mathsf{LEBESGUE\_AE\_FIXED\_PHASE}
> \Longrightarrow
> \mathsf{NAMED\_FIXED\_ATOM}
> \tag{11}\label{eq:bad-implication}$$ is invalid without a bridge satisfying [\[def:bridge\]](../main.tex#L185){reference-type="ref" reference="def:bridge"}. Therefore the arithmetic method $$\mathsf{phase\_metric\_uncontrolled\_atomic}
> \tag{12}\label{eq:method}$$ has status $\textnormal{\textsc{stop-scoped}}$ in scope $\mathsf{UNCONTROLLED\_ATOMIC\_PROMOTION\_ONLY}$.

> **Proof** disproves the inference at the only step where the almost-everywhere theorem is promoted to the named atom. Adding a source-backed proof of [\[eq:avoid\]](../main.tex#L178){reference-type="eqref" reference="eq:avoid"} changes the premise and lies outside the uncontrolled method. A direct pointwise theorem also changes the phase quantifier rather than using [\[eq:bad-implication\]](../main.tex#L252){reference-type="eqref" reference="eq:bad-implication"}. Hence the stop has exactly the stated scope.
