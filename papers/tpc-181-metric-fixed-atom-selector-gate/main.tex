\begin{abstract}
TPC-170 proves a fixed-\(X\)-power, all-prefix packet theorem for
Lebesgue-almost every fixed phase on a prescribed determinant-two
schedule.  TPC-180 shows that the frozen source corpus does not yet
contain the value-bearing production phase registry required to name
the physical atom.  We isolate the remaining selector question even
conditionally on a future positive registry.

For the schedule-dependent bad sets \(E_n\), Borel--Cantelli proves
\(\operatorname{meas}(\limsup E_n)=0\).  It does not prove that a
prespecified singleton \(\alpha_\star\) lies outside that limsup: the
null set may be exactly \(\{\alpha_\star\}\).  We formulate the
minimal source-backed bridge contract, whose load-bearing theorem is
the schedule-specific assertion
\(\alpha_\star\notin\limsup E_n\).  No artifact currently supplies
this assertion.  Therefore
\(\mathsf{H2.metric\_fixed\_atom\_crosswalk}\) remains
\(\mathsf{NOT\_TESTABLE}\), and only the uncontrolled atomic promotion
method receives a scoped stop.  The pointwise bad-endpoint and direct
additive-twist routes remain parent-ready and open.  This is a
rigorous \(\Lone\) quantifier obstruction, not a program-positive
\(\Ltwo\) estimate.
\end{abstract}

\section{The imported metric theorem}

At scale \(n\), let \(\mathcal P_n\) be the packet list prescribed
before its exceptional phases are read.  For \(p\in\mathcal P_n\),
write
\[
 G_{n,p}(\alpha)
 =
 \frac{q_{n,p}}{T_{n,p}}
 \max_{k\le L_{n,p}}|S_{n,p,k}(\alpha)|.
\tag{1}\label{eq:G}
\]
TPC-169 proves
\[
 \int_{\T}G_{n,p}(\alpha)^2\,d\alpha\le V_{n,p},
\tag{2}\label{eq:energy}
\]
where
\[
 V_{n,p}
 =
 D_{n,p}^2\|\rho_{n,p}\|_\infty^2
 \left(\frac{q_{n,p}}{T_{n,p}}
       +\frac{q_{n,p}^2}{T_{n,p}^2}\right)
\tag{3}\label{eq:V}
\]
\citep{WangTPC169}.  For thresholds \(\lambda_n>0\), define
\[
 E_n
 =
 \left\{\alpha\in\T:
 \max_{p\in\mathcal P_n}G_{n,p}(\alpha)>\lambda_n
 \right\}.
\tag{4}\label{eq:En}
\]
TPC-170 proves that
\[
 \sum_n\lambda_n^{-2}
 \sum_{p\in\mathcal P_n}V_{n,p}<\infty
\tag{5}\label{eq:summability}
\]
implies
\[
 \operatorname{meas}\!\left(\limsup_{n\to\infty}E_n\right)=0.
\tag{6}\label{eq:limsup}
\]
Equivalently, for Lebesgue-almost every fixed \(\alpha\), every
declared packet and every prefix is eventually at most
\(\lambda_n\) \citep{WangTPC170}.

Under the dyadic, polylogarithmic packet envelope, one may choose
\[
 \lambda_n=X_n^{-\delta},\qquad \delta<\frac14.
\tag{7}\label{eq:power}
\]
The exact imported signature is
\[
\begin{array}{c|c}
\text{axis}&\text{proved value}\\
\hline
\text{carrier}&\mathsf{EXPLICIT\_PACKET\_CORRIDOR}\\
\text{phase}&\mathsf{LEBESGUE\_AE\_FIXED\_PHASE}\\
\text{endpoint}&\mathsf{ALL\_PREFIX\_THETA\_SHELL}\\
\text{scale}&\mathsf{EVENTUALLY\_PRESCRIBED\_SCHEDULE}\\
\text{decay}&\mathsf{FIXED\_X\_POWER\_PHASE\_METRIC}\\
\text{support}&\mathsf{ACTUAL\_CORE}.
\end{array}
\tag{8}\label{eq:metric-signature}
\]
The word ``fixed'' in the phase row means that the same \(\alpha\) is
followed across \(n\).  It does not change the almost-everywhere
quantifier to evaluation at a named atom.

\section{The two independent missing inputs}

TPC-180 audits the value-bearing phase sources and obtains
\[
\begin{gathered}
\mathsf{H9.phase\_cell\_registry}=\NT,\\
\mathsf{named\_physical\_atom\_id}=\mathsf{null},\qquad
\mathsf{phase\_value\_mod\_1}=\mathsf{null}.
\end{gathered}
\tag{9}\label{eq:registry}
\]
The fixed-\(h_0=2\) data fact remains proved, but is not a phase value
\citep{WangTPC180}.

Even if a later source supplies \(\alpha_\star\), one more theorem is
needed:
\[
 \boxed{\alpha_\star\notin\limsup_{n\to\infty}E_n}
\tag{10}\label{eq:avoid}
\]
for the exact production schedule and bad sets \eqref{eq:En}.  The
registry is a data input; \eqref{eq:avoid} is an arithmetic or
selector theorem.  Their roles and evidence cannot be merged.

\begin{definition}[Legal metric-to-fixed-atom bridge]
\label{def:bridge}
A legal bridge contains:
\begin{enumerate}[label=(\roman*),leftmargin=2.2em]
\item the exact source-located named atom and phase value;
\item a hash and source locator for the exact production packet
  schedule;
\item the schedule's precise sets \(E_n\);
\item a theorem proving \eqref{eq:avoid};
\item the same fixed \(\alpha_\star\) at every scale; and
\item coverage of every production packet to which the conclusion is
  applied.
\end{enumerate}
\end{definition}

A source hash certifies only identity.  It does not prove item (iv).
Nor do a phase-\(L^2\) estimate, density-one control on a separated
registry, a phase selected after the bad set, or phases
\(\alpha_n\) that drift with \(n\)
\citep{WangTPC167,WangTPC168,WangTPC171,WangTPC172}.

\section{The singleton obstruction}

\begin{proposition}[Full measure does not select a singleton]
\label{prop:singleton}
Let \(\alpha_\star\in\T\) be prescribed.  From
\(\operatorname{meas}(G)=1\) alone one cannot infer
\(\alpha_\star\in G\).
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Take
\[
 N=\{\alpha_\star\},\qquad G=\T\setminus N.
\]
Then \(\operatorname{meas}(N)=0\) and
\(\operatorname{meas}(G)=1\), but
\(\alpha_\star\notin G\).
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[Borel--Cantelli selector nonimplication]
\label{cor:BC}
Equation \eqref{eq:limsup}, without an independent theorem about
\(\alpha_\star\), does not imply \eqref{eq:avoid}.
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
Apply \cref{prop:singleton} with
\[
 N=\limsup E_n,\qquad G=\T\setminus\limsup E_n.
\]
The metric theorem controls the measure of \(N\), not membership of a
prespecified singleton.
\end{proof}

TPC-170 also realizes the logical sharpness with constant
coefficients at phase zero.  That example is not asserted to be the
literal M\"obius packet.  The general obstruction in
\cref{prop:singleton} is a quantifier nonimplication, not a
coefficient lower bound.

\begin{theorem}[Exact scoped stop]
\label{thm:stop}
The implication
\[
\mathsf{LEBESGUE\_AE\_FIXED\_PHASE}
\Longrightarrow
\mathsf{NAMED\_FIXED\_ATOM}
\tag{11}\label{eq:bad-implication}
\]
is invalid without a bridge satisfying \cref{def:bridge}.  Therefore
the arithmetic method
\[
 \mathsf{phase\_metric\_uncontrolled\_atomic}
\tag{12}\label{eq:method}
\]
has status \(\STOPR\) in scope
\(\mathsf{UNCONTROLLED\_ATOMIC\_PROMOTION\_ONLY}\).
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
\Cref{cor:BC} disproves the inference at the only step where the
almost-everywhere theorem is promoted to the named atom.  Adding a
source-backed proof of \eqref{eq:avoid} changes the premise and lies
outside the uncontrolled method.  A direct pointwise theorem also
changes the phase quantifier rather than using
\eqref{eq:bad-implication}.  Hence the stop has exactly the stated
scope.
\end{proof}

\section{Selector verdict and sufficient bridge hypotheses}

The present state fails both inputs to \cref{def:bridge}: the
value-bearing registry in \eqref{eq:registry} is absent, and no
schedule-specific avoidance theorem \eqref{eq:avoid} is source-locked.
Thus
\[
\boxed{
\begin{gathered}
\mathsf{H2.metric\_fixed\_atom\_crosswalk}=\NT,\\
\mathsf{SelectorConstructed}=\mathsf{false},\\
\mathsf{FixedAtomDecayObtained}=\mathsf{false}.
\end{gathered}}
\tag{13}\label{eq:verdict}
\]

Three forms of genuinely new input could change a future snapshot:
\begin{enumerate}[label=(\alph*),leftmargin=2.2em]
\item a theorem proving \eqref{eq:avoid} for the exact named phase and
  exact schedule;
\item a source-compatible point-evaluation or continuity estimate
  strong enough to dominate \(G_{n,p}(\alpha_\star)\) by controlled
  values with a summable error; or
\item an independent pointwise fixed-phase theorem, which exits the
  metric bridge and enters a pointwise route.
\end{enumerate}
Merely declaring the named phase ``generic'' is not one of these
hypotheses unless a theorem with the exact schedule-dependent
quantifiers is supplied.

\begin{proposition}[Pointwise frontier preservation]
\label{prop:frontier}
The scoped stop in \cref{thm:stop} does not change either element of
\(\mathcal F_{\rm point}\) from
\(\mathsf{OPEN\_PARENT\_READY}\), and it does not stop or reroute an
architecture.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
The stopped node has arithmetic-negative role and the exact
uncontrolled-atomic-promotion scope.

whose hypotheses assert pointwise fixed-atom estimates.  Neither
depends on the invalid implication \eqref{eq:bad-implication}.
Architecture nodes have a disjoint route kind.  Therefore the scoped
stop has no typed edge that could close either pointwise target or an
architecture.
\end{proof}

\section{Level ledger and reproducible audit}

TPC-181 separates:
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=2em]
\item \(\Lzero\): source hashes, strict schemas, output regeneration,
  and finite mutation diagnostics;
\item \(\Lone\): \cref{prop:singleton,thm:stop,prop:frontier}, which
  are rigorous quantifier and typed-route statements; and
\item \(\Ltwo\): none.
\end{itemize}
The metric exponent \(\delta<1/4\) in \eqref{eq:power} remains in the
phase-metric ledger.  It is explicitly ineligible as fixed-atom power.

The generator writes
\begin{verbatim}

\end{verbatim}
and records the selector verdict, scoped stop, named-atom status,

regressions reject almost-everywhere promotion, fabrication of the
missing registry, charging metric power as fixed-atom power, using the
scoped stop against a pointwise target or architecture, and a
scale-dependent selector.

\section{Conclusion}

The metric route ends at a precise theorem interface.  TPC-170 gives
full-measure eventual control on its exact prescribed schedule;
TPC-181 proves that this measure statement cannot select a
prespecified singleton.  Since TPC-180 also lacks the named value and
locator, the source-backed selector is not testable.  The failure is
method-scoped, so the program returns to the two pointwise O161
targets without relabeling them as stopped and without claiming any
new \(\Ltwo\) progress.
