\title{\textbf{Literal Native-Atom Path Archives for the TPC-15 Hard Packet}\\
\large Local-to-Global Conservation, Metadata Intertwining, and
First-Failure Certificates}
\author{Liang Wang}
\date{July 2026}

\begin{document}
\maketitle

\begin{abstract}
TPC-119 gave a sufficient canonical-matrix criterion for reconnecting
the later TPC synthesis to the original fixed-shift hard packet, but
the audited repository did not contain the complete matrix.  We make
the missing object local.  Every declared partition, polarization,
content split, frequency split, boundary rule and return rule is
represented by an exact sparse stage matrix \(T_j\), and the
path-expanded archive records every nonzero edge rather than only its
final aggregate.  If
\[
 \one^TT_j=\one^T
\]
at every stage and every edge intertwines the native key, prescribed
shift and physical normalization, then the composite
\(M=T_s\cdots T_1\) is coefficientwise conservative and gives the
exact TPC-119 reconnection identity.  We prove the defect formula
\[
 \one_s^TM-\one_0^T
 =
 \sum_{j=1}^s
 \bigl(\one_j^TT_j-\one_{j-1}^T\bigr)
 T_{j-1}\cdots T_1,
\]
which separates a global defect into typed local defects.  Sharp
finite examples show why equality on one coefficient vector, a
conservative final composite, or an untagged scalar archive does not
verify the local path statement.  The theorem and exact rational
audit are \(\Lzero\); identifying the required TPC fields is an
\(\Lone\) interface.  The actual growing branch tables remain absent,
so the literal archive is presently \(\NT\).  No \(\Ltwo\)
fixed-shift cancellation, parity advance or prime-pair theorem is
claimed.
\end{abstract}

\section{Scope and the native entrance}

Fix a nonzero admissible shift \(h_0\), a smooth compactly supported
weight \(W\), and \(0<\delta<1/2\).  The TPC-15 hard packet has the
opened form
\begin{equation}
 B_{h_0,\delta}(X)
 =
 \sum_{\alpha=(\ell,k,d)\in\cA_X}c_X(\alpha),
 \qquad
 c_X(\alpha)=
 -\Lambda(\ell)\mu(d)r_Q(\ell k+h_0)W(\ell k/X),
 \label{eq:native}
\end{equation}
where \(\ell>U\), \(k>V\), \(d\mid k\), and \(d\le V\)
\citep{WangTPC15,WangTPC119}.  The divisor \(d\) is part of the key:
compressing \((\ell,k,d)\) to \((\ell,k)\) preserves the scalar in
\eqref{eq:native} but loses the provenance needed by later content
and prefix operations.

TPC-119 encodes a complete later analysis by a matrix \(M_X\) whose
columns are the atoms of \(\cA_X\) and whose rows are final leaves.
Its sufficient conservation condition is
\begin{equation}
 \one^TM_X=\one^T.
 \label{eq:global-conservation}
\end{equation}
The remaining difficulty is not linear algebra: it is the absence of
one object proving that every intermediate branch has been included
with the same \(h_0\), normalization and native source.  The present
paper replaces a monolithic final archive by a composable path
archive.  It does not estimate any coefficient in
\eqref{eq:native}.

\begin{definition}[Claim levels]
An exact finite identity independent of the arithmetic meaning of
the labels is \(\Lzero\).  A verified identification of those labels
with the literal TPC packet is \(\Lone\).  A growing,
coefficient-specific, fixed-\(h_0\) arithmetic power saving is
\(\Ltwo\).
\end{definition}

\section{Typed stages and the path-expanded archive}

Let
\[
 R_0=\cA_X,\quad R_1,\ldots,R_s=\cL_X
\]
be finite record sets.  A record may contain mathematical data in
addition to its identifier.  For each \(j\), let
\[
 T_j:\C^{R_{j-1}}\longrightarrow\C^{R_j}
\]
be the exact sparse matrix of the \(j\)-th declared operation.  Rows
are outgoing records and columns are incoming records.  Thus
\[
 M=T_sT_{s-1}\cdots T_1
\]
is the aggregate leaf matrix.

\begin{definition}[Path-expanded archive]
A path is a tuple
\[
 p=(r_0,r_1,\ldots,r_s),\qquad r_j\in R_j,
\]
such that \(T_j(r_j,r_{j-1})\ne0\) for every \(j\).  Its multiplier is
\[
 m(p)=\prod_{j=1}^sT_j(r_j,r_{j-1}).
\]
The path-expanded archive stores the tuple, its multiplier, its
native source \(r_0\), and the typed metadata on every edge.  Paths
remain distinct even when they end at the same leaf.
\end{definition}

\begin{proposition}[Path expansion]
\label{prop:path}
For every \(r_s\in R_s\) and \(r_0\in R_0\),
\[
 M(r_s,r_0)
 =
 \sum_{\substack{p:\,p_0=r_0\\p_s=r_s}}m(p).
\]
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
This is the entrywise definition of the product
\(T_s\cdots T_1\), with all intermediate indices displayed.
\end{proof}

The path form is deliberately more redundant than \(M\).  It
records whether an entry arose from the declared branches, whereas a
final matrix entry records only their sum.  This distinction is
essential when opposite errors cancel.

\section{Local conservation implies exact global reconnection}

Write \(\one_j\) for the all-ones column vector on \(R_j\).

\begin{definition}[Stage conservation]
Stage \(j\) is coefficientwise conservative when
\begin{equation}
 \one_j^TT_j=\one_{j-1}^T.
 \label{eq:stage-conservation}
\end{equation}
Equivalently, the outgoing multipliers above every incoming record
sum exactly to one.
\end{definition}

\begin{theorem}[Local-to-global path conservation]
\label{thm:local-global}
If \eqref{eq:stage-conservation} holds for every
\(1\le j\le s\), then
\[
 \boxed{\one_s^TM=\one_0^T.}
\]
Consequently, for every coefficient vector \(c\in\C^{R_0}\),
\[
 \one_s^TMc=\one_0^Tc.
\]
In particular, taking \(c=c_X\) from \eqref{eq:native} gives exact
scalar reconnection to \(B_{h_0,\delta}(X)\).
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Repeatedly use \eqref{eq:stage-conservation}:
\[
 \one_s^TT_s\cdots T_1
 =
 \one_{s-1}^TT_{s-1}\cdots T_1
 =\cdots=\one_0^T.
\]
Multiplying by \(c\) proves the scalar identity.  No positivity,
orthogonality or probabilistic interpretation is used.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[Retained--soft identity]
\label{cor:retsoft}
Suppose \(R_s=R_s^{\rm ret}\sqcup R_s^{\rm soft}\) is a disjoint
typed partition.  Let \(M^{\rm ret}\) and \(M^{\rm soft}\) be obtained
from \(M\) by retaining the corresponding rows and setting the other
rows to zero.  Under \cref{thm:local-global},
\[
 B_{h_0,\delta}(X)
 =
 \one^TM^{\rm ret}c_X+\one^TM^{\rm soft}c_X.
\]
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
The row partition gives \(M=M^{\rm ret}+M^{\rm soft}\); now apply
\cref{thm:local-global}.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}[What the theorem does not estimate]
The corollary is exact even when both terms are large.  To obtain the
analytic H8 conclusion, a separate complete theorem must prove
\(\one^TM^{\rm soft}c_X=o(X)\) in the original normalization.
Local conservation does not provide that bound.
\end{remark}

\section{The exact defect telescope}

Define the stage defect
\[
 d_j^T=\one_j^TT_j-\one_{j-1}^T.
\]
Use the convention \(T_0\cdots T_1=I_{R_0}\).

\begin{theorem}[Conservation-defect telescope]
\label{thm:defect}
For arbitrary compatible stage matrices,
\begin{equation}
 \boxed{
 \one_s^TM-\one_0^T
 =
 \sum_{j=1}^s
 d_j^TT_{j-1}\cdots T_1.}
 \label{eq:defect}
\end{equation}
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Insert and subtract
\(\one_{s-1}^TT_{s-1}\cdots T_1\):
\[
 \one_s^TT_s\cdots T_1-\one_0^T
 =
 d_s^TT_{s-1}\cdots T_1
 +\one_{s-1}^TT_{s-1}\cdots T_1-\one_0^T.
\]
Iterating proves \eqref{eq:defect}.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[First failing conservative stage]
If stages \(1,\ldots,j-1\) satisfy
\eqref{eq:stage-conservation} and \(d_j\ne0\), then the path archive
contains an exact local failure certificate at stage \(j\).  This
stops the declared locally conservative implementation, even if
later matrices happen to cancel its contribution in
\eqref{eq:defect}.
\end{corollary}

The last clause matters: global conservation is not the converse of
stagewise conservation.  A path audit asks whether the declared proof
operations themselves are exact partitions, not merely whether their
aggregate happens to preserve one scalar.

\section{Metadata intertwining}

Coefficient conservation cannot see a wrong shift or a second
normalization.  Let \(K\) be any discrete metadata set and let
\[
 \kappa_j:R_j\longrightarrow K
\]
record one field.  For \(q\in K\), let \(P_{j,q}\) be the coordinate
projection onto records with \(\kappa_j=q\).

\begin{definition}[Metadata intertwining]
Stage \(T_j\) preserves the field \(\kappa\) when
\begin{equation}
 P_{j,q}T_j=T_jP_{j-1,q}
 \qquad(q\in K),
 \label{eq:metadata}
\end{equation}
where the second occurrence of \(T_j\) is understood with the
compatible source and target projections.  Entrywise, every nonzero
edge has \(\kappa_j(r_j)=\kappa_{j-1}(r_{j-1})\).
\end{definition}

\begin{theorem}[Composite metadata preservation]
\label{thm:metadata}
If \eqref{eq:metadata} holds at every stage, then
\[
 P_{s,q}M=MP_{0,q}
 \qquad(q\in K).
\]
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Move the projection successively through \(T_s,\ldots,T_1\).
\end{proof}

The fields required by the present route include:
\begin{enumerate}[label=\textbf{F\arabic*.},leftmargin=2.7em]
\item the native atom \((\ell,k,d)\);
\item the prescribed shift \(h_0\) and the single inherited physical
normalization;
\item every partition, polarization, mask, content, boundary and
collision tag;
\item the retained or soft terminal type;
\item for the TPC-121 interface, the raw atom \(t\), determinant bin
\(\nu(t)\), complex coefficient \(u_t\), and the predeclared
\(X\)-indexed bin/good-set rule \citep{WangTPC121};
\item for the TPC-122 interface, the canonical fiber order, arithmetic
sign \(\sigma\), outer weight \(w\), and the content remainder
allocation \citep{WangTPC122}.
\end{enumerate}
The last two items are data fields, not exponent theorems.  Recording
them does not prove either \(\lambda_D^{\rm cert}\) or
\(\eta_Z^{\rm cert}\).

\section{Sharp counterexamples}

\begin{proposition}[Scalar coincidence is insufficient]
\label{prop:scalar}
There are \(M\) and \(c\) such that
\(\one^TMc=\one^Tc\) but \(\one^TM\ne\one^T\).
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Take
\[
 M=\begin{pmatrix}2&0\\0&0\end{pmatrix},
 \qquad c=\begin{pmatrix}1\\1\end{pmatrix}.
\]
Both scalar sums equal \(2\), whereas the column-sum row is
\((2,0)\).
\end{proof}

\begin{proposition}[Local defects may cancel]
\label{prop:cancel}
There are nonconservative stages whose composite is the identity.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Take
\[
 T_1=\begin{pmatrix}2&0\\0&1/2\end{pmatrix},
 \qquad
 T_2=\begin{pmatrix}1/2&0\\0&2\end{pmatrix}.
\]
Neither stage has column sums \((1,1)\), but \(T_2T_1=I\).
\end{proof}

\begin{proposition}[Conservation does not imply provenance]
\label{prop:tags}
A permutation matrix can satisfy coefficientwise conservation while
exchanging two incompatible metadata tags.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Every permutation matrix has unit column sums.  Permute a record
tagged \(h_0\) with one tagged \(h_1\ne h_0\).  The coefficient sum is
unchanged, but \eqref{eq:metadata} fails for the shift projection.
\end{proof}

These examples rule out three common shortcuts: testing only the
actual coefficient vector, testing only the final composite, and
testing only untagged weights.

\section{Invocation, data requirements and route verdicts}

\begin{definition}[Paper-specific verdicts]
\(\GOI\) means that the complete symbolic family of actual stage
tables, not merely finitely many numerical samples, passes
\cref{thm:local-global,thm:metadata}.  \(\STOP\) means that an exact
defect disproves the declared archive implementation.  It is not a
global impossibility theorem.  \(\NT\) means that a stage table,
boundary rule, metadata dictionary or typed comparison object is
absent.
\end{definition}

The local dictionaries of TPC-105 and TPC-107 explain how provenance
and signed filtering should be retained, while TPC-114 and TPC-117
specify later physical grouping and cover objects
\citep{WangTPC105,WangTPC107,WangTPC114,WangTPC117}.  They do not,
by themselves, provide every \(T_j\).  Consequently the current
repository still lacks the complete growing input needed to run the
present audit:
\[
 \boxed{
 \operatorname{Archive}_{123}
 =
 \NT\ \text{from current artifacts}.}
\]
This is a dated administrative statement, not a theorem that no
alternative exact H8 proof can exist.

\section{Reproducible finite audit and claim ledger}

The standard-library script
\texttt{experiments/tpc123\_path\_archive\_audit.py} uses exact
rational arithmetic.  It checks two conservative stages, their
composite, a retained--soft split, and \cref{thm:defect}.  It also
constructs the three counterexamples above and rejects a wrong shift
tag.  Its small model is a regression test for the finite theorem; it
is not the missing TPC archive.

\paragraph{Explicit exclusions.}
This paper proves no determinant-energy exponent, no zero-mode
exponent, no complete physical frame, no fixed-shift localization
bound, no signed affine M\"obius cancellation, no endpoint below
\(1/400\), no parity breakthrough, no Hardy--Littlewood asymptotic,
and no prime-pair or twin-prime theorem.  TPC-121 and TPC-122 remain
conditional exponent interfaces \citep{WangTPC120,WangTPC121,WangTPC122}.
