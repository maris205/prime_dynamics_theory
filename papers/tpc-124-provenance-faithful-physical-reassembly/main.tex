\title{\textbf{Provenance-Faithful Physical Reassembly of the TPC Hard Packet}\\
\large Native-Leaf and Physical Residuals, Exact Block Cover, and
Dual Certificates}
\author{Liang Wang}
\date{July 2026}

\maketitle

\begin{abstract}
TPC-117 gives the exact range test for a physical TPC-18 block
matrix, while TPC-119 requires every final scalar contribution to
remain connected to its original TPC-15 atom.  These are not the same
test.  Starting from a literal native-leaf vector \(z\), let
\(G\) be the declared leaf-to-physical grouping, \(C\) the
block-to-leaf synthesis and \(B=GC\) the physical block matrix.  We
compare the canonical residuals
\[
 r_{\rm leaf}=(I-CC^\dagger)z,
 \qquad
 r_{\rm phys}=(I-BB^\dagger)Gz.
\]
They give the two exact decompositions
\[
 z=CC^\dagger z+r_{\rm leaf},
 \qquad
 Gz=BC^\dagger z+Gr_{\rm leaf}
     =BB^\dagger Gz+r_{\rm phys}.
\]
Native-leaf cover implies physical cover, but the converse fails
sharply when \(G\) hides a provenance direction.  Separate dual
witnesses therefore stop the two cover claims, and the exact Gram
crosswalk is \(B^*B=C^*G^*GC\).  We also give a coefficientwise test
for identifying determinant fibers with zero-mode outer fibers;
agreement on one physical vector is insufficient.  These are
\(\Lzero\) identities and \(\Lone\) interfaces.  The actual growing
\(G,C,z,B\) archive and its residual estimates remain absent, so the
physical reassembly audit is \(\NT\).  No \(\Ltwo\) arithmetic
saving, endpoint gain or prime-pair theorem is claimed.
\end{abstract}

\section{Why two cover spaces are necessary}

TPC-123 replaces the missing global leaf archive by an exact
path-expanded object whose columns are original TPC-15 atoms
\citep{WangTPC15,WangTPC123}.  If that archive is complete, its
retained terminal records form a vector
\[
 z_X=M_X^{\rm ret}c_X\in\C^{\cL_X}.
\]
TPC-114 then groups residual labels into native physical labels,
whereas TPC-117 asks whether an actual physical vector lies in the
range of the TPC-18 block synthesis
\citep{WangTPC114,WangTPC117}.  Grouping is generally noninjective.
It follows that two different statements must be audited:
\begin{enumerate}[label=(\roman*),leftmargin=2.2em]
\item every native leaf is synthesized by the declared blocks; and
\item the grouped physical vector is synthesized by the grouped
blocks.
\end{enumerate}
The first is a provenance statement relevant to H8.  The second is a
physical range statement relevant to H6.  Neither statement estimates
the hard packet.

\begin{definition}[Claim levels]
Finite Hilbert-space identities are \(\Lzero\).  Identifying their
spaces and maps with the literal TPC records is \(\Lone\).  A uniform
growing fixed-\(h_0\) arithmetic power saving is \(\Ltwo\).
\end{definition}

\section{The literal reassembly diagram}

Let \(\cL_X\), \(\cP_X\), and \(\cQ_X\) be finite-dimensional Hilbert
spaces of native leaves, physical carriers, and block coefficients.
The declared maps are
\[
 C_X:\cQ_X\to\cL_X,\qquad
 G_X:\cL_X\to\cP_X,\qquad
 B_X=G_XC_X:\cQ_X\to\cP_X.
\]
Put \(z=z_X\), \(G=G_X\), \(C=C_X\), \(B=B_X\), and
\[
 w=Gz.
\]
All adjoints and Moore--Penrose inverses refer to the declared
physical Hilbert norms, not to a convenient surrogate norm
\citep{BenIsraelGreville,WangTPC113}.

\begin{definition}[Two canonical coefficients and residuals]
Define
\begin{align}
 q_{\rm leaf}&=C^\dagger z,
 &
 r_{\rm leaf}&=(I-CC^\dagger)z,
 \label{eq:leaf}\\
 q_{\rm phys}&=B^\dagger w,
 &
 r_{\rm phys}&=(I-BB^\dagger)w.
 \label{eq:phys}
\end{align}
\end{definition}

The coefficient vectors need not agree.  The first is least squares
before grouping; the second is least squares in the physical norm
after grouping.  Substituting one for the other without proving an
isometry is a normalization change.

\section{The two-residual theorem}

\begin{theorem}[Provenance--physical reassembly]
\label{thm:reassembly}
The following identities hold exactly:
\begin{align}
 z&=Cq_{\rm leaf}+r_{\rm leaf},
 \label{eq:zdec}\\
 w&=Bq_{\rm leaf}+Gr_{\rm leaf},
 \label{eq:wleaf}\\
 w&=Bq_{\rm phys}+r_{\rm phys}.
 \label{eq:wphys}
\end{align}
Moreover,
\[
 r_{\rm leaf}\perp\Ran(C),
 \qquad
 r_{\rm phys}\perp\Ran(B).
\]
In particular,
\[
 r_{\rm leaf}=0\quad\Longrightarrow\quad r_{\rm phys}=0.
\]
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
The matrices \(CC^\dagger\) and \(BB^\dagger\) are the orthogonal
projections onto \(\Ran(C)\) and \(\Ran(B)\), respectively.  This
proves \eqref{eq:zdec} and \eqref{eq:wphys} together with the
orthogonality statements.  Applying \(G\) to \eqref{eq:zdec} and
using \(GC=B\) proves \eqref{eq:wleaf}.  If \(r_{\rm leaf}=0\), then
\(w=BC^\dagger z\in\Ran(B)\), so its orthogonal physical residual
vanishes.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}[No reverse implication]
The theorem does not assert
\(r_{\rm phys}=0\Rightarrow r_{\rm leaf}=0\).  A component of
\(r_{\rm leaf}\) can lie in \(\Ker(G)\), or its grouped image can fall
back into \(\Ran(B)\).  Physical equality alone may therefore erase
the missing native route.
\end{remark}

\begin{theorem}[Separate dual certificates]
\label{thm:dual}
The two exact cover conditions are
\begin{align*}
 r_{\rm leaf}=0
 &\iff
 \langle y,z\rangle=0
 \quad\text{for every }y\in\Ker(C^*),\\
 r_{\rm phys}=0
 &\iff
 \langle v,w\rangle=0
 \quad\text{for every }v\in\Ker(B^*).
\end{align*}
Thus one \(y\in\Ker(C^*)\) with
\(\langle y,z\rangle\ne0\) stops exact native-leaf cover, and one
\(v\in\Ker(B^*)\) with \(\langle v,w\rangle\ne0\) stops exact physical
cover.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
For any finite-dimensional operator \(A\),
\(\Ran(A)=\Ker(A^*)^\perp\).  Apply this to \(C\) and \(B\), then use
\cref{eq:leaf,eq:phys}.
\end{proof}

\section{Scalar evaluation and the native Gram}

Let \(a\in\cP_X\) be the actual physical evaluator and let \(\nu_X\)
be the one inherited normalization.

\begin{corollary}[Exact scalar return]
\label{cor:scalar}
\[
 \boxed{
 \nu_X\langle a,w\rangle
 =
 \nu_X\langle B^*a,q_{\rm phys}\rangle
 +
 \nu_X\langle a,r_{\rm phys}\rangle.}
\]
There is also the provenance-resolving form
\[
 \nu_X\langle a,w\rangle
 =
 \nu_X\langle B^*a,q_{\rm leaf}\rangle
 +
 \nu_X\langle G^*a,r_{\rm leaf}\rangle.
\]
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
Pair \eqref{eq:wphys} and \eqref{eq:wleaf} with \(a\) and use the
adjoint identity.
\end{proof}

\begin{proposition}[Exact native Gram crosswalk]
\label{prop:gram}
The physical block Gram is
\[
 \boxed{B^*B=C^*G^*GC.}
\]
Consequently any determinant, singular value or condition number
used after grouping must be computed from the literal \(G\) and
\(C\).  Replacing \(G^*G\) by the identity requires an additional
isometry theorem.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Use \(B=GC\) and take adjoints.
\end{proof}

This identity attaches the native Gram warning of TPC-114 to the
physical range test of TPC-117.  It does not say that a large or small
condition number is an unavoidable endpoint exponent.  TPC-113 and
TPC-118 require the actual composite norm and a nonduplicated cost
registry \citep{WangTPC113,WangTPC118}.

\section{The quotient observability operator}

The two residuals are not unrelated.  Let
\[
 P_B=BB^\dagger
\]
and restrict the operator
\[
 H=(I-P_B)G\big|_{\Ker(C^*)}:
 \Ker(C^*)\longrightarrow\Ker(B^*).
\]
This is the part of the physical grouping that remains visible after
quotienting out the declared block range.

\begin{theorem}[Exact residual transfer]
\label{thm:residual-transfer}
For the residuals in \eqref{eq:leaf}--\eqref{eq:phys},
\begin{equation}
 \boxed{r_{\rm phys}=Hr_{\rm leaf}.}
 \label{eq:residual-transfer}
\end{equation}
Consequently,
\[
 \|r_{\rm phys}\|
 \le
 \|H\|\,\|r_{\rm leaf}\|
 \le
 \|G\|\,\|r_{\rm leaf}\|.
\]
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
By \eqref{eq:wleaf},
\[
 w=Bq_{\rm leaf}+Gr_{\rm leaf}.
\]
Apply \(I-P_B\).  The first term vanishes because it lies in
\(\Ran(B)\), while the left side becomes \(r_{\rm phys}\).
Moreover \(r_{\rm leaf}\in\Ker(C^*)\), so the remaining term is
exactly \(Hr_{\rm leaf}\).  The norm bounds follow from the
definition and from \(\|I-P_B\|\le1\).
\end{proof}

Define the quotient observability constant
\[
 \gamma_X
 =
 \inf_{\substack{r\in\Ker(C^*)\\\|r\|=1}}\|Hr\|,
\]
when \(\Ker(C^*)\ne\{0\}\).  In finite dimension, \(\gamma_X\) is the
minimum modulus of the restricted operator \(H\) on its full declared
domain.  Equivalently, it is the smallest singular value when zeros
coming from \(\Ker(H)\) are included.  Thus \(\gamma_X>0\) precisely
when \(H\) is injective.  The least \emph{positive} singular value on
\(\Ker(H)^\perp\) is a different quantity and does not give a reverse
bound for all native residuals when \(\Ker(H)\ne\{0\}\).  If
\(\Ker(C^*)=\{0\}\), then \(C\) is already onto the native-leaf space
and \(r_{\rm leaf}=0\) for every \(z\), so no reverse-transfer
constant is needed.

\begin{corollary}[When physical cover detects provenance cover]
\label{cor:observable}
If \(\Ker(C^*)\ne\{0\}\) and \(\gamma_X>0\), then
\[
 \gamma_X\|r_{\rm leaf}\|
 \le
 \|r_{\rm phys}\|
 \le
 \|H\|\|r_{\rm leaf}\|,
\]
and hence
\[
 r_{\rm phys}=0\iff r_{\rm leaf}=0.
\]
The same equivalence holds trivially when \(\Ker(C^*)=\{0\}\).
If \(\gamma_X=0\), there is a nonzero native residual direction that
is physically invisible modulo \(\Ran(B)\).
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
When the kernel is zero, \(r_{\rm leaf}=0\) and
\cref{thm:reassembly} applies.  Otherwise, the lower bound is the
definition of \(\gamma_X\), applied to
\(r_{\rm leaf}\).  The upper bound is
\cref{thm:residual-transfer}.  In finite dimension,
\(\gamma_X=0\) means that \(H\) has a nonzero kernel vector.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}[A criterion, not a free endpoint gain]
A growing lower bound for \(\gamma_X\) could certify that physical
residual control transfers back to native provenance control.  The
present paper neither supplies the actual operator \(H_X\) nor proves
such a lower bound.  Also, \(\gamma_X^{-1}\) would be a norm-conversion
cost, not an arithmetic saving; it must be placed in the physical
ledger only when the actual proof invokes this reverse transfer.
\end{remark}

\section{A sharp provenance-camouflage example}

\begin{proposition}[Physical cover can hide a native residual]
\label{prop:camouflage}
There exist \(G,C,z\) with \(r_{\rm leaf}\ne0\) and
\(r_{\rm phys}=0\).
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
In standard Euclidean coordinates take
\[
 C=
 \begin{pmatrix}
 1&0\\0&1\\0&0
 \end{pmatrix},
 \qquad
 z=
 \begin{pmatrix}0\\0\\1\end{pmatrix},
 \qquad
 G=
 \begin{pmatrix}
 1&0&0\\0&1&0
 \end{pmatrix}.
\]
Then \(r_{\rm leaf}=z\ne0\), while \(w=Gz=0\).  Hence the physical
vector lies in \(\Ran(GC)\) and \(r_{\rm phys}=0\).
\end{proof}

Here \(H=0\) on \(\Ker(C^*)=\C e_3\), so \(\gamma_X=0\).
The example is not a claim about the actual TPC archive.  It proves
that an H6 range certificate cannot be promoted to H8 without either
the native-leaf audit or a positive quotient-observability
certificate.

\section{Determinant fibers and zero-mode fibers}

TPC-121 groups raw atoms into determinant bins, while TPC-122 orders
literal outer fibers for signed-prefix control
\citep{WangTPC121,WangTPC122}.  These are different declared
dictionaries.  Denote their finite index sets by
\(\mathcal D_X\) and \(\mathcal Z_X\), respectively, and let
\[
 Q_D:\cL_X\to\C^{\mathcal D_X},
 \qquad
 Q_Z:\cL_X\to\C^{\mathcal Z_X}
\]
be their exact leaf-level aggregation matrices.  A proposed
identification
\(J:\C^{\mathcal D_X}\to\C^{\mathcal Z_X}\) must be proved, not
inferred from similar symbols.  The scalar determinant energy
\(D_X\) and scalar distinguished zero mode \(Z_X\) retain their
earlier meanings and are not index sets.

\begin{theorem}[Coefficientwise fiber-intertwining test]
\label{thm:fibers}
Let \(M:\C^{\cA_X}\to\cL_X\) be the complete native-leaf matrix.  The
two fiber descriptions agree on every original packet coefficient
vector precisely when
\[
 \boxed{(JQ_D-Q_Z)M=0.}
\]
Agreement on one coefficient vector \(c_X\),
\((JQ_D-Q_Z)Mc_X=0\), is not sufficient for this matrix identity.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
The two descriptions agree on every input if and only if the two
linear maps \(JQ_DM\) and \(Q_ZM\) are equal, which is the displayed
matrix condition.  Equality after applying them to one vector tests
only one element of their difference kernel.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}[No exponent transfer]
Even if \cref{thm:fibers} passes, TPC-121 and TPC-122 provide only
conditional formulas for \(\lambda_D^{\rm cert}\) and
\(\eta_Z^{\rm cert}\).  They do not prove their growing inputs, and
their determinant reserve cannot be charged against the physical
endpoint ledger.
\end{remark}

\section{Typed invocation and route decisions}

\begin{definition}[Paper-specific verdicts]
\(\GOI\) means that the indicated exact map and all its quantifiers
are present.  \(\STOP\) means that an exact dual witness,
normalization mismatch or provenance defect stops the declared
reassembly.  It does not stop every possible representation.
\(\NT\) means that the actual growing matrix or typed vector required
to execute a test is absent.
\end{definition}

At present, TPC-123 supplies the schema rather than the actual
growing leaf vector, and TPC-117 reports that the literal growing
\(B,w\) archive is unavailable.  Hence
\[
 \boxed{
 \operatorname{Reassembly}_{124}
 =
 \NT\ \text{from current artifacts}.}
\]
This is an executable-data verdict, not an impossibility theorem.

\section{Reproducible audit and claim boundary}

The standard-library script
\texttt{experiments/tpc124\_reassembly\_audit.py} uses exact rational
coordinate models.  It verifies both residual decompositions, a dual
witness, the scalar return, \cref{prop:gram}, and both extremal
quotient-observability regimes.  It also constructs
\cref{prop:camouflage} and two distinct fiber dictionaries that agree
on one vector but not coefficientwise.  The model is not the missing
physical archive.

\paragraph{Explicit exclusions.}
This paper proves no complete physical cover for the TPC packet, no
