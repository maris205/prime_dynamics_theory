# TPC-226 proof package

## Claim

Let $Q\ge8$, let $L\in\{1,2,3,4\}$, put $H=4Q^2$ and $h_L=4LQ$, and let
$\mathcal Q_Q$ be the primes in $(Q,2Q)$.  For each active prime define the literal
primitive support

$$
 S_{q,L}=\left\{m q^{-1}\pmod{h_L}:
 0<|m|\le\left\lfloor\frac{Lq}{Q}\right\rfloor,
 (m,h_L)=1\right\}.
$$

Then:

1. For $L=1,2,3$, the sets $S_{q,L}$ are pairwise disjoint across distinct primes.
2. For $L=4$, every cross-prime collision is, up to exchanging the primes and changing
   both signs, generated by

   $$
   7p+3r=16Q,\qquad m_p=3,\qquad m_r=-7. \tag{1}
   $$

   Conversely every pair satisfying the literal shell, cutoff, primitive conditions and
   (1) produces exactly two sign-symmetric shared coordinates.
3. The pair $Q=25$, $(p,r)=(37,47)$ is an exact witness.
4. On any nonempty dilation-four resonance graph, aligned and inherited affine profiles
   give $E_{\rm AP}>E_{\rm diag}$, whereas balanced sign profiles give
   $E_{\rm AP}<E_{\rm diag}$ and $E_{\rm pol}=E_{\rm all}=0$.

## Status

**PROVABLE AS STATED** for the declared finite dilated-clock family.

The statement is structural.  It does not identify this family with the physical V46
clock and does not import an arithmetic distribution estimate.

## Assumptions

- $Q\ge8$ is integral and $L\in\{1,2,3,4\}$.
- Active labels are primes $Q<q<2Q$.
- Every multiplier is primitive modulo $h_L$.
- All rows use $C_h=1/h_L$ and the same residue space.
- The energy statement uses the literal rows defined in the derivation package.

## Notation

Write $R_L(q)=\lfloor Lq/Q\rfloor$.  For a collision use positive absolute
multipliers $a,b$.  At $L=4$, write $\mathcal R_Q$ for the prime pairs $(p,r)$
satisfying (1) and the literal support conditions.

## Proof strategy

Convert a collision into a bounded divisibility equation.  Stable-range size excludes
zero wrap and equal signs.  Primitive parity and cutoff lower bounds then perform a
finite exact classification.  The energy assertions follow by inserting the two shared
coordinates into the Gram expansion.

## Dependency map

1. Support injectivity uses $|m|\le7<q$ and $|m_1-m_2|<h_L$.
2. Cross-row classification uses the exact congruence
   $m_1q_2\equiv m_2q_1\pmod{h_L}$.
3. The $L\le3$ obstruction uses primitivity, parity, and cutoff lower bounds.
4. The $L=4$ classification uses a finite multiplier table plus the same bounds.
5. The energy sign uses only the exact Gram expansion and the declared profile values.

## Proof

### Step 1: inverses and support injectivity

Every active prime is odd.  It cannot divide $Q$, since it is larger than $Q$, and it
cannot divide $L\le4$, since it is larger than $Q\ge8$.  Hence $(q,h_L)=1$.

The cutoff satisfies $L\le R_L(q)\le2L-1\le7$.  If two multipliers in one row have
the same coordinate, multiplication by $q$ gives $m_1\equiv m_2\pmod{h_L}$.  Since
$|m_1-m_2|\le14<h_L$, they are equal.  Each row is internally injective.

### Step 2: reduction to one positive equation

Suppose distinct primes $q_1,q_2$ share a coordinate.  Then

$$
 m_1q_2-m_2q_1=k h_L \tag{2}
$$

for some integer $k$.  If $m_1,m_2$ have the same sign, the absolute value of the
left side is strictly smaller than

$$
 (2L-1)2Q-Q=(4L-3)Q<4LQ=h_L.
$$

Thus $k=0$.  But (2) with $k=0$ implies $q_1\mid m_1q_2$; since the primes are
distinct, $q_1\mid m_1$, contradicting $0<|m_1|\le7<q_1$.

The signs are therefore opposite.  With $a=|m_1|$ and $b=|m_2|$, the left side in
absolute value is less than $2h_L$, so (2) is equivalent to

$$
 a q_2+b q_1=4LQ. \tag{3}
$$

Because $h_L$ is even and both multipliers are primitive, $a,b$ are odd.  The shell
bounds in (3) imply

$$
 2L<a+b<4L. \tag{4}
$$

### Step 3: exclusion for $L=1,2,3$

For $L=1$, the only positive primitive multiplier is $1$, whose pair sum is $2$ and
does not satisfy (4).

For $L=2$, positive primitive multipliers are among $1,3$.  Condition (4) leaves only
$(a,b)=(3,3)$.  Each occurrence of multiplier $3$ requires
$R_2(q_i)\ge3$, hence $q_i\ge3Q/2$.  Therefore the left side of (3) is at least
$9Q$, larger than $8Q$.

For $L=3$, primitivity modulo $12Q$ excludes multiplier $3$, so the only possible
positive multipliers are $1,5$.  Condition (4) leaves only $(5,5)$.  Its two cutoff
conditions imply $q_i\ge5Q/3$, making the left side at least $50Q/3>12Q$.

This proves pairwise disjointness for $L\le3$.

### Step 4: classification for $L=4$

Positive primitive multipliers are drawn from $1,3,5,7$.  From (4), the unordered
candidate pairs are

$$
 (3,7),\ (5,5),\ (5,7),\ (7,7). \tag{5}
$$

For $(5,7)$, the cutoff conditions give $q_1\ge5Q/4$ and $q_2\ge7Q/4$
after matching the multipliers to their rows.  The corresponding left side of (3) is
at least

$$
 5\frac{7Q}{4}+7\frac{5Q}{4}=\frac{35Q}{2}>16Q.
$$

The pair $(7,7)$ is even larger.  For $(5,5)$, equation (3) is
$5(q_1+q_2)=16Q$.  Primitivity of multiplier $5$ requires $5\nmid h_4=16Q$,
so $5\nmid Q$; the displayed equality would imply the opposite.  Thus only $(3,7)$
and its exchange remain, proving the necessity of (1).

Conversely, assume (1), the shell and cutoff inequalities, and $(21,16Q)=1$.  Then
$3$ and $7$ are allowed primitive multipliers, and multiplication of (1) by the
inverses gives

$$
 3p^{-1}\equiv-7r^{-1}\pmod{16Q}.
$$

Changing both signs gives the second collision.  The two coordinates are distinct,
because equality between them would imply $16Q\mid6$.  The classification above shows
there are no further collisions for this pair.

### Step 5: the first witness

For $Q=25$, both $37$ and $47$ are active primes, their cutoffs are $5$ and $7$, and
$(21,400)=1$.  Directly,

$$
 7\cdot37+3\cdot47=400.
$$

The modular inverses are $173$ and $383$, yielding the shared coordinates $119$ and
$281$.  This proves existence at the first certified stable scale.

### Step 6: exact energy correction

For a resonance $(p,r)$, define $u_p=3Q/(4p)$ and $v_r=7Q/(4r)$.  The two shared
coordinates contribute

$$
 \frac{2}{h^2}\Re\sum_j\left(
 \psi_j(u_p)\overline{\psi_j(-v_r)}+
 \psi_j(-u_p)\overline{\psi_j(v_r)}\right) \tag{6}
$$

to $E_{\rm AP}-E_{\rm diag}$.  All other coordinates are disjoint, so summing (6)
over $\mathcal R_Q$ is the complete correction.

For $\psi_j=1$, (6) is $4J/h^2>0$.  For
$\psi_j(t)=1+s_jt$, the bracket summed over $j$ is
$2(J-S_2u_pv_r)$, where $S_2=3/50$; since $0<u_p,v_r\le1$, it is positive.

Let $\chi$ be a smooth compactly supported odd plateau with
$\chi(t)=\operatorname{sgn}(t)$ for $1/8\le|t|\le1$.  Every sampled nonzero
argument lies in this interval.  For $\psi_j(t)=\alpha_j\chi(t)$ with
$\alpha=(1,-1,1,-1)$, (6) is

$$
 -\frac4{h^2}\left(\sum_j\alpha_j^2\right)<0.
$$

If at least one resonance exists, the aligned and affine inequalities are strict in the
amplifying direction, while the balanced sign inequality is strict in the saving
direction.  Finally $\sum_j\alpha_j=0$, so every prime-polarized row vanishes and
$E_{\rm pol}=E_{\rm all}=0$.

At $Q=25$, direct rational substitution gives the three ratios stated in the claim's
derivation package.  Therefore all four assertions follow.  ∎

## Corrections or missing assumptions

- The stable assumption $Q\ge8$ is essential for the uniform argument $|m|<q$.
- The primitive condition is essential; dropping it creates a false earlier transition.
- The packet profile sign is not derived from the V46 arithmetic source.

## Open risks

The proof determines the geometry and its exact signed interface, but not the sign of the
physical packet correlation.  A later theorem must derive that sign from the literal
source rather than select it as a profile fixture.
