# Exact residue decomposition

Let $\alpha=r/R$ in lowest terms and let $$C_r(Z)=\sum_{z\in Z}c(z)\mathrm{e}(-rz/R).$$ Partitioning by $z=b+Rm$ gives the identity $$\label{eq:dft}
 C_r(Z)=\sum_{b\bmod R}\mathrm{e}(-rb/R)
 \sum_{\substack{m\\b+Rm\in Z}}c(b+Rm).$$ For the determinant-two forms $d+sz,u+az$, the two forms on the $b$-th class are $$(d+sb)+sRm,\qquad (u+ab)+aRm.$$

> **Theorem: Determinant and slope-gcd ledger** If $su-ad=2$ and $as$ is odd, then $(a,s)=1$. After residue splitting the two slopes $sR,aR$ have gcd exactly $R$, while their determinant is $$(sR)(u+ab)-(aR)(d+sb)=2R.$$ Equation [\[eq:dft\]](../main.tex#L40){reference-type="eqref" reference="eq:dft"} is one DFT coordinate. Recovering every residue sum requires all $R$ Fourier coordinates and DFT inversion.

> **Proof** Any common divisor of $a,s$ divides $2$; oddness forces it to be one. The displayed determinant follows by expansion. The final assertion is the invertibility of the $R\times R$ Fourier matrix; a single row has a kernel of dimension $R-1$ when $R>1$.
