\begin{abstract}
We test whether a single balanced aggregation rule can replicate a finite
prime-shell preference across a held-out tolerance slice.  The input is the
locked TPC-309 atlas of three source-backed profile ladders, three adjacent
shell transitions, two kernel exponents, three tolerances, and three finite
completion radii.  The declared rule first pools the three profile ladders
inside each fixed design cell and then gives every design cell equal arithmetic
weight.  At the primary native radius, the calibration slice
$\{0.25,0.5\}$ has ratio interval
$[4.0615814676,4.0617439341]$ and is left-lower, whereas the held-out slice
$\{0.75\}$ has interval $[0.6818442327,0.6818715070]$ and is right-lower.
The strict class is therefore reversed.  Including adversarial radii one and
two leaves the calibration block left-lower but makes the confirmation block
unresolved.  We prove the finite two-stage interval algebra and independently
replay 54 strata, six blocks, and 22 sensitivity controls.  This is a scoped
parameter-slice obstruction on one padded float-replay atlas, not a fresh-data,
causal, asymptotic, arithmetic, or twin-prime theorem.
\end{abstract}

\section{Question and route position}

The recent finite diagnostics have progressively separated several sources of
instability.  TPC-307 introduced a common ambient operator with overlap-only
fits and exclusive shell holdouts \cite{tpc307}.  TPC-308 enumerated binary
completion envelopes on those holdouts \cite{tpc308}.  TPC-309 shifted a
source-backed 17-coordinate profile window through one 19-prime pool and found
that the location of strict discordance changes with the profile
\cite{tpc309}.  TPC-310 then showed that pooling losses and averaging row ratios
can reverse a finite class \cite{tpc310}.

TPC-311 asks the smallest next question suggested by that obstruction: after
fixing one balanced design rule, does a preference found on two tolerance
slices survive the third?  The answer is deliberately scoped.  The held-out
slice below is a disjoint parameter slice of the same locked parent atlas; it
is not an independent physical sample.  Likewise, the rule is declared in
this child project but is not an externally timestamped preregistration.

\section{Locked data and notation}

For a profile ladder $j\in\{\mathrm{LOW},\mathrm{BASE},\mathrm{HIGH}\}$,
transition $q\in\{50\!\to\!60,60\!\to\!70,70\!\to\!90\}$, exponent
$e\in\{1,2\}$, tolerance $t\in\{0.25,0.5,0.75\}$, and radius
$r\in\{0,1,2\}$, the TPC-309 certificate supplies positive extrema
\begin{align*}
 R_{j,q,e,t,r}^{-}&\le R_{j,q,e,t,r}^{+},\\
 L_{j,q,e,t,r}^{-}&\le L_{j,q,e,t,r}^{+}.
\end{align*}
They are the lower endpoint of the corresponding right/left minimum envelope
and the upper endpoint of the corresponding maximum envelope.  There are
\[
  3\cdot2\cdot3\cdot3=54
\]
design cells and three profile rows in each, hence 162 locked parent
observations.  We do not regenerate labels, shell partitions, physical
matrices, or completion balls.

The strict class map used throughout is
\[
 \mathsf{class}([a,b])=
 \begin{cases}
 \mathsf{RIGHT},&b<0.9,\\
 \mathsf{LEFT},&a>1.1,\\
 \mathsf{UNRESOLVED},&\text{otherwise.}
 \end{cases}
\]

\section{Declared two-stage protocol}

Fix a design cell $s=(q,e,t,r)$.  First pool the replicated profile ladders:
\begin{equation}
 P_s=\left[
 \frac{\sum_j R_{j,s}^{-}}{\sum_j L_{j,s}^{+}},
 \frac{\sum_j R_{j,s}^{+}}{\sum_j L_{j,s}^{-}}
 \right].
 \label{eq:within}
\end{equation}
The second stage gives each selected design cell equal weight.  For a nonempty
block $B$ of cells, set
\begin{equation}
 S(B)=\left[
 \frac{1}{|B|}\sum_{s\in B}P_s^{-},
 \frac{1}{|B|}\sum_{s\in B}P_s^{+}
 \right].
 \label{eq:between}
\end{equation}
This removes the loss-scale weights from the profile dimension, while making
the design-cell weighting explicit rather than implicit.

The calibration block uses $t\in\{0.25,0.5\}$ and the confirmation block uses
$t=0.75$.  They are disjoint and together cover the declared tolerance grid.
The primary endpoint is $r=0$, the native completion.  The all-radius blocks
with $r\in\{0,1,2\}$ are adversarial stress controls, not a second primary
choice.  We also compute exponent slices, transition slices, leave-one-
transition controls, and leave-one-ladder controls without changing the
primary rule.

\section{Finite statements}

\begin{lemma}[Independent finite extrema]
Let $X_j$ be nonempty finite sets with extrema $x_j^-$ and $x_j^+$.  If the
choices are independent, then
\[
 \min\sum_jx_j=\sum_jx_j^-,\qquad
 \max\sum_jx_j=\sum_jx_j^+.
\]
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Termwise endpoint inequalities give both bounds.  Choosing all lower, or all
upper, endpoints attains the corresponding bound.
\end{proof}

\begin{proposition}[Soundness of the two-stage map]
Equations \eqref{eq:within} and \eqref{eq:between} are ordered positive
interval enclosures for the declared finite operations.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
All four endpoint sums in \eqref{eq:within} are positive.  The lemma gives
the independent numerator extrema, and quotient monotonicity reverses the
denominator endpoint, proving the first enclosure.  Each $P_s^-$ is at most
$P_s^+$; summing these inequalities and dividing by the positive integer
$|B|$ proves the second enclosure.  The two tolerance sets are visibly
disjoint and exhaustive on the three-point grid.
\end{proof}

The finite class relation between calibration $C$ and confirmation $H$ is
called \emph{strictly replicated} when their strict classes agree, \emph{strictly
reversed} when one is RIGHT and the other LEFT, and \emph{unresolved-slice}
otherwise.  This terminology is a finite decision diagnostic, not a
statistical generalization claim.

\section{Adversarial localization controls}

The reversal is not evidence that the equal-stratum rule has become canonical.
The controls locate several remaining dependencies.  At native radius, the
exponent slices in Table~\ref{tab:exponent} have opposite calibration classes;
the exponent-one confirmation is unresolved, while exponent two remains
right-lower.

The native calibration class is also not profile-subset invariant.  Omitting
LOW, BASE, or HIGH gives respectively Left, Right, and Left; each corresponding
confirmation control is Right.  In particular, deleting BASE changes the
calibration class from the primary Left to Right without altering the
confirmation slice.  Transition controls show the same localization: the
calibration classes for $50\to60$, $60\to70$, and $70\to90$ are respectively
Right, Left, and Right, whereas their confirmation classes are respectively
Right, Unresolved, and Right.  These are controls on the finite atlas, not
multiple-testing-adjusted statistical estimates.

\section{Interpretation and obstruction}

The declared rule does accomplish one useful separation.  Within a design cell,
profile ladders are treated symmetrically; between cells, the factorial design
is explicit.  The exact proof is therefore reusable whenever a future source
supplies positive finite extrema with the same independence structure.

It does not solve the problem exposed by TPC-310.  The primary orientation
does not replicate from the two-point calibration tolerance slice to the
held-out third tolerance.  Adding completion adversaries makes the held-out
interval unresolved, and the exponent and profile controls show that the
finite orientation is still structured by other axes.  A weighting convention
can be made explicit without becoming arithmetically justified.

There are two important scope boundaries.  First, the confirmation rows come
from the same locked parent certificate, so this is a parameter-slice holdout,
not fresh physical data.  Second, the child protocol was declared for this
release but was not externally timestamped before the broader research
session.  The result should therefore be read as a reproducible obstruction
to one fixed rule, not as an unbiased preregistered statistical test.

\section{Claim firewall and conclusion}

The exact claims are the 54-cell census, the independent profile-pooling
extrema, the positive equal-stratum interval map, and the finite tau partition.
The numerical claims are a replay of padded float-derived decimal intervals.
They are not directed-rounding guarantees for the unrounded physical source.
TPC-302 target-generation leakage remains inherited.  No arithmetic $L^2$
estimate, fixed-power credit, uniform asymptotic budget, causal identification,
full Gate-B passage, or twin-prime conclusion is obtained.

The scoped conclusion is:
\begin{quote}
For the locked TPC-309 finite atlas, the declared profile-pooled,
design-equal stratification rule is strictly LEFT on the calibration tolerance
slice but strictly RIGHT on the native held-out slice $\tau=0.75$.  It does
not provide a replication-stable global preference.  Closing this obstruction
requires an externally justified weighting law and a genuinely fresh physical
holdout.
\end{quote}
