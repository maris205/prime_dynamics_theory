\section{Route and exponent ledger}
\label{sec:route-ledger}

The positive exceptional route now reads
\[
\begin{array}{c|c}
\text{piece}&\text{status}\\ \hline
\text{conductor one}
 &O(X^{o(1)}Q_X^2)\text{ in TPC-98}\\
q_X\nmid\Omega,\ q_X\mid u
 &O(X^{o(1)}Q_X^2)\text{ in TPC-100}\\
\text{principal nonconstant resonance}
 &\displaystyle\sum_H\frac{2H}{q_X-1}
 M_H^{\mathrm{inv}}\text{ gate}\\
\text{centered nonconstant resonance}
 &\displaystyle\sum_Hg_{q_X}(H)
 \|(a_H^{\mathrm{inv}})^\circ\|_2\text{ gate}.
\end{array}
\]
The centered row is strictly sharper than the TPC-99
worst-eigenvalue return.  Under a subpower opened-atom cap, its
diagonal part follows from the principal row by
\cref{thm:principal-diagonal-transfer}.  The remaining positive
obligation is therefore:
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=2em]
\item verify the literal principal mass;
\item verify the normalized opened-atom cap; and
\item control the width-weighted cross-cell excess
  \eqref{eq:weighted-cross-gate}.
\end{enumerate}
If the atom cap fails at polynomial scale, it must be entered in the
physical budget and the diagonal gate reopens.  If proof-carrying
cross-cell alignment makes \eqref{eq:weighted-cross-gate} fail at a
polynomial scale, this termwise positive modulus-incidence
implementation stops and one must use a sharper filter-sensitive
bound or retain signed affine arithmetic.

The ledgers remain independent:
\[
 \lambda_D\le2\eta_Z
\]
is the determinant/zero-mode compatibility, whereas
\[
 \Lambda_{\mathrm{phys}}<\frac1{400}
\]
is the strict endpoint-loss condition.  No part of the
Hilbert--Schmidt gain is charged twice as a physical exponent, and
equality at \(1/400\) remains a stop.

TPC-18 full-block reassembly and primitive determinant dispersion
remain independent.  The present theorem improves one resonance
operator estimate; it proves neither global reassembly nor
fixed-\(h_0\) M\"obius cancellation.
