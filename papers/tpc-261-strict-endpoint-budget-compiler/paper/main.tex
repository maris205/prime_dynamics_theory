\begin{abstract}
The recent V59 analysis has reached a precise interface obstruction.  A
four-packet DFT identifies mode zero as the missing datum, while the known
same-clock null suppression is only logarithmic.  This paper compiles the
endpoint arithmetic into an exact finite-lane budget.  The current baseline is
$E_0=5/3$ and the target is $E_*=1997/1200$, so the required strict saving is
$E_0-E_*=1/400$.  If lane $j$ saves $\delta_j$ powers and pays a reassembly loss
$\lambda_j$, the effective credit is $\sigma_j=\delta_j-\lambda_j$ and a finite
reassembly closes the target only when $\min_j\sigma_j>1/400$.  Equality is
borderline at the power level.  We also prove that any fixed logarithmic saving
has zero fixed-power credit.  Finally, a scaled version of the exact TPC-260
plus/alternating witness has identical packet marginals and Haar/null data but
full energies $16x^{5/3}$ and $0$.  This is a structural synthetic obstruction,
not a prime-shell counterexample: the literal mode-zero or signed cross-Gram
estimate remains open.
\end{abstract}

\section{Scope and motivation}

The Route-B object is a signed coupling built on one fixed V59 clock.  The
four-block Haar construction of TPC-257 and the source-frozen transverse null
direction of TPC-258--259 isolate a useful channel, but they do not by
themselves control the whole output.  TPC-260 made the missing information
explicit.  For four packets, packet diagonal energy fixes the sum of Fourier
mode energies, whereas full reassembly asks for mode zero; equal packet norms
and all currently observed contrast data can coexist with full residual energy
zero or sixteen.

The present paper answers the next, narrower question: exactly how much
effective power saving would a new literal theorem have to provide after every
lane loss?  This question is elementary once stated, but it is operationally
important.  It prevents a logarithmic $o(1)$ statement, a local boundary gap,
or a conditional comparison estimate from being silently counted as global
endpoint credit.

Our contributions are:
\begin{enumerate}
 \item an exact baseline-to-target exponent identity;
 \item a strict finite-lane endpoint compiler with a sharp $1/400$ threshold;
 \item a proof-level separation between logarithmic and fixed-power savings;
 \item a scaled, null-compatible four-packet obstruction; and
 \item a minimum-sufficient formulation of the next literal theorem.
\end{enumerate}

All claims are deliberately scoped.  No result here proves arithmetic $L^2$,
full Gate B, fixed-atom credit, or the twin-prime conjecture.

\section{Endpoint normalization}

We use the unnormalized exponent ledger inherited from the current big road:
\begin{equation}
 E_0=\frac53=\frac{2000}{1200},\qquad
 E_* = \frac{1997}{1200}.
 \label{eq:endpoints}
\end{equation}
Subtracting gives the exact identity
\begin{equation}
 \Delta_*:=E_0-E_* = \frac{3}{1200}=\frac1{400}.
 \label{eq:gap}
\end{equation}
The denominator $1200$ is only a common bookkeeping denominator; no
approximation is involved.  The strict inequality in the next theorem is
essential because the available analytic estimates are conventionally stated
with an arbitrary $x^\varepsilon$ loss.

Let $\mathcal L$ be a finite set of lanes.  A lane may represent a packet,
boundary, mask, deleted diagonal, or a signed reassembly component.  The
symbols $\delta_j$ and $\lambda_j$ refer to proved quantities on the same
clock; they are not labels for hoped-for estimates.

\begin{definition}[effective lane credit]
Suppose lane $j$ has a saving $\delta_j$ and a loss $\lambda_j$.  Its effective
credit and the bottleneck credit are
\begin{equation}
 \sigma_j=\delta_j-\lambda_j,\qquad
 \sigma=\min_{j\in\mathcal L}\sigma_j.
 \label{eq:credit}
\end{equation}
\end{definition}

\section{The strict budget compiler}

\begin{theorem}[finite-lane endpoint compiler]
Let $\mathcal L$ be finite and nonempty.  Suppose that for every $j\in\mathcal L$
and every $\varepsilon>0$, there is a constant $C_{j,\varepsilon}$ such that,
for all sufficiently large $x$,
\begin{equation}
 |T_j(x)|\leq C_{j,\varepsilon}
 x^{E_0-\delta_j+\lambda_j+\varepsilon}.
 \label{eq:lane-bound}
\end{equation}
If $\sigma>\Delta_*=1/400$, then
\begin{equation}
 \sum_{j\in\mathcal L}|T_j(x)|=o(x^{E_*}).
 \label{eq:target}
\end{equation}
If $\sigma=\Delta_*$, the hypotheses are only borderline at the power level;
if $\sigma<\Delta_*$, the power ledger does not close.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Put $\eta=\sigma-\Delta_*>0$ and choose
$\varepsilon=\eta/2$.  Since $\sigma_j\geq\sigma$,
\begin{align*}
 E_0-\delta_j+\lambda_j+\varepsilon
 &=E_0-\sigma_j+\varepsilon\\
 &\leq E_0-\sigma+\varepsilon
 =E_*-\eta/2.
\end{align*}
Thus every term in the finite sum is $O(x^{E_*-\eta/2})$, and the sum has the
same bound.  Dividing by $x^{E_*}$ gives $O(x^{-\eta/2})\to0$.  At equality,
the displayed hypotheses leave exponent $E_*+\varepsilon$ for arbitrary
$\varepsilon>0$ but supply no negative fixed margin.  Below equality the
resulting exponent is larger than the target.  \end{proof}

The theorem is a compiler, not an estimate of any particular lane.  Its main
use is to expose the exact obligation: every lane must be attached to one
common literal object, and the smallest effective saving must be strictly
larger than $1/400$.

\section{The logarithmic firewall}

\begin{theorem}[logarithmic savings do not buy a power]
For every fixed $M>0$ and every fixed $\delta>0$,
\begin{equation}
 \frac{x^\delta}{(\log x)^M}\longrightarrow\infty.
 \label{eq:logpower}
\end{equation}
Consequently a bound $x^{E_0}/(\log x)^M$ has zero fixed-power credit in the
compiler of Theorem~1.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Taking logarithms of the left side of \eqref{eq:logpower} gives
$\delta\log x-M\log\log x$.  Since $\log\log x/\log x\to0$, this tends to
infinity.  If a logarithmic bound could pay a fixed $\delta$ power, one could
choose an $\varepsilon<\delta$ and obtain a uniform comparison with
$x^{E_0-\delta+\varepsilon}$, contradicting \eqref{eq:logpower}.
\end{proof}

This distinction is the relevant one for the current route.  TPC-258 and
TPC-259 provide a source-backed fixed-log suppression for a selected null
channel.  That is a genuine signed-coupling advance, but it records
$\delta=0$ in the fixed-power ledger.  It cannot pay the strict $1/400$ gap,
and it does not estimate the orthogonal residual left by TPC-259.

\section{A scaled four-packet obstruction}

We now show that the endpoint firewall is not merely a bookkeeping convention.
Let $z,w\in\HH$ be orthonormal, with $z$ the source-frozen null direction and
$w$ an orthogonal residual direction.  In the four-block Haar space, $w$ may
be the normalized scaling mode.  Set
\begin{equation}
 a(x)=x^{5/6},\qquad
 V_j^+(x)=a(x)w,\qquad
 V_j^-(x)=(-1)^j a(x)w,\quad 0\leq j\leq3.
 \label{eq:scaled}
\end{equation}

\begin{theorem}[scaled null-compatible witness]
The two families in \eqref{eq:scaled} have the same packet diagonal, zero
projection on every Haar contrast orthogonal to $w$, and zero projection on
$z$.  Their full residual energies are
\begin{equation}
 \left\|\sum_{j=0}^3V_j^+(x)\right\|^2=16x^{5/3},\qquad
 \left\|\sum_{j=0}^3V_j^-(x)\right\|^2=0.
 \label{eq:scaled-energy}
\end{equation}
Therefore those marginal and null data alone cannot imply a universal positive
fixed-power saving for the full four-packet residual.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Each packet has norm $a(x)$, so each diagonal energy is
$a(x)^2=x^{5/3}$ in both families.  Orthogonality gives all stated zero
projections.  The plus sum is $4a(x)w$, whereas the alternating sum cancels
exactly.  Squaring the norms gives \eqref{eq:scaled-energy}.  In particular,
for any $\delta>0$ the plus family is at baseline scale and is incompatible
with a universal upper bound of order $x^{5/3-\delta}$ based only on these
observables.  \end{proof}

The witness is the TPC-260 finite construction with its amplitude scaled to the
baseline exponent.  It is a structural synthetic family, not a sequence of
prime-shell outputs.  The conclusion is therefore a scoped non-identifiability
statement, not a proof that the literal route is impossible.

For completeness, the four-point DFT makes the same point in mode language:
\begin{equation}
 \widehat V_k=\frac12\sum_{j=0}^3\ii^{-jk}V_j,\qquad
 \sum_k\norm{\widehat V_k}^2=\sum_j\norm{V_j}^2,\qquad
 \norm{\sum_jV_j}^2=4\norm{\widehat V_0}^2.
 \label{eq:dft}
\end{equation}
The plus family places all packet energy in mode zero, while the alternating
family places it in mode two.  The diagonal ledger sees the same total in both
cases and cannot select the required mode.

\section{Current route and the minimum sufficient theorem}

The present evidence can be summarized as follows.  The TPC-257 local boundary
gap is $1/48$, the TPC-259 null channel is logarithmically small, and TPC-260
shows that the remaining four-packet mode zero is not determined by current
marginals.  These facts are compatible: a local gap is not a global saving, and
null suppression is not residual reassembly.

The minimum sufficient next theorem is consequently precise:

\begin{corollary}[minimum sufficient literal bridge]
A common-clock estimate for the literal V59 mode-zero output, or an equivalent
signed cross-Gram estimate, with effective saving strictly greater than
$1/400$ after all masks, deleted-diagonal, hard-window, boundary, and
normalization losses, is sufficient to close the finite-lane endpoint compiler.
\end{corollary}

The corollary is conditional on the displayed literal estimate; no such
estimate is proved here.  It also explains why the next route should not spend
the same credit twice: the four packet lanes must be reassembled before a
global endpoint claim is made.

\section{Conclusion}

TPC-261 turns the current endpoint intuition into a reusable theorem.  The
target gap is exactly $1/400$, and the correct currency is effective fixed-power
credit, not an isolated logarithm or a local exponent.  The scaled TPC-260
witness proves that the current marginal/Haar/null interface can leave a
baseline-scale mode-zero residual.  Thus the next genuine advance must estimate
that literal mode or its signed cross-Gram on the common clock.  Until then the
arithmetic $L^2$ gate, full Gate B, fixed-atom credit, and the twin-prime
conclusion remain open.

The release includes an exact rational certificate, an independent checker, and
a stress audit.  They verify the finite algebra and claim firewall only; no
finite computation is treated as asymptotic evidence.
