\begin{abstract}
For a fixed clock $q$, let $(f_r)_{r\bmod q}$ be a universally
distance-two-safe family of lag-two tables
$f_r:\{-1,0,+1\}^2\to\{-1,+1\}$.  We allow the interpolation
coefficient $c_{11}(r)$ of the shift-two M\"obius correlation to be active.
For every $1\le\omega(X)\le X$ with $\omega(X)\to\infty$, we prove the
unconditional terminal logarithmic limit
\[
 \frac1{\log\omega(X)}
 \sum_{X/\omega(X)<n\le X}
 \frac{\mob(n)f_{n\bmod q}(\mob_0(n-2),\mob(n))}{n}
 \longrightarrow
 \sum_{r\bmod q}\{c_{02}(r)\dd_{q,r}+c_{22}(r)\thh_{q,r}\}.
\]
The active $c_{11}$ channel is closed by the fixed determinant-two theorem
of TPC-137; the remaining zero and density channels are transferred from
ordinary prefixes by an Abel lemma that also covers bounded lower endpoints
$X/\omega(X)$.

We then project all $512$ truth tables pointwise onto eight actions.  A
finite compatibility rule and an injective predecessor charge give, for
every individually fixed $q$,
\[
 \Glog(q)=\max_f|L_q(f)|
 =\frac6{\pi^2}-\frac{\kap}{2},
 \qquad \kap=\prod_p\left(1-\frac2{p^2}\right).
\]
The constant action $\{-1,0\}$ attains the positive value for every fixed
$q$, and input reflection attains its negative.  Thus the supremum over all
fixed clocks, taken only after the individual limits, has the same value.
This does not prove an ordinary Ces\`aro limit, growing-clock uniformity, or
an unbounded-clock maximum-before-limit theorem.  A $602$-row exact artifact reproduces the
finite algebra and rejects $24$ semantic mutations; it is not the analytic
proof.
\end{abstract}

\noindent\textbf{Keywords:} M\"obius function; logarithmically averaged
Chowla theorem; active lag-two memory; fixed finite clocks; exact capacity.

\section{Setting and main theorem}

Put $\mob_0(m)=\mob(m)$ for $m\ge1$ and $\mob_0(m)=0$ for $m\le0$.
Throughout, every later shifted value $\mob(n-2)$ with $n\le2$ is understood
as $\mob_0(n-2)$; for $n>2$ the two notations agree.
Fix an integer $q\ge1$.  For each $r\in\Zq$, let
\[
 f_r:\T^2\longrightarrow\{-1,+1\},
 \qquad E_r=\{(x,z)\in\T^2:f_r(x,z)=+1\}.
\]
The family is \emph{universally distance-two-safe} when no
$x,z,w\in\T$ and $r\in\Zq$ satisfy simultaneously
\begin{equation}
 (x,z)\in E_r,
 \qquad (z,w)\in E_{r+2}.
 \label{eq:safety}
\end{equation}
This finite compatibility condition is equivalent to forbidding two output
$+1$ symbols at distance two for every ternary input.  Denote the finite set
of such $q$-periodic families by $\mathcal A_q$.

The unique current-zero interpolation from RH-378 is
\begin{align}
 zf_r(x,z)={}&c_{01}(r)z+c_{02}(r)z^2+c_{11}(r)xz
  +c_{12}(r)xz^2 \notag\\
 &+c_{21}(r)x^2z+c_{22}(r)x^2z^2.
 \label{eq:interpolation}
\end{align}
Unlike RH-379, we impose no condition $c_{11}(r)=0$.  Define the phase
densities
\begin{align}
 \dd_{q,r}&=\lim_{N\to\infty}\frac1N
  \sum_{\substack{n\le N\\n\equiv r\ (q)}}\mob(n)^2,
 \label{eq:delta}\\
 \thh_{q,r}&=\lim_{N\to\infty}\frac1N
  \sum_{\substack{n\le N\\n\equiv r\ (q)}}
  \mob(n-2)^2\mob(n)^2.
 \label{eq:theta}
\end{align}
Finite initial terms are immaterial in \eqref{eq:theta}.  The standard
squarefree progression sieve gives
\begin{equation}
 0\le\thh_{q,r}\le\dd_{q,r},
 \qquad
 \thh_{q,r}\le\dd_{q,r-2},
 \label{eq:density-cone}
\end{equation}
and the totals
\begin{equation}
 \sum_{r\bmod q}\dd_{q,r}=\frac6{\pi^2},
 \qquad
 \sum_{r\bmod q}\thh_{q,r}=\kap
 :=\prod_p\left(1-\frac2{p^2}\right).
 \label{eq:density-totals}
\end{equation}
These facts, including the phasewise formulas, are recorded in RH-379 and
ultimately follow from the squarefree-pattern sieve
\cite{Mirsky1948,RH379}.

For an admissible clock function
\begin{equation}
 1\le\omega(X)\le X,
 \qquad \omega(X)\longrightarrow\infty,
 \label{eq:omega}
\end{equation}
set
\begin{equation}
 S_X^\omega(q,f)=\frac1{\log\omega(X)}
 \sum_{X/\omega(X)<n\le X}
 \frac{\mob(n)f_{n\bmod q}(\mob_0(n-2),\mob(n))}{n}.
 \label{eq:terminal-score}
\end{equation}
Here $q$, the entire table family $f$, and hence every periodic coefficient
are fixed before $X\to\infty$.

\begin{theorem}[Fixed-table terminal logarithmic limit]
\label{thm:limit}
For every fixed $q\ge1$, every $f\in\mathcal A_q$, and every $\omega$
satisfying \eqref{eq:omega},
\begin{equation}
 \lim_{X\to\infty}S_X^\omega(q,f)=L_q(f),
 \qquad
 L_q(f)=\sum_{r\bmod q}
 \bigl(c_{02}(r)\dd_{q,r}+c_{22}(r)\thh_{q,r}\bigr).
 \label{eq:limit-formula}
\end{equation}
In particular, active phasewise $c_{11}(r)$ is allowed.
\end{theorem}

\begin{definition}[Post-limit fixed-clock capacity]
\label{def:capacity}
After the limits in Theorem~\ref{thm:limit} have been formed, define
\begin{equation}
 \Glog(q)=\max_{f\in\mathcal A_q}|L_q(f)|,
 \qquad
 \Alog=\frac6{\pi^2}-\frac{\kap}{2}.
 \label{eq:Glog}
\end{equation}
\end{definition}

\begin{theorem}[Active-$c_{11}$ all-clock capacity]
\label{thm:capacity}
For every individually fixed integer $q\ge1$,
\begin{equation}
 \boxed{\Glog(q)=\Alog.}
 \label{eq:fixed-capacity}
\end{equation}
The constant projected action $\{-1,0\}$ attains $+\Alog$ for every such
$q$.  Its reflected table attains $-\Alog$.  Consequently
\begin{equation}
 \boxed{\sup_{q\in\mathbb N}\Glog(q)=\Alog,}
 \label{eq:all-clock}
\end{equation}
where the supremum is taken only over already-established fixed-$q$
limits; $q=1$ already attains it.
\end{theorem}

The numerical constant and the table $\{-1,0\}$ are not first discovered
here.  RH-378 obtained the same value for the unphased $q=1$ table under
the unproved ordinary-average hypothesis $D_2(N)=o(N)$
\cite{RH378}.  The new theorem is the unconditional terminal-log closure of
active periodic $c_{11}$, followed by an all-$q$ charge showing that every
fixed clock has exactly the same capacity.  Equation \eqref{eq:all-clock}
is not a claim about $\lim_X\sup_q$, and neither theorem is uniform over
$q=q(X)$.

\section{The analytic fixed-table limit}

The first lemma keeps the endpoint $X/\omega(X)$ honest.  It is not enough
to prove only the case in which that endpoint tends to infinity.

\begin{lemma}[Terminal Abel transfer]
\label{lem:abel}
Let $(a_n)$ be a bounded sequence and
$A(t)=\sum_{n\le t}a_n$.  If
\begin{equation}
 A(t)=\alpha t+o(t),
 \label{eq:prefix-alpha}
\end{equation}
then every admissible $\omega$ satisfies
\begin{equation}
 \frac1{\log\omega(X)}
 \sum_{X/\omega(X)<n\le X}\frac{a_n}{n}
 \longrightarrow\alpha.
 \label{eq:abel-conclusion}
\end{equation}
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Put $Y=X/\omega(X)$ and $B(t)=A(t)-\alpha t$.  Stieltjes summation gives
\begin{equation}
 \sum_{Y<n\le X}\frac{a_n}{n}
 =\alpha\log\frac XY
 +\frac{B(X)}X-\frac{B(Y)}Y
 +\int_Y^X\frac{B(t)}{t^2}\,dt,
 \label{eq:abel-identity}
\end{equation}
with an immaterial bounded endpoint convention.  Fix $\varepsilon>0$ and
choose $T$ so that $|B(t)|\le\varepsilon t$ for $t\ge T$.

If $Y\ge T$, the last three terms in \eqref{eq:abel-identity} have absolute
value at most $2\varepsilon+\varepsilon\log\omega(X)$.  If $Y<T$, split the
sum at $T$.  The part below $T$ is bounded, whereas the part above $T$ has
error at most $O_T(1)+\varepsilon\log(X/T)$.  In this second case
\[
 \log X-\log T<\log\omega(X)\le\log X,
 \qquad
 \frac{\log X}{\log\omega(X)}\longrightarrow1.
\]
Thus both cases give normalized limsup error at most $\varepsilon$.
Letting $\varepsilon\downarrow0$ proves the result.
\end{proof}

\begin{proposition}[Five prefix channels independent of $c_{11}$]
\label{prop:five-channels}
For every fixed $q$, the following statements hold with any fixed bounded
$q$-periodic coefficient in front of the displayed sequence:
\begin{align}
 \sum_{n\le N}\mob(n)&=o(N),
 \label{eq:c01-prefix}\\
 \sum_{n\le N}\mob(n-2)\mob(n)^2&=o(N),
 \qquad
 \sum_{n\le N}\mob(n-2)^2\mob(n)=o(N),
 \label{eq:masked-prefix}
\end{align}
while the phase-restricted prefixes of $\mob(n)^2$ and
$\mob(n-2)^2\mob(n)^2$ have slopes \eqref{eq:delta} and
\eqref{eq:theta}.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Finite Fourier inversion reduces \eqref{eq:c01-prefix} to M\"obius sums in
fixed arithmetic progressions, which are $o(N)$ by Davenport cancellation
\cite{Davenport1937}.  For the first masked sum, insert
\[
 \mob(n)^2=\sum_{d^2\mid n}\mob(d).
\]
With $d\le R$ fixed, the phase congruence and $d^2\mid n$ leave finitely
many progressions for the remaining factor $\mob(n-2)$, so Davenport again
applies.  The absolute $d>R$ tail is
\[
 O\left(\sum_{R<d\le\sqrt N}\left(\frac N{d^2}+1\right)\right)
 =O(N/R+\sqrt N).
\]
First let $N\to\infty$ with $q,R$ fixed, then let $R\to\infty$.  Expanding
$\mob(n-2)^2$ proves the second masked estimate in the same way.  The two
density statements are their defining squarefree progression limits.

This is precisely the three zero-channel argument inside the proof of
RH-379's phase-limit proposition, extracted independently of that
proposition's declared $c_{11}=0$ domain.  No statement about the
$c_{11}$ channel has been used.
\end{proof}

\begin{proposition}[The active determinant-two channel]
\label{prop:c11}
For every fixed $q$ and fixed $q$-periodic coefficient $\rho$,
\begin{equation}
 \sum_{X/\omega(X)<n\le X}
 \frac{\mob_0(n-2)\mob(n)\rho(n)}n
 =o(\log\omega(X)).
 \label{eq:c11-terminal}
\end{equation}
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Use the affine forms
\[
 D(n)=n-2,
 \qquad V(n)=n,
 \qquad
 \det\begin{pmatrix}1&-2\\1&0\end{pmatrix}=2.
\]
TPC-137 proves full-M\"obius terminal logarithmic cancellation under the
primitive determinant-two hypotheses
\[
 (d,s)=(u,a)=(a,s)=1,
 \qquad su-ad=2,
\]
for $D(n)=d+sn$, $V(n)=u+an$, and every fixed bounded periodic mask
\cite{TPC137}.  Here
$(d,s,u,a)=(-2,1,0,1)$ satisfies all three gcd conditions and the determinant
identity, while $D(n),V(n)>0$ for $n>2$.  The theorem therefore applies
after deleting $n\le2$.  Those finitely many
terms, and the difference between $\mob$ and $\mob_0$ there, contribute
$O(1)/\log\omega(X)=o(1)$ after normalization.

TPC-137 is the full-M\"obius source used here.  Tao's Theorem~2,
equation~(3), printed page~3, is its upstream fixed-affine Liouville input
\cite{Tao2016LogChowla}; we do not substitute Tao's Liouville theorem
directly for the squarefree-completed statement.
\end{proof}

\begin{proof}[Proof of Theorem~\ref{thm:limit}]
Insert \eqref{eq:interpolation} with
$x=\mob_0(n-2)$ and $z=\mob(n)$.  Proposition~\ref{prop:c11} makes the
$c_{11}$ term zero after terminal-log normalization.  Apply
Lemma~\ref{lem:abel} to Proposition~\ref{prop:five-channels}: the
$c_{01},c_{12},c_{21}$ channels tend to zero, and the $c_{02},c_{22}$
channels tend to $\dd_{q,r},\thh_{q,r}$ phase by phase.  There are only
finitely many phases.  Summing them yields \eqref{eq:limit-formula}.
\end{proof}

\section{Pointwise projection from 512 tables to eight actions}

For a truth table $f$ with plus-edge set $E$, define its
\emph{positive-current projection} $f^+$ by
\begin{equation}
 E^+=E\cap(\T\times\{+1\}).
 \label{eq:projection}
\end{equation}

\begin{lemma}[Pointwise projection]
\label{lem:projection}
For every $(x,z)\in\T^2$,
\begin{equation}
 zf^+(x,z)-zf(x,z)\ge0.
 \label{eq:pointwise-gain}
\end{equation}
Moreover $E^+\subseteq E$, so applying the projection phase by phase
preserves universal safety.  The $512$ input tables project onto exactly
eight actions, each with $64$ preimages.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
At $z=+1$ the projection changes nothing, and at $z=0$ both scores vanish.
At $z=-1$, the only change is $f=+1$ to $f^+=-1$, which changes $zf$ from
$-1$ to $+1$.  This proves \eqref{eq:pointwise-gain}.  Removing plus edges
cannot create a composable pair in \eqref{eq:safety}.  Finally, the action
is the subset
\[
 A=\{x\in\T:(x,+1)\in E\}\subseteq\T.
\]
There are $2^3=8$ choices for $A$.  The six discarded truth values, at
$z=-1$ and $z=0$, are free, giving $2^6=64$ preimages of every action.
\end{proof}

The interpolation and limiting weight of the eight projected actions are
shown below.  Only $c_{02}$ and $c_{22}$ survive in
Theorem~\ref{thm:limit}; $c_{11}$ is displayed to make the active channel
visible.

\small
\begin{tabular}{@{}c@{\qquad}c@{\qquad}c@{\qquad}c@{\qquad}c@{}}
\toprule
$A$&$c_{02}$&$c_{11}$&$c_{22}$&$w_r(A)$\\
\midrule
$\varnothing$&$0$&$0$&$0$&$0$\\
$\{-1\}$&$0$&$-1/2$&$1/2$&$\thh_r/2$\\
$\{0\}$&$1$&$0$&$-1$&$\dd_r-\thh_r$\\
$\{-1,0\}$&$1$&$-1/2$&$-1/2$&$\dd_r-\thh_r/2$\\
$\{+1\}$&$0$&$1/2$&$1/2$&$\thh_r/2$\\
$\{-1,+1\}$&$0$&$0$&$1$&$\thh_r$\\
$\{0,+1\}$&$1$&$1/2$&$-1/2$&$\dd_r-\thh_r/2$\\
$\{-1,0,+1\}$&$1$&$0$&$0$&$\dd_r$\\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{center}
Here and below $\dd_r=\dd_{q,r}$ and $\thh_r=\thh_{q,r}$.  The table is an
eight-case substitution into \eqref{eq:interpolation}, not an asymptotic
inference from the finite certificate.

\begin{lemma}[Eight-action compatibility]
\label{lem:action-compatibility}
Let $A$ be the projected action at phase $r$ and $B$ the action at phase
$r+2$.  They are compatible if and only if
\begin{equation}
 A=\varnothing\quad\text{or}\quad +1\notin B.
 \label{eq:action-compatibility}
\end{equation}
Thus the empty left action admits all eight targets, while every nonempty
left action admits exactly the four targets that exclude $+1$.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
A composable pair of projected edges must have the form
$(x,+1)\in E_A$ and $(+1,w)\in E_B$.  Such a pair exists precisely when
$A$ is nonempty and $+1\in B$.
\end{proof}

Since every summand in \eqref{eq:terminal-score} has positive weight
$1/n$, Lemma~\ref{lem:projection} shows directly, at finite $X$, that the
maximum signed score is no smaller after projection.  Conversely every
projected action is itself a truth table.  The signed capacity problem is
therefore exactly the eight-action problem, not merely bounded by it.

\section{The all-\texorpdfstring{$q$}{q} predecessor charge}

Use the constant baseline action
\begin{equation}
 B_0=\{-1,0\},
 \qquad
 H_r=w_r(B_0)=\dd_r-\frac{\thh_r}{2}.
 \label{eq:baseline}
\end{equation}
For actions not containing $+1$, the action table and
\eqref{eq:density-cone} give $w_r(A)\le H_r$.  For every action containing
$+1$, they give the uniform local cap
\begin{equation}
 w_r(A)-H_r\le\frac{\thh_r}{2}.
 \label{eq:plus-gain}
\end{equation}
Indeed the eight differences, in the order of the table, are
\[
 -\dd_r+\frac{\thh_r}{2},\quad
 -\dd_r+\thh_r,\quad
 -\frac{\thh_r}{2},\quad0,\quad
 -\dd_r+\thh_r,\quad
 -\dd_r+\frac{3\thh_r}{2},\quad0,\quad
 \frac{\thh_r}{2}.
\]

\begin{proposition}[Injective predecessor charge]
\label{prop:charge}
Every compatible $q$-periodic projected action family satisfies
\begin{equation}
 \sum_{r\bmod q}w_r(A_r)
 \le\sum_{r\bmod q}H_r
 =\frac6{\pi^2}-\frac{\kap}{2}.
 \label{eq:charge-upper}
\end{equation}
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Let
\[
 P=\{r\in\Zq:+1\in A_r\}.
\]
For $r\in P$, compatibility \eqref{eq:action-compatibility} forces the
predecessor action $A_{r-2}$ to be empty.  The map
$r\mapsto r-2$ is a bijection of $\Zq$, with inverse $s\mapsto s+2$.
Compatibility also gives $P\cap(P-2)=\varnothing$.  In the self-loop cases
$q=1,2$, translation by $-2$ is the identity, so this assertion says
directly that $P=\varnothing$; there is no exceptional small clock.

The empty predecessor loses $H_{r-2}$ relative to the baseline, and this
loss pays the cap in \eqref{eq:plus-gain}.  More precisely,
\begin{align}
 H_{r-2}-\frac{\thh_r}{2}
 &=\frac12(\dd_{r-2}-\thh_{r-2})
   +\frac12(\dd_{r-2}-\thh_r)\ge0.
 \label{eq:charge-identity}
\end{align}
The first nonnegative term uses the pair inclusion at phase $r-2$; the
second uses the fact that a squarefree pair counted by $\thh_r$ has a
squarefree predecessor in residue $r-2$.  Since the predecessor map is
injective, distinct plus phases are charged to distinct empty phases.  All
remaining phases have actions not containing $+1$ and hence weight at most
$H_r$.  Summing proves the inequality, and \eqref{eq:density-totals}
evaluates the baseline total.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[Signed optimum and attainment]
\label{cor:signed}
For every fixed $q$,
\begin{equation}
 \max_{f\in\mathcal A_q}L_q(f)=\Alog.
 \label{eq:signed-optimum}
\end{equation}
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
Projection cannot decrease a finite signed score, so
Proposition~\ref{prop:charge} bounds every original family as well.  The
constant action $A_r=B_0$ is compatible because it excludes $+1$ at every
phase, and its total weight is the right side of
\eqref{eq:charge-upper}.  It therefore attains the bound for every fixed
$q$.  Notice also that $\Alog\ge3/\pi^2>0$ by
$\sum_r\thh_r\le\sum_r\dd_r$.
\end{proof}

\section{Input reflection and absolute capacity}

For a table $f$, define its input reflection by
\begin{equation}
 f^\rho(x,z)=f(-x,-z).
 \label{eq:reflection}
\end{equation}

\begin{lemma}[Reflection parity]
\label{lem:reflection}
Simultaneously reflecting every phase preserves compatibility and universal
safety.  Under \eqref{eq:reflection}, the six coefficients in the order
$(c_{01},c_{02},c_{11},c_{12},c_{21},c_{22})$ transform with signs
\begin{equation}
 (+,-,-,+,+,-).
 \label{eq:reflection-parity}
\end{equation}
Consequently $L_q(f^\rho)=-L_q(f)$.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Negating a composable triple $(x,z,w)$ gives a composable triple
$(-x,-z,-w)$, and conversely.  If $g(x,z)=zf(x,z)$, then
\[
 zf^\rho(x,z)=-g(-x,-z).
\]
A monomial $x^az^b$ therefore acquires the factor
$-(-1)^{a+b}$, which gives \eqref{eq:reflection-parity}.  In particular
$c_{02},c_{11},c_{22}$ change sign, whereas
$c_{01},c_{12},c_{21}$ do not.  This is not an all-six sign reversal.
Theorem~\ref{thm:limit} kills $c_{11}$ as well as the three unchanged zero
channels and retains only $c_{02},c_{22}$, so the limiting score negates.
\end{proof}

The baseline table has truth-table identifier $36$ and full coefficient
vector
\[
 (0,1,-1/2,-1/2,-1/2,-1/2).
\]
Its input reflection is table $72$, with vector
\[
 (0,-1,+1/2,-1/2,-1/2,+1/2).
\]
The latter is safe and attains $-\Alog$ for every fixed $q$; it is not the
output complement of table $36$.

\begin{proof}[Proof of Theorem~\ref{thm:capacity}]
Corollary~\ref{cor:signed} gives $L_q(f)\le\Alog$ for every family.
Apply the same bound to $f^\rho$ and use Lemma~\ref{lem:reflection} to get
$-L_q(f)\le\Alog$.  Hence $|L_q(f)|\le\Alog$.  Tables $36$ and $72$ attain
the two signs, proving \eqref{eq:fixed-capacity}.  Since the equality holds
for every individually fixed $q$, its post-limit supremum is
\eqref{eq:all-clock}; no interchange of a supremum with $X\to\infty$ has
occurred.
\end{proof}

\section{Limitations and claim boundary}

The theorem has deliberately frozen quantifiers.  It proves a terminal
logarithmic limit for each fixed $q$, fixed family, and admissible $\omega$.
It proves neither an ordinary Ces\`aro limit nor an effective rate or an
all-prefix power saving.  There is no uniform theorem for $q=q(X)$, no
unrestricted simultaneous clock, and no selector $K_N$.  The maximum in
\eqref{eq:Glog} is defined after each fixed-table limit exists.  For one
fixed $q$, the set $\mathcal A_q$ is finite, so its finite maximum can also
be interchanged with the established limits.  This automatic fixed-$q$
observation supplies no maximum-before-limit theorem over unbounded or
growing clocks and no adaptive or $K_N$ selector.

The active $c_{11}$ channel is closed only in the fixed periodic
determinant-two setting supplied by TPC-137.  The result does not activate
an operator, a trace identity, a zero correspondence, the Riemann
Hypothesis, or any of Gates A--E.  Every such gate remains false/open.
