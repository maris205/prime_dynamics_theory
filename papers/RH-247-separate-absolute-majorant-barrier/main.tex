\begin{abstract}
The cloud-extracted trace is a cancellation-sensitive residual.  We prove
that a sectorwise absolute majorant, formed before physical/atomic
cancellation, has root rate at least the Hardy-scaled Perron rate
$r_H^{-1}>1$.  The obstruction is exact and independent of the cloud size.
An audit of 352 archived order cases confirms root rates from 1.2532 to
3.5568 and enormous majorant-to-residual gains.  This rules out one
cancellation-blind estimate, not signed or grouped quotient loops.
\end{abstract}

\section{The cancellation-blind quantity}

Write the scaled trace decomposition at a fixed noise value as
\[
 \tau_n=t_n-p^n-q^n-\sum_{s\in S}s^n,
\]
where $t_n=\Tr A^n$, $p$ is the Perron root, $q$ the parity root, and $S$ the
selected cloud.  Define the separate-absolute trace majorant
\begin{equation}\label{eq:majorant}
 U_n^{\rm sep}=|t_n|+|p|^n+|q|^n+\sum_{s\in S}|s|^n.
\end{equation}
An absolute periodic-loop integral for the physical sector is no smaller
than the first term in \eqref{eq:majorant}; hence \eqref{eq:majorant} is the
most favorable sectorwise absolute test for the present purpose.

\begin{theorem}[Perron lower barrier]\label{thm:barrier}
Suppose the scaled Perron root obeys $|p_\sigma|\ge\rho>1$ on a noise set.
Then for every selected cloud and every $n\ge1$,
\[
 U_n^{\rm sep}(\sigma)\ge\rho^n,
 \qquad
 \liminf_{n\to\infty}(U_n^{\rm sep}(\sigma))^{1/n}\ge\rho.
\]
Consequently no estimate of the form
$U_n^{\rm sep}(\sigma)\le Mq^n$ with $q<1$ can hold for all sufficiently
large $n$ on that set.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
The Perron summand in \eqref{eq:majorant} is nonnegative and equals
$|p_\sigma|^n\ge\rho^n$.  Taking $n$th roots and then a liminf proves the
claim.  A subunit geometric upper bound would force the limsup of the same
roots to be at most $q<1$, a contradiction.
\end{proof}

For the current Hardy scaling $r_H=0.85$, the archived Perron value is exactly
\[
 |p_\sigma|=r_H^{-1}=1.1764705882352942.
\]
This is the same normalization that makes the deterministic numerator target
in RH-243 nontrivial.  The obstruction is therefore structural, not a poor
choice of numerical tolerance.

\section{Finite audit}

Using the RH-222 clouds and RH-236 full/residual trace powers, we evaluated
\eqref{eq:majorant} for all 32 endpoints and orders 2--12
\citep{WangRH236}.  There are 352 cases.  The resulting individual root rates
\(U_n^{\rm sep})^{1/n}\) lie in
\[
 1.253165378203787\le (U_n^{\rm sep})^{1/n}
 \le3.5568338003788416,
\]
so all 352 exceed one.  The cancellation gain
$U_n^{\rm sep}/|\tau_n|$ ranges from
\(42.68449383956666\) to \(7.802289221403802\times10^{15}\); its maximum is
at $(\sigma,\mathrm{side},n)=(0.005,\mathrm{right},10)$.

\section{Scope and route consequence}

Theorem~\ref{thm:barrier} rules out only estimates that take absolute values
of the physical, Perron, parity, and cloud sectors separately.  It does not
rule out the exact graded cancellation of RH-242, the orthogonal quotient of
RH-245, complex kernel cancellations, or a different normalization in a new
route.  The finite audit uses floating archived roots and orders 2--12; it is
not an all-order interval proof.

The viable envelope route must group sectors before majorization.  The
deterministic numerator anchor remains unresolved.  Gate A remains open and
Gates B--E are untouched.  No Hilbert--Polya operator, zeta-divisor equality,
Riemann-zero identification, or RH implication is claimed.
