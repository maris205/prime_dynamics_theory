\begin{abstract}
The RH-373 composite clock gives one strict improvement to the unconditional
lower floor for the RH-366 distance-two M\"obius capacity.  We construct and
optimize a whole square-clock family.  If $3=p_1<p_2<\cdots$ are the odd
primes, $P_y=\prod_{i\le y}p_i^2$, $q_y=4P_y$, and
$A_y=\prod_{i\le y}(p_i^2-1)$, then the exact optimum among universally safe
one-site phase/current-input factors at the fixed clock $q_y$ is
\[
 B_y=\frac{4+2O_y/A_y}{\pi^2}.
\]
Here $O_y$ counts odd-length positive runs in one period of a squarefree
word, and it has a finite Euler-product formula.  Adjoining a prime $p\ge5$
gives $A'=(p^2-1)A$ and
$O'=(p^2-1)O+L_{\mathrm{even}}$, where $L_{\mathrm{even}}$ counts sites in even-length
old runs.  A length-eight run persists by the Chinese remainder theorem, so
$B_y$ increases strictly.  The fixed-clock certificates converge to an
explicit Euler-product constant $B_\infty$, and
\[
        \liminf_{N\to\infty}\frac{K_N}{N}\ge B_\infty.
\]
The quantifier fixes $y$ before $N\to\infty$; no growing clock, uniform
Davenport theorem, or infinite selector is asserted.  The optimization does
not include arbitrary memory transducers or all clocks, and the adaptive
capacity limit, intrinsic operators, traces, Riemann-zero identification,
and RH remain open or outside scope.
\end{abstract}

\section{Frozen capacity and the exact selector class}

The RH-366 distance-two capacity is
\begin{equation}
 K_N=\max_{\substack{\eps_1,\ldots,\eps_N\in\{-1,+1\}\\
 \text{not }(\eps_n,\eps_{n+2})=(+1,+1)}}
 \left|\sum_{n\le N}\Mob(n)\eps_n\right|.
 \label{eq:capacity}
\end{equation}
Its optimizer may read the full finite prefix and may change with $N$.
RH-366 proves
\begin{equation}
 \frac4{\pi^2}\le\liminf_{N\to\infty}\frac{K_N}{N}
 \le\limsup_{N\to\infty}\frac{K_N}{N}\le\frac6{\pi^2},
 \label{eq:old-bracket}
\end{equation}
without proving an ordinary limit \cite{RH366}.  RH-371 isolates an exact
eight-run finite-prefix ledger, but the required M\"obius run frequencies
remain open \cite{RH371}.  RH-373 instead supplies the externally prescribed
$q=180$ certificate $97/(24\pi^2)$ \cite{RH373}.

We now freeze the narrower class that is optimized here.

\begin{definition}[Universally safe one-site factor]
For a fixed clock $q$, a one-site phase/current-input factor is a family
\[
       g_r:\{-1,0,+1\}\longrightarrow\{-1,+1\},
       \qquad r\in\Z/q\Z.
\]
It is \emph{universally safe} when, for every ternary input word $a_n$, the
word $g_{n\bmod q}(a_n)$ has no two $+1$ values at distance two.  Its active
phase set is
\[
       S_g=\{r:g_r(a)=+1\text{ for at least one }a\in\{-1,0,+1\}\}.
\]
\end{definition}

Universal safety is equivalent to $S_g\cap(S_g+2)=\varnothing$.  Necessity
follows by choosing the two activating input values independently; sufficiency
is immediate.  This is the one-site factor boundary used for finite
transducer certificates in RH-372 \cite{RH372}.  It is not the class of all
finite-memory transducers.

For $r\pmod q$, let
\[
 \dd_{q,r}=\lim_{N\to\infty}\frac1N
 \#\{n\le N:n\equiv r\pmod q,\ \Mob(n)^2=1\}.
\]
Fixed-frequency Davenport cancellation, followed by finite Fourier
inversion, gives
\[
 M_r(N):=\sum_{\substack{n\le N\\ n\equiv r\ (q)}}\Mob(n)=o(N)
\]
for every fixed $q,r$.
The squarefree progression sieve supplies $\dd_{q,r}$
\cite{Davenport1937,Mirsky1948}.  Splitting the squarefree terms according to
$\Mob(n)=\pm1$ therefore gives
\begin{equation}
 \lim_{N\to\infty}\frac1N\sum_{n\le N}
 \Mob(n)g_{n\bmod q}(\Mob(n))
 =\frac12\sum_{r\bmod q}\dd_{q,r}
       \bigl(g_r(+1)-g_r(-1)\bigr).
 \label{eq:factor-correlation}
\end{equation}

For an active phase, the absolute contribution in
\eqref{eq:factor-correlation} is at most $\dd_{q,r}$; an inactive phase
contributes zero.  Conversely, any distance-two independent set $I$ realizes
its full positive weight by taking
\begin{equation}
 g_r(+1)=+1,\quad g_r(0)=g_r(-1)=-1\quad(r\in I),
 \qquad g_r\equiv-1\quad(r\notin I).
 \label{eq:canonical-factor}
\end{equation}
Thus the maximum absolute limiting correlation in this fixed-$q$ class is
exactly the maximum-weight independent-set value for the phase graph, with
vertex weights $\dd_{q,r}$.

\section{Square clocks and the run formula}

Let
\begin{equation}
 3=p_1<p_2<\cdots,\qquad
 P_y=\prod_{i\le y}p_i^2,\qquad q_y=4P_y,\qquad
 A_y=\prod_{i\le y}(p_i^2-1),
 \label{eq:clock-def}
\end{equation}
and, for $1\le m\le9$, put
\begin{equation}
          E_m^{(y)}=\prod_{i\le y}\left(1-\frac{m}{p_i^2}\right).
 \label{eq:E-def}
\end{equation}

\begin{lemma}[Equal positive progression weights]
For $q=q_y$, a squarefree progression density is zero exactly when
$4\mid r$ or $p_i^2\mid r$ for some $i\le y$.  Every other phase has
\begin{equation}
                    \dd_{q_y,r}=\frac{2}{A_y\pi^2}.
 \label{eq:equal-density}
\end{equation}
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
The zero cases force a square divisor.  In every other class the fixed
progression sieve gives
\[
 \frac1{q_y}\prod_{p\nmid q_y}(1-p^{-2})
 =\frac1{4P_y}\frac{6/\pi^2}
 {(1-2^{-2})\prod_{i\le y}(1-p_i^{-2})}
 =\frac2{A_y\pi^2}.
\]
\end{proof}

The distance-two phase graph has an even and an odd cycle, each of length
$2P_y$.  On the even cycle, every positive-weight phase is $2\pmod4$.
There are $A_y$ such phases by the Chinese remainder theorem, and no two are
adjacent in the distance-two graph.  Hence the even contribution to the
maximum independent-set cardinality is exactly $A_y$.

Parametrize the odd phases by $r=2k+1$.  Their positive-weight indicator is
the $P_y$-periodic word
\begin{equation}
 w_y(k)=\mathbf 1_{\{p_i^2\nmid 2k+1\text{ for every }i\le y\}}.
 \label{eq:word}
\end{equation}
One period has $A_y$ one-sites.  Because $p_1^2=9$, a zero occurs every nine
positions, so every positive run has length at most eight.  Let $R_j^{(y)}$
be the number of cyclic runs of exact length $j$ in one period and define
\[
                   O_y=R_1^{(y)}+R_3^{(y)}+R_5^{(y)}+R_7^{(y)}.
\]

\begin{lemma}[Exact run ledger]
For $1\le j\le7$,
\begin{equation}
 R_j^{(y)}=P_y\left(E_j^{(y)}-2E_{j+1}^{(y)}+E_{j+2}^{(y)}\right),
 \label{eq:run-count}
\end{equation}
whereas the terminal row is separately
\begin{equation}
                   R_8^{(y)}=P_yE_8^{(y)}.
 \label{eq:run-eight}
\end{equation}
In particular,
\begin{equation}
 O_y=P_y\sum_{j\in\{1,3,5,7\}}
 \left(E_j^{(y)}-2E_{j+1}^{(y)}+E_{j+2}^{(y)}\right).
 \label{eq:O-formula}
\end{equation}
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Let $N_m$ count cyclic starting positions at which $m$ consecutive entries
of $w_y$ equal one.  For $m\le9$, the $m$ forbidden congruence classes
modulo each $p_i^2$ are distinct.  The Chinese remainder theorem gives
\[
                         N_m=P_yE_m^{(y)}.
\]
For $j\le7$, inclusion--exclusion on extension by one site to the left and
right gives $R_j=N_j-2N_{j+1}+N_{j+2}$.  The factor at $p_1=3$ makes
$E_9^{(y)}=0$, hence $N_9=0$ and no run exceeds eight.  Therefore a
length-eight block is already a whole run, so $R_8=N_8$; no $E_{10}$ term is
introduced.  Summing the four odd rows proves \eqref{eq:O-formula}.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Fixed square-clock optimum]
Among universally safe one-site phase/current-input factors at the fixed
clock $q_y$, the exact maximum absolute limiting correlation is
\begin{equation}
             \boxed{B_y=\frac{4+2O_y/A_y}{\pi^2}}.
 \label{eq:B-y}
\end{equation}
Consequently
\begin{equation}
             B_y\le\liminf_{N\to\infty}\frac{K_N}{N}.
 \label{eq:fixed-floor}
\end{equation}
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
The odd phase cycle contains two copies of the $P_y$-periodic word.  A run of
length $\ell$ contributes $\lceil\ell/2\rceil$ vertices to a maximum
independent set.  Hence the two copies contribute
\[
 2\sum_{\mathrm{runs}}\left\lceil\frac\ell2\right\rceil
 =\sum_{\mathrm{runs}}\bigl(\ell+\mathbf1_{\{\ell\ \mathrm{odd}\}}\bigr)
 =A_y+O_y.
\]
Adding the $A_y$ even phases gives $2A_y+O_y$ positive phases.  Every one has
weight $2/(A_y\pi^2)$ by \eqref{eq:equal-density}, so the phase MWIS value is
\eqref{eq:B-y}.  Equation \eqref{eq:canonical-factor} realizes that value,
and its output is admissible in \eqref{eq:capacity} for every $N$.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}[Exact scope of ``optimum'']
The theorem optimizes one fixed $q_y$ and only the universally safe one-site
phase/current-input factor class.  It is not an optimum over arbitrary
memory transducers, over all clocks, or over the adaptive finite-prefix
optimizers defining $K_N$.
\end{remark}

\section{Prime adjoining and strict monotonicity}

Adjoin a new odd prime $p\ge5$ to an old clock, and write $A',O'$ for the
new quantities.  Let
\begin{equation}
 L_{\mathrm{even}}=\sum_{\substack{\text{old runs }R\\ |R|\ \mathrm{even}}}|R|
 \label{eq:L-even}
\end{equation}
be the number of old one-sites contained in even-length runs.

\begin{proposition}[Run recurrence]
For every newly adjoined $p\ge5$,
\begin{equation}
       A'=(p^2-1)A,\qquad
       O'=(p^2-1)O+L_{\mathrm{even}}.
 \label{eq:recurrence}
\end{equation}
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Before imposing the new condition $p^2\nmid2k+1$, one new period consists of
$p^2$ copies of the old cyclic word.  Every old one-site has $p^2$ lifts,
and exactly one lift is deleted, proving the formula for $A'$.  Within a
fixed old run of length $\ell\le8$, its $\ell$ deleted lifts occur in
distinct copies: two positions assigned to the same copy would differ by
zero modulo $p^2$, impossible because $0<|j-j'|\le7<p^2$.

Suppose first that $\ell$ is odd.  There are $p^2-\ell$ untouched copies of
the odd run.  Deleting position $t\in\{0,\ldots,\ell-1\}$ leaves runs of
length $t$ and $\ell-1-t$.  The $(\ell+1)/2$ even values of $t$ produce no
odd subrun, while the $(\ell-1)/2$ odd values produce two.  The total number
of odd runs descending from this old run is therefore
\[
 (p^2-\ell)+2\frac{\ell-1}{2}=p^2-1.
\]
If $\ell$ is even, each of its $\ell$ deletions splits the run into one odd
and one even subrun, contributing $\ell$ odd runs in total.  Summing over old
odd and even runs proves \eqref{eq:recurrence}.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[Persistent length-eight run]
For every $y\ge1$, the cyclic word $w_y$ has a run of exact length eight.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Choose $k\equiv5\pmod9$.  Then the two boundary values at $k-1$ and $k+8$
are zero modulo $9$, while the eight values $2(k+j)+1$, $0\le j\le7$, are
nonzero modulo $9$.  For each other prime square $p_i^2\ge25$, choose
$k\pmod{p_i^2}$ outside the eight forbidden classes
$2(k+j)+1\equiv0\pmod{p_i^2}$.  Such a choice exists because $p_i^2>8$.
The Chinese remainder theorem joins these choices.  The eight interior sites
are then one-sites for every old prime square, and the two boundary sites are
zeros because of $9$.
\end{proof}

The lemma gives $L_{\mathrm{even}}\ge8$.  Dividing \eqref{eq:recurrence} by
$A'=(p^2-1)A$ yields
\begin{equation}
        \frac{O'}{A'}=\frac OA+
        \frac{L_{\mathrm{even}}}{(p^2-1)A}>\frac OA.
 \label{eq:strict-ratio}
\end{equation}
Thus $B_{y+1}>B_y$ at every step.

\section{Euler limit and the capacity floor}

For $1\le m\le9$, define
\begin{equation}
 e_m=\prod_{p\ \mathrm{odd}}\left(1-\frac{m}{p^2}\right).
 \label{eq:e-infinite}
\end{equation}
The products are positive for $m\le8$ because $\sum_p p^{-2}<\infty$, while
$e_9=0$ because the $p=3$ factor vanishes.  Moreover
\begin{equation}
             e_1=\prod_{p\ \mathrm{odd}}(1-p^{-2})=\frac8{\pi^2}.
 \label{eq:e-one}
\end{equation}
Put
\begin{equation}
 C=\frac{1}{e_1}\sum_{j\in\{1,3,5,7\}}
       (e_j-2e_{j+1}+e_{j+2}),
 \qquad
 B_\infty=\frac{4+2C}{\pi^2}.
 \label{eq:B-infinity}
\end{equation}

\begin{theorem}[Euler-product capacity floor]
The sequence $B_y$ is strictly increasing and converges to $B_\infty$.
Furthermore,
\begin{equation}
       \boxed{\displaystyle
       \liminf_{N\to\infty}\frac{K_N}{N}\ge B_\infty.}
 \label{eq:limit-floor}
\end{equation}
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Since $A_y=P_yE_1^{(y)}$, equations \eqref{eq:O-formula} and
\eqref{eq:B-y} give
\[
 \frac{O_y}{A_y}=
 \frac{\sum_{j\in\{1,3,5,7\}}
 (E_j^{(y)}-2E_{j+1}^{(y)}+E_{j+2}^{(y)})}{E_1^{(y)}}.
\]
Termwise convergence of the finitely many Euler products proves
$B_y\to B_\infty$; strictness was proved in \eqref{eq:strict-ratio}.

For the last assertion, fix $y$ first.  The clock $q_y$ is then finite, so
the fixed-frequency arithmetic used in \eqref{eq:factor-correlation} proves
$\liminf K_N/N\ge B_y$.  The same scalar liminf dominates every $B_y$.
It therefore dominates $\sup_yB_y=B_\infty$.  This argument neither chooses
$y=y(N)$ nor requires a Davenport estimate uniform in $q_y$.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}[No infinite selector]
Equation \eqref{eq:limit-floor} takes the supremum of already proved scalar
lower bounds.  It does not construct a selector with an infinite clock and
does not exchange the $y$ and $N$ limits.
\end{remark}

\section{Route verdict and Gate ledger}

Route A is \texttt{GO}: the fixed-clock optimization, run recurrence, strict
monotonicity, Euler limit, and scalar-supremum argument are unconditional.
Route B is \texttt{STOP\_SCOPED}.  The result does not optimize arbitrary
memory transducers or all clocks, does not supply a growing-clock theorem,
and does not prove convergence of the adaptive capacity $K_N/N$.  A theorem
for the RH-371 run envelope or higher-order M\"obius correlations would be a
different mathematical input.

Gates A--E remain false/open.  The selectors use externally prescribed
arithmetic phase and the current M\"obius input.  They supply no canonical
dynamical operator, Fredholm determinant, scattering completion, or
self-adjoint generator.  They also supply no intrinsic $T\log T$ law,
von-Mangoldt trace, completed-zeta divisor equality, Riemann-zero
identification, Hilbert--Polya construction, or proof of RH.

\section{Conclusion}

Square clocks turn the RH-373 example into a rigorous family theorem.  The
finite combinatorics are exact: equal positive progression weights reduce the
fixed-clock selector class to a cyclic MWIS, the run ledger has a closed
Euler-product form, and adjoining a prime creates a positive correction from
even-length runs.  The supremum of these fixed certificates is therefore an
explicit Euler-product lower floor.  The next route must address a genuinely
larger selector class or the adaptive run envelope, rather than promote this
family optimum beyond its proved scope.
