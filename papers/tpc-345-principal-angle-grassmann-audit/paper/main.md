## Abstract

The preceding finite panel-contrast audit found a narrow raw-weighted residual crossing, but the crossing disappeared under equal-row weighting. We remove the nuisance-coordinate choice and compare the two panel nuisance column spaces by principal angles and orthogonal projectors. On the locked TPC-341/TPC-342 response panels, the raw principal cosines are $0.9957018010$ and $0.0799456793$, while equal-row weighting gives $0.9144519860$ and $0.0787084493$. Thus one direction is strongly aligned but a second direction is nearly orthogonal, and the leading angle changes from $5.3142$ to $23.8720$ degrees. A fixed nonsingular coordinate shear preserves the projectors and cosines to numerical precision, confirming that the separation is a subspace statement rather than a basis artifact. Mutual cross-panel target transfer fails a residual-retention threshold in both weightings, and all 18 leave-one-control-out angle pairs preserve the transverse separation. This is a finite geometric obstruction only; it provides no arithmetic, asymptotic, Route-A/Route-B, or twin-prime result.

# Finite Grassmann geometry

Let $$N_p=Q_p\Sigma_pV_p^{T}$$ be a positive-SVD factorization, with the columns of $Q_p$ retained according to the declared floating-point rank rule. Write $P_p=Q_pQ_p^T$. The two nonzero singular values of $Q_{341}^TQ_{342}$ are denoted $c_1\geq c_2\geq0$ and their principal angles are $\theta_i=\arccos(c_i)$. The observed ranks are three and two, so two angles are reported and one direction is panel-specific.

> **Proposition: principal-angle invariance** <span id="prop:angle" label="prop:angle">\[prop:angle\]</span> The singular values of $Q_1^TQ_2$ depend only on the two finite column spaces, not on the choice of orthonormal bases or on nonsingular reparameterizations of the original nuisance columns.

> **Proof** If $\widetilde Q_i=Q_iU_i$ for orthogonal matrices $U_i$, then $$\widetilde Q_1^T\widetilde Q_2
>  =U_1^T(Q_1^TQ_2)U_2,$$ which has the same singular values. If $N_i$ is replaced by $N_iS_i$ for nonsingular $S_i$, its column space and hence its orthogonal projector are unchanged. Choosing any orthonormal bases of those unchanged spaces gives the same principal cosines.

> **Proposition: finite projection identity** <span id="prop:projection" label="prop:projection">\[prop:projection\]</span> For every finite target $Y$ and orthogonal projector $P$, $$\lVert Y\rVert_2^{2}=\lVert PY\rVert_2^{2}+\lVert (I-P)Y\rVert_2^{2}.$$

> **Proof** The two vectors $PY$ and $(I-P)Y$ are orthogonal and sum to $Y$. The identity is therefore the Pythagorean theorem. The implementation evaluates the two energies and checks the numerical decomposition gap.

For a target from one panel projected onto the other panel’s nuisance space, we record the finite residual retention $$\rho(Y,N)=\frac{\lVert (I-P_N)Y\rVert_2^{2}}{\lVert Y\rVert_2^{2}}.       \tag{1}$$ The mutual-transfer criterion used here requires both directions to have $\rho<0.30$. This is a declared finite diagnostic, not a theorem about future source intervals.

# Coordinate and control robustness

To test coordinate dependence, both nuisance matrices are multiplied on the right by the fixed nonsingular shear $$S=\begin{pmatrix}1&1&0\\0&1&1\\0&0&1\end{pmatrix}.$$ In the raw audit the maximum entrywise projector change is $7.65\times10^{-15}$ and the maximum principal-cosine change is $5.56\times10^{-16}$. The equal-row errors are smaller than these values. The observed ranks remain $(3,2)$. This is a numerical check of the exact coordinate-invariance proposition, not a claim that the finite spaces are canonical arithmetic objects.

As a hostile control check, each of the nine controls is omitted in turn. The nuisance means are formed from the other eight controls and the omitted twin output is used as the target block. The raw leading-cosine minimum over the nine pairs is $0.9947001951$, and the raw transverse-cosine maximum is $0.1549751276$. Under equal-row weighting the transverse maximum is $0.1675760096$. Thus the near-orthogonal transverse direction persists over all 18 leave-one-control-out geometry pairs.

The transfer readout is deliberately asymmetric. TPC-341 targets projected onto the TPC-342 space have residual retentions $0.2306$ and $0.2746$ under the two weightings, whereas TPC-342 targets projected onto TPC-341 have retentions $0.3589$ and $0.3235$. Since the mutual criterion requires both directions below $0.30$, it is refuted in both finite weightings. These numbers are orthogonal projection residuals and do satisfy Proposition [\[prop:projection\]](main.tex#L123){reference-type="ref" reference="prop:projection"}; they are distinct from coefficient cross-fit prediction errors.

# Conclusion and next question

The TPC-344 panel-adaptive repair has a clear finite geometric profile: one dominant direction is almost shared under raw weighting, a second direction is nearly orthogonal in both weightings, and the dominant angle is strongly weight-sensitive. The coordinate-shear test shows that this profile is not caused by the selected nuisance coordinates. The asymmetric transfer failure prevents a mutual common-law interpretation, while the leave-one- control-out audit preserves the transverse obstruction.

The reusable route is $$\begin{gathered}
\text{panel-adaptive span}\longrightarrow
\text{coordinate-invariant subspaces}\\
\longrightarrow\text{principal angles}\longrightarrow
\text{weighting/transfer obstruction}.
\end{gathered}$$ The next minimal question is a finite no-go or freeze test: determine whether adding further panel-specific degrees of freedom produces a robust model class or merely overfits these locked panels. Until a source-uniform arithmetic theorem is supplied, Route-A, Route-B, and the twin-prime endpoint remain open.
