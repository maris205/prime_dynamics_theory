\begin{abstract}
The preceding finite panel-contrast audit found a narrow raw-weighted
residual crossing, but the crossing disappeared under equal-row weighting.
We remove the nuisance-coordinate choice and compare the two panel nuisance
column spaces by principal angles and orthogonal projectors.  On the locked
TPC-341/TPC-342 response panels, the raw principal cosines are
$0.9957018010$ and $0.0799456793$, while equal-row weighting gives
$0.9144519860$ and $0.0787084493$.  Thus one direction is strongly aligned
but a second direction is nearly orthogonal, and the leading angle changes
from $5.3142$ to $23.8720$ degrees.  A fixed nonsingular coordinate shear
preserves the projectors and cosines to numerical precision, confirming that
the separation is a subspace statement rather than a basis artifact.  Mutual
cross-panel target transfer fails a residual-retention threshold in both
weightings, and all 18 leave-one-control-out angle pairs preserve the
transverse separation.  This is a finite geometric obstruction only; it
provides no arithmetic, asymptotic, Route-A/Route-B, or twin-prime result.
\end{abstract}

\section{Question and scope}

TPC-344 added a signed panel-contrast column to each nuisance category and
found a raw pooled residual retention just below $0.30$.  Its equal-row value
was above $0.30$, so the apparent repair could reflect row weighting rather
than a stable relation between the two panels.  The present paper asks the
coordinate-invariant version of that question:
\emph{what is the geometry of the two nuisance subspaces themselves?}

All objects in this paper are finite vectors generated by the repository's
hash-locked source and operator implementations.  The labels Twin and
nuisance name declared masks and response vectors; they are not an arithmetic
decomposition.  In particular, a principal angle between two finite
response subspaces is not a source-uniform estimate and does not pay any
Route-B loss budget.

\section{Frozen finite protocol}

The two inherited panels are
\begin{align*}
\mathcal P_{341}
 &= [48097,48608],\ [48609,49120],\ [49217,49728],\\
\mathcal P_{342}
 &= [40097,40608],\ [40609,41120],\ [41121,41632].
\end{align*}
Every row has source length $512$ and scale $1024$.  The response operator is
the all-plus deleted-diagonal operator with $Q=54$, kernel exponent one, and
height $H=66$.  Nine fixed coordinate bijections act on four source masks:
twin-prime, non-twin-prime shift, prime-power shift, and zero support.

For a panel $p$ and nuisance category $j$, let $n_{p,r,j}$ be the
nine-control mean output in row $r$, and let $y_{p,r}$ be the corresponding
twin-prime mean.  The nuisance matrix is formed by vertically stacking the
three $n_{p,r,j}$ columns:
\[
 N_p =
 \begin{bmatrix}
 n_{p,1,1} & n_{p,1,2} & n_{p,1,3}\\
 n_{p,2,1} & n_{p,2,2} & n_{p,2,3}\\
 n_{p,3,1} & n_{p,3,2} & n_{p,3,3}
 \end{bmatrix},
\]
where each displayed entry is a length-$512$ block.  The target $Y_p$ is
the analogous stack of the three $y_{p,r}$ vectors.  For the declared
equal-row sensitivity audit, every block in row $r$ is divided by
$\norm{y_{p,r}}$ before stacking.  Raw weighting uses no such rescaling.

\section{Finite Grassmann geometry}

Let
\[
 N_p=Q_p\Sigma_pV_p^{T}
\]
be a positive-SVD factorization, with the columns of $Q_p$ retained according
to the declared floating-point rank rule.  Write $P_p=Q_pQ_p^T$.  The two
nonzero singular values of $Q_{341}^TQ_{342}$ are denoted $c_1\geq c_2\geq0$
and their principal angles are $\theta_i=\arccos(c_i)$.  The observed ranks
are three and two, so two angles are reported and one direction is
panel-specific.

\begin{proposition}[principal-angle invariance]
\label{prop:angle}
The singular values of $Q_1^TQ_2$ depend only on the two finite column
spaces, not on the choice of orthonormal bases or on nonsingular
reparameterizations of the original nuisance columns.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
If $\widetilde Q_i=Q_iU_i$ for orthogonal matrices $U_i$, then
\[
\widetilde Q_1^T\widetilde Q_2
 =U_1^T(Q_1^TQ_2)U_2,
\]
which has the same singular values.  If $N_i$ is replaced by $N_iS_i$ for
nonsingular $S_i$, its column space and hence its orthogonal projector are
unchanged.  Choosing any orthonormal bases of those unchanged spaces gives
the same principal cosines.
\end{proof}

\begin{proposition}[finite projection identity]
\label{prop:projection}
For every finite target $Y$ and orthogonal projector $P$,
\[
\norm{Y}^{2}=\norm{PY}^{2}+\norm{(I-P)Y}^{2}.
\]
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
The two vectors $PY$ and $(I-P)Y$ are orthogonal and sum to $Y$.  The
identity is therefore the Pythagorean theorem.  The implementation evaluates
the two energies and checks the numerical decomposition gap.
\end{proof}

For a target from one panel projected onto the other panel's nuisance space,
we record the finite residual retention
\[
 \rho(Y,N)=\frac{\norm{(I-P_N)Y}^{2}}{\norm{Y}^{2}}.       \tag{1}
\]
The mutual-transfer criterion used here requires both directions to have
$\rho<0.30$.  This is a declared finite diagnostic, not a theorem about
future source intervals.

\section{Coordinate and control robustness}

To test coordinate dependence, both nuisance matrices are multiplied on the
right by the fixed nonsingular shear
\[
 S=\begin{pmatrix}1&1&0\\0&1&1\\0&0&1\end{pmatrix}.
\]
In the raw audit the maximum entrywise projector change is
$7.65\times10^{-15}$ and the maximum principal-cosine change is
$5.56\times10^{-16}$.  The equal-row errors are smaller than these values.
The observed ranks remain $(3,2)$.  This is a numerical check of the exact
coordinate-invariance proposition, not a claim that the finite spaces are
canonical arithmetic objects.

As a hostile control check, each of the nine controls is omitted in turn.
The nuisance means are formed from the other eight controls and the omitted
twin output is used as the target block.  The raw leading-cosine minimum over
the nine pairs is $0.9947001951$, and the raw transverse-cosine maximum is
$0.1549751276$.  Under equal-row weighting the transverse maximum is
$0.1675760096$.  Thus the near-orthogonal transverse direction persists over
all 18 leave-one-control-out geometry pairs.

The transfer readout is deliberately asymmetric.  TPC-341 targets projected
onto the TPC-342 space have residual retentions $0.2306$ and $0.2746$ under
the two weightings, whereas TPC-342 targets projected onto TPC-341 have
retentions $0.3589$ and $0.3235$.  Since the mutual criterion requires both
directions below $0.30$, it is refuted in both finite weightings.  These
numbers are orthogonal projection residuals and do satisfy
Proposition~\ref{prop:projection}; they are distinct from coefficient
cross-fit prediction errors.

\section{Conclusion and next question}

The TPC-344 panel-adaptive repair has a clear finite geometric profile: one
dominant direction is almost shared under raw weighting, a second direction
is nearly orthogonal in both weightings, and the dominant angle is strongly
weight-sensitive.  The coordinate-shear test shows that this profile is not
caused by the selected nuisance coordinates.  The asymmetric transfer
failure prevents a mutual common-law interpretation, while the leave-one-
control-out audit preserves the transverse obstruction.

The reusable route is
\[
\begin{gathered}
\text{panel-adaptive span}\longrightarrow
\text{coordinate-invariant subspaces}\\
\longrightarrow\text{principal angles}\longrightarrow
\text{weighting/transfer obstruction}.
\end{gathered}
\]
The next minimal question is a finite no-go or freeze test: determine whether
adding further panel-specific degrees of freedom produces a robust model
class or merely overfits these locked panels.  Until a source-uniform
arithmetic theorem is supplied, Route-A, Route-B, and the twin-prime endpoint
remain open.
