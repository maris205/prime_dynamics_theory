\begin{abstract}
RH-255 excludes every single-use shell subset in the expanded margin-32
window, leaving a signed/complex quotient selector as the next proposed input.
We prove a necessary firewall: if the selector is an idempotent polynomial in
the finite operator, then it is zero or the identity on each generalized root
space.  Its monomial coefficients may be signed or complex, but its spectral
mask remains binary.  Hence no real, conjugate-closed invariant idempotent
polynomial supported on the resolved RH-254 window escapes the RH-255
obstruction.  A finite interpolation audit exhibits complex polynomial
coordinates with nodal residual below
$7.31\times10^{-14}$; this is only a coordinate diagnostic.  Non-idempotent
quotient grouping remains open.
\end{abstract}

\section{Invariant spectral algebra}

Let $A$ be a finite complex matrix, with primary decomposition
\begin{equation}
 V=\bigoplus_{j=1}^s E_j,
 \qquad A|_{E_j}=\lambda_j I+N_j,
 \label{eq:primary}
\end{equation}
where each $N_j$ is nilpotent.  A polynomial selector has the form $P=p(A)$.
The quotient identity of RH-245 uses a genuine invariant root space and its
orthogonal quotient; it does not authorize arbitrary moment weights
\citep{WangRH245}.

\begin{theorem}[Binary collapse of polynomial idempotents]
\label{thm:collapse}
If $P=p(A)$ and $P^2=P$, then for every primary component $E_j$,
\begin{equation}
 P|_{E_j}=\varepsilon_j I_{E_j},
 \qquad \varepsilon_j\in\{0,1\}.
 \label{eq:binary}
\end{equation}
Consequently
\begin{equation}
 \operatorname{Tr}(A^nP)
 =\sum_{j=1}^s\varepsilon_j\operatorname{Tr}(A^n|_{E_j}).
 \label{eq:trace}
\end{equation}
For a simple spectrum this is $\sum_j\varepsilon_j\lambda_j^n$.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
On $E_j$, polynomial functional calculus gives
$P=c_jI+K_j$, where $c_j=p(\lambda_j)$ and $K_j$ is nilpotent.  The spectrum
of an idempotent lies in $\{0,1\}$, while the spectrum of $c_jI+K_j$ is the
singleton $\{c_j\}$; hence $c_j\in\{0,1\}$.  If $c_j=0$, then $P|_{E_j}$ is
both nilpotent and idempotent, so it is zero.  If $c_j=1$, then
$I-P|_{E_j}$ is nilpotent and idempotent, so it is zero.  This proves
\eqref{eq:binary}; the trace identity follows from the direct sum.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[Real conjugate shells]
If $A$ and $P$ are real, conjugate primary components receive the same
$\varepsilon_j$.  Thus a real polynomial idempotent is a binary mask on real
roots and complete conjugate shells.
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
Complex conjugation commutes with real $A$ and $P$, carrying the action on
$E_\lambda$ to the action on $E_{\bar\lambda}$.
\end{proof}

\section{Finite consequence for the expanded window}

RH-255 proves that the full box relaxation of the expanded shell masks misses
the deterministic anchor at all 32 endpoints \citep{WangRH255}.  Since every
binary mask lies in that box, Theorem~\ref{thm:collapse} gives:

\begin{corollary}[Scoped idempotent-selector obstruction]
No real, conjugate-closed idempotent polynomial selector supported on the
RH-254 expanded roots reaches the archived deterministic anchor tolerance.
The finite class contains 62,030,604,700 eligible binary masks in aggregate.
\end{corollary}

The word ``supported'' is essential.  The result says nothing about unresolved
root spaces, infinite-dimensional functional calculi, or non-idempotent
quotient kernels.

\section{Polynomial-coordinate diagnostic}

For the first six complete shells at each endpoint we interpolate the mask
that selects the first three shells.  The polynomial is represented in the
monomial basis.  Across 32 endpoints we find
\begin{center}
\small
\begin{tabular}{lr}
\toprule
quantity & range or maximum\\
\midrule
interpolation residual & $\le7.31\times10^{-14}$\\
nodal idempotence error & finite floating diagnostic\\
coefficient $\ell^1$ norm & 7.92--33,664.62\\
Vandermonde condition number & 223--$1.07\times10^7$\\
idempotent anchor passes & 0/32\\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{center}
Large and sometimes complex-looking coefficients therefore do not imply new
spectral multiplicities.  At the spectral nodes the values are still exactly
the binary mask, subject to interpolation error.

\section{Route boundary}

The only surviving signed route is non-idempotent grouping forced by an exact
quotient or loop identity.  Arbitrary signed least squares would merely fit
moments and would not define a legal spectral selector.  Gates A--E remain
false/open.  No Hilbert--Polya operator, zeta-divisor equality, Riemann-zero
identification, or RH implication is claimed.
