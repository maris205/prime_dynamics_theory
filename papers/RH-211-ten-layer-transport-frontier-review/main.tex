\title{Ten Layers at the Cross-Scale Transport Frontier\\
\large RH-202--RH-210 Review and the Divisor-First Gate-A Route}
\author{Liang Wang}\date{July 2026}
\begin{document}\maketitle
\begin{abstract}
This paper reviews RH-202--RH-210, a nine-paper investigation of whether the
finite source-observable edge quartet can be transported across small-noise
levels.  The literal answer is negative: the dyadic Haar map has right/left
principal sines up to $0.82388$, oblique-projector defect up to $2.29068$,
and residue displacement up to $8.18374$.  Enlarging the top-modulus cloud
through rank 32 gives no two-sided angle-gate pass.

The batch also identifies what survives.  A rectangular resolvent identity
and two-term source-channel budget are proved exactly.  All four adjacent
quartets have unique conjugate branch correspondences, with left/right
branch mismatch at most $0.007578$.  Endpoint spectral isolation is
numerically feasible in all six packets, although naive transport
certification fails in all four transitions.  The unweighted quartic
divisor agrees between physical channels to relative coefficient error at
most $0.008112$, while the physical residues require a branch-dependent,
source-dependent cocycle.

An exact rotating-similarity counterexample shows that divisor stability
does not require raw projector stability.  The revised coordinate is a
finite dual-channel divisor flow with its intrinsic renormalization still
open.  The
aggregate reproducibility ledger contains 649 finite items and zero identity
failures.  Gate A remains open; Gates B--E, Hilbert--P\'olya, zeta-zero
identification, and RH are untouched.
\end{abstract}

\section{Coordinate inherited from RH-201}

RH-201 ended with a corrected source-relative state type and a canonical
finite outer-edge quartet \citep{WangRH201}.  The next declared wall was a
cross-level map carrying Riesz projectors, source/observation states,
residues, and determinant data between $\sigma=0.04,0.02,0.01$.

The new batch answers that question in layers.  It does not force one binary
verdict on every object.  Instead it separates three ledgers:
\begin{equation}\label{eq:ledgers}
 \begin{aligned}
 Q&=\text{spectral branches, divisors, unweighted traces},\\
 R&=\text{right/left states, Riesz projectors, transport maps},\\
 W&=\text{source--observation residues and weighted moments}.
 \end{aligned}
\end{equation}
The central discovery is that these ledgers have different stability.

\section{The naive shell map is rejected finitely}

Let $J$ be the dyadic Haar embedding.  RH-202 compares adjacent packet
spaces and projectors \citep{WangRH202}.  The four transition/channel cases
give
\begin{equation}\label{eq:state-negative}
 \max\sin\theta_R=0.82175,
 \qquad \max\sin\theta_L=0.82388,
 \qquad \max\frac{\norm{P_f-JP_cJ^*}_F}{\norm{P_f}_F}=2.29068.
\end{equation}
The quartet-restricted intertwining defect reaches $0.65573$.  Source defects
are at least $0.78134$, and one relative residue displacement is $8.18374$.

This rejects one concrete equation:
\begin{equation*}
 \text{fine packet}=\text{Haar lift of coarse packet}+\text{small error}.
\end{equation*}
It does not reject the endpoint quartets or all possible renormalized maps.

\section{Exact anatomy of transport}

RH-203 proves, for $E=A_fJ-JA_c$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:resolvent}
 R_f(z)J-JR_c(z)=R_f(z)ER_c(z)
\end{equation}
and integrates it to
\begin{equation}\label{eq:projector}
 P_fJ-JP_c=\frac{1}{2\pi i}\int_\Gamma R_f(z)ER_c(z)\,dz
\end{equation}
\citep{WangRH203}.  For source matrices and a column embedding $K$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:channel}
 P_fS_f-JP_cS_cK^*
 =P_f(S_f-JS_cK^*)+(P_fJ-JP_c)S_cK^*.
\end{equation}

These exact identities show that contour refinement alone cannot repair the
physical map: both the operator intertwining term and source term are large.
The 240 identity tests have zero failures.

\section{The spectral branches survive}

Every quartet is two nonreal conjugate pairs.  RH-204 reduces it to upper
representatives $a_-,a_+$ ordered by real part and compares direct versus
swapped assignments \citep{WangRH204}.  All four direct assignments are
unique.  The minimum pointwise margin is $0.08884$ and grows beyond $0.714$
on the finer transition.

At fixed $\sigma$, the two physical channels agree strongly:
\begin{equation}\label{eq:channel-branch}
 \max_{\sigma,j}|\lambda^L_{\sigma,j}-\lambda^R_{\sigma,j}|
 =0.007578.
\end{equation}
The maximum branch displacement falls descriptively by a ratio below
$0.359$ from the first to the second transition.  Two transitions do not
establish convergence, but they produce a non-post-hoc label for denser
experiments.

\section{Exact endpoint maps are retrospective}

For orthonormal packet frames, RH-205 forms the polar factor of
$Q_f^*JQ_c$ and the partial isometry
\begin{equation}\label{eq:procrustes}
 H=Q_fUQ_c^*.
\end{equation}
It maps one endpoint packet exactly onto the other and is optimally close to
the embedded frame \citep{WangRH205}.  The rank-normalized physical costs are
$0.44075$--$0.70099$.

This proves existence but not prediction: $H$ requires the fine packet as
input.  Moreover, exact range transport leaves a dynamical defect determined
in part by $\Lambda_f-\Lambda_c$, whose maximum movement is $0.38160$.

\section{Physical residues require a cocycle}

Residues are gauge invariant.  RH-206 defines exact branch multipliers
\begin{equation}\label{eq:cocycle}
 m_j(c,f)=\frac{r_{f,j}}{r_{c,j}},
 \qquad m_j(a,c)=m_j(b,c)m_j(a,b)
\end{equation}
\citep{WangRH206}.  Conjugate branches have conjugate multipliers.

The best common complex scalar leaves relative residuals
$0.32371$--$0.99985$.  The exact diagonal cocycle has roundoff residual, but
the corresponding left/right multipliers differ by as much as $1.05364$.
Thus $W$ is branch and channel dependent at the current anchors.  It cannot
be substituted for the unweighted divisor ledger $Q$.

\section{The quartic divisor is the stable finite object}

RH-207 studies
\begin{equation}\label{eq:quartic}
 D_\sigma(z)=\prod_{j=1}^4(z-\lambda_{\sigma,j})
 =z^4+c_1z^3+c_2z^2+c_3z+c_4
\end{equation}
\citep{WangRH207}.  Left/right coefficient errors are at most $0.008112$.
The relative constant-term discrepancy decreases from $0.02716$ to
$0.007749$ over the three anchors.

Across scales, however, coefficient errors are $0.240$--$0.317$.  The raw
quartic is a coherent dual-channel flow, not a converged flow.  Newton
identities exactly equate its coefficients with all unweighted power traces;
120 implementation cases have zero failures.

\section{Endpoint validation and transport validation separate}

RH-208 uses the local feasibility ratio
\begin{equation}\label{eq:beta}
 \beta=\frac{2\kappa\norm{Av-\lambda v}}{\delta}.
\end{equation}
At all six endpoint packets, the maximum is below
$3.25\times10^{-13}$ \citep{WangRH208}.  Direct interval validation of each
finite endpoint therefore appears plausible.

For Haar-lifted coarse modes, the smallest transport ratio is $3.3486$ and
the largest is $29.4916$; no transition passes.  These are floating
feasibility indicators, not certificates.  They nevertheless justify
validating endpoints directly rather than attempting to validate a failed
homotopy.

\section{Cloud enlargement is not the missing correction}

RH-209 tests top-modulus clouds of ranks
\begin{equation*}
 2,4,6,8,12,16,24,32.
\end{equation*}
None of the 32 rank/transition/channel records has both right and left
maximum principal sines below $0.5$ \citep{WangRH209}.  The best joint sine
is $0.69373$, and expanded clouds often approach one.

This rejects only equal-rank modulus selection with Haar transport.  It
leaves adaptive contours, unequal ranks, source-weighted metrics, and
renormalized coordinates open.

\section{Why the divisor-first pivot is logically allowed}

RH-210 considers $A_\theta=U_\theta DU_\theta^*$ with fixed diagonal $D$.
Its characteristic polynomial is independent of $\theta$, while a selected
rank-one projector moves by $|\sin\theta|$ \citep{WangRH210}.  Hence a divisor
can be stable while projector distance reaches one.

This does not make operator theory unnecessary.  It proves only that raw
projector convergence is not a logical prerequisite for first discovering a
stable scalar divisor.  A later dynamical/Fredholm realization will still
require analytic operator or symbolic structure.

\section{Revised Gate-A subroute}

The new order is:
\begin{equation}\label{eq:route}
 \begin{gathered}
 \text{finite branch labels}
 \longrightarrow\text{intrinsically normalized coefficient flow}\\
 \longrightarrow\text{growing locally uniform divisor family}
 \longrightarrow\text{dynamical/Fredholm realization}.
 \end{gathered}
\end{equation}
In parallel, endpoint projectors can be interval validated and the residue
cocycle retained as a separate transfer ledger.  Raw state transport should
resume only after the scalar flow suggests the correct coordinates.

\section{Recommended RH-212 experiment}

RH-212 should densify the interval near the finest anchors on both physical
channels.  Before seeing results, compare at least two intrinsic quartic
normalizations:
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=2em]
\item determinant-radius normalization
$\rho=|c_4|^{1/4}$ and $\lambda_j\mapsto\lambda_j/\rho$;
\item centered second-moment normalization
$\mu=-c_1/4$ followed by a scale derived from
$\sum_j|\lambda_j-\mu|^2$.
\end{enumerate}
The audit should test branch uniqueness, left/right discrepancy, adjacent
coefficient variation, and whether one normalization improves all
coefficients without fitting each level separately.  A positive result gives
a candidate renormalization map; a negative result redirects Gate A toward
adaptive growing contours.

\section{Reproducibility ledger}

The aggregate count is 649 finite endpoint, mode, random identity,
correspondence, rank, and counterexample records.  Exact-identity audits
report zero failures.  Each paper contains focused source, frozen JSON,
tests, claim ledgers, and a publication archive.  The archive machinery
checks reproducibility, not truth of asymptotic extrapolations.

\section{Position in the five macro gates}

\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=2em]
\item Gate A: a finite dual-channel divisor flow exists; intrinsic
renormalization, growing cloud, and determinant limit remain open.
\item Gate B: time-oriented unitary/scattering completion is untouched.
\item Gate C: self-adjoint generator and internal $T\log T$ count are
untouched.
\item Gate D: prime-power/von Mangoldt trace identity is untouched.
\item Gate E: equality with the completed zeta divisor is untouched.
\end{itemize}

The route coordinate is
\begin{equation*}
 \boxed{\texttt{finite\_dual\_channel\_divisor\_flow\_open\_renormalization}}.
\end{equation*}
No Hilbert--P\'olya operator, zeta-zero identification, Riemann-hypothesis
implication, or arithmetic trace formula is asserted.
