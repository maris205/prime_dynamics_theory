\subsection{The positive-to-signed switch}

The positive resonance strategy takes absolute values before the
outer signed reconstruction.  If its principal or cross-map ledger
has a literal polynomial obstruction, the correct fallback is
\[
\text{signed filter}\quad\longrightarrow\quad
\text{arithmetic estimate}\quad\longrightarrow\quad
\text{majorization},
\]
not another paper on positive cross-cell dispersion.  The signed
object must retain:
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=2em]
\item the reconstruction multiplier and its sign;
\item the \(r\)-dependent unit phase;
\item the affine character and actual moving row;
\item the M\"obius factors inside each resolved fiber; and
\item coherent outer reassembly across frequency-free branches.
\end{itemize}
An estimate for independent \(r\) and branch variables does not
control this product.

\subsection{The three principal route families}

\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=2em]
\item \textbf{Positive resonance route.}  Test \(W_X\), then \(\PX\),
  then the identical-map quotient, and only then \(\XX\).  This is the
  cheapest falsifiable route.
\item \textbf{Signed native route.}  Attack H3 directly in the
  physical affine/determinant coordinates, bypassing a failed
  positive majorant or continuous Mellin lower frame.
\item \textbf{Coherent prime-square route.}  Prove a
  coefficient-specific Bessel or dispersion estimate inside one
  residual coordinate, preserving the three nonsmooth coefficient
  slots and the physical prime weights.
\end{enumerate}
The second and third routes may share exact dictionaries, but gains
may be combined only when they act on disjoint costs or a theorem
places them in one common carrier.
