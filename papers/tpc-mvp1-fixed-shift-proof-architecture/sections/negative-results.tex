\section{Publication-worthy negative outcomes}
\label{sec:negative}

The architecture is useful even if a main route fails, provided the
failure is mathematical rather than autobiographical.  The following
would be legitimate standalone outcomes when proved on actual
coefficients:
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=2em]
\item a polynomial opened-atom concentration theorem;
\item a sharp principal-mass obstruction to the positive majorant;
\item a literal example showing that positive majorization destroys a
  signed cancellation present before the filter;
\item a canonical frame condition-number lower bound;
\item an equivalence or no-reduction theorem between the generic
  affine gate and a prescribed-shift Chowla-type input;
\item determinant energy and zero-mode incompatibility on an actual
  family;
\item an average-to-fixed-\(h_0\) localization obstruction;
\item an actual-support remainder obstruction to full-block cover;
\item a proof that the present parameter geometry forces
  \(\Lambda_{\mathrm{phys}}\ge1/400\); or
\item nonidentifiability of the required physical Gram from the
  available surrogate Gram.
\end{enumerate}

\begin{remark}[Failure to prove is not a theorem]
Computational saturation, a stalled argument, or the absence of a
known estimate is not a stop certificate.  A negative paper requires
an exact family, lower bound, kernel, counterexample, or logical
nonimplication with the physical typing visible.
\end{remark}

\subsection{Weaker positive outputs}

If a fixed-\(h_0\) gate remains open, the same methods may yield
valuable but strictly qualified results:
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=2em]
\item averaged-shift or density-one-shift cancellation;
\item a fixed-power gain on a literal selected block;
\item a density-one cofactor range;
\item a theorem after smoothing or freezing one coefficient slot;
\item a complete classification of saturating resonance families; or
\item a parameter range in which the endpoint is feasible.
\end{itemize}
These results may be publishable.  They remain \(\Lzero/\Lone\) or a
restricted arithmetic theorem unless a fixed-\(h_0\) return is proved.

\subsection{A portfolio rather than a single bet}

The MVP program therefore has three kinds of success:
\begin{enumerate}[label=(\alph*),leftmargin=2em]
\item a gate closes and the dependency graph advances;
\item a gate fails by a sharp theorem and an entire false route is
  retired; or
\item the literal audit reveals a better carrier on which a new route
  can be formulated.
\end{enumerate}
What it does not permit is indefinitely renaming the same unresolved
arithmetic cancellation.
