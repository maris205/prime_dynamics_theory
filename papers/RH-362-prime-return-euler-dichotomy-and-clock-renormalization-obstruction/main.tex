\begin{abstract}
For the integral H\'enon automorphism
\(
H(x,y)=(1-6x^2-y,x)
\)
and an integral point \(P\), let \(r_p(P)\) be the return period of
\(P\bmod p\).
Starting from the exact return--divisibility identity
\(
p\mid a_n(P)\Longleftrightarrow r_p(P)\mid n
\),
we construct two zeta objects from the complete marked cycle of \(P\bmod p\).
For a nonperiodic integral orbit, the countable cycle bouquet has finite
fixed-point counts in every order and a well-defined formal Artin--Mazur zeta
function.  Its Dirichlet specialization
\[
 Z_P(s)=\prod_p(1-p^{-s r_p(P)})^{-1}
\]
converges normally, is holomorphic, and is zero-free on the full half-plane
\(\RePart s>0\).  We identify its multiplicative \(0/1\) coefficients and its
logarithmic derivative, whose weights are \(r_p(P)\log p\) on
\(p^{j r_p(P)}\), rather than von Mangoldt weights.  The natural cycle-block
operator is compact for \(\RePart s>0\) and trace class for \(\RePart s>3\),
where its Fredholm determinant equals \(Z_P(s)^{-1}\).  Conversely, dividing
each prime's clock by \(r_p(P)\) forces the Riemann Euler factor locally but
produces an operator in no finite Schatten class.  This is a rigorous
nonpromotion result: the forced product erases the return lengths instead of
recovering a canonical spectral determinant.  The paper is an independent
post-four-volume theorem edge.  It does not alter the RH-1--RH-361 physical
frontier, close Gates A--E, identify Riemann zeros, or prove the Riemann
Hypothesis.
\end{abstract}

\section{Integral and modular return data}

Fix
\[
 H(x,y)=(1-6x^2-y,x),
 \qquad
 H^{-1}(u,v)=(v,1-6v^2-u).
\]
Thus reduction modulo every integer \(M\ge1\) is a permutation.  For
\(P\in\Z^2\), define
\[
 r_M(P)=\min\{n\ge1:H^n(P)\equiv P\pmod M\},
 \qquad r_1(P)=1,
\]
and, writing \(H^n(P)=(x_n,y_n)\),
\[
 a_n(P)=\gcd(|x_n-x_0|,|y_n-y_0|),
\]
with \(\gcd(0,0)=0\).  The source theorem is
\begin{equation}
 M\mid a_n(P)
 \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
 r_M(P)\mid n.
 \label{eq:return-divisibility}
\end{equation}
For a nonperiodic integral orbit, every \(a_n(P)>0\), and the source also
proves the strong divisibility identity
\[
 \gcd(a_m(P),a_n(P))=a_{\gcd(m,n)}(P).
\]
Only \eqref{eq:return-divisibility} is needed for the first new theorem.

\begin{theorem}[Exact finite set of low return ranks]
\label{thm:low-ranks}
Let \(P\in\Z^2\) have a nonperiodic integral orbit.  For every integer
\(K\ge2\),
\[
 \{p:r_p(P)<K\}
 =
 \left\{p:p\mid\prod_{1\le n<K}a_n(P)\right\}.
 \label{eq:low-rank-set}
\]
In particular the set on the left is finite for every \(K\), and
\(r_p(P)\to\infty\) in the cofinite-prime sense.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
If \(r_p<K\), take \(n=r_p\) in \eqref{eq:return-divisibility}; then
\(p\mid a_{r_p}(P)\).  Conversely, if \(p\mid a_n(P)\) for some \(n<K\),
then \eqref{eq:return-divisibility} gives \(r_p\mid n\), hence \(r_p<K\).
All factors in the product are positive integers, so it has finitely many
prime divisors.
\end{proof}

The concrete point used in the executable artifact is unconditional.

\begin{lemma}[Escape of the primary integral seed]
\label{lem:escape}
The orbit of \(P_0=(0,0)\) is nonperiodic.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
The first coordinate satisfies
\[
 x_{n+1}=1-6x_n^2-x_{n-1},
\]
and begins
\[
 x_0=0,\qquad x_1=1,\qquad x_2=-5,\qquad x_3=-150.
\]
Suppose \(x_{n-1}=-b_{n-1}\), \(x_n=-b_n\), with
\(b_n>b_{n-1}\ge5\).  Then
\[
 x_{n+1}=1-6b_n^2+b_{n-1}<0,
 \qquad
 |x_{n+1}|=6b_n^2-b_{n-1}-1>b_n.
\]
The base pair \(b_2=5,b_3=150\) starts the induction.  Hence the absolute
values grow strictly and the orbit cannot return to \(P_0\).
\end{proof}

\section{Pointed cycles and a formal Artin--Mazur bouquet}

For a prime \(p\), let
\[
 \cO_{p,P}=\{P,H_pP,\ldots,H_p^{r_p-1}P\}
\]
be the complete cycle containing \(P\bmod p\), and let \(U_{p,P}\) be its
cyclic permutation matrix.  The complete cycle, rather than a single return
indicator, is essential.

\begin{proposition}[Local marked-cycle determinant]
\label{prop:local-cycle}
For every \(n\ge1\),
\[
 \Tr(U_{p,P}^n)=r_p\mathbf 1_{r_p\mid n},
 \qquad
 \det(I-zU_{p,P})=1-z^{r_p}.
\]
Consequently the Artin--Mazur zeta function of this finite cycle is
\[
 \zeta_{\cO_{p,P}}(z)=(1-z^{r_p})^{-1}.
\]
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
The \(n\)-th power of a cyclic shift fixes every basis vector exactly when
\(r_p\mid n\).  The eigenvalues of \(U_{p,P}\) are all \(r_p\)-th roots of
unity, whose characteristic product gives \(1-z^{r_p}\).
\end{proof}

Define the countable disjoint union
\[
 \cX_P=\bigsqcup_p\cO_{p,P},
 \qquad
 F_P|_{\cO_{p,P}}=H_p.
\]

\begin{theorem}[Locally finite formal bouquet]
\label{thm:bouquet}
If \(P\) is nonperiodic over \(\Z\), then every fixed-point set of \(F_P^n\)
is finite and
\[
 T_n(P):=\#\Fix(F_P^n)
 =\sum_{p:r_p\mid n}r_p
 =\sum_{p\mid a_n(P)}r_p.
 \label{eq:bouquet-trace}
\]
The formal Artin--Mazur zeta function is therefore
\[
 \mathcal Z_P(z)
 =\exp\!\left(\sum_{n\ge1}\frac{T_n(P)}n z^n\right)
 =\prod_p(1-z^{r_p})^{-1}
 \in\Z[[z]].
 \label{eq:formal-zeta}
\]
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
By theorem~\ref{thm:low-ranks}, only finitely many primes have \(r_p\le n\).
Proposition~\ref{prop:local-cycle} then gives the first sum in
\eqref{eq:bouquet-trace}; equation~\eqref{eq:return-divisibility} gives the
second.  Each coefficient of the exponential depends on finitely many fixed
point counts, so the usual finite-cycle Euler-product calculation is valid
coefficientwise.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}
The theorem claims a formal power series, not a positive analytic radius,
entropy formula, pressure identity, or zeta function of the full maps
\(H_p:\Fp^2\to\Fp^2\).  It uses one marked cycle from each distinct finite
dynamical system.
\end{remark}

\section{The return-rank Dirichlet Euler product}

Use the real logarithm of \(p\) and write \(p^{-s}=e^{-s\log p}\).

\begin{theorem}[Zero-free return Euler product]
\label{thm:euler-product}
For a nonperiodic integral point \(P\),
\[
 Z_P(s)
 :=\prod_p\det(I-p^{-s}U_{p,P})^{-1}
 =\prod_p(1-p^{-s r_p})^{-1}
 \label{eq:dirichlet-zeta}
\]
converges normally on every closed half-plane
\(\RePart s\ge\delta>0\).  It defines a holomorphic, zero-free function on
\(\RePart s>0\), with canonical logarithm
\[
 \log Z_P(s)
 =\sum_p\sum_{j\ge1}\frac{p^{-s j r_p}}j.
 \label{eq:log-zeta}
\]
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Fix \(\delta>0\) and choose \(K\) with \(\delta K>1\).  Theorem
\ref{thm:low-ranks} leaves only finitely many primes with \(r_p<K\).  For all
remaining primes and \(\RePart s\ge\delta\),
\[
 |p^{-s r_p}|\le p^{-\delta K},
 \qquad
 \sum_p p^{-\delta K}<\infty.
\]
After discarding finitely many initial factors, the logarithmic geometric
series is uniformly dominated by a constant multiple of this convergent
prime sum.  Thus \eqref{eq:log-zeta} converges normally.  Its exponential is
holomorphic and never zero; every finite exceptional factor is also
holomorphic and nonzero in the open right half-plane.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[Multiplicative coefficients and trace weights]
\label{cor:coefficients}
The absolutely convergent Dirichlet expansion is
\[
 Z_P(s)=\sum_{m\ge1}\frac{c_P(m)}{m^s},
 \qquad
 c_P(m)=
 \mathbf 1_{\{r_p(P)\mid v_p(m)\ \text{for every }p\}}.
 \label{eq:coefficients}
\]
The function \(c_P\) is multiplicative, takes only the values zero and one,
and need not be completely multiplicative.  Both its ordinary and absolute
convergence abscissae equal zero.  Moreover
\[
 -\frac{Z_P'}{Z_P}(s)
 =\sum_p\sum_{j\ge1}
 r_p\log p\;p^{-s j r_p}.
 \label{eq:log-derivative}
\]
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
Expand each local geometric series and use unique factorization; this gives
\eqref{eq:coefficients}.  Theorem~\ref{thm:euler-product} gives absolute
convergence for every positive real part.  At \(s=0\), the infinitely many
distinct terms \(m=p^{r_p}\) all have coefficient one, so even ordinary
convergence fails.  Normal convergence permits termwise differentiation on
compact subsets, giving \eqref{eq:log-derivative}.
\end{proof}

The support and weight in \eqref{eq:log-derivative} differ from the classical
von Mangoldt ledger
\(
-\zeta'/\zeta=\sum_{p,j}\log p\,p^{-sj}
\).
This difference is a theorem, not a missing numerical calibration.

\begin{theorem}[No meromorphic crossing at the origin]
\label{thm:origin}
Neither \(Z_P\) nor \(D_P=Z_P^{-1}\) admits a meromorphic continuation through
\(s=0\).  This statement does not assert that the full line
\(\RePart s=0\) is a natural boundary.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
For real \(\sigma>0\), \(0<D_P(\sigma)<1\).  Fix any \(N\) primes
\(p_1,\ldots,p_N\).  Since \(1-e^{-x}\le x\),
\[
 0<D_P(\sigma)
 \le\prod_{j=1}^N(1-p_j^{-\sigma r_{p_j}})
 \le \sigma^N\prod_{j=1}^N r_{p_j}\log p_j.
\]
If \(D_P\) were meromorphic at zero, its decay along the positive real axis
would make the singularity removable.  The resulting analytic function would
have a zero of every order and hence vanish identically, contradicting
\(D_P(\sigma)>0\).  A meromorphic continuation of \(Z_P\) would give one for
its reciprocal, so it is also impossible.
\end{proof}

\section{Natural cycle blocks and the Fredholm region}

Let
\[
 \cH_P=\bigoplus_p\ell^2(\cO_{p,P}),
 \qquad
 T_s=\bigoplus_p p^{-s}U_{p,P}.
\]

\begin{theorem}[Compact and Schatten regions]
\label{thm:operator}
For \(\sigma=\RePart s>0\), \(T_s\) is compact.  For every \(q>0\),
\[
 \|T_s\|_{S_q}^q=\sum_p r_p p^{-q\sigma}.
 \label{eq:schatten}
\]
Since \(r_p\le p^2\), the condition \(q\sigma>3\) is sufficient for
\(T_s\in S_q\).  In particular, for \(\RePart s>3\),
\[
 \det_F(I-T_s)=\prod_p(1-p^{-s r_p})=Z_P(s)^{-1}.
 \label{eq:fredholm}
\]
If an integer \(m\ge1\) satisfies \(m\RePart s>3\), then
\[
 Z_P(s)^{-1}
 =\exp\!\left(-\sum_{n=1}^{m-1}\frac{\tau_n(s)}n\right)
 \det_m(I-T_s),
 \quad
 \tau_n(s)=\sum_{p:r_p\mid n}r_p p^{-sn}.
 \label{eq:regularized}
\]
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
The block norms \(p^{-\sigma}\) tend to zero, so the finite-rank truncations
converge to \(T_s\) in operator norm.  Each block contributes \(r_p\) equal
singular values \(p^{-\sigma}\), which gives \eqref{eq:schatten}.  The orbit
of \(P\bmod p\) lies in a set of \(p^2\) points, so \(r_p\le p^2\), and
\(
\sum_p p^{2-q\sigma}<\infty
\)
when \(q\sigma>3\).  The trace-class determinant factors blockwise and
proposition~\ref{prop:local-cycle} gives \eqref{eq:fredholm}.  The standard
definition of the regularized determinant \citep{Simon2005}, together with
the finite combinatorial trace sums \(\tau_n\), gives
\eqref{eq:regularized}.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}
Equation \eqref{eq:fredholm} licenses an ordinary Fredholm interpretation
only in the proved trace-class region.  The scalar function \(Z_P^{-1}\)
continues holomorphically to \(\RePart s>0\), but this continuation is not by
itself a theorem that \(T_s\) is trace class there.  Moreover \(T_s\) is an
\(s\)-dependent direct sum, uses a marked point, and mixes distinct finite
fields.  It is not the canonical physical determinant required by Gate A.
\end{remark}

\section{Periodic comparison}

The nonperiodic hypothesis is exactly what makes the bouquet locally finite
and moves the Euler product's convergence boundary to zero.

\begin{proposition}[Periodic branch]
\label{prop:periodic}
Suppose \(P\in\Z^2\) has exact integral period \(N\).  Then \(r_p(P)\mid N\)
for every prime.  The exceptional set
\[
 E_P=\{p:r_p(P)<N\}
 =\left\{p:p\mid
 \prod_{\substack{d\mid N\\d<N}}a_d(P)\right\}
\]
is finite, and for \(\RePart s>1/N\),
\[
 Z_P(s)=\zeta(Ns)
 \prod_{p\in E_P}
 \frac{1-p^{-Ns}}{1-p^{-r_p(P)s}}.
 \label{eq:periodic-factor}
\]
The right side gives a meromorphic continuation and a simple pole at
\(s=1/N\).  In this branch \(F_P^N\) fixes infinitely many bouquet points, so
the formal locally finite Artin--Mazur construction of
theorem~\ref{thm:bouquet} is unavailable.  For \(N=1\), the proper-divisor
product defining \(E_P\) and the finite Euler correction over \(E_P\) are both
understood as empty products.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Integral periodicity gives \(H^N(P)=P\), hence \(r_p\mid N\).  If \(r_p<N\),
then \(r_p\) is a proper divisor of \(N\) and
\eqref{eq:return-divisibility} puts \(p\) among the displayed finite prime
divisors.  The converse follows in the same way.  Outside \(E_P\), every
local factor is \(1-p^{-Ns}\), so separating the finite exceptional factors
from Euler's product proves \eqref{eq:periodic-factor}.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}
The source lock does not assert that this particular H\'enon map has an
integral periodic point.  More importantly, \eqref{eq:periodic-factor}
imports \(\zeta(Ns)\) because the local periods are almost everywhere equal
to \(N\).  It is not an independent spectral reconstruction of zeta or its
zeros.
\end{remark}

\section{Why inverse-length weighting is not a bridge}

One may force the desired Euler factor by changing every prime's clock:
\[
 \widetilde T_s
 =\bigoplus_p p^{-s/r_p}U_{p,P}.
\]
Blockwise,
\[
 \det(I-p^{-s/r_p}U_{p,P})=1-p^{-s},
\]
and therefore the scalar product of inverse block determinants is
\(\zeta(s)\) for \(\RePart s>1\).  The output no longer depends on \(r_p\).

\begin{theorem}[Clock-renormalization Schatten obstruction]
\label{thm:forced-zeta}
Let \(P\) be nonperiodic.  For every finite \(q>0\) and every
\(\sigma=\RePart s>0\),
\[
 \|\widetilde T_s\|_{S_q}^q
 =\sum_p r_p p^{-q\sigma/r_p}=\infty.
 \label{eq:forced-divergence}
\]
Thus the forced Riemann Euler product is not the ordinary or finite-order
regularized Fredholm determinant of this block direct sum on any positive
half-plane.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
By theorem~\ref{thm:low-ranks}, eventually \(r_p>2q\sigma\).  For those
primes, \(q\sigma/r_p<1/2\), so each summand in
\eqref{eq:forced-divergence} is at least \(p^{-1/2}\).  The prime sum
diverges.
\end{proof}

This is the decisive scoped negative result.  The local normalization
\(s\mapsto s/r_p\) prescribes that one complete cycle has weight \(p^{-s}\).
It therefore deletes the very return-length data that the construction was
supposed to explain.  The exact scalar Euler product is not evidence for a
Hilbert--Polya operator, a time-oriented scattering completion, or an
intrinsic arithmetic trace.

\section{Conclusion}

The return--divisibility theorem supports a natural all-prime zeta object, but
its analytic behavior is sharply different from the Riemann zeta function on
the nonperiodic branch: the return Euler product is already holomorphic and
zero-free on the whole open right half-plane.  The natural block operator
retains the return ranks and has a genuine, though restricted, Fredholm
region.  The inverse-length clock produces the desired Riemann local factor
only by making every cycle contribute the same prescribed prime weight, and
the resulting direct sum loses all finite Schatten classes.

The narrow next arithmetic questions are therefore source-backed constraints
on the ranks themselves---average-over-primes theorems, Chebotarev-type
finite-period events, primitive divisors, or height growth---and a genuinely
intrinsic global dynamics whose determinant does not arise from independently
retiming every prime.  The original physical RH route remains available and
unchanged on top of the four-volume foundation.
