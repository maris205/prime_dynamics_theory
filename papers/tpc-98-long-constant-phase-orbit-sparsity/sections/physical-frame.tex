\section{The literal frame and a stronger harmless padding}
\label{sec:physical-frame}

\subsection{Rows, orbit points, and opened divisors}

Fix \(h_0\in\Z\setminus\{0\}\).  On one physical dyadic packet a row
is
\[
 \alpha=(\ell_\alpha,d_\alpha),\qquad
 m_\alpha=\ell_\alpha d_\alpha\asymp_{\mathscr D}Q_X,
\]
and its orbit target is
\begin{equation}
 N_\alpha(j)=m_\alpha j+h_0.
\label{eq:physical-target}
\end{equation}
The joint orbit support lies in a fixed union of intervals contained
in an interval \(\cJ_X\) of diameter \(O_{\mathscr D}(J_X)\).
The two row coefficients satisfy the imported bounds
\begin{equation}
 \sum_\alpha|\gamma_\alpha^{(i)}|
 \ll_{\mathscr D}Q_X,
 \qquad i=1,2.
\label{eq:row-l1}
\end{equation}
These are the literal row coefficients, including prime-source,
squarefree-divisor, smooth, and residue factors
\citep{WangTPC33}.

Opening the ultra increment on the moving row gives
\begin{equation}
 N_\alpha(j)=u\sigma,
 \qquad T_X<u\le U_{0,X},
\label{eq:opened-factorization}
\end{equation}
where \(u\) carries the M\"obius--logarithmic ultra coefficient and
\(\sigma\) is the complementary divisor.  The actual-mask
disintegration further introduces a row-gcd divisor \(v\mid
d_\alpha,d_\gamma\), at most two row-coordinate intervals, and
projector children whose total absolute coefficient mass, for each
fixed opposite row (one physical column), is
\begin{equation}
 X^{o(1)}.
\label{eq:projector-soft-mass}
\end{equation}
The same columnwise statement holds after exchanging the two
polarizations.  No full-matrix low-rank or projective-mass conclusion
is inferred from it.

\subsection{Soft divisor envelopes}

All effective targets are \(X^{O(1)}\).  The elementary divisor
bound and the explicit matched coefficients therefore give,
uniformly on the literal support,
\begin{align}
 \sum_{\substack{u\mid N\\T_X<u\le U_{0,X}}}
 |\mu(u)|\log u
 &\le X^{o(1)},\label{eq:ultra-envelope}\\
 |\mathsf H_m(j)|
 +|\mathsf A_{m,T_X}(j)|
 +|\mathsf A_{m,U_{0,X}}(j)|
 &\le X^{o(1)}.
\label{eq:prefix-envelope}
\end{align}
Every \(o(1)\) here is uniform over the fixed dyadic data
\(\mathscr D\).  No cancellation is used in
\eqref{eq:ultra-envelope}--\eqref{eq:prefix-envelope}.

\subsection{The strengthened no-wrap prime}

TPC-86 chooses an auxiliary prime \(q_X\asymp Q_X\) large enough to
embed the determinant support injectively \citep{WangTPC86}.  Since
all of the following quantities are \(O_{\mathscr D}(Q_X)\),
Bertrand's postulate permits the same choice to satisfy
\begin{equation}
\begin{split}
 q_X>{}&
 2\max_\alpha m_\alpha,\qquad
 2\max_{\alpha,\gamma}|m_\alpha-m_\gamma|,\qquad
 2\max_\alpha d_\alpha,\qquad
 2\operatorname{diam}(\cJ_X),
\end{split}
\label{eq:strong-padding}
\end{equation}
as well as the original determinant no-wrap inequalities.  Enlarging
the fixed comparison constant changes neither the physical packet
nor any exponent.

\begin{lemma}[Immediate consequences]
\label{lem:padding-consequences}
For all sufficiently large \(X\), the choice
\eqref{eq:strong-padding} has
\[
 q_X\nmid m_\alpha,\qquad q_X\nmid\ell_\alpha,\qquad
 q_X\nmid v
\]
on every active row and row-gcd layer.  Moreover any residue class
modulo \(q_X\) meets \(\cJ_X\) in at most one integer.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Every displayed positive integer is strictly between \(0\) and
\(q_X\).  The final assertion follows because two distinct
representatives of one residue class differ by at least \(q_X\),
whereas \(\operatorname{diam}(\cJ_X)<q_X\).
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[Literal content steps are below \(q_X\)]
\label{lem:content-step-below-q}
Call a child \emph{opened-active} when its two row targets, primitive
masks, and exact-content divisibilities all hold before row-gcd
projector reassembly; its parent is allowed to cancel after that
reassembly.  For every opened-active exact-content child,
\[
 1\le B_\xi\le b_\xi<q_X.
\]
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Write \(b_\xi=c_\xi\kappa_\xi\).  This integer divides both
physical targets
\[
 M_\xi j_\xi+h_0,\qquad n_\xi j_\xi+h_0.
\]
The primitive target provenance imported in TPC-93--TPC-94 gives
\((b_\xi,j_\xi)=1\).  Subtracting the targets therefore gives
\[
 b_\xi\mid(M_\xi-n_\xi)j_\xi
 \quad\Longrightarrow\quad
 b_\xi\mid M_\xi-n_\xi.
\]
The physical off-diagonal mask has \(M_\xi\ne n_\xi\), while
\eqref{eq:strong-padding} gives
\(|M_\xi-n_\xi|<q_X\).  Hence
\(b_\xi\le|M_\xi-n_\xi|<q_X\).  Finally
\(B_\xi=b_\xi/(b_\xi,\sigma_\xi)\le b_\xi\).
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}
The modulus is still only an auxiliary Fourier-padding device.
\Cref{lem:padding-consequences} does not impose a congruence on the
original sum and does not select a subsequence of scales.
\end{remark}
