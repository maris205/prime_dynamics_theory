\title{The Nonnormal Frobenius Tail-Budget Barrier\\
\large Why a Valid Whole-Matrix Bound Does Not Close the Determinant}
\author{Liang Wang}
\date{July 2026}

\begin{document}
\maketitle

\begin{abstract}
RH-228 controls every complete shell resolved beyond the selected cloud, but
not the unresolved operator complement. This paper tests the first
whole-matrix certificate available without constructing a reducing
projection.

For any finite matrix $A$, Schur triangularization gives
\[
 \sum_j|\lambda_j(A)|^2\le\norm{A}_{\mathrm F}^2.
\]
After factoring the Perron root, negative parity root, and selected bulk
cloud, their squared eigenvalue masses may be subtracted from the Frobenius
square. Combining the remainder with the regularized logarithmic tail
inequality yields a valid finite-matrix upper bound.

The bound fails quantitatively at every physical endpoint. On the closed unit
disk it ranges from $5.15135$ to $169.27116$, so none of 32 cases passes the
predeclared bound $<1$. Power fits grow as $\sigma^{-1.025}$ and
$\sigma^{-1.033}$ on the two channels. The whole-matrix certificate is up to
$5.56\times10^3$ times larger than the resolved-shell bound.

This is a barrier for the raw Frobenius certificate, not for the determinant.
Nonnormal singular mass can make $\norm A_{\mathrm F}$ large without producing
comparably large eigenvalue products. A useful next step must isolate a
reducing complement or control a two-step trace-class object rather than
apply the full one-step Frobenius norm.
\end{abstract}

\section{From resolved roots to a whole operator}

The finite bound of RH-228 depends on the actual omitted roots
\citep{WangRH228}. For an unresolved finite matrix, one can seek an upper
bound on their squared moduli. The Frobenius norm is natural because it is
exactly computable from the sparse physical matrix.

Let the eigenvalues of $A\in\mathbb C^{N\times N}$, with algebraic
multiplicity, be partitioned into selected values $S$ and omitted values $T$.

\section{Schur--Frobenius control}

\begin{theorem}[Eigenvalue-square budget]\label{thm:schur}
For every finite matrix $A$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:schur}
 \sum_{j=1}^N|\lambda_j(A)|^2\le\norm A_{\mathrm F}^2.
\end{equation}
Consequently,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:tailmass}
 \sum_{\lambda\in T}|\lambda|^2
 \le H_T(A):=
 \norm A_{\mathrm F}^2-\sum_{\lambda\in S}|\lambda|^2.
\end{equation}
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Take a unitary Schur decomposition $A=URU^*$ with $R$ upper triangular and
diagonal entries $\lambda_j(A)$. Unitary invariance gives
\[
 \norm A_{\mathrm F}^2=\norm R_{\mathrm F}^2
 =\sum_j|\lambda_j(A)|^2+\sum_{i<j}|R_{ij}|^2,
\]
which proves \eqref{eq:schur}. Subtract the selected eigenvalue masses to get
\eqref{eq:tailmass}.
\end{proof}

For a normal matrix, the off-diagonal Schur term vanishes and the bound is
exact. For a strongly nonnormal matrix it can dominate.

\begin{corollary}[Whole-matrix regularized tail]\label{cor:det}
Suppose every omitted eigenvalue obeys $|\lambda|\le\rho_T$ and
$R\rho_T<1$. Then
\begin{equation}\label{eq:detbound}
 \sup_{|z|\le R}
 \left|\sum_{\lambda\in T}
 [\log(1-z\lambda)+z\lambda]\right|
 \le
 \frac{R^2H_T(A)}{2(1-R\rho_T)}.
\end{equation}
\end{corollary}
\begin{proof}
Combine Theorem~\ref{thm:schur} with the logarithmic tail theorem of RH-228.
\end{proof}

The corollary is rigorous when the selected eigenvalues and the tail radius
are exact. In the physical audit they come from a deterministic
floating-point spectral ordering, so the resulting number is a numerical
certificate candidate rather than an interval proof.

\section{Physical budget}

For each raw folded Markov matrix $M$, use the Hardy-scaled matrix
$A=M/0.85$. The selected set contains:
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=2em]
\item the scaled Perron resonance;
\item the scaled negative parity resonance;
\item the RH-222 shell-complete bulk cloud.
\end{enumerate}
The next complete shell supplies the numerical tail-radius upper candidate
\begin{equation}
 \rho_T=
 \min_{\lambda\in\Lambda_{\rm sel}}|\lambda|
 -g_{\rm shell}.
\end{equation}
The sparse Frobenius square is computed directly from all matrix entries.

The disk radius is frozen at $R=1$, and the gate is
\begin{equation}
 \frac{H_T(A)}{2(1-\rho_T)}<1.
\end{equation}
No endpoint passes.

The condition $R\rho_T<1$ is comfortably satisfied. The failure is entirely
in the squared-mass budget.

\section{Small-noise scaling}

For each channel, fit
\begin{equation}
 B_\sigma=C\sigma^{-\alpha}
\end{equation}
to the sixteen whole-matrix upper bounds in logarithmic least squares. The
descriptive exponents are
\[
 \alpha_L=1.02472,\qquad \alpha_R=1.03258.
\]
The fit is not an asymptotic theorem, but it identifies the scale of the
certificate failure: the bound worsens rather than stabilizes as noise
decreases.

The behavior is consistent with narrow stochastic rows. As $\sigma$ shrinks,
the transition matrix approaches a sparse near-deterministic transport;
many singular directions remain large even though most nonperipheral
eigenvalues may be small. The Frobenius norm records those directions.

\section{Why the negative result is limited}

Theorem~\ref{thm:schur} cannot distinguish eigenvalue mass from the strictly
upper triangular part of the Schur form. Therefore failure of
\eqref{eq:detbound} does not imply divergence of
\[
 \sum_{\lambda\in T}|\lambda|^2.
\]
It only says that the raw Frobenius norm is too coarse to prove a useful
bound.

Several stronger routes remain:
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=2em]
\item construct a Riesz or reducing projection for the moving outer cloud and
measure the actual complementary block;
\item remove dominant singular directions before applying an ideal norm;
\item square the complement, seeking a trace-class bound for a two-step
Fredholm determinant;
\item use power traces and phase cancellation instead of singular values.
\end{enumerate}

These possibilities are compatible with the abstract relative determinant
route in RH-80 \citep{WangRH80} and with trace-ideal determinant theory
\citep{Simon2005}. None is constructed here.

\section{Route consequence}

The resolved shell tail is small, while the whole-matrix Frobenius tail is
large. This sharply locates the next wall:
\[
 \text{explicit outer roots}
 \quad\longleftrightarrow\quad
 \text{unresolved nonnormal complement}.
\]
Before attacking that complement, RH-230 tests whether the selected
regularized factors at least agree between the two physical channels and
whether their adjacent-scale differences contract.

No determinant divergence, self-adjoint realization, or arithmetic
identification is proved. Gate A remains open.
