\section{Scale and exponent ledger}
\label{sec:exponent-ledger}

The current physical scales are
\[
 Q_X=X^{267/400+o(1)},\qquad
 J_X=X^{133/400+o(1)},\qquad
 q_X\asymp Q_X.
\]
The endpoint-scale branch return is
\[
 Q_X^2=X^{534/400+o(1)}.
\]

\subsection{What TPC-101 saves on a long fiber}

The previous worst-eigenvalue factor was
\[
 q_X^{1/2}
 =
 X^{267/800+o(1)}
\]
apart from logarithms.  On a resolved branch of length
\(N\ge J_X\), TPC-101 gives
\[
 g_{q_X}(H_{q_X}(N))
 \le
 \sqrt{\frac{2q_X}{N}}
 \le
 X^{67/400+o(1)}.
\]
The difference is
\[
 \frac{267}{800}-\frac{67}{400}
 =
 \frac{133}{800}.
\]
Thus the spectral return gains a factor
\[
 J_X^{1/2}=X^{133/800+o(1)}
\]
on that length block.

\begin{remark}[Support qualification]
This gain is rigorous \emph{on branches known to satisfy}
\(N\ge J_X\).  The full literal packet also contains short fibers.
No global \(J_X^{1/2}\) saving may be entered until their weighted
mass is separately returned.
\end{remark}

\subsection{No new physical loss}

The function \(g_q(H)\) is part of an exact finite-group norm bound.
It is not a new smoothing parameter and spends no physical exponent
by itself.  The obligations
\[
 \mathfrak P_X,\qquad W_X,\qquad\mathfrak X_X
\]
must be estimated in the already normalized physical frame.
Artificially enlarging the support to make a large-sieve theorem
applicable would create a new loss, which must be entered explicitly
in \(\Lambda_{\mathrm{phys}}\).

\subsection{Endpoint stop}

The final rule remains
\[
 \Lambda_{\mathrm{phys}}<\frac1{400}.
\]
A derivation reaching equality does not close the endpoint.
Subpower factors, logarithms, and fixed comparison constants are
absorbed in \(X^{o(1)}\); a fixed positive power is not.
