\subsection{Six-item claim audit}

Before any new result is promoted, check:
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=2em]
\item Are the literal physical coefficients present?
\item Is one fixed nonzero \(h_0\) present throughout?
\item Is the atomic quantity normalized in the physical frame?
\item Is the representation canonical or at least losslessly
  invertible?
\item Is the estimate on the actual active support?
\item Does the final physical ledger remain strictly below
  \(1/400\)?
\end{enumerate}
Failure of any item prevents a claim of final proof progress.

\subsection{What a negative theorem can publish}

A sharp family with
\(B_X\mathfrak P_X\) or \(B_X\mathfrak X_X\) polynomially above the
target would prove that the present three-ledger,
principal-plus-absolute-remainder implementation cannot close.
It would not prove the positive carrier itself large or exclude a
sharper filter-sensitive estimate.  A polynomial atom-cap failure
would identify the exact coefficient responsible and stop only the
atom-cap transfer.  A projection counterexample would show
why a compressed model loses the physical zero mode.  Each is a
valid mathematical conclusion when stated at its actual level.

\begin{remark}[No inference to RH or prime pairs]
Spectral terminology here concerns finite incidence operators and
literal dynamical bookkeeping.  It does not identify Riemann zeros
with eigenvalues, prove the Riemann hypothesis, or imply a
prime-pair asymptotic.
\end{remark}
