\title{Orientation-Separating Marked Cycle Traces\\
\large Why the Geometric Determinant Needs a Directed Rank-One Datum}
\author{Liang Wang}
\date{July 2026}

\begin{document}
\maketitle

\begin{abstract}
The double-cycle model of RH-177 realizes the RH-80 geometric cloud factor
exactly, but a scalar determinant cannot determine temporal orientation.
For every length $L$, the forward and reverse cycles $C_L$ and $C_L^{-1}$
have the same determinant and the same ordinary power traces.  Hence the
three doubled completions ``forward--forward'', ``forward--reverse'', and
``reverse--reverse'' are spectrally indistinguishable.

We add one rank-one edge marker.  Let
$J=|e_0\rangle\langle e_1|$, let
$Q=I-|\mathbf1\rangle\langle\mathbf1|$, and compress to the zero-mean cycle
space.  For $L>2$,
\[
 \Tr(QJQ C_L)=1-\frac1L,
 \qquad
 \Tr(QJQ C_L^{-1})=-\frac1L.
\]
The orientation gap is exactly one.  Thus the enhanced finite datum
\[
 \bigl(\det(I-zC_L^\circ),
 \{\Tr(J^\circ(C_L^\circ)^m)\}_{m\ge1}\bigr)
\]
retains both the cloud divisor and the time arrow.

The marker is stable.  If $\norm{A-B}\le\varepsilon$ and
$\norm A,\norm B\le R$, then for trace-class $J$,
\[
 |\Tr J(A^m-B^m)|
 \le \norm J_1\,mR^{m-1}\varepsilon.
\]
In particular, the one-step orientation remains separated under two
perturbations whenever their total marked error is less than one.  A
120-case complex perturbation audit records zero bound failures; the exact
orientation gap is one for all ten tested lengths.

This closes an algebraic directed-marker leaf and explains why RH-161 kept
marked traces as data independent of the determinant.  It does not establish
a physical marker, common-space marked-trace convergence, or a directed limit
for the noisy transfer dynamics.
\end{abstract}

\section{The scalar blindness problem}

RH-177 constructs the finite normal cloud
\[
 K_N=\lambda^{-1}(C_{N+1}^\circ\oplus C_{N+1}^\circ)
\]
with determinant $\Pi_N(w/\lambda)^2$ \citep{WangRH177}.  The construction
contains a hidden choice: each cycle can run forward or backward.  Spectral
data alone cannot recover that choice because inversion merely permutes the
roots of unity.

This is the finite-cycle version of a general obstruction in noncommutative
dynamics.  Products $AB$ and $BA$ have the same nonzero spectrum, while
directed words can differ.  RH-8 and RH-161 therefore retained marked traces
as an independent output type \citep{WangRH8,WangRH161}.  The present model
allows the need for that enhancement to be proved in one line and repaired by
one rank-one marker.

\section{Forward and reverse cycles are spectrally identical}

Let $L>2$ and define
\[
 C_Le_j=e_{j+1\bmod L}.
\]
Its inverse is the reverse cycle.  Both have eigenvalue multiset
$\{e^{2\pi ik/L}:0\le k<L\}$.

\begin{theorem}[Determinant and ordinary-trace blindness]\label{thm:blind}
For every $z\in\mathbb C$ and every integer $m\ge1$,
\begin{align}
 \det(I-zC_L)&=\det(I-zC_L^{-1})=1-z^L,
 \label{eq:det-blind}\\
 \Tr C_L^m&=\Tr C_L^{-m}
 =\begin{cases}L,&L\mid m,\\0,&L\nmid m.
 \end{cases}
 \label{eq:trace-blind}
\end{align}
The same equalities hold after restriction to the zero-mean space.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Inversion permutes the $L$th roots of unity, proving
\eqref{eq:det-blind}.  A cyclic permutation has a fixed basis vector under
the $m$th power exactly when $L\mid m$; in that case every basis vector is
fixed.  Removing the common stationary eigenvalue one subtracts one from both
power traces and divides both determinants by $1-z$.
\end{proof}

Thus even the complete sequence of ordinary traces contains no orientation
information.  An additional noncentral observable is necessary.

\section{A rank-one edge marker}

Define
\begin{equation}\label{eq:J}
 J=|e_0\rangle\langle e_1|.
\end{equation}
For every matrix $X$,
\[
 \Tr(JX)=\langle e_1,Xe_0\rangle.
\]
The marker therefore asks whether one application of the dynamics transports
the vertex $0$ to the vertex $1$.

\begin{theorem}[Exact orientation separation]\label{thm:marker}
For $L>2$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:full-marker}
 \Tr(JC_L)=1,
 \qquad
 \Tr(JC_L^{-1})=0.
\end{equation}
Let $P=L^{-1}|\mathbf1\rangle\langle\mathbf1|$ be the stationary projection,
$Q=I-P$, and $J^\circ=QJQ$.  Then on the zero-mean cycle,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:reduced-marker}
 \Tr(J^\circ C_L^\circ)=1-\frac1L,
 \qquad
 \Tr(J^\circ(C_L^\circ)^{-1})=-\frac1L.
\end{equation}
In both the full and reduced models, the forward--reverse gap equals one.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Since $C_Le_0=e_1$ and $C_L^{-1}e_0=e_{L-1}\perp e_1$, the full identities
follow.  Because $Q$ commutes with the cycle and $PC_L=C_LP=P$,
\[
 \Tr(QJQ C_L)=\Tr(J(C_L-P))
 =\Tr(JC_L)-\Tr(JP).
\]
Now $\Tr(JP)=\langle e_1,Pe_0\rangle=1/L$.  Apply the same calculation to
$C_L^{-1}$.
\end{proof}

The compression correction $-1/L$ is the marker analogue of the $-1$
background in the reduced power-trace ledger.  It affects both orientations
equally and therefore preserves the unit gap.

\section{Higher marked words}

For $m\ge1$,
\[
 \Tr(JC_L^m)=
 \begin{cases}
 1,&m\equiv1\pmod L,\\
 0,&\text{otherwise},
 \end{cases}
\]
whereas
\[
 \Tr(JC_L^{-m})=
 \begin{cases}
 1,&m\equiv-1\pmod L,\\
 0,&\text{otherwise}.
 \end{cases}
\]
Thus the entire marked sequence records the orientation and cycle length.
One step already separates direction; the longer sequence supplies
redundancy and can diagnose a noisy or perturbed cycle.

\section{Reconstruction and marker minimality}

The determinant and one marked trace form a complete descriptor of an exact
oriented single cycle within this model class.

\begin{proposition}[Reconstruction of length and orientation]
\label{prop:reconstruct}
Suppose $A$ is known a priori to be either $C_L^\circ$ or
$(C_L^\circ)^{-1}$ for some $L>2$.  Then:
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=2em]
\item the degree of $\det(I-zA)=\Pi_{L-1}(z)$ determines $L$;
\item the sign relative to $-1/L$ of $\Tr(J^\circ A)$ determines the
orientation.
\end{enumerate}
Specifically, the marked value is $1-1/L$ in the forward case and $-1/L$ in
the reverse case.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
The reduced dimension and determinant degree are $L-1$.  Once $L$ is known,
Theorem~\ref{thm:marker} gives two distinct marked values separated by one.
\end{proof}

No scalar augmentation can use rank zero, while Theorem~\ref{thm:marker}
shows rank one suffices.  In that elementary sense the edge marker is
minimal.  The claim is model-relative: a physical system may require a more
complicated state--observable marker to remain target independent.

For a doubled cloud, retain ordered channel markers $J_1,J_2$.  Put
$a=1-1/L$ and $b=-1/L$.  The four orientation choices have signatures
\begin{center}
\begin{tabular}{lcc}
\toprule
orientation & first mark & second mark\\
\midrule
forward--forward & $a$ & $a$\\
forward--reverse & $a$ & $b$\\
reverse--forward & $b$ & $a$\\
reverse--reverse & $b$ & $b$\\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{center}
If only the sum $J_1+J_2$ is observed, the two mixed orientations coincide.
Thus an unordered scalar marker distinguishes three classes, whereas an
ordered marker pair distinguishes all four.  This is directly relevant when
the two cloud copies are assigned to left/right or parity channels.

For a double cycle, choose markers $J_1,J_2$ on the two summands.  The scalar
determinant is the same for all orientation pairs, while
\[
 \bigl(\Tr(J_1K),\Tr(J_2K)\bigr)
\]
distinguishes forward--forward, forward--reverse, and reverse--reverse after
the common scale $\lambda^{-1}$ is removed.

\section{Perturbative stability}

\begin{theorem}[Marked-power Lipschitz bound]\label{thm:stability}
Let $A,B$ be bounded operators with $\norm A,\norm B\le R$, let
$J\in\mathcal S_1$, and let $m\ge1$.  Then
\begin{equation}\label{eq:stability}
 |\Tr J(A^m-B^m)|
 \le \norm J_1\,mR^{m-1}\norm{A-B}.
\end{equation}
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Use the telescoping identity
\[
 A^m-B^m=\sum_{k=0}^{m-1}A^{m-1-k}(A-B)B^k.
\]
Every summand has operator norm at most
$R^{m-1}\norm{A-B}$.  The trace duality inequality
$|\Tr(JX)|\le\norm J_1\norm X$ and the triangle inequality give
\eqref{eq:stability} \citep{Simon2005}.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[Robust one-step orientation]
Let $A_+$ and $A_-$ approximate $C_L$ and $C_L^{-1}$ with errors
$\varepsilon_+$ and $\varepsilon_-$.  For the reduced marker,
\[
 |\Tr(J^\circ A_+)-\Tr(J^\circ A_-)|
 \ge1-\norm{J^\circ}_1(\varepsilon_++\varepsilon_-).
\]
Hence the marked traces remain distinct whenever the rightmost error term is
less than one.
\end{corollary}

This criterion is target independent: the marker is specified by the cycle
edge, not fitted to the perturbed eigenvectors.  A physical application must
still identify what state--observable pair corresponds to that edge.

\section{Enhanced finite determinant datum}

The natural finite cyclic output is not the scalar determinant alone but
\begin{equation}\label{eq:enhanced}
 \mathfrak D_L(A,J)=
 \left(
 \det(I-zA),
 \{\Tr(JA^m)\}_{m\in\mathcal M}
 \right),
\end{equation}
where $\mathcal M$ is a fixed set of directed word lengths.  The first
component determines the spectral divisor.  The second records temporal
orientation.  They are logically independent: Theorem~\ref{thm:blind}
shows that the first cannot reconstruct the second.

For the prime-dynamics route, the finite enhanced datum should eventually be
transported through the Riesz projection and then shown to converge in common
coordinates.  RH-161 names this downstream obligation $T$; the present paper
only proves that the finite model has a minimal and stable directional
observable.

\section{Audit}

The exact audit uses lengths
$3,4,5,7,11,16,24,32,48,64$.  In every case the reduced forward and reverse
one-step marked traces have gap one to floating precision, while ordinary
traces agree.  The minimum recorded gap is
$0.9999999999999998$.

For each length, twelve complex perturbations with operator norms
$0.01,0.02,0.03$ are applied at powers one through five.  The exact observed
marked error is compared with Theorem~\ref{thm:stability}.  All 120 cases
satisfy the bound, with zero failures.  The audit validates arithmetic and
does not certify a physical noisy cycle.

\section{Boundary and next interface}

RH-178 proves an algebraic directed-marker leaf:
\[
 \text{cycle determinant} + \text{rank-one marked traces}
 \quad\text{retains spectrum and orientation}.
\]
It does not prove that the physical transfer dynamics carries this marker,
that the marker is trace class uniformly across scales, or that marked words
converge after Riesz transport.  Those remain parts of the physical $T$ leaf.

The next paper turns to the integer calibration problem: how should the cycle
length $L$ relate to the half-log reset rank and the observed RH-15 cloud
degree?  No Gate-A closure, self-adjoint completion, zeta identity, or
Riemann-hypothesis conclusion is made.
