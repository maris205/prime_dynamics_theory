\begin{abstract}
TPC-216 proved a complete-period direct-sum envelope for the literal V46
reciprocal emitter but left the physical interval open.  This paper attaches
that result to the interval $I_x=(x/2,x]\cap\mathbb Z$.  The common-source
kernel is first regrouped exactly by reduced rational frequencies: divisor rows
with denominator $d$ contribute to the same frequency $a/h$ through the single
coefficient $C_h=\sum_{h\mid d}\mu(d)\log(d)/d$.  Distinct reduced fractions with
denominators at most $U$ have circular spacing at least $U^{-2}$, so the
additive large sieve gives a finite-window factor $N+U^2$, where
$N=|I_x|$.  TPC-215 and TPC-216 then yield
\[
 N^{-1}\sum_{n\in I_x}|\Kphys(n)|^2
 \ll_{\psi} x^{11/32}(\log x)^5.
\]
The $U^2$ term is lower order since $U^2/x=x^{-67/200}$, giving an
unnormalized exponent $43/32+o(1)$.  A finite aligned-shell fixture has exact
one-point coherent-to-diagonal ratio $2$, so the attachment cannot be replaced
by free finite-window orthogonality.  This is a structural result only: prime-
shell reassembly, arithmetic $L^2$, the strict $1/400$ budget, and the
twin-prime endpoint remain open.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}

The transition-native twin-prime route has reached a useful intermediate
object.  Its physical reciprocal emitter is coupled across a squarefree divisor
band, so a divisor-by-divisor orthogonal decomposition is not literal.  TPC-213
constructed the common physical source and its cross-divisor Gram
\cite{WangV46,WangTPC214}.  TPC-214 then showed that on a complete period the
correct coordinates are reduced rational frequencies.  If $h$ is the reduced
denominator, all rows with $h\mid d$ share one emitter row $B_h$ and their
coefficients add to
\[
 C_h=\sum_{\substack{d\in\Dx\\h\mid d}} c_d,
 \qquad c_d=\frac{\mu(d)\log d}{d}.
\]

TPC-215 bounded the resulting cluster coefficient energy by an
$O((\log x)^2)$ multiple of the divisor direct-sum energy, and TPC-216 bounded
that direct-sum energy by
\[
 L^{-1}\Edir\ll_{\psi}x^{11/32}(\log x)^3
 \cite{WangTPC215,WangTPC216}.
\]
Those results still used a complete common period.  The next question is
whether the same object can be evaluated on the actual interval $I_x$ without
silently restoring orthogonality.

The answer is a clean structural attachment.  Reduced fractions with
denominator at most $U$ form a $U^{-2}$-separated set modulo one.  The additive
large sieve therefore controls the physical Gram by $(N+U^2)$ times the
reduced-frequency coefficient energy.  Since $U^2=o(x)$, the power exponent is
unchanged.  The result does not exploit signs of $\mu$, cancellation among
primes, or the four-packet polarization.  It establishes the finite-window
interface and leaves the arithmetic bridge visible.

Our contributions are:
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=2em]
  \item an exact finite-window regrouping of the literal common-source kernel;
  \item a source-locked large-sieve attachment with normalized exponent
        $11/32$ and logarithmic cost $(\log x)^5$;
  \item an independent finite certificate and a one-point aligned-shell
        adversary showing why free finite-window orthogonality is invalid.
\end{enumerate}

\section{Literal source and inherited complete-period bounds}

We retain the V46 parameters
\begin{equation}
 H=x^{21/32},\qquad Q=x^{1/3},\qquad
 Y_0=\frac{H}{4Q},\qquad U=x^{133/400},
 \label{eq:scales}
\end{equation}
and the prime shell and squarefree band
\begin{equation}
 \Qx=\{q\text{ prime}:Q<q\le2Q\},\qquad
 \Dx=\{d\in\ZZ:Y_0<d\le U,\ \mu(d)^2=1\}.
 \label{eq:band}
\end{equation}
For a bounded profile $\psi$, define
\begin{equation}
 B_d(r)=\sum_{q\in\Qx}\sum_{0<|m|\le\lfloor dq/H\rfloor}
 \psi\!\left(\frac{Hm}{dq}\right)
 \one_{m\overline q\equiv r\pmod d}.
 \label{eq:emitter}
\end{equation}
For sufficiently large $x$,
\begin{equation}
 \frac{H}{4Q}=\frac14x^{31/96}\to\infty,
 \qquad \frac{U}{Q}=x^{-1/1200}\to0,
 \label{eq:source}
\end{equation}
so $4Q<H$ and $U<Q$.

The complete-period direct energy from TPC-216 is
\begin{equation}
 \Edir=L\sum_{d\in\Dx}|c_d|^2\sum_{r\bmod d}|B_d(r)|^2,
 \qquad c_d=\frac{\mu(d)\log d}{d},
 \label{eq:direct}
\end{equation}
with
\begin{equation}
 L^{-1}\Edir\ll_{\psi}\frac{Q^3}{H}(\log U)^3
 =x^{11/32}(\log x)^3.
 \label{eq:tpc216}
\end{equation}
TPC-215 supplies the complete-period coefficient comparison
\begin{equation}
 \sum_{h}\sum_{\substack{a\bmod h\\(a,h)=1}}
 |C_hB_h(a)|^2
 \le A_x\frac{\Edir}{L},
 \qquad A_x=O((\log x)^2),
 \label{eq:tpc215}
\end{equation}
where
\begin{equation}
 C_h=\sum_{\substack{d\in\Dx\\h\mid d}}c_d.
 \label{eq:Ch}
\end{equation}
The sum includes only nonzero reduced-frequency rows; the additive zero row
vanishes because $q<H$.

\section{Exact reduced-frequency representation}

\begin{lemma}[divisor dilation]
\label{lem:dilation}
If $h\mid d$, $d=kh$, and $(a,h)=1$, then
\[
 B_d(ka)=B_h(a).
\]
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
The congruence in \eqref{eq:emitter} becomes
\[
 m\overline q\equiv ka\pmod{kh}.
\]
It forces $m=kn$ because $q$ is a unit modulo $kh$.  The cutoff becomes
$|n|\le\lfloor hq/H\rfloor$, and the profile argument satisfies
$Hm/(dq)=Hn/(hq)$.  The summands are therefore identical.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[exact reduced-frequency regrouping]
\label{thm:regroup}
Define the common-source kernel
\begin{equation}
 \Kphys(n)=\sum_{d\in\Dx}c_d\sum_{r\bmod d}B_d(r)\ee(nr/d).
 \label{eq:kernel}
\end{equation}
Then, for every integer $n$,
\begin{equation}
 \Kphys(n)=
 \sum_{h\le U}\sum_{\substack{a\bmod h\\(a,h)=1}}
 C_hB_h(a)\ee(na/h).
 \label{eq:regroup}
\end{equation}
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
For a residue $r\bmod d$, let $g=(r,d)$ and put $h=d/g$, $a=r/g$.
Then $r/d=a/h$ in lowest terms.  If $r=0$, use $h=1$ and $a=0$.
For nonzero $r$, the reduced numerator is coprime to $h$.  The map from
residues $r\bmod d$ to their reduced fractions partitions every row.  Lemma
\ref{lem:dilation} identifies the row value in each divisor $d$ with $B_h(a)$,
so the coefficients of the same reduced frequency add to $C_h$.  The zero row
is absent because $\lfloor q/H\rfloor=0$ for $q<H$.
\end{proof}

\section{Finite-window large-sieve theorem}

We use the standard additive large-sieve inequality in the following form
\cite{MontgomeryVaughan1973}.  If $\alpha_j$ are separated modulo one by
\(\delta>0\), then for every consecutive interval $I$ of $N$ integers,
\begin{equation}
 \sum_{n\in I}\left|\sum_j z_j\ee(n\alpha_j)\right|^2
 \le (N-1+\delta^{-1})\sum_j|z_j|^2.
 \label{eq:large-sieve}
\end{equation}
The inequality follows from the dual Hilbert-inequality proof of the additive
large sieve; no arithmetic property of the coefficients is required here.

\begin{lemma}[Farey spacing]
\label{lem:spacing}
Distinct reduced fractions $a/h$ and $a'/h'$ with $h,h'\le U$ satisfy
\[
 \dist(a/h,a'/h')\ge U^{-2},
\]
where the distance is taken modulo one.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
The difference, or its complement modulo one, is a nonzero rational with
denominator dividing $hh'$.  Its numerator is a nonzero integer, so its
absolute value is at least $(hh')^{-1}\ge U^{-2}$.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[finite-window attachment]
\label{thm:finite}
Let $I_x=(x/2,x]\cap\ZZ$ and $N=|I_x|$.  Then
\begin{equation}
 \sum_{n\in I_x}|\Kphys(n)|^2
 \ll_{\psi}(N+U^2)x^{11/32}(\log x)^5.
 \label{eq:finite-bound}
\end{equation}
In particular,
\begin{equation}
 \frac1N\sum_{n\in I_x}|\Kphys(n)|^2
 \ll_{\psi}x^{11/32}(\log x)^5,
 \label{eq:normalized-bound}
\end{equation}
and the unnormalized exponent is $43/32+o(1)$.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Insert the exact representation \eqref{eq:regroup} into \eqref{eq:large-sieve}.
Lemma~\ref{lem:spacing} permits $\delta=U^{-2}$, giving
\begin{align}
 \sum_{n\in I_x}|\Kphys(n)|^2
 &\le (N+U^2)
 \sum_h\sum_{(a,h)=1}|C_hB_h(a)|^2\\
 &\le (N+U^2)A_x\frac{\Edir}{L}.
 \label{eq:chain}
\end{align}
Use $A_x=O((\log x)^2)$ from \eqref{eq:tpc215} and
\eqref{eq:tpc216} to obtain \eqref{eq:finite-bound}.  Finally,
\(U^2/x=x^{-67/200}\) and $N\asymp x$, so division by $N$ gives
\eqref{eq:normalized-bound}.  The unnormalized power is
\(1+11/32=43/32\).
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}[scope]
The proof controls the finite-window off-frequency Gram by spacing.  It does
not use cancellation in $\mu(d)$, cancellation among primes, or the signed
four-packet reassembly.  The result is consequently structural and does not
pay the arithmetic Gate-B ledger.
\end{remark}

\section{Finite certificates and the crowding obstruction}

The reproducible finite fixture uses $Q=\{11,13,17\}$, $H=40$, the full
squarefree family in $(2,35]$, and $\psi(t)=(1+t^2)^{-2}$.  It contains 14
active divisors and 16 reduced denominators.  The direct divisor expansion and
the reduced-frequency expansion agree pointwise to floating-point rounding in
three translated windows of lengths 1, 17, and 60.  The finite checks use exact
rational row values before the final logarithmic evaluation.

\[

 \quad \psi(t)=(1+t^2)^{-2}.
\]
Every row has cutoff one and support $\{1,4\}\pmod 5$.  At a one-point window,
the coherent energy is exactly twice the diagonal row-energy sum.  Thus the
finite-window Gram is not freely diagonal even in this small admissible model.
The statement is scoped: it does not contradict the long-interval large sieve
and is not an asymptotic lower bound for the V46 prime shell.

\section{Conclusion}

The common-source V46 kernel admits an exact reduced-frequency representation
on every integer, not only on a complete period.  Farey spacing and the
additive large sieve then attach the TPC-216 direct-energy envelope to the
physical interval with normalized exponent $11/32$ and five logarithmic
factors.  The $U^2$ spacing term is lower order at the frozen source scales.
The one-point aligned-shell fixture shows why this attachment must use a
length-dependent large-sieve argument rather than free orthogonality.  The
remaining work is arithmetic: reassemble the prime shell and four-packet
signed scalar without losing the literal Gate-B interface.
