\begin{abstract}
RH-221 ends with a finite gauge-complete quartet and an exact requirement:
the next determinant candidate must add genuinely new roots. This review
collects nine subsequent layers.

RH-222--RH-223 construct shell-complete conjugate resonance clouds whose ranks
grow from four to $34$--$35$ across sixteen scales and two channels. Naive
fixed-cardinality prefixes split a conjugate pair at 23 of 32 endpoints,
whereas minimal shell completion is stable under four candidate margins.

RH-224 proves that one global center and RMS radius make every empirical cloud
measure uniformly tight. RH-225 then proves the complementary obstruction:
a tight probability cloud with rank tending to infinity cannot itself be a
locally finite unweighted zero divisor. The correct Fredholm zeros are
reciprocal resonances. RH-226 restores that exact dictionary and reconnects
the finite atlas to RH-7's rigorous fixed-positive-noise
Hilbert--Schmidt determinant.

The small-noise problem remains singular. Reciprocal zero counts fail the
frozen local-count gate. Twelve to fourteen resolved omitted roots per
endpoint have a successful unit-disk logarithmic tail bound, but the only
whole-matrix Frobenius certificate grows from $5.15$ to $169.27$, approximately
as $\sigma^{-1.03}$. All sixteen dual-channel determinant comparisons pass a
$0.02$ gate, while adjacent-scale differences do not contract.

The new coordinate is
\[
\texttt{rank\_growing\_reciprocal\_cloud\_open\_uniform\_complement\_ideal\_limit}.
\]
The aggregate ledger contains 9,870 finite cases and zero identity failures.
Gate A remains open; Gates B--E, Hilbert--Polya, zeta-divisor equality, and RH
are untouched.
\end{abstract}

 \texttt{finite\_gauge\_complete\_shape\_flow
 \_open\_rank\_growing\_divisor}.
\end{equation}

The recommended next route has five logically distinct tasks:
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=2em]
\item build a rank-growing physical root atlas;
\item apply one global gauge and prove empirical tightness;
\item determine the correct divisor variable;
\item control omitted regularized-product factors;
\item obtain a locally uniform determinant family.
\end{enumerate}

RH-222--RH-230 complete the first three at the finite or exact level, partially
complete the fourth, and expose the wall in the fifth.

\section{RH-222--RH-223: a symmetry-correct rank-growing atlas}

At each frozen scale, RH-222 resolves the folded Gaussian fine operator and
its Haar compression, removes Perron and negative parity resonances, and
Hardy-scales the bulk by $0.85$ \citep{WangRH222}. The target ladder is
\[
 k_\ell=4+2\ell,\qquad 0\le\ell\le15.
\]
Real eigenvalues form singleton shells and nonreal eigenvalues form conjugate
pair shells. Whole shells are included until the target is reached.

Actual ranks increase strictly on both channels. They range from four to
$35$ on the left and four to $34$ on the right. The selected conjugacy error
is zero at archived precision, overshoot is at most one, and the smallest
resolved post-cloud gap is $7.3991\times10^{-5}$.

RH-223 proves minimality of this completion \citep{WangRH223}. A naive exact
target prefix splits a pair in 23 of 32 cases. Exactly those 23 cases require
one-root overshoot. Prefix margins $4,8,12,14$ all recover the reference
multiset with zero matching error.

The selection is canonical only relative to the resolved radial ordering. No
all-level rank law or interval-certified Riesz separation is obtained.

\section{RH-224: probability compactness}

For a cloud $\Lambda$ of rank $n$, define
\[
 m_\Lambda=\frac1n\sum_{\lambda\in\Lambda}\lambda,\qquad
 s_\Lambda^2=\frac1n\sum_{\lambda\in\Lambda}
 |\lambda-m_\Lambda|^2
\]
and
\[
 \mu_\Lambda=\frac1n\sum_{\lambda\in\Lambda}
 \delta_{(\lambda-m_\Lambda)/s_\Lambda}.
\]
Then
\begin{equation}\label{eq:moments}
 \int z\,d\mu_\Lambda=0,\qquad
 \int|z|^2\,d\mu_\Lambda=1.
\end{equation}
Markov's inequality gives
\begin{equation}\label{eq:tight}
 \mu_\Lambda(|z|>R)\le R^{-2}.
\end{equation}
Therefore every globally normalized cloud family is uniformly tight and has
weakly convergent subsequences by Prokhorov compactness
\citep{WangRH224,Billingsley1999}.

The numerical atlas has maximum normalized modulus $1.83188$ and
second-moment error below $6.7\times10^{-16}$. Tightness is exact, while
uniqueness of the weak limit remains open.

\section{RH-225: why tight roots cannot be direct zeros}

Let
\[
 \nu_n=\sum_{\lambda\in\Lambda_n}\delta_\lambda,\qquad
 \mu_n=\frac1{d_n}\nu_n,\qquad d_n\to\infty.
\]
Uniform tightness puts at least half of each probability measure in one
compact set $K$. Hence
\[
 \nu_n(K)\ge d_n/2\longrightarrow\infty.
\]
The integer divisors cannot converge vaguely to a locally finite divisor.
They also cannot be complete zero divisors of a holomorphic family converging
locally uniformly to a nonzero limit \citep{WangRH225,Conway1978}.

This exact result rules out globally normalized resonances as determinant
zeros. In the finite atlas the direct compact counts grow by 31 and 30 roots.
All raw selected resonances also lie in the unit disk, so the same finite
failure is visible before global normalization.

The result does not apply after inversion. If compact-operator resonances
$\lambda_j$ tend to zero, reciprocal points $1/\lambda_j$ escape to infinity.

\section{RH-226: the Fredholm variable}

For
\[
 p_\Lambda(w)=\prod_{\lambda\in\Lambda}(w-\lambda),
\]
RH-226 restores the identity
\begin{equation}\label{eq:reciprocal}
 \prod_{\lambda\in\Lambda}(1-z\lambda)
 =z^{|\Lambda|}p_\Lambda(1/z).
\end{equation}
The second-regularized factor
\begin{equation}\label{eq:det2}
 P_{\Lambda,2}(z)=
 \prod_{\lambda\in\Lambda}(1-z\lambda)e^{z\lambda}
\end{equation}
has the same zeros, namely $z=1/\lambda$
\citep{WangRH226,Simon2005}.

The physical reciprocal moduli range from $1.15129$ to $13.54281$.
Equation \eqref{eq:reciprocal} has maximum grid error
$9.16\times10^{-15}$.

This does not construct a new fixed-noise determinant. RH-7 already proves
that the centered Gaussian transfer operator is Hilbert--Schmidt and that its
$\det_2$ is entire with reciprocal resonance zeros \citep{WangRH7}. The new
question is uniformity as noise decreases.

\section{RH-227: local zero counts do not stabilize yet}

If $f_\sigma\to f\not\equiv0$ locally uniformly, Rouche's theorem forces
eventual zero-count stability on every contour avoiding zeros of $f$.
RH-227 tests the selected reciprocal clouds on
\[
 R=1.2,\;1.5,\;2,\;3,\;5
\]
\citep{WangRH227}.

Only one of ten side-radius sequences is constant over the last four scales.
The largest first-to-last count growth is 18, and the minimum finite contour
clearance is $1.3338\times10^{-4}$. Radius-two counts grow from two to ten on
both channels.

Finite nonstability does not disprove eventual stabilization. It shows that a
raw small-noise limit is not supported by the current range and that
near-unit factors cannot be ignored.

\section{RH-228: resolved shells are controllable}

For $|w|<1$,
\[
 |\log(1-w)+w|
 \le\frac{|w|^2}{2(1-|w|)}.
\]
If $T$ is an omitted resonance multiset and
$q=R\max_{\lambda\in T}|\lambda|<1$, then
\begin{equation}\label{eq:tail}
 \sup_{|z|\le R}
 \left|\log P_{T,2}(z)\right|
 \le\frac{R^2}{2(1-q)}
 \sum_{\lambda\in T}|\lambda|^2.
\end{equation}

Every endpoint has 12--14 complete resolved roots beyond its selected cloud.
On $|z|\le1$, the worst $q$ is $0.29818$, the worst bound is $0.16804$, and
the worst observed grid tail is $0.07532$ \citep{WangRH228}. This is a genuine
positive omitted-factor result, but only inside the resolved candidate
window.

\section{RH-229: the full complement wall}

Schur triangularization gives
\[
 \sum_j|\lambda_j(A)|^2\le\|A\|_{\mathrm F}^2.
\]
After subtracting selected Perron, parity, and cloud eigenvalue masses, the
remainder bounds the squared mass of all omitted finite-matrix eigenvalues.
Combining it with \eqref{eq:tail} produces a whole-matrix certificate.

The certificate fails $0/32$ unit-disk gates. It ranges from
\[
 5.15135\quad\text{to}\quad169.27116
\]
and grows descriptively as $\sigma^{-1.025}$ and $\sigma^{-1.033}$
\citep{WangRH229}. It can exceed the resolved-shell bound by a factor
$5563.49$.

This is a certificate obstruction, not a determinant obstruction. The
Frobenius norm sees nonnormal singular mass represented by off-diagonal Schur
entries. A reducing complement or two-step trace-class estimate may be much
smaller.

\section{RH-230: coherent channels, noncontracting scales}

Selected factor logarithms are compared on 256 points in $|z|\le1$:
\[
 L_\Lambda(z)=
 \sum_{\lambda\in\Lambda}
 [\log(1-z\lambda)+z\lambda].
\]
All sixteen same-scale channel differences are below $0.02$, with maximum
$0.017855$. Thirty adjacent-scale differences range from $0.042131$ to
$0.183660$, and neither channel decreases strictly over the final four
transitions \citep{WangRH230}.

The determinant representation therefore preserves finite dual-channel
coherence but supplies no Cauchy evidence across scale. This mixed result
supports one shared renormalization, not convergence of the selected factor
alone.

\section{Revised Gate-A route}

The coordinate after this batch is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:coordinate}
 \boxed{\begin{gathered}
 \texttt{rank\_growing\_reciprocal\_cloud}\\
 \texttt{\_open\_uniform\_complement\_ideal\_limit}
 \end{gathered}}.
\end{equation}

The next target is not another empirical normalization. It is a physical
moving-cloud relative determinant:
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=2em]
\item construct a Riesz or reducing projection for the near-unit cloud;
\item factor its finite $\det_2$ contribution without losing conjugate and
dual-channel coherence;
\item bound the complementary block uniformly in Hilbert--Schmidt norm, or
bound its square uniformly in trace norm, on a nonshrinking $z$-disk;
\item prove local reciprocal-zero count stabilization for the complement;
\item take a locally uniform relative-determinant subsequential limit and
show it is nonzero.
\end{enumerate}

RH-80 supplies an abstract moving-cloud relative determinant template
\citep{WangRH80}. The new work must connect that template to the
operator-derived RH-222 shells and overcome the nonnormal Frobenius barrier.
This is the recommended RH-232 target.

\section{Macro-gate boundary}

Gate A is not closed: there is no uniform small-noise relative determinant or
dynamical limiting realization. Consequently:
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=2em]
\item Gate B, a time-oriented unitary or scattering completion, is untouched;
\item Gate C, a self-adjoint generator and internally derived $T\log T$
count, is untouched;
\item Gate D, a prime-power/von Mangoldt trace identity, is untouched;
\item Gate E, equality with the completed zeta divisor, is untouched.
\end{itemize}

No Markov resonance or reciprocal zero is identified with a Riemann zero. No
Hilbert--Polya operator or Riemann-hypothesis implication is asserted. The
batch advances the internal determinant architecture and identifies its next
precise wall.
