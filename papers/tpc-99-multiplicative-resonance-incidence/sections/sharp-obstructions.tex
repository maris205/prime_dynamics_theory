\section{Sharp obstructions and stop tests}
\label{sec:sharp-obstructions}

\begin{example}[A full resonant low window at conductor \(q\)]
\label{ex:delta-obstruction}
Take \(\omega=1\), \(1\le R\le H\), and let \(b\) be supported on
\(\{1,\ldots,R\}\) with nonnegative values.  Then
\[
 r\omega=r\in E_H
\]
for every active frequency, so
\[
 (K_Hb)(1)=\|b\|_1.
\]
The phase has algebraic conductor \(q\) for every nonzero \(r\), yet
the incidence gives no saving at all.
\end{example}

\begin{proposition}[No conductor-only density bound]
\label{prop:no-conductor-only}
There is no constant \(C\), independent of \(q,H\), such that
\[
 (K_Hb)(\omega)
 \le C\frac{H}{q}\|b\|_1
\]
for all nonnegative \(b\) and all \(\omega\in G\).
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Use \cref{ex:delta-obstruction} with \(b=\one_{\{1\}}\).  The left
side is \(1\), whereas \(CH/q\) can tend to \(0\).
\end{proof}

\begin{example}[Principal concentration]
\label{ex:principal-concentration}
If \(a\equiv A\) with \(A\ge0\), and \(b\ge0\), then
\(a^\circ=0\) and the
nonprincipal remainder vanishes exactly, but
\[
 \mathcal I_H(a,b)
 =\frac{2H}{q-1}
 \|a\|_1\sum_rb(r).
\]
Thus perfect multiplicative equidistribution does not remove the
principal density ledger.
\end{example}

\begin{example}[Odd-character null mode]
\label{ex:odd-null}
If \(a=\chi\) is an odd multiplicative character and \(b\) is
arbitrary, then the \(\chi\)-component of the resonance carrier is
annihilated exactly.  This is the strongest possible spectral
cancellation, but the literal positive envelope \(a_H\) need not
lie in that subspace.
\end{example}

\subsection{Route decision}

The incidence route continues only if a proof-carrying native census
or theorem supplies both \eqref{eq:principal-gate} and
\eqref{eq:dispersion-gate} for this termwise implementation.  A
proved polynomial obstruction in the principal bound calls for
restoring cancellation before that split, or for a genuinely
filter-sensitive positive theorem.  An obstruction in the centered
even-character bound calls for a second native averaging variable,
the signed route, or the full-block route.  These are stop tests for
the displayed sufficient method, not lower bounds for the original
signed coefficient.  No amount of repeating ``conductor \(q_X\)''
changes them.
