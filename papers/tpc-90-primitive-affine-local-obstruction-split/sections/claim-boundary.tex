\section{Claim boundary and conclusion}
\label{sec:claim-boundary}

\subsection{What is proved}

\begin{theorem}[Audited primitive affine split]
\label{thm:audited-primitive-split}
Let
\[
 L_1(r)=d+sr,\qquad
 L_2(r)=u+ar
\]
be individually primitive integral affine forms with
\(su-ad=h_0\ne0\).  Then:
\begin{enumerate}[label=(\roman*),leftmargin=2.2em]
\item
\[
 \gcd(a,s)\mid h_0,\qquad
 \gcd(L_1(r),L_2(r))\mid h_0;
\]
\item for every prime \(p\) and \(k\ge1\),
\[
 \nu_{p,k}
 =
 \ind_{p\nmid s}+\ind_{p\nmid a}
 -\ind_{p\nmid as}\ind_{p^k\mid h_0};
\]
\item the simultaneous squarefree local product is positive and
uniformly bounded below:
\[
 \Mloc(L_1,L_2)
 =
 \prod_p\left(1-\frac{\nu_{p,2}}{p^2}\right)
 \ge
 \prod_p\left(1-\frac2{p^2}\right)>0;
\]
\item for fixed coefficients, \(\Mloc(L_1,L_2)\) is the natural
density of \(r\) for which both values are squarefree;
\item for squarefree \(q_1,q_2\), the simultaneous square-divisor
system is soluble exactly under
\[
 (q_1,s)=(q_2,a)=1,\qquad
 \gcd(q_1,q_2)^2\mid h_0,
\]
and then gives one class modulo
\(\lcm(q_1^2,q_2^2)\);
\item on every positive finite interval and for every complex
weight \(W\),
\[
 \sum W\,\mu(L_1)\mu(L_2)
 =
 \sum W\,\mu^2(L_1)\mu^2(L_2)
 \lambda(L_1)\lambda(L_2).
\]
\end{enumerate}
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Combine
\cref{thm:gcd-confinement,thm:prime-power-root-count,prop:euler-product-positive,thm:fixed-pair-squarefree-density,thm:square-divisor-compatibility,thm:two-form-split}.
\end{proof}

All statements in \cref{thm:audited-primitive-split}, including the
fixed-form density theorem, are \(\Lzero\).  They apply to the
abstract affine pair exactly as stated.

\subsection{What requires a literal
\texorpdfstring{\(\Lone\)}{L1} verification}

To use the split in the physical packet, one must verify all of the
following:
\begin{enumerate}[label=(\alph*),leftmargin=2.2em]
\item the displayed coefficients are the literal growing
coefficients and satisfy the same prescribed nonzero \(h_0\);
\item each pair is primitive, or the exact content constants,
changed determinant, finite coprimality masks, and identically zero
branches of \cref{prop:content-factorization} are retained;
\item the interval origin, positivity range, all residue,
coprimality, smooth, and joint masks, and all logarithmic or ordinary
weights are unchanged;
\item both polarizations and every native outer key are present;
\item short or bounded-length fibers receive a separate exact or
outer estimate;
\item every prefix required by partial summation or dyadic recursion
is controlled;
\item \eqref{eq:literal-outer-reassembly} is the complete raw
physical coefficient under one global post-aggregation
normalization;
\item the physical-loss ledger satisfies
\(\Lambda_{\mathrm{phys}}<1/400\).
\end{enumerate}
Only after these checks does
\eqref{eq:literal-local-parity-split} become a literal \(\Lone\)
certificate rather than an abstract identity.

\subsection{What is not proved}

This paper does not prove:
\begin{enumerate}[label=(\alph*),leftmargin=2.2em]
\item a uniform squarefree-density asymptotic on the actual growing,
translated, possibly short physical fibers;
\item cancellation of
\(\lambda(L_{1,\theta,X})\lambda(L_{2,\theta,X})\) on the literal
squarefree support;
\item the prefix estimate \eqref{eq:uniform-prefix-target};
\item a power saving for the complete outer correlation
\eqref{eq:hard-growing-correlation};
\item uniformity of a fixed-form logarithmic theorem in the growing
physical coefficients;
\item selection of the prescribed \(h_0\) from an averaged-shift
theorem;
\item a determinant-energy lower bound for \(D_X\);
\item either conditional hypothesis
\eqref{eq:conditional-zero-mode} or
\eqref{eq:conditional-energy};
\item a fixed-\(h_0\) Hardy--Littlewood asymptotic, a breach of the
sieve parity barrier, or a prime-pair lower bound.
\end{enumerate}
These missing signed estimates are \(\Ltwo\), not consequences of
the local classification.

\subsection{Conclusion and next gate}

The primitive affine determinant resolves the local geometry more
completely than a generic ``finite bad primes'' slogan.  It proves
two different facts:
\[
 \text{common value primes divide }h_0,
\]
while
\[
 \text{one-slope primes may move but delete one local root.}
\]
At square level, the exact formula
\[
 \nu_{p,2}
 =
 \ind_{p\nmid s}+\ind_{p\nmid a}
 -\ind_{p\nmid as}\ind_{p^2\mid h_0}
\]
shows that the squarefree support has no local annihilation.  The
identity \(\mu=\mu^2\lambda\) then places every remaining sign issue
in the explicit correlation \eqref{eq:hard-growing-correlation}.

The next positive gate is therefore not another local-density
calculation.  It is a theorem that gains cancellation from the
Liouville product while retaining the literal growing forms, fixed
nonzero determinant, support, origins, weights, masks, prefixes, and
outer reassembly.  A viable weaker step could control one literal
residual block or a certified range of square-divisor moduli, provided
it is labeled at its actual proof level and its loss is entered in
the correct ledger.  A proof that such a route necessarily loses
\(1/400\) or more would be a valid negative endpoint.

Nothing here specializes \(h_0\) to \(2\), proves that the affine
values are prime, gives a prime-pair lower bound, or proves the
twin-prime conjecture.  The result is an exact local-obstruction
split and a sharper statement of the parity gate.
