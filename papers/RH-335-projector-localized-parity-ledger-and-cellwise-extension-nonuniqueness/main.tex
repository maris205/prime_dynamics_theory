and Cellwise Extension Nonuniqueness}
\author{Bin Wang}

\begin{abstract}
The repaired physical observation map localizes the noisy cyclic trace and
the deterministic flat trace on the same folded basepoint cells, but its
global parity scalar has no canonical cellwise allocation.  We freeze one
finite-order gauge using the rank-one Riesz projector of the real noisy
parity eigenvalue.  The set function
$\pi_\sigma(J)=\Tr(M_JE_{-,\sigma})$ is a real finite signed measure,
is invariant under independent left/right eigenvector rescaling, and has
total mass one.  Adding its weighted parity correction to each localized
raw defect gives cells whose sum is exactly
$c^H_{\sigma,n}-c^H_n$.  At the first alias the Hardy full-trace constituent
is the cell sum minus the counterloop alias packet; the minus sign cannot be
discarded.  A positive rational row-stochastic fixture verifies the exact
ledger and proves that local Perron/parity deflation need not commute with a
window even though the commutator trace is zero.  We then prove that a global
parity scalar admits infinitely many local signed extensions: any nonzero
zero-total signed measure changes cells while preserving every partition
aggregate.  Thus the projector-density allocation is a frozen gauge, not a
canonical physical parity localization or deterministic/noisy projector
transport.  Finally, the planned adapted-norm upper-exponent route remains
not testable because all-leg operator errors, physical observation/product
norm upper bounds, and a subcritical stability-weight upper exponent are
absent.  All statements are fixed-order identities or scoped negative
results; no moving-order trace replacement or Riemann-hypothesis conclusion
is obtained.
\end{abstract}

\section{Frozen finite-order data type}

Let $(X,m)$ be a measure space and $\cH=L^2(X,m)$.  For fixed noise
$\sigma>0$, let $K_\sigma$ be a real operator whose powers of order
$n\ge2$ are trace class.  Assume that $1$ and
$\lambda_-(\sigma)\in\mathbb R$ are simple eigenvalues with rank-one Riesz
projectors $E_{0,\sigma}$ and $E_{-,\sigma}$, respectively.  The operator
$E_{-,\sigma}$ is the projector itself, not
$\lambda_-(\sigma)E_{-,\sigma}$.

For a measurable set $J\subset X$, let $M_J$ denote multiplication by
$\one_J$ and put
\begin{equation}\label{eq:L-local}
 L_{\sigma,n}(J)=\Tr(M_JK_\sigma^n),\qquad n\ge2.
\end{equation}
The RH-334 physical observation map supplies the corrected deterministic
localization $P_n^{\rm abs}(J)$, with $X=[0,1]$ in that application
\citep{WangObservation2026}.  It is additive on every frozen measurable
partition and satisfies $P_n^{\rm abs}(X)=P_n$.

We retain the Hardy coefficients and signs fixed in RH-326
\citep{WangFirstAlias2026}:
\begin{align}
 c^H_{\sigma,n}
 &=r_H^{-n}\{\Tr K_\sigma^n-1-\lambda_-(\sigma)^n\},
 \label{eq:c-noisy}\\
 c^H_n
 &=r_H^{-n}\{P_n-1-(-1)^n\}.
 \label{eq:c-deterministic}
\end{align}
The scalar Perron terms $-1$ cancel in their difference.  That global scalar
cancellation will not be confused with a commutation statement for localized
projectors.

\section{The parity Riesz-projector measure}

Choose real right and left parity eigenfunctions $v_\sigma,w_\sigma$.
Without fixing their normalization, the rank-one projector has the form
\begin{equation}\label{eq:rank-one}
 E_{-,\sigma}h
 =\frac{v_\sigma\langle w_\sigma,h\rangle}
 {\langle w_\sigma,v_\sigma\rangle},
 \qquad \langle w_\sigma,v_\sigma\rangle\ne0.
\end{equation}

\begin{theorem}[Signed projector measure]\label{thm:projector-measure}
The set function
\begin{equation}\label{eq:pi-def}
 \boxed{\pi_\sigma(J)=\Tr(M_JE_{-,\sigma})}
\end{equation}
is a real finite signed measure.  It has density
\begin{equation}\label{eq:pi-density}
 d\pi_\sigma(x)
 =\frac{v_\sigma(x)w_\sigma(x)}
 {\langle w_\sigma,v_\sigma\rangle}\,dm(x),
\end{equation}
is invariant under independent nonzero rescalings of either eigenfunction,
and satisfies
\begin{equation}\label{eq:pi-total}
 \pi_\sigma(X)=1.
\end{equation}
It need not be positive.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
The product $v_\sigma w_\sigma$ belongs to $L^1(X,m)$ by Cauchy--Schwarz.
The standard rank-one trace formula applied to $M_JE_{-,\sigma}$ gives
\eqref{eq:pi-density}; hence countable additivity, finiteness, and reality
follow.  Replacing $(v_\sigma,w_\sigma)$ by
$(av_\sigma,bw_\sigma)$ multiplies both numerator and denominator by $ab$,
so the measure is unchanged.  Finally,
$\pi_\sigma(X)=\Tr E_{-,\sigma}=1$ because a rank-one projector has one
nonzero eigenvalue, equal to one.  The product
$v_\sigma w_\sigma$ may change sign, so positivity does not follow.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}[The frozen gauge]\label{rem:frozen-gauge}
Because \eqref{eq:pi-total} holds, one may write
\[
 (-1)^n=\sum_{J\in\cP}(-1)^n\pi_\sigma(J)
\]
for any finite partition $\cP$.  This uses the \emph{noisy} projector
density to allocate the \emph{deterministic} scalar.  We adopt it only as a
gauge frozen before cell evaluation.  It is not a canonical physical local
parity density, a localization of a deterministic parity projector, or a
theorem transporting deterministic and noisy projectors.
\end{remark}

\section{Exact corrected ledger and the first-alias sign}

For fixed $n\ge2$ define the signed cell
\begin{equation}\label{eq:C-def}
 \boxed{
 \cC_{\sigma,n}(J)=r_H^{-n}\!\left[
 L_{\sigma,n}(J)-P_n^{\rm abs}(J)
 +\{(-1)^n-\lambda_-(\sigma)^n\}\pi_\sigma(J)
 \right].}
\end{equation}

\begin{theorem}[Frozen partition ledger]\label{thm:partition-ledger}
For every frozen finite measurable partition $\cP$ of $X$ and every
$n\ge2$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:partition-ledger}
 \boxed{
 \sum_{J\in\cP}\cC_{\sigma,n}(J)
 =c^H_{\sigma,n}-c^H_n.}
\end{equation}
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Trace linearity gives
$\sum_JL_{\sigma,n}(J)=\Tr K_\sigma^n$.  The corrected deterministic
localization gives $\sum_JP_n^{\rm abs}(J)=P_n$, while
\cref{thm:projector-measure} gives $\sum_J\pi_\sigma(J)=1$.
Substitution in \eqref{eq:C-def} yields
\[
 r_H^{-n}\{\Tr K_\sigma^n-P_n+(-1)^n-\lambda_-(\sigma)^n\},
\]
which is exactly the difference of \eqref{eq:c-noisy} and
\eqref{eq:c-deterministic}.
\end{proof}

Let $s_{k,n}$ be the finite counterloop moment,
$p_n^{\rm pole}$ the limiting pole moment, and
\begin{equation}\label{eq:alias-anchor}
 \cA_{k,n}=s_{k,n}-p_n^{\rm pole},
 \qquad
 a_n^{\rm num}=c^H_n-p_n^{\rm pole}.
\end{equation}
The first-alias constituent has the data type fixed in RH-334,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:q-type}
 q_{{\rm FT},\sigma,k,n}
 =c^H_{\sigma,n}-s_{k,n}-a_n^{\rm num},
 \qquad
 \mathtt{coefficient\_type}
 =\mathtt{hardy\_full\_trace\_constituent}.
\end{equation}

\begin{corollary}[Alias packet is subtracted]\label{cor:first-alias}
For $k\ge2$ and $n=2k$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:q-ledger}
 \boxed{
 q_{{\rm FT},\sigma,k,2k}
 =\sum_{J\in\cP}\cC_{\sigma,2k}(J)-\cA_{k,2k}.}
\end{equation}
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
The definitions give
\[
 q_{\rm FT}
 =(c^H_{\sigma,n}-c^H_n)-(s_{k,n}-p_n^{\rm pole}).
\]
Apply \cref{thm:partition-ledger}.  In particular, replacing
\eqref{eq:q-ledger} by the bare cell sum omits the nonzero first-alias packet
and has the wrong algebraic sign ledger.
\end{proof}

\section{Global Perron cancellation is not local commutation}

The absence of a Perron cell in \eqref{eq:C-def} comes from cancellation of
the two global scalars $-1$ in \eqref{eq:c-noisy} and
\eqref{eq:c-deterministic}.  It does not say that a window preserves the
peripheral deflated subspace.  Put
\begin{equation}\label{eq:deflation}
 D_{\sigma,n}=E_{0,\sigma}+\lambda_-(\sigma)^nE_{-,\sigma}.
\end{equation}
Even when both products are trace class,
$\Tr[M_J,D_{\sigma,n}]=0$ follows from trace cyclicity and contains no
information about whether the commutator itself vanishes
\citep{Simon2005}.

\begin{proposition}[Exact strict commutator fixture]
\label{prop:commutator-fixture}
Consider the positive rational row-stochastic matrix
\begin{equation}\label{eq:K-fixture}
 K=\begin{pmatrix}
 3/17&7/51&35/51\\
 4/85&83/255&32/51\\
 58/85&1/51&76/255
 \end{pmatrix}.
\end{equation}
Its spectrum is $\{1,-2/5,1/5\}$, and the Riesz projector at $-2/5$ is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:Eminus-fixture}
 E_-=\begin{pmatrix}
 10/17&-5/51&-25/51\\
 8/17&-4/51&-20/51\\
 -10/17&5/51&25/51
 \end{pmatrix}.
\end{equation}
It satisfies $E_-^2=E_-$,
$KE_-=E_-K=(-2/5)E_-$, and $\Tr E_-=1$.
The Perron projector is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:Ezero-fixture}
 E_0=\begin{pmatrix}
 7/17&5/51&25/51\\
 7/17&5/51&25/51\\
 7/17&5/51&25/51
 \end{pmatrix}.
\end{equation}
For $M_2=\operatorname{diag}(0,1,0)$ and $n=2$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:strict-commutator}
 [M_2,E_0+(4/25)E_-]
 =\begin{pmatrix}
 0&-7/85&0\\
 207/425&0&109/255\\
 0&-29/255&0
 \end{pmatrix}\ne0,
\end{equation}
although its trace is zero.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Exact multiplication gives
$E_-^2=E_-$ and the two spectral intertwining identities.  The polynomial
projector
\[
 E_0=\frac{25}{28}(K+\tfrac25I)(K-\tfrac15I)
\]
has the displayed value.  Setting $E_+=I-E_0-E_-$ gives three pairwise
annihilating nonzero projectors with
$KE_0=E_0$, $KE_-=(-2/5)E_-$, and $KE_+=(1/5)E_+$; hence the stated
spectrum follows.  Direct subtraction of the two products with $M_2$ gives
\eqref{eq:strict-commutator}.
\end{proof}

This fixture is algebraic and nonphysical.  Its role is to reject the invalid
inference ``global scalar deflation implies windowwise invariant
deflation.''

\section{Exact singleton-cell ledger}

Use the fixture above with $r_H=17/20$, $n=2$, and set the three
deterministic localized values to zero.  The diagonal of $K^2$ and the
projector masses are
\begin{align}
 (L_1,L_2,L_3)&=(43/85,53/425,242/425),\label{eq:L-fixture}\\
 (\pi_1,\pi_2,\pi_3)&=(10/17,-4/51,25/51).
 \label{eq:pi-fixture}
\end{align}
The negative middle entry proves that $\pi$ is not a probability vector.
Since $1-(-2/5)^2=21/25$, \eqref{eq:C-def} gives
\begin{equation}\label{eq:C-fixture}
 (\cC_1,\cC_2,\cC_3)
 =\left(\frac{400}{289},\frac{400}{4913},
 \frac{6672}{4913}\right).
\end{equation}
Their sum and the independently evaluated global expression agree:
\begin{equation}\label{eq:C-total-fixture}
 \sum_{i=1}^3\cC_i
 =\frac{48}{17}
 =\left(\frac{20}{17}\right)^2
 \left\{\Tr K^2+1-\left(-\frac25\right)^2\right\}.
\end{equation}
The archived counterloop definition requires $k\ge2$.  Therefore the
$n=2$ computation is only a fixed-order ledger reproduction; it is not an
attempt to set $k=1$ in \eqref{eq:q-ledger}.

\section{Cellwise extension nonuniqueness}

The measure $\pi_\sigma$ makes \eqref{eq:C-def} exact, but the global scalar
does not select it uniquely.

\begin{theorem}[Zero-total extension freedom]
\label{thm:extension-nonuniqueness}
Let $g\in\mathbb R$ and let $\mu$ be any finite signed measure on $X$ with
$\mu(X)=g$.  For every finite signed measure $\zeta$ with $\zeta(X)=0$,
the measure $\mu'=\mu+\zeta$ has the same global mass.  Consequently, for
every finite measurable partition $\cP$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:partition-aggregate}
 \sum_{J\in\cP}\mu'(J)
 =\sum_{J\in\cP}\mu(J)=g.
\end{equation}
If $\zeta$ is nonzero on at least one cell, the corresponding cellwise
allocations are distinct.

If $X$ contains disjoint measurable sets $A,B$ of positive finite measure,
there are infinitely many such absolutely continuous perturbations.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
The first statement is finite additivity and
$\mu'(X)=\mu(X)+\zeta(X)=g$.  For the last statement, for any $t\ne0$ put
\[
 d\zeta_t=t\left(
 \frac{\one_A}{m(A)}-\frac{\one_B}{m(B)}
 \right)dm.
\]
Then $\zeta_t(X)=0$, while its values on $A$ and $B$ are $t$ and $-t$.
Distinct values of $t$ give distinct extensions.
\end{proof}

For the rational fixture, the parity scalar is $g=21/25$ and the
projector-gauge allocation is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:base-allocation}
 g(\pi_1,\pi_2,\pi_3)
 =(42/85,-28/425,7/17).
\end{equation}
Adding $(1/51,-1/51,0)$ changes two cells and leaves the total $21/25$
unchanged.  This finite row is a witness to nonuniqueness, not evidence that
any particular continuum interval has nonzero projector mass.  In
particular, we make no claim about the corrected mass of the specific
fixed-point-free interval used in the RH-334 window-shift reproduction.

\section{Why the adapted-norm route remains inactive}

RH-325 proves a sufficient trace-observation Duhamel majorant
\citep{WangMovingDuhamel2026}.  With local operator errors $\delta_j$ and
stability weights $W_j$, the required target estimate follows from
\begin{equation}\label{eq:duhamel-majorant}
 \sum_jW_j\delta_j=o(k_\sigma R^{-2k_\sigma}).
\end{equation}
If there are $O(k_\sigma)$ terms, uniformly
$\delta_j=O(\sigma)$, and
\begin{equation}\label{eq:gamma-upper}
 \max_jW_j=O(\sigma^{-\gamma}),
\end{equation}
then the archived sufficient exponent window is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:gamma-star}
 \gamma<\gamma_*
 =1-\frac{\log(1.4)}{\log\lambda}
 =0.3503698834605293\ldots .
\end{equation}

The repository does not supply the hypotheses needed to apply this result to
the physical cyclic observation:
\begin{enumerate}[label=\textup{(M\arabic*)},leftmargin=3em]
\item no uniform physical $\delta_j=O(\sigma)$ theorem covers all legs;
\item no physical upper bounds are available for the trace observation and
the required prefix/suffix operator products; and
\item no bound \eqref{eq:gamma-upper} is proved with
$\gamma<\gamma_*$.
\end{enumerate}
RH-18 proves instead only
\begin{equation}\label{eq:rh18-lower}
 \operatorname{cond}(D^{\rm G}_{k_\sigma})
 \ge \sigma^{-1/4+o(1)},
\end{equation}
a lower conditioning bound for an auxiliary Gaussian-packet balance
\citep{WangGaussianReturn2026}.  A lower bound cannot replace the upper
bound required in \eqref{eq:gamma-upper}, and RH-18 does not identify that
balance with the physical RH-325 observation weight.  The positive
adapted-norm route is therefore \texttt{STOP\_SCOPED/NOT\_TESTABLE}.
Failure of a sufficient majorant is not a divergence theorem.

\section{Reproduction protocol and claim boundary}

The executable artifact uses exact rational arithmetic.  It checks the
spectral-projector decomposition, independent eigenvector rescaling,
idempotence, both intertwining identities, the signed singleton masses, the
strict commutator, the three corrected cells, their total, and a nonzero
zero-total extension.  These finite calculations reproduce algebra only;
they are not continuum certificates or moving-order evidence.

RH-335 proves an exact fixed-order projector-gauge ledger and a rigorous
cellwise extension nonuniqueness theorem.  It does not prove a physical local
parity density, deterministic/noisy projector transport, a moving-order
ledger, an $o(H_k)$ estimate, signed Duhamel cancellation, a physical upper
stability exponent, off-alias or head closure, or determinant gluing.  No
result constructs a Hilbert--Polya operator, identifies Riemann zeros, proves
a von Mangoldt trace formula or completed-zeta divisor equality, or proves
the Riemann hypothesis.  Gates A--E remain false/open.
