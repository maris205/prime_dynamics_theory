\section{Audited conclusion and next certificate}
\label{sec:claim-boundary}

\begin{theorem}[Audited finite-dimensional conclusion]
\label{thm:audited-conclusion}
Let \(w\) be a literal finite atom coefficient, let \(B\) contain the
exact candidate block columns, and let \(\mathscr C\) encode the
allowed synthesis coefficients.
\begin{enumerate}[label=(\roman*),leftmargin=2.2em]
\item For unrestricted coefficients,
  \[
  \eps_{\cov}
  =
  \|(I-BB^\dagger)w\|_2,
  \]
  and, when the residual is nonzero, its normalized direction is an
  optimal dual obstruction.
\item For nonnegative real coefficients, Farkas' alternative is exact
  and
  \[
  \eps_{\cov}
  =
  \max_{\substack{\|y\|_2\le1\\B^{\mathsf T}y\le0}}
  \langle y,w\rangle.
  \]
\item For a closed convex attainable image, the support-function
  formula \eqref{eq:convex-distance-dual} is exact.
\item Bounded overlap, even overlap one with every atom covered, does
  not imply an exact or \(o(1)\)-relative cover.
\item An exact cover transports the original global normalization
  with coefficient cost
  \(\sum_j|c_j|E_j\).  An approximate cover leaves the independent
  physical remainder \(\mathcal T_X(w-Bc)\).
\end{enumerate}
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Combine
\cref{thm:subspace-certificate,thm:farkas-cone,thm:convex-dual,thm:partition-counterexample,thm:exact-reassembly,prop:residual-remainder}.
\end{proof}

\paragraph{What is proved.}
The literal synthesis model, exact residual formulas, dual
certificates, explicit finite counterexamples, target and column
stability bounds, and coefficient-amplification identity are
unconditional \(\Lzero\) results.  They provide an exact
\(\Lone\) format for auditing a proposed TPC-18 cover.

\paragraph{What is not proved.}
No complete physical atom list \(w_X\), incidence matrix \(B_X\), or
allowed exact solution \(c_X\) is constructed for the full TPC-18
normal form, and no nonzero residual is controlled in the physical
statistic.  No primitive generic determinant-dispersion estimate,
prefix extension, determinant-energy survival theorem, or
distinguished zero-mode estimate is proved.  Hence the TPC-18
all-block representation and every \(\Ltwo\) arithmetic gate remain
open.

\paragraph{Support and key rule.}
The certificate must be checked on the union of actual target and
candidate-block supports.  Native atoms may not be merged merely
because they have the same determinant.  Every extra column atom is a
row with target coefficient zero.  A certificate after aggregation is
only necessary unless a separate lifting theorem is proved.

\paragraph{Coefficient rule.}
The allowed set \(\mathscr C_X\) comes from the upstream
decomposition.  A signed solution does not certify a nonnegative
cover, and an arbitrary solution does not certify the original unit
all-block identity.  Exact feasibility and coefficient amplification
must both be verified.

\paragraph{Normalization rule.}
Only \(B_Xc_X=w_X\) gives pure algebraic transport under the original
global normalizer.  A least-squares residual, however small in
\(\ell^2\), requires a new physical remainder theorem.  Such a theorem
could verify the original asymptotic TPC-18 route, but the residual may
not be discarded by bounded overlap or numerical conditioning.

\paragraph{Ledger rule.}
The exact-cover condition \(w_X\in B_X\mathscr C_X\) (equivalently
\(\eps_{\cov}=0\) when the attainable image is closed), the post-bin determinant rule
\(\lambda_D\le2\eta_Z\), and the strict endpoint rule
\(\Lambda_{\phys}<1/400\) are independent.  Equality or excess in the
last rule stops the route.  No gain or loss is charged twice.

\paragraph{Arithmetic claim boundary.}
All statements retain one prescribed nonzero \(h_0\).  Nothing
specializes it to \(2\), proves a fixed-shift Hardy--Littlewood
asymptotic, gives a prime-pair lower bound, breaches the parity
barrier, or implies the twin-prime conjecture.

\paragraph{Next exact task.}
A genuine advance on this gate must export the upstream hard packet
and all candidate TPC-18 blocks to the same literal row list, then do
one of the following:
\begin{enumerate}[label=(\alph*),leftmargin=2.2em]
\item produce an exact symbolic or interval-verified allowed
  coefficient \(c_X\) with \(B_Xc_X=w_X\) and acceptable
  amplification;
\item produce a dual residual or Farkas vector proving that the
  proposed block family cannot exactly cover the packet; or
\item isolate an explicit residual \(r_X\) and prove a new physical
  bound for \(\mathcal T_X(r_X)\).
\end{enumerate}
The first would close only the exact zero-remainder interface.  The
second would rule out that block family only as an exact
zero-remainder route; it would not exclude a separately controlled
residual route.  The third would define such a residual route.  None,
by itself, proves the independent TPC-18 determinant-dispersion input.
