\begin{abstract}
We audit two possible weaknesses in a finite normalized operator certificate:
whether its Schur envelope is nearly saturated, and whether computing the
true spectrum only for the all-plus sign law is representative.  On the three
origins fixed by the preceding geometry-adversarial holdout, we compute true
spectra for all four fixed sign laws at counts 256 and 512, producing 144
rows.  The largest spectral/Schur ratio is 0.77628391453148915 and the largest
spectral/Frobenius ratio is 0.62110877254133434.  All 144 normalized spectra
are below 0.64; all-plus wins 30 of 36 setting-wise comparisons and the mod-4
law wins 6.  These are finite, scoped observations: they quantify envelope
slack and law variation but do not imply a growing operator theorem, an
arithmetic estimate, or a twin-prime result.
\end{abstract}

\section{Question and scope}

TPC-359 transferred a finite normalized cap to a high-origin panel selected

\section{Operator and finite protocol}

For $I=[x,x+N-1]\cap\mathbb Z$, primes $Q<p\leq2Q$, and $s\in\{1,2\}$,
we use the literal component
\begin{equation}
 B_p(u,t)=p\frac{66^{2s}}{(66^2+(u-t)^2)^s}
 \left({\bf1}_{p\mid u-t}-\frac1{p-1}\right)
 {\bf1}_{u\ne t}{\bf1}_{p\nmid u}{\bf1}_{p\nmid t}.
 \label{eq:block}
\end{equation}
For each fixed sign law $\varepsilon$, let $A_\varepsilon=\sum_p\varepsilon_pB_p$.
The unsigned geometry is $G_u=\sum_{p,t}B_p(u,t)^2$, and
\begin{equation}
 A_\varepsilon^\#=D_G^{-1/2}A_\varepsilon D_G^{-1/2},
 \qquad D_G=\operatorname{diag}(G_u).
 \label{eq:norm}
\end{equation}
We inherit origins $(267175,261267,269074)$ from TPC-359 and use
$N\in\{256,512\}$, $Q\in\{24,54,80\}$, $s\in\{1,2\}$, and four laws:
all-plus, alternating-index, the prime mod-$4$ character, and half-split.
This gives $3\cdot2\cdot3\cdot2\cdot4=144$ rows and 36 within-setting
law comparisons.

\section{Finite envelope facts}

For every finite real symmetric matrix $T$,
\begin{equation}
 \opnorm{T}\leq S(T):=\max_u\sum_t|T(u,t)|,
 \qquad
 \opnorm{T}\leq F(T):=\left(\sum_{u,t}|T(u,t)|^2\right)^{1/2}.
 \label{eq:envelope}
\end{equation}
The inequalities are exact finite statements.  We use the ratios
$\rho_S=\opnorm{T}/S(T)$ and $\rho_F=\opnorm{T}/F(T)$ only as descriptive
quantities for the declared matrices.

census itself rules out silently treating it as a universal proxy.

\section{Conclusion}

The finite normalized cap is not close to saturation by either elementary
envelope on this panel, and the all-plus law captures the largest spectrum in
most but not all settings.  This identifies the next useful control: repeat
the tightness ledger on an independently selected high-origin panel, keeping
all four laws in the spectral audit.  Nothing in this finite diagnostic pays
an arithmetic loss.
