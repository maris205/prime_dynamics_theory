\begin{abstract}
We classify two finite-memory layers of the distance-two M\"obius capacity
problem.  A fixed periodic causal window table is universally safe exactly
when a finite de Bruijn block test passes, and its current-zero score has a
canonical ternary monomial expansion with unique coefficients.  Among all
$512$ lag-two tables, exactly $13$
are universally safe.  Their six-term arithmetic ledger has rank five; seven
tables have unconditional limiting correlations, whereas each of the other
six has a limit exactly when the ordinary shift-two Chowla sum is $o(N)$.
Separately, two fixed four-state causal machines produce the two endpoint
extrema for every finite ternary word.  Four states are necessary for either
exact output stream, but no single deterministic universally safe causal policy can attain
the absolute capacity at every prefix.  A length-$15$ stateless table realizes
each orientation on the run-at-most-eight class containing the M\"obius word;
that window length is minimal only in this narrow stateless model.  Exact
finite certificates accompany every enumeration.  We prove neither
shift-two Chowla cancellation nor convergence of the adaptive capacity.
No operator, trace formula, zeta-zero identification, or implication for the
Riemann hypothesis is claimed.
\end{abstract}

\section{Problem and claim boundary}

Let $a=(a_n)_{n\ge1}$ be a word in $\TT$.  A sign word
$\epsilon=(\epsilon_n)_{n\ge1}\in\SSS^{\mathbb N}$ is
\emph{distance-two safe} when
\begin{equation}
       \epsilon_n=\epsilon_{n+2}=+1\quad\text{never occurs}.
       \label{eq:safety}
\end{equation}
For a finite prefix put
\[
 S_N(a,\epsilon)=\sum_{n\le N}a_n\epsilon_n,
 \quad S_N^{\max}(a)=\max_{\epsilon\ \mathrm{safe}}S_N(a,\epsilon),
 \quad S_N^{\min}(a)=\min_{\epsilon\ \mathrm{safe}}S_N(a,\epsilon),
\]
and
\begin{equation}
                 K_N(a)=\max\{|S_N^{\max}(a)|,|S_N^{\min}(a)|\}.
                 \label{eq:capacity}
\end{equation}
For $a_n=\mob(n)$ these are the frozen RH-366/RH-371 objects
\cite{RH366,RH371}.  The optimization is adaptive in the finite input word;
it is not an intrinsic dynamical trace.

This paper has two logically separate outputs.  First, it gives exact finite
classifications of fixed-memory safe factors.  Second, it gives exact online
realizations of the two extrema.  Arithmetic limits enter only in the lag-two
classification.  The unproved object is
\begin{equation}
                   D_2(N)=\sum_{m\le N-2}\mob(m)\mob(m+2)=o(N).
                   \label{eq:chowla}
\end{equation}
We never infer \eqref{eq:chowla} from finite data.

\section{Periodic causal windows}

Fix integers $q,\ell\ge1$.  A $q$-periodic causal $\ell$-window table is a
family
\[
 F_r:\TT^\ell\longrightarrow\SSS,\qquad r\in\mathbb Z/q\mathbb Z,
\]
with output
\[
 \epsilon_n=F_{n\bmod q}(a_{n-\ell+1},\ldots,a_n).
\]
At the beginning of a finite word we pad by zeros.  Universal safety means
that \eqref{eq:safety} holds for every ternary input, not merely for the
M\"obius word.

\begin{theorem}[Compatible-block safety test]
\label{thm:block-test}
A fixed $q$-periodic $\ell$-window table is universally safe if and only if,
for every phase $r$ and every block
$(x_0,\ldots,x_{\ell+1})\in\TT^{\ell+2}$,
\begin{equation}
 F_r(x_0,\ldots,x_{\ell-1})\quad\text{and}\quad
 F_{r+2}(x_2,\ldots,x_{\ell+1})
 \label{eq:block-check}
\end{equation}
are not both $+1$.  Thus the exact test has
$q3^{\ell+2}$ compatible-block checks.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
The two displayed windows are precisely the overlapping windows at times
$n$ and $n+2$.  Universal safety therefore implies every check.  Conversely,
every possible distance-two output pair occurs inside such a compatible
block, so the checks imply safety.  This argument also covers $\ell=1$ and
$\ell=2$: overlap is then empty or confined to the appropriate symbols, but
the same length-$\ell+2$ block still records both windows.  Any phase can be
realized by placing the block sufficiently far into a finite input.
\end{proof}

The score function of one phase is
\[
 h_r(x_1,\ldots,x_\ell)=x_\ell F_r(x_1,\ldots,x_\ell).
\]
It vanishes when the current symbol $x_\ell$ is zero.

\begin{proposition}[Displayed current-zero basis]
\label{prop:basis}
The displayed monomials
\begin{equation}
 \prod_{j<\ell}x_j^{\alpha_j}x_\ell^e,\qquad
 \alpha_j\in\{0,1,2\},\quad e\in\{1,2\}.
 \label{eq:basis}
\end{equation}
form a basis for current-zero functions, and every $h_r$ has a unique
expansion in this basis.  Across $q$ phases the formal dimension is
$2q3^{\ell-1}$.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
On $\TT$, the three functions $1,x,x^2$ are linearly independent and hence
form an interpolation basis for all scalar functions.  Tensoring
gives the $3^\ell$ monomials with exponents in $\{0,1,2\}$.  Setting
$x_\ell=0$ kills exactly the monomials with current exponent one or two; the
vanishing condition forces all coefficients with current exponent zero to
vanish.  This leaves $2\cdot3^{\ell-1}$ basis functions per phase.
\end{proof}

This is a formal function-space dimension.  It is not a minimal number of
M\"obius correlations and does not use any arithmetic identities among
their averages.

\section{Complete lag-two classification}

Consider a stateless lag-two rule
\begin{equation}
       \epsilon_n=f(a_{n-2},a_n),\qquad f:\TT^2\to\SSS,
       \label{eq:lag-rule}
\end{equation}
with $a_j=0$ for $j\le0$.  Write
$P=\{(x,z):f(x,z)=+1\}$ for its plus-edge relation.  This is an
$\ell=3$ table that ignores the middle coordinate.

\begin{theorem}[The thirteen safe tables]
\label{thm:thirteen}
The rule \eqref{eq:lag-rule} is universally safe if and only if $P$ contains
no composable pair:
\begin{equation}
       (x,z),(z,w)\in P\quad\text{for no }x,z,w\in\TT.
       \label{eq:no-compose}
\end{equation}
Among all $2^9=512$ tables, exactly $13$ are safe: the empty relation, the
six single off-diagonal edges, and six two-edge relations, consisting of
three in-stars and three out-stars.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Outputs two sites apart are $f(x,z)$ and $f(z,w)$, proving the equivalence.
Condition \eqref{eq:no-compose} makes every loop illegal.  More generally,
the source set of $P$ is disjoint from its target set.  On three vertices
this bounds $|P|$ by two.  There are six allowed one-edge relations.  A
two-edge relation must share its source or share its target, giving three
out-stars and three in-stars.  These relations all satisfy
\eqref{eq:no-compose}.
\end{proof}

Every score $h(x,z)=zf(x,z)$ has the exact interpolation
\begin{equation}
 h(x,z)=c_{01}z+c_{02}z^2+c_{11}xz+c_{12}xz^2
             +c_{21}x^2z+c_{22}x^2z^2.
 \label{eq:six-basis}
\end{equation}
For $N\ge1$ define, always with the common endpoint $m\le N-2$,
\begin{align*}
 M(N)&=\sum_{n\le N}\mob(n),& Q_1(N)&=\sum_{n\le N}\mob(n)^2,\\
 D_2(N)&=\sum_{m\le N-2}\mob(m)\mob(m+2),&
 Q_2(N)&=\sum_{m\le N-2}\mob(m)^2\mob(m+2)^2,\\
 U_2(N)&=\sum_{m\le N-2}\mob(m)\mob(m+2)^2,&
 V_2(N)&=\sum_{m\le N-2}\mob(m)^2\mob(m+2).
\end{align*}
Zero padding in \eqref{eq:lag-rule} makes the following identity exact,
including the first two sites:
\begin{equation}
 L_f(N):=\sum_{n\le N}\mob(n)f(\mob(n-2),\mob(n))
 =c_{01}M+c_{02}Q_1+c_{11}D_2+c_{12}U_2+c_{21}V_2+c_{22}Q_2.
 \label{eq:ledger}
\end{equation}

\begin{proposition}[Rank-five safe ledger]
\label{prop:rank-five}
For the thirteen safe tables, the coefficient matrix in the order appearing
in \eqref{eq:six-basis} has rank five.  Its unique column relation is
\begin{equation}
                         c_{22}=-c_{02}-c_{11}.
                         \label{eq:relation}
\end{equation}
The multiplicities of $(A,B)=(c_{02},c_{11})$ are
\begin{center}
\begin{tabular}{ccc@{\qquad}ccc}
\toprule
$A$&$B$&count&$A$&$B$&count\\
\midrule
$0$&$0$&5&$0$&$-1/2$&2\\
$0$&$1/2$&2&$-1$&$0$&1\\
$1$&$0$&1&$1$&$-1/2$&1\\
$-1$&$1/2$&1&&&\\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{center}
In particular, seven tables have $c_{11}=0$ and six have $c_{11}\ne0$.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Ternary interpolation in \eqref{eq:six-basis}, applied to the thirteen
relations in Theorem~\ref{thm:thirteen}, gives the displayed multiplicities.
Exact rational row reduction gives rank five and \eqref{eq:relation}.  The
accompanying artifact performs both calculations with
\texttt{fractions.Fraction}; the theorem does not depend on floating-point
rank detection.
\end{proof}

Put
\begin{equation}
 \rhoone=\frac6{\pi^2},\qquad
 \kappatwo=\prod_p\left(1-\frac2{p^2}\right).
 \label{eq:constants}
\end{equation}
Davenport gives $M=o(N)$ \cite{Davenport1937}.  The squarefree density and
two-site squarefree density give
$Q_1/N\to\rhoone$ and $Q_2/N\to\kappatwo$
\cite{Mirsky1948}.  RH-376 proves, with this fixed endpoint,
\begin{equation}
        U_2=o(N),\qquad V_2=o(N),\qquad
        D_2(N)/N\text{ converges }\Longleftrightarrow D_2(N)=o(N).
        \label{eq:rh376-input}
\end{equation}
The final equivalence uses the frozen logarithmic Chowla input only to show
that any ordinary limit would have to be zero; it does not prove that the
ordinary limit exists \cite{RH376}.

\begin{theorem}[Unconditional and Chowla-boundary subclasses]
\label{thm:lag-limits}
For every safe lag-two table,
\begin{equation}
 \frac{L_f(N)}N
 =c_{02}(\rhoone-\kappatwo)
  +c_{11}\left(\frac{D_2(N)}N-\kappatwo\right)+o(1).
 \label{eq:limit-formula}
\end{equation}
Consequently:
\begin{enumerate}
\item all seven tables with $c_{11}=0$ have unconditional limits; the
largest absolute limit within this subclass is
$\rhoone-\kappatwo$, attained for example by $P=\{(0,+1)\}$;
\item each of the six tables with $c_{11}\ne0$ has a limit if and only if
$D_2=o(N)$; under that condition the largest absolute limit within this
subclass is $\rhoone-\kappatwo/2$, attained for example by
$P=\{(-1,+1),(0,+1)\}$.
\end{enumerate}
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Insert the four established asymptotics into \eqref{eq:ledger}, then use
\eqref{eq:relation}; this gives \eqref{eq:limit-formula}.  The thirteen
$(c_{02},c_{11})$ pairs in Proposition~\ref{prop:rank-five} give both
optimizations by exact finite comparison, using
$0<\kappatwo<\rhoone$.  When $c_{11}\ne0$, convergence
of the left side is equivalent to convergence of $D_2/N$, which is
equivalent to \eqref{eq:chowla} by \eqref{eq:rh376-input}.
\end{proof}

The two constants are optima only inside the thirteen-table lag-two class.
In particular, $\rhoone-\kappatwo/2$ is conditional, not an unconditional
capacity floor.

\subsection{Exact comparison with the one-site supremum}

The conditional lag-two value is strictly larger than the all-clock
one-site supremum $B_\infty$ of RH-374/RH-375
\cite{RH374,RH375}.  This comparison is analytic rather than a finite
decimal observation.  Let
\[
 e_m=\prod_{p\ \mathrm{odd}}(1-m/p^2),\qquad e_9=0,
\]
and let $C$ be the normalized odd-run density in the frozen square-clock
limit, so that $e_1=8/\pi^2$ and
$B_\infty=(4+2C)/\pi^2$.  Since
$\kappatwo=e_2/2$,
\begin{equation}
 \rhoone-\frac{\kappatwo}{2}-B_\infty
        =\frac{e_1-e_2-e_1C}{4}.
 \label{eq:strict-comparison}
\end{equation}
Here $e_1-e_2$ is the positive-run-boundary density on the odd lattice and,
by shift invariance, also the positive-run-start density, whereas $e_1C$ is
the density of odd-run starts.  Their difference is the even-run-start
density.  Exact length-eight runs alone have density $e_8$:
the adjacent sites are forced to be zero modulo $9$.  Therefore
\begin{equation}
       \rhoone-\frac{\kappatwo}{2}-B_\infty
       \ge \frac{e_8}{4}>0.
       \label{eq:positive-gap}
\end{equation}
This strict gap becomes available only if \eqref{eq:chowla} is supplied.

\section{Two exact online extrema machines}

For $\sigma\in\SSS$, define a Mealy transducer $T_\sigma$ with state
\begin{equation}
             s_n=(\epsilon_{n-1},\epsilon_{n-2}),\qquad s_1=(-1,-1),
             \label{eq:state}
\end{equation}
output and update
\begin{equation}
 \epsilon_n=+1\Longleftrightarrow
        (a_n=\sigma\ \text{and}\ \epsilon_{n-2}=-1),
 \qquad s_{n+1}=(\epsilon_n,\epsilon_{n-1}).
 \label{eq:greedy}
\end{equation}

\begin{theorem}[Exact orientation extrema]
\label{thm:greedy}
For every finite ternary word,
\begin{equation}
 S_N(a,T_{+})=S_N^{\max}(a),\qquad
 S_N(a,T_{-})=S_N^{\min}(a).
 \label{eq:two-extrema}
\end{equation}
Hence
\begin{equation}
 K_N(a)=\max\{|S_N(a,T_+)|,|S_N(a,T_-)|\}.
 \label{eq:two-machine-K}
\end{equation}
Each $T_\sigma$ is universally safe.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Split the integer sites into the two parity paths.  Relative to the all-minus
output, changing a site to plus changes the score by $2a_n$.  For the maximum
only sites with $a_n=+1$ can help, and one needs a largest independent subset
of those marked vertices on each path.  Left-greedy selection is maximum
cardinality on a path: choosing the first available marked vertex never
reduces the attainable number to its right.  This is exactly $T_+$.  Applying
the same argument to minimization selects the $a_n=-1$ vertices and gives
$T_-$.  Rule \eqref{eq:greedy} can emit plus only when the output two sites
earlier was minus, proving universal safety.
\end{proof}

Equation \eqref{eq:two-machine-K} takes a maximum of two endpoint registers.
It must not be described as one transducer directly outputting $K_N$.

\begin{proposition}[Four-state exact-output minimality]
\label{prop:four-state}
For either fixed orientation $\sigma$, all four states in
\eqref{eq:state} are reachable, and every exact deterministic Mealy
realization of the same input-output map has at least four states.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Starting from $(-1,-1)$, suitable symbols reach
$(+1,-1)$, $(+1,+1)$, and $(-1,+1)$, so all states are reachable.  Take two
states.  If their second coordinates differ, the next input $a=\sigma$
immediately distinguishes their outputs.  If their second coordinates agree
but their first coordinates differ, first feed $a\ne\sigma$, which emits
minus in both states, and then feed $\sigma$; the second output distinguishes
them.  Thus the four residual input-output behaviors are pairwise distinct.
\end{proof}

This is a lower bound only for exact realization of the frozen orientation
stream.  It is not a lower bound for all capacity algorithms, offline
encodings, or machines that return only a final scalar.

\begin{theorem}[No universal deterministic single online $K$ policy]
\label{thm:no-single}
There is no single deterministic causal universally safe policy which, for
every ternary input and at every prefix, satisfies
\begin{equation}
                  |S_N(a,\epsilon)|=K_N(a).
                  \label{eq:online-demand}
\end{equation}
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Begin with $a_1=+1$.  If the policy emits $\epsilon_1=+1$, extend by
$(a_2,a_3)=(-1,+1)$.  Prefix optimality at time two forces
$\epsilon_2=-1$ and gives score $2$.  Reaching $K_3=3$ would then force
$\epsilon_3=+1$, contradicting safety with $\epsilon_1=+1$.

If instead $\epsilon_1=-1$, extend by
$(a_2,a_3,a_4)=(-1,-1,-1)$.  Prefix optimality successively forces
$\epsilon_2=\epsilon_3=+1$ and scores $-2,-3$.  Reaching $K_4=4$ would
force $\epsilon_4=+1$, contradicting safety with $\epsilon_2=+1$.
Thus either first output has a finite adversarial continuation.
\end{proof}

The obstruction is to one deterministic policy required to be optimal on
every branch and every prefix.  It does not exclude a policy designed offline for one known
endpoint.  The artifact independently counts the admissible deterministic causal policy
trees: the counts at horizons $1,2,3,4$ are respectively
$8,256,65536,0$.

\section{A length-fifteen stateless realization}

Fix $\sigma\in\SSS$.  Define the contiguous $15$-window table
\begin{align}
 b_n&=\sum_{k=1}^{8}(-1)^{k+1}
       \prod_{j=0}^{k-1}{\bf1}_{\{a_{n-2j}=\sigma\}},
       \label{eq:b-table}\\
 \widehat\epsilon_n&=2b_n-1,
       \label{eq:window-output}
\end{align}
again padding the past by zeros.

\begin{theorem}[Safety, agreement, and the first divergence]
\label{thm:ell15}
The table \eqref{eq:b-table} is universally safe for every ternary input.
If every step-two $\sigma$-run has length at most eight, then its entire
output stream equals $T_\sigma$.  The M\"obius input belongs to this class.
For an unrestricted ternary input the statement is false: the shortest
divergence is a run of nine same-parity $\sigma$ sites, spanning a
length-$17$ integer prefix.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Let $r$ be the length of the current same-parity $\sigma$-run, with $r=0$
when $a_n\ne\sigma$.  The first $\min(r,8)$ product indicators in
\eqref{eq:b-table} equal one and the rest vanish.  Hence $b_n=1$ precisely
for $r\in\{1,3,5,7\}$, and $b_n=0$ for $r=0$, for even $r$, and for
$r\ge8$.  A plus output is therefore followed two sites later by a minus
output whether the run continues or breaks.  This proves universal safety.

Along a $\sigma$-run, recursion \eqref{eq:greedy} alternates plus and minus,
starting with plus.  It agrees with the displayed rule through run length
eight.  At run length nine the recursion emits plus while
\eqref{eq:b-table} emits minus, giving the claimed $17$-site example.

For odd M\"obius sites, any nine consecutive step-two positions contain a
multiple of $9$, whose M\"obius value is zero.  For even sites, two
consecutive step-two positions include a multiple of $4$.  Thus neither
orientation has a step-two run longer than eight.
\end{proof}

\begin{proposition}[Narrow stateless window minimality]
\label{prop:ell15-minimal}
On the class in Theorem~\ref{thm:ell15}, among $q=1$ causal contiguous
stateless windows that reproduce the complete $T_\sigma$ output stream,
the minimum window length is exactly $15$.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Theorem~\ref{thm:ell15} supplies the upper bound.  At $n=15$, construct two
words identical on the last fourteen sites $a_2,\ldots,a_{15}$ and zero on
the other parity.  Set
$a_3,a_5,\ldots,a_{15}=\sigma$.  In the first word take $a_1=0$; in the
second take $a_1=\sigma$, with a break before the prefix.  The current
same-parity run lengths are seven and eight, so $T_\sigma$ emits respectively
plus and minus.  No contiguous window of length at most fourteen can
distinguish the two inputs.
\end{proof}

The proposition excludes stateful realizations, sparse or noncontiguous
access, noncausal models, and algorithms that compute only a score or
$K_N$.  It is not a global memory lower bound.

\section{Reproducible finite protocol}

\texttt{fractions.Fraction}.  It performs:
\begin{itemize}
\item the $512$-table census, rational rank, relation, and multiplicities;
\item two independent $243$-block graph-lift safety tests for the witnesses
$P=\{(0,+1)\}$ and
$P=\{(-1,+1),(0,+1)\}$;
\item all $72=2\cdot4\cdot3^2$ two-step Mealy safety cases and pairwise state
distinguishability;
\item both extrema on all $\sum_{n=1}^{10}3^n=88572$ ternary words, checked

\item the deterministic causal-policy tree counts through horizon four, including direct

\section{Frontier and gates}

Route A is \texttt{GO}: finite-window safety, the thirteen-table census,
the rank-five arithmetic interface, exact online extrema, two narrow
minimality results, and the single-policy obstruction are proved.  Route B
is \texttt{STOP\_SCOPED}: the six hard lag tables stop exactly at ordinary
shift-two Chowla cancellation, and the full adaptive capacity still stops at
the RH-371/RH-377 envelope rather than at a proved limit
\cite{RH371,RH377}.  RH-372's bounded-transducer framework does not remove
these higher-order arithmetic requirements \cite{RH372}.

All Gates A--E remain false/open.  We have no canonical dynamical spectral
determinant, scattering or unitary completion, self-adjoint generator,
von-Mangoldt prime-power trace law, or completed-zeta divisor identity.
Nothing here constructs a Hilbert--P\'olya operator, identifies Riemann zeros,
or proves RH.  A genuine next step requires either a theorem implying
$D_2=o(N)$ or a new exact reduction of the adaptive capacity envelope; a
larger finite fit alone is not a reopen trigger.
