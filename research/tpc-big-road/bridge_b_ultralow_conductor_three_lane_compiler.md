# Bridge A / Gates A--B V49: critical conductor collar and the exceptional-aware ultra-low three-lane compiler

Date: 2026-08-11

Status: unnumbered big-road research artifact. V48 reduced the transition
problem to one low-conductor signed prime--hybrid scalar. V49 first reuses the
V45 primitive large-sieve block estimate at its actual endpoint rather than at
the convenient interpolation point. This pays every primitive conductor
\(c\geq D_1=x^{49/9600}\), including the thin collar below
\(D_0=x^{1/192}\), with a strict \(1/19200\) numerator margin. The remaining
object is then centered by the already frozen proper-factor Euler carrier and
split exactly into principal, generic nonexceptional, and possible exceptional
quadratic lanes before the first outer absolute value. The direct signed sum of
the three lanes is the selected theorem gate; separate marginal estimates are
only a stronger fallback. No checked primary theorem proves that literal
ultra-low signed scalar with fixed power. Thus V49 advances the route and pays
a genuine additional conductor band, but does not create arithmetic endpoint
credit.

\[
 \boxed{

## 2. The critical conductor collar is paid

Let \(c\asymp D\) be a dyadic primitive-conductor block. V45 proved, for
every reduced-modulus block \(s\asymp S\),

\[
 \mathcal V_D^{(2)}(S)
 \ll P^2\left(\frac DQ+\frac1D\right)x^{o(1)}.
 \tag{2.1}
\]

For \(D_1\leq D<D_0\), dyadic summation and
\(D_0D_1<Q\) give

\[
 \begin{aligned}
 \mathcal K_{[D_1,D_0)}
 &\ll P^2\left(\frac{D_0}{Q}+\frac1{D_1}\right)x^{o(1)}\\
 &\ll \frac{P^2}{D_1}x^{o(1)}
 =x^{151/9600+o(1)}.
 \end{aligned}
 \tag{2.2}
\]

The previously paid range \(D\geq D_0\) has coefficient energy

\[
 \mathcal K_{\geq D_0}\ll P^{3/2}x^{o(1)}
 =x^{150/9600+o(1)}.
 \tag{2.3}
\]

Therefore the full complement of the new cut satisfies

\[
 \boxed{
 \mathcal K_{\geq D_1}
 \ll x^{151/9600+o(1)}.}
 \tag{2.4}
\]

Combining (2.4) with the unchanged physical additive-large-sieve energy
\(x^{2+o(1)}\) yields

\[
 \boxed{
 |\mathfrak V_{\geq D_1}^{\rm tr}|
 \ll x^{21/32+1+151/19200+o(1)}
 =x^{31951/19200+o(1)}.}
 \tag{2.5}
\]

The transition numerator target is

\[
 \frac{1997}{1200}=\frac{31952}{19200},
 \tag{2.6}
\]

so the newly enlarged paid range has the strict margin

\[
 \boxed{
 \frac{1997}{1200}-\frac{31951}{19200}
 =\frac1{19200}.}
 \tag{2.7}
\]

This is a genuine additional arithmetic payment inherited from the
source-backed V45 block estimate. It is not yet a payment of the full
transition window.

## 4. Exact exceptional-aware three-lane split

For \(s=ce\), \((c,e)=1\), and primitive \(\chi^*\pmod c\), retain V48's
literal factors

\[
 K_{c,e}(\chi^*)=
 \sum_{q\in\mathcal Q}
 \sum_{\substack{0<|n|\le ceq/H\\(n,ce)=1}}
 \psi\!\left(\frac{Hn}{ceq}\right)
 \overline{\chi^*(n)}\chi^*(q),
 \tag{4.1}
\]

\[
 W_{c,e}(\chi^*)=
 \sum_{u\in I_x}
 \frac{\Lambda(u+2)-b_x^{(z)}(u)}{\log u}
 \overline{\chi^*(u)}c_e(u).
 \tag{4.2}
\]

For large \(x\), apply the Landau--Page theorem at level \(D_1\). Let
\(\mathfrak X_{\rm exc}(D_1)\) be empty if no exceptional character occurs,
and otherwise contain the unique primitive quadratic character singled out by
that theorem, provided its conductor occurs in the square-free V45 spectrum.
Thus

\[
 \boxed{
 |\mathfrak X_{\rm exc}(D_1)|\leq1.}
 \tag{4.3}
\]

This set is declared from the level \(D_1\) before estimating the scalar. It
does not depend on a favorable sign or on the value of the final sum.

Define three disjoint primitive-character classes:

\[
 \mathfrak X_{\rm pr}=\{\mathbf1\pmod1\},
 \tag{4.4}
\]

\[
 \mathfrak X_{\rm exc}=\mathfrak X_{\rm exc}(D_1),
 \qquad
 \mathfrak X_{\rm gen}=
 \{\chi^*:1<c<D_1\}\setminus\mathfrak X_{\rm exc}.
 \tag{4.5}
\]

For \(\star\in\{{\rm pr},{\rm gen},{\rm exc}\}\), let

\[
 \begin{aligned}
 \mathfrak M_{\star}
 :={}&H\sum_{\substack{s=ce,\ \mu^2(ce)=1\\(c,e)=1,\ c<D_1}}
 \frac{\lambda_{ce}}{\varphi(ce)}
 \sum_{\chi^*\in\mathfrak X_{\star}\ ({\rm mod}\ c)}
 \tau(\chi^*)\chi^*(e)
 K_{c,e}(\chi^*)W_{c,e}(\chi^*).
 \end{aligned}
 \tag{4.6}
\]

The classes are disjoint and exhaustive, hence

\[
 \boxed{
 \mathfrak M_{<D_1}^{\rm tr}
 =\mathfrak M_{\rm pr}
 +\mathfrak M_{\rm gen}
 +\mathfrak M_{\rm exc}.}
 \tag{4.7}
\]

Put

\[
 \mathfrak C_{\rm pr}:=\mathfrak M_{\rm pr}-\mathfrak L_x^{\rm pf},
 \qquad
 \mathfrak C_{\rm gen}:=\mathfrak M_{\rm gen},
 \qquad
 \mathfrak C_{\rm exc}:=\mathfrak M_{\rm exc}.
 \tag{4.8}
\]

Then, before the first outer absolute,

\[
 \boxed{
 \mathfrak C_{<D_1}^{\rm ul}
 =\mathfrak C_{\rm pr}
 +\mathfrak C_{\rm gen}
 +\mathfrak C_{\rm exc}.}
 \tag{4.9}
\]

## 5. Which theorem is actually selected

The primary theorem contract is the one-scalar statement

\[
 \boxed{
 |\mathfrak C_{\rm pr}
 +\mathfrak C_{\rm gen}
 +\mathfrak C_{\rm exc}|
 \ll x^{1997/1200-\eta_{\rm ul}+o(1)}.}
 \tag{5.1}
\]

It preserves possible cancellation among all three typed lanes. A stronger,
source-friendly fallback would prove separately

\[
 |\mathfrak C_{\rm pr}|
 \ll x^{1997/1200-\eta_{\rm pr}+o(1)},
 \tag{5.2}
\]

\[
 |\mathfrak C_{\rm gen}|
 \ll x^{1997/1200-\eta_{\rm gen}+o(1)},
 \qquad
 |\mathfrak C_{\rm exc}|
 \ll x^{1997/1200-\eta_{\rm exc}+o(1)}.
 \tag{5.3}
\]

The marginal triad implies (5.1) by the triangle inequality, but is not
equivalent to it. It is recorded as an explicit heuristic theorem package,
not as arithmetic progress.

There are three important type firewalls.

1. The principal lane and \(\mathfrak L_x^{\rm pf}\) are related here only by
   the scalar subtraction in (4.8); no occurrencewise or characterwise
   equality is known.
2. An exceptional character is rank one in primitive-conductor type, but its
   induced cofactors \(e\) still form the complete tower in (4.6). Rank one
   does not mean one summand.
3. The prime-deletion device used in some Landau--Page applications changes
   an ensemble. It cannot be inserted after the common \(q\)-shell and
   \(\lambda_s\) have been frozen.

   [*The first moment of primes in arithmetic progressions: Beyond the

   [*Products of primes in arithmetic progressions*,
   arXiv:2301.07679v3](https://arxiv.org/abs/2301.07679), builds a
   multiplicative dense model and explicitly studies a possible quadratic

\[
 \frac{\Lambda(u+2)-b_x^{(z)}(u)}{\log u}
 \overline{\chi^*(u)}c_e(u).
 \tag{6.1}
\]

No checked theorem controls the signed sum (5.1), or all three stronger
marginals, at the required numerator scale. That is the first analytic fatal.

## 8. Sanity checks and finite falsifiers

The checker freezes the following exact facts.

1. The exponents satisfy

   \[
   \frac1{200}<\frac{49}{9600}<\frac1{192},\quad
   \frac{P^2}{D_1}=x^{151/9600},\quad
   P^{3/2}=x^{150/9600}.
   \tag{8.1}
   \]

   Hence the output is \(31951/19200\), the target is
   \(31952/19200\), and the margin is \(1/19200\).

2. In a finite typed scalar take

   \[
   (M_{\rm pr},M_{\rm gen},M_{\rm exc},L_{\rm pf},V_{\geq D_1})
   =(11,-7,5,4,3).
   \tag{8.2}
   \]

   Then \(C_{\rm pr}=7\), \(C_{\rm ul}=5\), and
   \(R_{\rm AP}=C_{\rm ul}+V_{\geq D_1}=8\). The marginal absolute sum is
   \(19\), not \(5\), so triangle reassembly is strictly stronger.

3. Empty and singleton exceptional sets are accepted; a two-character
   exceptional set is rejected.

4. One primitive exceptional label with three induced cofactors and weights
   \((2,-1,4)\) contributes \(5\). This rejects the false substitution
   “primitive rank one = one summand.”

5. Principal and local vectors \((3,1)\) and \((2,2)\) have the same scalar
   sum but differ termwise. This rejects an occurrencewise projection claim.

These are algebra, type, sign, and loss checks only. They do not test an
asymptotic prime theorem.
